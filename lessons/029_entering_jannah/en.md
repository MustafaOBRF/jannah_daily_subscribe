# How the Believers Enter Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the account of Hafsah, may Allah be pleased with her, who asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about the ayah `There is not one of you who will not come to it`, and whom he answered with the ayah that follows it (Muslim 2496), and explain that her question was a search for understanding.
- Set out, in order, the stations on the road to Jannah as the texts describe them: coming to it and passing over, then rescue for those who were mindful of Allah (Maryam 19:71-72); then being held back on the qantarah while wrongs are settled, until the believers are refined and purified (al-Bukhari 2440); then being led to Jannah in throngs (az-Zumar 39:73); then entering it holding on to one another, with faces like the full moon (al-Bukhari 6543).
- Conclude that the believers do not enter Jannah while any claim between them remains outstanding, and connect this to the Prophet's command to clear oneself of wrongs done to others "today" (al-Bukhari 2449).
- Carry out the "Settle It Before the Bridge" activity: identifying one thing still owed to another person, choosing the right way to make it good, completing it by a set date, and noting that it is done without revealing the details.
- Learn by heart the lesson's bedtime du'a, which asks Allah to rescue us along with the mindful, to help us return what we owe to others, and to bring us into Jannah in their company.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Many people picture entering Jannah as a single moment: a door swings open, and in you go. The authentic texts, however, sketch a road with stations laid out in order. First comes the coming-to (*al-wurud*), from which no one is exempt, and then the rescue of those who were mindful of Allah. Next, those who have been rescued are held back on a raised bridge, the qantarah, between Jannah and the Fire, where the wrongs between them are settled until they are refined and purified. Then they are led to Jannah in throngs, each according to its rank, and a vast company of them enters together, holding on to one another.

This lesson stays with those stations alone. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, being the first to reach the gate of Jannah was covered in the lesson on the forerunners. The greeting of the keepers and the angels, the calls and glad tidings of Jannah, and the security of its people together with the slaughter of death each have a lesson of their own still to come. The removal of rancor from the hearts of its people is likewise a separate subject, taken up later.

And the practical question this lesson carries is this: if the wrongs between believers are settled on the qantarah before they enter, what is stopping me from settling them today?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2496" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of Umm Mubashshir: Hafsah's Question About the Coming-To

> عَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رضي الله عنهما، قَالَ: أَخْبَرَتْنِي أُمُّ مُبَشِّرٍ، أَنَّهَا سَمِعَتِ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقُولُ عِنْدَ حَفْصَةَ: **«لَا يَدْخُلُ النَّارَ إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مِنْ أَصْحَابِ الشَّجَرَةِ أَحَدٌ، الَّذِينَ بَايَعُوا تَحْتَهَا»**. قَالَتْ: بَلَى يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ. فَانْتَهَرَهَا، فَقَالَتْ حَفْصَةُ: {وَإِنْ مِنْكُمْ إِلَّا وَارِدُهَا}. فَقَالَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«قَدْ قَالَ اللَّهُ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: {ثُمَّ نُنَجِّي الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا وَنَذَرُ الظَّالِمِينَ فِيهَا جِثِيًّا}»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Jabir ibn Abd Allah, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: Umm Mubashshir told me that she heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say in the presence of Hafsah: **"If Allah wills, not one of the people of the Tree, those who pledged allegiance beneath it, will enter the Fire."** Hafsah said, "Surely they will, O Messenger of Allah!" He rebuked her sharply, and Hafsah recited: `There is not one of you who will not come to it.` The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has also said: `Then We will rescue those who were mindful of Us, and leave the wrongdoers in it, on their knees.`"**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The people of the Tree are those who pledged allegiance to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, beneath the tree on the day of al-Hudaybiyah, in what is known as the Pledge of Ridwan (*Bay'at ar-Ridwan*). An-Nawawi reported from the scholars that the meaning is that not one of them will ever enter it, and that the words "if Allah wills" were said to seek blessing, not out of doubt. He also said that the hadith is evidence for discussion, objection and reply when the aim is to seek guidance, and that this was Hafsah's aim; she did not mean to reject what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had said. And he established that the sound view of the coming-to (*al-wurud*) is that it means passing over the Sirat, a bridge laid across Jahannam: its own people fall into it, and the rest are saved.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Hafsah did not keep her difficulty to herself. She laid it out with an ayah from the Book of Allah, and the answer she received was not an opinion but the very next ayah. So the road to Jannah runs through the coming-to, and safety on it comes through mindfulness of Allah (*taqwa*), not through wishful thinking.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-19-71-72" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### The Two Ayat of the Coming-To and the Rescue

> **{وَإِنْ مِنْكُمْ إِلَّا وَارِدُهَا ۚ كَانَ عَلَىٰ رَبِّكَ حَتْمًا مَقْضِيًّا ۝ ثُمَّ نُنَجِّي الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا وَنَذَرُ الظَّالِمِينَ فِيهَا جِثِيًّا}** [مريم: ٧١-٧٢][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"There is not one of you who will not come to it: that is, with your Lord, a decree that must be fulfilled. Then We will rescue those who were mindful of Us, and leave the wrongdoers in it, on their knees."** (Maryam 19:71-72)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Hatman maqdiyya* means a matter that must come to pass, one that Allah has decreed. *Jithiyya* is the plural of *jathin*, one who sits upon his knees. And the coming-to, according to the view an-Nawawi held to be strongest, is passing over the Sirat.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

These two ayat hold fear and hope together: the coming-to includes everyone, while the rescue belongs to those who were mindful of Allah. That is why a believer never feels secure from Allah's plan, and never despairs of His mercy.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2440" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of the Qantarah: "Once They Have Been Cleansed and Refined"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«إِذَا خَلَصَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ مِنَ النَّارِ حُبِسُوا بِقَنْطَرَةٍ بَيْنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، فَيَتَقَاصُّونَ مَظَالِمَ كَانَتْ بَيْنَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا، حَتَّى إِذَا نُقُّوا وَهُذِّبُوا أُذِنَ لَهُمْ بِدُخُولِ الْجَنَّةِ، فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسُ مُحَمَّدٍ صلى الله عليه وسلم بِيَدِهِ لَأَحَدُهُمْ بِمَسْكَنِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ أَدَلُّ بِمَنْزِلِهِ كَانَ فِي الدُّنْيَا»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"When the believers have come safely through the Fire, they will be held back on a qantarah between Jannah and the Fire, and there they will settle with one another the wrongs that passed between them in the world. Then, once they have been cleansed and refined, they will be given permission to enter Jannah. By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, each of them will find his way to his dwelling in Jannah more surely than he ever found his way home in the world."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Khalasa* means to come through safely after the crossing. *Al-qantarah* is a bridge or a raised place between Jannah and the Fire. *Yataqassun* means that each person who was wronged takes his due from the one who wronged him. Ibn Kathir mentions this holding back in his commentary on the words of Allah, exalted is He, `until, when they reach it`, placing it after the crossing of the Sirat and before arrival at the gates of Jannah.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

These are believers, already rescued, and still they do not go in until the claims between them have been settled. Jannah is a home that no one enters while still owing a brother for a wrong done to him. As for the hadith's closing words about knowing one's dwelling, they were discussed earlier in the lesson on recognizing one's home in Jannah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2449" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Let Him Clear Himself of It Today"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«مَنْ كَانَتْ لَهُ مَظْلَمَةٌ لِأَحَدٍ مِنْ عِرْضِهِ أَوْ شَيْءٍ فَلْيَتَحَلَّلْهُ مِنْهُ الْيَوْمَ، قَبْلَ أَنْ لَا يَكُونَ دِينَارٌ وَلَا دِرْهَمٌ، إِنْ كَانَ لَهُ عَمَلٌ صَالِحٌ أُخِذَ مِنْهُ بِقَدْرِ مَظْلَمَتِهِ، وَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ لَهُ حَسَنَاتٌ أُخِذَ مِنْ سَيِّئَاتِ صَاحِبِهِ فَحُمِلَ عَلَيْهِ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever has wronged someone, whether in his honor or in anything else, let him clear himself of it with that person today, before the day comes when there will be neither dinar nor dirham. If he has righteous deeds, an amount equal to his wrong will be taken from them; and if he has no good deeds left, some of the bad deeds of the one he wronged will be taken and loaded onto him."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Fal-yatahallalhu* means that he should ask the other person to release him from the claim, either by restoring what is owed or by asking forgiveness. "In his honor" (*min 'irdihi*) covers backbiting, mockery and slander, while "or in anything else" (*aw shay'*) covers money, belongings and every other right.

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith of the qantarah describes the settling that takes place there; this hadith points to the easier road: settling it here, "today," while the currency is still that of this world and not good deeds.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-73" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Those Who Were Mindful of Their Lord Will Be Led to Jannah in Throngs"

> **{وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا ۖ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ}** [الزمر: ٧٣][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who were mindful of their Lord will be led to Jannah in throngs, until, when they reach it, and its gates are opened, and its keepers say to them, 'Peace be upon you! You have been purified, so enter it, to abide here forever.'"** (az-Zumar 39:73)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that *zumaran* means one company after another: those brought near to Allah, then the righteous, then those who come after them, each group with those who resemble it: prophets with prophets, the truthful with their like, martyrs with their peers, and scholars with their fellows.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

This lesson pauses at the leading in companies, each according to its deeds. The opening of the gates and the keepers' greeting, `Peace be upon you! You have been purified`, are the subject of the next lesson. Notice how the word *tibtum*, "you have been purified," meets the hadith's "once they have been cleansed and refined": only the pure and good go in.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6543" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Holding On to One Another, Each Taking Hold of the Next"

> عَنْ سَهْلِ بْنِ سَعْدٍ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«لَيَدْخُلَنَّ الْجَنَّةَ مِنْ أُمَّتِي سَبْعُونَ أَلْفًا أَوْ سَبْعُمِائَةِ أَلْفٍ -شَكَّ فِي أَحَدِهِمَا- مُتَمَاسِكِينَ، آخِذٌ بَعْضُهُمْ بِبَعْضٍ، حَتَّى يَدْخُلَ أَوَّلُهُمْ وَآخِرُهُمُ الْجَنَّةَ، وَوُجُوهُهُمْ عَلَى ضَوْءِ الْقَمَرِ لَيْلَةَ الْبَدْرِ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Sahl ibn Sa'd, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Seventy thousand of my ummah, or seven hundred thousand (the narrator was unsure which), will surely enter Jannah holding on to one another, each taking hold of the next, so that the first of them and the last of them enter Jannah together, and their faces will shine like the moon on the night it is full."**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The uncertainty about the number comes from the narrator, Abu Hazim. An-Nawawi explained "holding on to one another" (*mutamasikin*) to mean that each takes another by the hand, and that they enter abreast, in a single row, side by side.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

An entry with no crowding and no selfish scramble: hands joined and faces full of light. And how fitting that it comes after the qantarah: people who once had wrongs between them have become one hand.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. How do you reconcile what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said about the people of the Tree with the ayah of the coming-to, as his answer to Hafsah made clear?
2. What is the difference between an objection meant to refute and an objection meant to seek guidance? And how do you ask about something in the religion that puzzles you?
3. Set out the stations on the road to Jannah in order, as the texts of this lesson present them, with the evidence for each station.
4. Why are the rescued believers held back on the qantarah, even though they are people of Jannah?
5. Which wrong would be easy to put right today but hard to settle tomorrow with good deeds?

### Activity: Settle It Before the Bridge

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.029.activity.settle-it-before-the-bridge" -->

In a private notebook, review four kinds of rights: a debt or a trust you have not returned; something you borrowed; a word that hurt someone, or backbiting; and a right you have neglected toward a relative or a worker. Choose one specific right and decide how you will settle it: returning the money or the item, apologizing directly, or asking to be forgiven. If you fear that telling the person you backbit would cause greater harm, ask people of knowledge about the most fitting course. Set a deadline no more than a week away, and once it is done, write a single line, "Settled on [date]," with no details.

How do the believers get into Jannah? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that there is a way to get there. Everyone will pass along a path, and Allah will keep safe the people who loved Him and obeyed Him.[^2] Then they will stop for a little while at a place just before Jannah. If anyone took something from his brother, or made him sad, the right thing is given back to its owner, until every heart is clean and good.[^3] Then they will walk to Jannah, group after group,[^5] and lots and lots of them will go in together, holding each other's hands, with faces that shine like the moon on the night it is full and round.[^6]

### A True Account: Hafsah Asks About an Ayah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-2496" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened to the Mother of the Believers Hafsah, may Allah be pleased with her. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2496" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was at the home of his wife Hafsah, may Allah be pleased with her. A woman from the Ansar named Umm Mubashshir was there too, and she heard him say about the Companions who had made a promise to him under a tree: "Not one of them will go into the Fire, if Allah wills."

Hafsah said, "But they will, O Messenger of Allah!" The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke to her firmly. Then Hafsah said, "Allah says: `There is not one of you who will not come to it`." That means: everyone will pass by it.

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered her with the very next ayah: `Then We will rescue those who were mindful of Us.` That means: then Allah will keep safe the people who feared Him and obeyed Him.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Hafsah wanted to understand, so she asked with an ayah from the Qur'an, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered her with an ayah from the Qur'an. And we learn that on the way to Jannah, everyone passes by, and Allah keeps safe the people who obey Him.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Is getting into Jannah just one moment? The authentic texts describe a journey with stops along the way:

1. **The crossing:** Everyone passes over the Sirat, above the Fire, and then Allah rescues those who were mindful of Him.[^2]
2. **The qantarah:** The believers who made it across stop on a raised bridge, the qantarah, between Jannah and the Fire. There everyone who was wronged takes back what he is owed from the one who wronged him, until they are cleansed and refined, and only then are they given permission to go in.[^3]
3. **The groups:** Those who were mindful of Allah are led to Jannah *zumaran*, which means one group after another.[^5]
4. **Going in together:** A huge crowd from this ummah goes in holding on to one another, each taking another by the hand, with faces as bright as the moon on the night it is full.[^6]

Here is the amazing part: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about a way that is much easier than waiting for the qantarah. Whoever has wronged someone, by hurting his good name or by taking something that was his, should ask that person to forgive him "today," before a day when there is no money, only good deeds and bad deeds.[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2496" -->

- **`The coming-to (al-wurud)`** — passing over the Sirat, which is stretched across Jahannam; this is the meaning the scholars considered strongest in explaining the ayah.[^7]
- **`The people of the Tree (ashab ash-shajarah)`** — the Companions who pledged allegiance to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, beneath the tree on the day of al-Hudaybiyah.
- **`The qantarah (al-qantarah)`** — a bridge or raised place between Jannah and the Fire, where the believers are held back before they enter.
- **`A wrong (mazlimah)`** — something you took from someone else without any right to it, whether it was money or a hurtful word.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: A Question in Hafsah's Home

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-2496" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account recorded by Imam Muslim in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2496" audience="8-12" -->

In the home of the Mother of the Believers Hafsah, daughter of Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, a woman of the Ansar named Umm Mubashshir was present. She heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"If Allah wills, not one of the people of the Tree, those who pledged allegiance beneath it, will enter the Fire."** The people of the Tree were the ones who had pledged allegiance to him on the day of al-Hudaybiyah.

Hafsah had an ayah in mind that seemed to her to say something different, so she said, "Surely they will, O Messenger of Allah!" The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, rebuked her, meaning he corrected her firmly. But she did not answer with an opinion of her own. Instead, she recited: `There is not one of you who will not come to it`; the ayah says that every single person will come to it.

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered her with the words of Allah, too: **"Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has also said: `Then We will rescue those who were mindful of Us, and leave the wrongdoers in it, on their knees.`"**[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Imam an-Nawawi said that Hafsah wanted to understand and to be guided, not to reject what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had said, and that the coming-to means passing over the Sirat.[^7] Put the two ayat together and they explain the road: everyone crosses, and those who were mindful of Allah are rescued. The people of the Tree are among the mindful, so they cross over and do not enter the Fire.

<!-- story:end -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. Which ayah did Hafsah ask about? And which ayah did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answer her with?
2. How do we know that Hafsah was asking in order to understand?
3. Put the four stops in order: the groups, the crossing, going in together, the qantarah.
4. Why do the believers enter Jannah only after the wrongs between them have been put right?
5. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say "today"?

### Activity: Settle It Before the Bridge

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.029.activity.settle-it-before-the-bridge" -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a culture of instant access, we imagine Jannah as a gate you get through with one tap. Revelation describes a road instead: a coming-to that no one skips, and then the rescue of those who were mindful of Allah.[^2] Then a raised bridge, the qantarah, where the rescued believers are held back and the wrongs between them are settled until they are cleansed and refined, and only after that are they given permission to enter.[^3] Then they are led in throngs, each group with those who are like it in their deeds,[^5] and a vast company of them goes in holding on to one another, hand in hand, with faces like the full moon.[^6]

Notice the twist: even the people who made it across the Sirat do not go in while there is unfinished business between them. That is why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that whoever has wronged someone, in his honor or in anything else, should clear himself of it with that person today, before there is neither dinar nor dirham, only good deeds taken away or bad deeds loaded on.[^4] And wrongs today are not only about money: a sarcastic comment, a screenshot that got shared, a rumor you forwarded.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2440" -->

- **`Refined and cleansed (hudhdhibu wa nuqqu)`** — purified and cleared of the consequences of the wrongs between them, so that no one enters Jannah still owing a brother his due.
- **`Settling scores (yataqassun)`** — each person who was wronged takes his due from the one who wronged him, and the settling there is paid in good deeds and bad deeds.[^4]
- **`Clearing yourself (at-tahallul)`** — asking the person you owe to release you from the claim, either because you have given back what is theirs or because they have forgiven you.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: When Hafsah Pushed Back with an Ayah

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-2496" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by Jabir ibn Abd Allah from Umm Mubashshir al-Ansariyyah in Sahih Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2496" audience="13+" -->

The setting is the home of the Mother of the Believers Hafsah, daughter of Umar. Also present is a woman of the Ansar, Umm Mubashshir, who hears the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say about those who pledged allegiance to him beneath the tree on the day of al-Hudaybiyah: **"If Allah wills, not one of the people of the Tree, those who pledged allegiance beneath it, will enter the Fire."**

Hafsah says, "Surely they will, O Messenger of Allah!" He rebukes her sharply. It was not an easy moment, but she neither backed down nor dug in out of stubbornness; she put forward what was on her mind as evidence, not opinion: `There is not one of you who will not come to it.`

And the answer came from the very same place in the mushaf: **"Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has also said: `Then We will rescue those who were mindful of Us, and leave the wrongdoers in it, on their knees.`"**[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

An-Nawawi said that the words "if Allah wills" were said to seek blessing, not out of doubt; that the hadith is evidence for objection and reply when the aim is to seek guidance, which was Hafsah's aim; and that the coming-to means passing over the Sirat.[^7] The difficulty was not resolved by shutting the question down, but by reading the ayah together with the one that follows it. And that is how the road to Jannah is understood: no confidence without deeds, and no fear without hope.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What do you do when you hear something about the religion that seems to clash with an ayah you know? What can you learn from the way Hafsah handled it?
2. Why was answering with the very next ayah more powerful than any other explanation?
3. What does holding back the rescued believers on the qantarah reveal about how much people's rights weigh with Allah?
4. Name three "digital" wrongs that young people might not even count as wrongs. How would you clear yourself of each one?
5. Why might apologizing today be harder on the ego than almost anything else? And what makes it easier?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Settle It Before the Bridge

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.029.activity.settle-it-before-the-bridge" -->

Open a private page with three columns: "What I owe," "How I'll clear it," and "Deadline." Look over your offline life and your online life together: something you borrowed, a little money, a sarcastic comment, a photo you posted without permission, a rumor you shared. Pick one and make it right within 72 hours: delete what you posted, return what you took, or apologize directly, in one sentence with no excuses attached. Do not contact anyone who hurts you or who is not safe to be around; talk to a parent or a teacher you trust instead. When it's done, write one word on the page, "Settled," and then one line about what changed inside you.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the account of Hafsah in sequence and explains the Prophet's answer with the next ayah; sets out the four stations on the road to Jannah in order, with the evidence for each; explains what it means for the believers to be held back on the qantarah; and draws up a plan, with a fixed date, to clear himself or herself of one right owed to another.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Printed copies of the six pieces of evidence; a mushaf for Surat Maryam and Surat az-Zumar; a board with a horizontal line divided into four stations; private notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the hadith in Sahih Muslim (Book of the Virtues of the Companions, Chapter on the Virtues of the People of the Tree) with an-Nawawi's commentary, Ibn Kathir's tafsir of az-Zumar 39:73, and the Chapter on Retribution for Wrongs in Sahih al-Bukhari. The teacher also prepares a brief answer to the question: "How does someone clear himself of backbiting if telling the person concerned would cause harm?" and refers the detailed legal ruling to people of knowledge.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Describe the moment you enter Jannah, the way you picture it." Then say: "Today we will see that the texts describe a road, not a moment, and that one of its stations touches our everyday relationships."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Umm Mubashshir in full, then the two ayat of Surat Maryam, and ask: "What puzzled Hafsah? And how was the difficulty resolved?" Then read the hadith of the qantarah and the hadith of clearing oneself one after the other, followed by the ayah of az-Zumar and the hadith of Sahl ibn Sa'd. After each text, participants place it at its station on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Lead a guided discussion using the third and fourth questions in the questions unit. The teacher stresses: the coming-to includes everyone, and rescue comes through mindfulness of Allah; the qantarah is for believers who have already been rescued; and wrongs are settled there in good deeds, while here they can be settled far more easily. The teacher also marks the limits of the lesson: the greeting of the angels, the glad tidings, the security of the people of Jannah, and the removal of rancor each come in their own lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Carry out the "Settle It Before the Bridge" activity as described in the activity unit: reviewing the four kinds of rights, choosing one right, and deciding how and by when it will be settled. Each person works in a private notebook, and no one is asked to share details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: (1) Set out the four stations in order, with one piece of evidence for each. (2) What does the coming-to mean, according to the view an-Nawawi held to be strongest? (3) Write down the date you set for settling the right. Close with a question for the next session: "Was it settled?"

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the hadith of Umm Mubashshir, the hadith of the qantarah, and the hadith of clearing oneself. Ask advanced learners to read al-Bukhari's narration of the same hadith at number 6535 and compare its wording with narration 2440, and to review what Ibn al-Qayyim wrote in *Hadi al-Arwah* about how the people of Jannah enter it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the Sirat or the Fire with frightening details that are not in the texts of this lesson. Do not ask anyone to confess a wrong publicly. Point out that returning what is owed does not mean contacting someone who is abusive or dangerous, and that difficult questions about clearing oneself should be taken to people of knowledge.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child retells the account in three sentences; says that Hafsah asked with an ayah and that the answer was an ayah; puts pictures in order: passing along the path, then stopping to give back what is owed, then going in together; and gives something back, or says sorry, for one thing.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Tape on the floor to mark a path; three picture cards (a path, a little bridge, joined hands); paper and child-safe colors; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account calmly and prepares the three cards. No pictures of the Fire, or of any prophet or Companion, are used.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask: "When we go on a trip, do we get there right away, or do we travel along a road first?" Then say: "Today we're going to learn about the way the believers go to Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Account — 6 minutes:** The caregiver tells the account as given in the story unit, holding up one finger at the first ayah and two fingers at the second, and the children repeat: "Then We will rescue those who were mindful of Us."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 5 minutes:** The children walk along the tape, stop at the bridge card, and each child says: "I give back what belongs to others." Then they hold hands and walk together to the end of the path.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Carry out the "Settle It Before the Bridge" activity as described in the activity unit: drawing the bridge and choosing one thing to fix today, with a parent's help.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The child puts the three cards in order and says what he or she will give back, or to whom he or she will say sorry. Then read the bedtime du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, two cards are enough: the bridge and the joined hands. Ask older children to tell the account to a brother or sister.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not frighten the child with the Fire; keep the focus on Allah keeping safe those who obey Him. Do not make a child apologize to someone he or she is afraid of, and tell the parents if the child says anything suggesting that he or she is being harmed.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the account in order, explains the four terms, puts the four stops in order with the evidence for each, and prepares a Bridge Card with one thing owed, a way to fix it, and a date.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Four large cards for the stops; small cards for the evidence; card stock for the Bridge Card; notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the four stops and the pieces of evidence on separate cards, and prepares age-appropriate everyday examples of small wrongs.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Can a team walk onto the field while two of its players are still in a fight that hasn't been sorted out? What happens?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Account — 10 minutes:** Read the story. The teacher pauses at Hafsah's words "Surely they will!" and asks: "What did Hafsah mean?" Then the teacher reads an-Nawawi's explanation, and the terms are explained as they come up.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Play the ordering game: groups put the cards of the stops in order and match each piece of evidence to its stop. Then the class discusses the fourth and fifth questions in the questions unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Carry out the "Settle It Before the Bridge" activity as described in the activity unit. The teacher checks that each student has chosen one specific thing, a clear way to fix it, and a date within three days, without writing any names.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes the four stops in order, the meaning of "the qantarah," and why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said "today." Then read the du'a, and at the next session ask who has completed their card.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, present the stops as four pictures with no faces, and focus on just two of them: the qantarah and going in. Ask advanced students to memorize the hadith of clearing oneself and explain it to a classmate.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask any student to say in front of the class who wronged them or whom they wronged. Be mindful of a student who is being bullied: this lesson does not ask them to apologize to the person hurting them, and the matter should be referred to the responsible staff member.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes how Hafsah went about asking her question, explains how the ayah of the coming-to and the ayah of the rescue fit together, explains why the believers are held back on the qantarah, and applies the hadith of clearing oneself to real-life and online wrongs with a time-bound plan.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The texts of the hadith of Umm Mubashshir, the hadith of the qantarah, and the hadith of clearing oneself from the adults' academic section; the two ayat of Surat Maryam; a three-column page; private notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares three neutral examples of online wrongs (a sarcastic comment, a photo posted without permission, a rumor), reviews an-Nawawi's commentary on the hadith, and prepares a calm answer to a likely question about fear of the Sirat.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Have you ever heard something about the religion and felt it clashed with an ayah? What did you do?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Students read the hadith in small groups, and each group identifies: the difficulty, how it was raised, and how it was resolved. Then read the hadith of the qantarah and the hadith of clearing oneself, and ask: "How are these two connected?"

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Lead a discussion using the questions in the questions unit, focusing on the first and fourth. The teacher makes clear the difference between asking in order to be guided and arguing in order to refute, and that wrongs include harm to people's honor, not just to their money.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Carry out the "Settle It Before the Bridge" activity as described in the activity unit: filling in the private page, choosing one right, and setting a deadline within 72 hours.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes a three-sentence paragraph: what they learned from Hafsah's question, what the qantarah means, and what their step toward clearing themselves is. At the next session, ask a general question with no details: "Is it settled?"

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, focus only on the hadith of Umm Mubashshir and the hadith of clearing oneself. Ask advanced students to prepare a short presentation, drawn from the books of sirah, on the Pledge of Ridwan and what led to it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The pages remain private, and the teacher does not collect them. Remind students not to contact anyone who is abusive or unsafe, and to involve a parent or a teacher they trust when needed. Do not inflate fear of the Sirat: the very same ayah gives the glad tidings that the mindful will be rescued.
