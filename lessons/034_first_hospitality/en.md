# The Welcome Gift and First Meal of Those Who Enter Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate, in sequence, Thawban's account (may Allah be pleased with him) of the Jewish rabbi who came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him (Muslim 315): the shove and the matter of the name; the question "Will it benefit you if I tell you?"; the questions about crossing, the welcome gift, the meal, and the drink; and finally the Prophet's statement that he had known none of it until Allah brought it to him.
- Explain that the first honor given to the people of Jannah when they enter is a welcome gift, the extra lobe of the fish's liver; that the bull of Jannah is then slaughtered for them; and that they drink from a spring called Salsabil, supporting this with the hadith of Abd Allah ibn Salam (al-Bukhari 3938).
- Explain the meanings of "welcome gift" (*tuhfah*), "hospitality" (*nuzul*), and "the extra lobe of the liver" (*ziyadat al-kabid*) from an-Nawawi's commentary, and connect them to the words of Allah `a welcome from One Most Forgiving, Most Merciful` and as-Sa'di's tafsir of them.
- Affirm what is stated in the hadith "The earth will be a single loaf of bread" (al-Bukhari 6520) just as it came, without asking how and without likening Allah to His creation, and without adding anything to the text when describing the unseen.
- Draw from the account the etiquette of asking and answering: gentleness toward the one who asks, seeking knowledge that benefits, and honesty in attributing knowledge to revelation.
- Carry out the "Welcome in Three Stations" activity: receiving a real guest with a welcome gift prepared in advance and offered on arrival, then food, then drink, while observing the effect and reflecting on it.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to make Jannah our place of welcome and to help us honor our own guests.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

When a dear guest arrives after a long journey, a generous host does not wait for them to sit down and ask for something. He meets them at the door with something good, prepared before they ever arrived. That familiar human instinct opens the door to this lesson. Once the people of Jannah have crossed the Sirat and entered Jannah, they are received with a hospitality the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described in remarkable detail: first a welcome gift, then a meal after it, then a drink. And this report came as the answer to questions from a learned Jewish rabbi, in a scene that also teaches us how to ask about the unseen and how to answer.

This lesson follows the lessons on entering Jannah, on the reception, and on the glad tidings, and it pauses at a single moment: the very first thing offered to the people of Jannah. The food and drink of Jannah in general have their own lesson later in the series.

### The Hadith of Thawban: "What Will Be Their Welcome Gift When They Enter Jannah?"

> عَنْ ثَوْبَانَ مَوْلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **كُنْتُ قَائِمًا عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ، فَجَاءَ حَبْرٌ مِنْ أَحْبَارِ الْيَهُودِ فَقَالَ: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ يَا مُحَمَّدُ، فَدَفَعْتُهُ دَفْعَةً كَادَ يُصْرَعُ مِنْهَا، فَقَالَ: لِمَ تَدْفَعُنِي؟ فَقُلْتُ: أَلَا تَقُولُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟ فَقَالَ الْيَهُودِيُّ: إِنَّمَا نَدْعُوهُ بِاسْمِهِ الَّذِي سَمَّاهُ بِهِ أَهْلُهُ، فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «إِنَّ اسْمِي مُحَمَّدٌ الَّذِي سَمَّانِي بِهِ أَهْلِي». فَقَالَ الْيَهُودِيُّ: جِئْتُ أَسْأَلُكَ، فَقَالَ لَهُ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «أَيَنْفَعُكَ شَيْءٌ إِنْ حَدَّثْتُكَ؟» قَالَ: أَسْمَعُ بِأُذُنَيَّ، فَنَكَتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ بِعُودٍ مَعَهُ، فَقَالَ: «سَلْ». فَقَالَ الْيَهُودِيُّ: أَيْنَ يَكُونُ النَّاسُ يَوْمَ تُبَدَّلُ الْأَرْضُ غَيْرَ الْأَرْضِ وَالسَّمَاوَاتُ؟ فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «هُمْ فِي الظُّلْمَةِ دُونَ الْجِسْرِ». قَالَ: فَمَنْ أَوَّلُ النَّاسِ إِجَازَةً؟ قَالَ: «فُقَرَاءُ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ». قَالَ الْيَهُودِيُّ: فَمَا تُحْفَتُهُمْ حِينَ يَدْخُلُونَ الْجَنَّةَ؟ قَالَ: «زِيَادَةُ كَبِدِ النُّونِ». قَالَ: فَمَا غِذَاؤُهُمْ عَلَى إِثْرِهَا؟ قَالَ: «يُنْحَرُ لَهُمْ ثَوْرُ الْجَنَّةِ الَّذِي كَانَ يَأْكُلُ مِنْ أَطْرَافِهَا». قَالَ: فَمَا شَرَابُهُمْ عَلَيْهِ؟ قَالَ: «مِنْ عَيْنٍ فِيهَا تُسَمَّى سَلْسَبِيلًا». قَالَ: صَدَقْتَ. قَالَ: وَجِئْتُ أَسْأَلُكَ عَنْ شَيْءٍ لَا يَعْلَمُهُ أَحَدٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا نَبِيٌّ أَوْ رَجُلٌ أَوْ رَجُلَانِ.** [ثُمَّ سَأَلَهُ عَنْ مَسْأَلَةٍ فِي شَأْنِ الْوَلَدِ فَأَجَابَهُ، وَهِيَ خَارِجَ مَوْضُوعِ الدَّرْسِ] **قَالَ الْيَهُودِيُّ: لَقَدْ صَدَقْتَ، وَإِنَّكَ لَنَبِيٌّ، ثُمَّ انْصَرَفَ فَذَهَبَ. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «لَقَدْ سَأَلَنِي هَذَا عَنِ الَّذِي سَأَلَنِي عَنْهُ، وَمَا لِي عِلْمٌ بِشَيْءٍ مِنْهُ حَتَّى أَتَانِيَ اللَّهُ بِهِ»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Thawban, the freed servant of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **I was standing beside the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, when one of the rabbis of the Jews came and said, "Peace be upon you, O Muhammad." I gave him such a shove that he nearly fell to the ground. He said, "Why are you pushing me?" I said, "Why don't you say, 'O Messenger of Allah'?" The Jewish man said, "We only call him by the name his family gave him." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "My name is Muhammad, the name my family gave me." The Jewish man said, "I have come to ask you some questions." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: "Will anything benefit you if I tell you?" He said, "I will listen with both my ears." So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, scratched the ground with a stick he had with him, and said: "Ask." The Jewish man said, "Where will people be on the Day when the earth is changed into another earth, and the heavens as well?" The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "They will be in the darkness, this side of the Bridge." He said, "Then who will be the first people to cross?" He said: "The poor among the Emigrants." The Jewish man said, "And what will be their welcome gift when they enter Jannah?" He said: "The extra lobe of the fish's liver." He said, "And what will be their meal after it?" He said: "The bull of Jannah, which used to graze on its outer reaches, will be slaughtered for them." He said, "And what will they drink with it?" He said: "From a spring in it named Salsabil." He said, "You have spoken the truth." He said, "And I have come to ask you about something that no one among the people of the earth knows except a prophet, or one man or two."** [He then asked him about a matter concerning the child, and he answered him; it lies outside the subject of this lesson.] **The Jewish man said, "You have indeed spoken the truth, and you are indeed a prophet." Then he turned and went away. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "This man asked me about what he asked me, and I had no knowledge of any of it until Allah brought it to me."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, explains: a *habr* is a learned man; "he scratched with a stick" (*nakata bi-'ud*) means he traced lines with it on the ground, "and this is what a person deep in thought does"; "the Bridge" (*al-jisr*) here is the Sirat; *ijazah* means passing and crossing over; the *tuhfah* is "what is given to a man as a gift, singled out for him, as a mark of kindness"; *an-nun* is the fish; and "*ziyadah* and *za'idah* are one and the same thing: the tip of the liver, which is its choicest part."[^5] Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, devoted a whole chapter to this hadith, which he titled: "On the welcome gift of the people of Jannah when they enter it."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith maps out the order of the hospitality: a welcome gift on entry, then a meal "after it," then a drink. This is an honor prepared before the guests arrive, not food to stave off hunger. The account also carries two points of etiquette. The first is the gentleness of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, toward a questioner who addressed him by his bare name: he did not grow angry, but simply stated that his name was Muhammad. The second is his question, "Will anything benefit you if I tell you?", a reminder that the purpose of knowledge is to benefit from it. And he closed, peace and blessings be upon him, by declaring that his knowledge of these things came from Allah alone, for the unseen is received only through revelation.

### The First Food the People of Jannah Will Eat

> عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ عَبْدَ اللَّهِ بْنَ سَلَامٍ بَلَغَهُ مَقْدَمُ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ الْمَدِينَةَ، فَأَتَاهُ يَسْأَلُهُ عَنْ أَشْيَاءَ، فَقَالَ: **إِنِّي سَائِلُكَ عَنْ ثَلَاثٍ لَا يَعْلَمُهُنَّ إِلَّا نَبِيٌّ: مَا أَوَّلُ أَشْرَاطِ السَّاعَةِ؟ وَمَا أَوَّلُ طَعَامٍ يَأْكُلُهُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ؟ وَمَا بَالُ الْوَلَدِ يَنْزِعُ إِلَى أَبِيهِ أَوْ إِلَى أُمِّهِ؟ قَالَ: «أَخْبَرَنِي بِهِ جِبْرِيلُ آنِفًا»... قَالَ: «أَمَّا أَوَّلُ أَشْرَاطِ السَّاعَةِ فَنَارٌ تَحْشُرُهُمْ مِنَ الْمَشْرِقِ إِلَى الْمَغْرِبِ، وَأَمَّا أَوَّلُ طَعَامٍ يَأْكُلُهُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ فَزِيَادَةُ كَبِدِ الْحُوتِ»** [ثُمَّ أَجَابَهُ عَنْ شَبَهِ الْوَلَدِ] **قَالَ: أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَأَنَّكَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that when news reached Abd Allah ibn Salam that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had arrived in Madinah, he came to him to ask him about certain things. He said: **"I am going to ask you about three things that no one knows except a prophet: What is the first of the signs of the Hour? What is the first food the people of Jannah will eat? And why does a child take after its father, or after its mother?" He said: "Jibril told me about them just now."... He said: "As for the first of the signs of the Hour, it is a fire that will drive people together from the east to the west. And as for the first food the people of Jannah will eat, it is the extra lobe of the fish's liver."** [He then answered him about a child's resemblance to its parents.] **He said: "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and that you are the Messenger of Allah."**[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

A second question, from a different questioner, at a different time, and the answer is the same: the first food of the people of Jannah is the extra lobe of the fish's liver. This answer was one of the reasons Abd Allah ibn Salam, the leading Jewish scholar of Madinah, embraced Islam. The agreement of the two reports confirms the meaning, and it shows that, among the People of the Book, this question was counted among the things only a prophet could know.

### The Welcome Feast of the People of Jannah

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: **«تَكُونُ الْأَرْضُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ خُبْزَةً وَاحِدَةً، يَتَكَفَّؤُهَا الْجَبَّارُ بِيَدِهِ، كَمَا يَكْفَأُ أَحَدُكُمْ خُبْزَتَهُ فِي السَّفَرِ، نُزُلًا لِأَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ». فَأَتَى رَجُلٌ مِنَ الْيَهُودِ فَقَالَ: بَارَكَ الرَّحْمَنُ عَلَيْكَ يَا أَبَا الْقَاسِمِ، أَلَا أُخْبِرُكَ بِنُزُلِ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ؟ قَالَ: «بَلَى». قَالَ: تَكُونُ الْأَرْضُ خُبْزَةً وَاحِدَةً، كَمَا قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ، فَنَظَرَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِلَيْنَا ثُمَّ ضَحِكَ حَتَّى بَدَتْ نَوَاجِذُهُ، ثُمَّ قَالَ: أَلَا أُخْبِرُكَ بِإِدَامِهِمْ؟ قَالَ: إِدَامُهُمْ بَالَامٌ وَنُونٌ، قَالُوا: وَمَا هَذَا؟ قَالَ: ثَوْرٌ وَنُونٌ، يَأْكُلُ مِنْ زَائِدَةِ كَبِدِهِمَا سَبْعُونَ أَلْفًا**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"On the Day of Resurrection the earth will be a single loaf of bread, which the Compeller will turn over with His Hand, just as one of you turns over his flatbread when traveling, as a welcome feast for the people of Jannah." Then a man from among the Jews came and said, "May the Most Merciful bless you, O Abu al-Qasim! Shall I not tell you about the welcome feast of the people of Jannah on the Day of Resurrection?" He said: "Yes, do." He said, "The earth will be a single loaf of bread," just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had said. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, looked at us and then laughed until his back teeth showed. Then he said, "Shall I not tell you what they will eat with it?" He said, "What they will eat with it is *balam* and *nun*." They said, "And what is that?" He said, "A bull and a fish; seventy thousand will eat from the extra lobe of their livers."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, explains that *nuzul* is "what is prepared for a guest when he arrives"; that *balam* is a Hebrew word meaning "bull"; and that the extra lobe of the liver is "the separate piece that hangs from the liver, which is its choicest part." He also relates two possibilities that al-Qadi 'Iyad mentioned concerning the seventy thousand: that they are those who enter Jannah without reckoning, "and so they were singled out for the choicest part of the welcome feast," or that the number simply expresses a great multitude.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed because the Jewish man's words matched what he himself had said. We affirm what is stated in the hadith, including the attribute of the Hand for Allah, exalted is He, in the way that befits His majesty, for `There is nothing whatsoever like Him`; we do so without asking how and without likening Him to anything, and we add nothing to the text in describing the unseen.

### "A Welcome from One Most Forgiving, Most Merciful"

> **{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ قَالُوا رَبُّنَا اللَّهُ ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا وَأَبْشِرُوا بِالْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي كُنْتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ ۝ نَحْنُ أَوْلِيَاؤُكُمْ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ ۖ وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِي أَنْفُسُكُمْ وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا مَا تَدَّعُونَ ۝ نُزُلًا مِنْ غَفُورٍ رَحِيمٍ}** [فصلت: ٣٠-٣٢][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, those who say, 'Our Lord is Allah,' and then stand firm: the angels descend upon them, saying, 'Do not fear and do not grieve, but rejoice in the glad tidings of the Jannah you were promised. We are your allies in the life of this world and in the Hereafter. In it you will have whatever your souls desire, and in it you will have whatever you ask for: a welcome from One Most Forgiving, Most Merciful.'"** (Fussilat 41:30-32)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, explains that this abundant reward is "a welcome and a hospitality `from One Most Forgiving`, who forgave you your misdeeds, `Most Merciful`, in that He granted you success in doing good deeds and then accepted them from you. By His forgiveness He removed from you what you feared, and by His mercy He gave you what you sought."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Allah called the whole of Jannah's bliss a *nuzul*, a hospitality laid out for an arriving guest. The welcome gift in Thawban's hadith is a miniature of something far greater: the people of Jannah are the guests of the Most Merciful, and the One who hosts them is Most Forgiving, Most Merciful. And the condition for this hospitality, according to the ayah, is faith followed by standing firm.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between the "welcome gift" and the "meal" in Thawban's hadith, and what does their order signify?
2. How did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, deal with a rabbi who called him by his bare name, and with Thawban's impulsive shove?
3. What does the question "Will anything benefit you if I tell you?" teach us about our intention in seeking knowledge?
4. Why is the closing statement of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, "I had no knowledge of any of it until Allah brought it to me," a governing principle whenever we speak about the unseen?
5. How does as-Sa'di bring together the names "Most Forgiving" and "Most Merciful" in explaining the meaning of *nuzul*?
6. Who was the last guest you welcomed? Did you prepare anything for them before they arrived?

When Grandma comes to visit, what does your mom do at the door? She smiles and says, "Welcome, welcome!" Then she gives Grandma something nice, like a date and a glass of water. She got it all ready before Grandma even arrived, because she loves her and wants her to be happy. Allah honors the people of Jannah in a much, much greater way. When they come into Jannah, they are given a first lovely treat that Allah has made ready for them. This very first present has a special name: the welcome gift.

### A True Account: The Scholar Who Came with Questions

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-315" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. A Companion named Thawban, may Allah be pleased with him, told us about it. It is not a made-up story.**

Thawban was standing next to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Along came one of the scholars of the Jews, and he said, "Peace be upon you, O Muhammad."

Thawban gave him a hard push, and the man almost fell over! Thawban said to him, "Why don't you say, 'O Messenger of Allah'?" The scholar said, "We call him by the name his family gave him."

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered calmly: "My name is Muhammad. That is the name my family gave me."

Then the scholar said, "I have come to ask you some questions." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him, "Will it help you if I tell you?" The man said, "I will listen with both my ears."

So the scholar asked lots of questions. One of them was: "What is the very first present the people of Jannah get when they come in?"

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The extra piece of the fish's liver." It is a small piece at the tip of a fish's liver, and scholars say it is the tastiest part of all.[^5]

The man asked, "And what do they eat after that?" He said: "The bull of Jannah, which used to graze all around its edges, is slaughtered for them."

The man asked, "And what do they drink?" He said: "From a spring in Jannah called Salsabil."

The scholar said, "You have told the truth." Then he asked one more question, the Prophet answered it, and the man went on his way.

After he left, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that he had not known any of this until Allah taught it to him.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-315" audience="4-7" -->

So here is what it means: the people of Jannah are honored guests. They are welcomed with a first present, then food, then a drink. Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, knew this because Allah told him. And our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, teaches us to answer people's questions calmly and kindly.[^1]

### A True Account: "What Will Be Their Welcome Gift When They Enter Jannah?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-315" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Thawban, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

Thawban, the freed servant of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was standing beside him. Then a *habr*, one of the Jewish rabbis (that is, one of their scholars), came up and said, "Peace be upon you, O Muhammad."

Thawban shoved him so hard that he almost fell. The man said, "Why are you pushing me?" Thawban said, "Why don't you say, 'O Messenger of Allah'?" The rabbi said, "We only call him by the name his family gave him."

That was when the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke, calmly: "My name is Muhammad, the name my family gave me." He did not turn it into a quarrel.

The rabbi said, "I have come to ask you some questions." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Will anything benefit you if I tell you?" The man said, "I will listen with both my ears." So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, traced lines on the ground with a stick he had with him, the way someone does when they are thinking,[^5] and then said, "Ask."

The rabbi asked: Where will people be on the Day the earth and the heavens are changed? He said: "In the darkness, this side of the Bridge," that is, before the Sirat. Then he asked: Who will be the first people to cross? He said: "The poor among the Emigrants."

Then came the question this lesson is about: "And what will be their welcome gift when they enter Jannah?" He said: "The extra lobe of the fish's liver." The man asked, "And what will be their meal after it?" He said: "The bull of Jannah, which used to graze on its outer reaches, will be slaughtered for them." He asked, "And what will they drink with it?" He said: "From a spring in it named Salsabil." The rabbi said, "You have spoken the truth."

Then he asked him about another matter, and the Prophet answered him. The man said, "You have indeed spoken the truth, and you are indeed a prophet," and he left. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "This man asked me about what he asked me, and I had no knowledge of any of it until Allah brought it to me."[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-315" audience="8-12" -->

To put it another way: the people of Jannah are received with a welcome gift, then a meal, then a drink, like guests whose host has prepared everything before they arrive. And when Abd Allah ibn Salam asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Madinah about the first food of the people of Jannah, he gave him the very same answer, and that was one of the reasons Abd Allah ibn Salam became Muslim.[^1][^2]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A classmate from another faith might ask you, or someone might comment under a post, "So what are you all going to eat in your Paradise?" in a tone that is testing you or mocking you. You have three possible responses: get worked up and defend yourself aggressively, stay quiet because you don't know, or answer calmly from a reliable source. This lesson contains a real scene in which a scholar from among the Jewish rabbis tested the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with questions about the unseen, including this one: What is the first honor given to the people of Jannah? The answer itself is astonishing, but the way it was answered is a second lesson that matters just as much.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-315" -->

- **`Rabbi (habr)`** — a learned scholar; the word was used for the scholars of the Jews.[^5]
- **`Welcome gift (tuhfah)`** — a present singled out for a guest as a mark of kindness; here, it is the first thing offered to the people of Jannah.[^5]
- **`Hospitality (nuzul)`** — what is prepared for a guest on arrival; Allah has called the whole of Jannah's bliss a *nuzul*.[^6]
- **`Salsabil`** — the name of a spring in Jannah; among the meanings given for it are "swift-flowing" and "smooth and gentle to drink."[^5]

### A True Account: The Rabbi's Questions and the Prophet's Answer, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-315" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated in Sahih Muslim by Thawban, may Allah be pleased with him, the freed servant of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not an imagined scene.**

Thawban is the freed servant of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, always at his side and seeing to his needs. A rabbi from among the Jews walks in and says, "Peace be upon you, O Muhammad." Thawban shoves him so hard he nearly goes down, and says, "Why don't you say, 'O Messenger of Allah'?" The rabbi replies, "We only call him by the name his family gave him."

Notice how the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, handles it. He doesn't demand a title for himself, and he doesn't escalate. He just says, "My name is Muhammad, the name my family gave me." Thawban's words make it clear that he meant to honor the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, but what actually opened the door to conversation was the Prophet's calm, not the shove.

The rabbi says, "I have come to ask you some questions." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asks him, "Will anything benefit you if I tell you?" It is a question that brings intentions into the open: are you asking to benefit, or to argue? The man says, "I will listen with both my ears." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, scratches the ground with a stick, then says, "Ask."

The questions come one after another. Where will people be on the Day the earth is changed? "In the darkness, this side of the Bridge." Who will be the first to cross? "The poor among the Emigrants." What will be their welcome gift when they enter Jannah? "The extra lobe of the fish's liver." And their meal after it? "The bull of Jannah, which used to graze on its outer reaches, will be slaughtered for them." And their drink? "From a spring in it named Salsabil." The rabbi says, "You have spoken the truth."

Then he asks about another matter, one he says no one on earth knows except a prophet, or one man or two. He gets his answer, says, "You have indeed spoken the truth, and you are indeed a prophet," and leaves.

And then comes the final line: "This man asked me about what he asked me, and I had no knowledge of any of it until Allah brought it to me."[^1] The most knowledgeable of all creation declares, in front of his Companions, that his knowledge of the unseen does not come from himself.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-315" audience="13+" -->

In other words: a scholar tested the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with questions about the unseen. The Prophet met him with gentleness, asked him about his intention, and answered him with what Allah had revealed to him: that the people of Jannah are received with a welcome gift, then a meal, then a drink. The same answer was given to Abd Allah ibn Salam, and it was one of the reasons he embraced Islam.[^1][^2] Calm, a sound source, and honesty about where your knowledge comes from: that is what makes your answer about your faith convincing.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates Thawban's hadith in sequence; sets out the order of the hospitality of the people of Jannah (welcome gift, meal, drink), citing Muslim 315 and al-Bukhari 3938; explains "welcome gift" (*tuhfah*), "hospitality" (*nuzul*), and "the extra lobe of the liver" from an-Nawawi's words; connects all of this to Fussilat 41:32 and as-Sa'di's tafsir; draws out two points of etiquette from the account about asking and answering; and plans to receive a guest in three stations.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf open to Surah Fussilat; a board with three columns headed "Welcome Gift," "Meal," and "Drink"; a planning sheet for the activity; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Muslim 315 in full in both its narrations, al-Bukhari 3329, 3938, and 4480, and al-Bukhari 6520 with Muslim 2792; reviews an-Nawawi's commentary on both hadiths, as-Sa'di's tafsir of Fussilat 41:32, and Chapter Forty-One of *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher decides how to handle the question about the child that has been omitted from the text (it is mentioned without detail in a mixed public setting), and keeps in mind that the food of the people of Jannah in general is deferred to a later lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Think of the most beautiful welcome you have ever received in someone's home. What made it beautiful?" Listen to two or three answers, and steer toward the point that advance preparation is the secret of true hospitality.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read Thawban's hadith, pausing at three moments: the shove and the name, the question "Will anything benefit you?", and the closing in which knowledge is attributed to Allah (10 minutes). Learners fill in the three columns, then read the hadith of Abd Allah ibn Salam for comparison (4 minutes). Then read the hadith of the welcome feast and the ayat of Fussilat, together with the words of an-Nawawi and as-Sa'di (6 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 2, 3, and 4: How do we hold a gentle conversation with someone of another faith? What is the difference between asking in order to benefit and asking in order to argue? The teacher reinforces the governing principle for speaking about the unseen: we affirm what has been reported, we stop where the text stops, and we affirm the attribute of the Hand in the hadith of Abu Sa'id without asking how and without likening Allah to anything.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses a specific guest and writes out the three stations in detail, along with a date for the visit (8 minutes). Then pairs swap plans, and each suggests one improvement that would bring the "arrival gift" closer to what the guest actually loves (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Set out the order of the hospitality of the people of Jannah, with the evidence" and "Name one point of etiquette in answering that the hadith teaches." Then recite the ayah `a welcome from One Most Forgiving, Most Merciful` and make du'a for those present.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, Thawban's hadith and Fussilat 41:32 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the wordings of the hadith (*ziyadah*/*za'idah*, *an-nun*/*al-hut*), compare the narrations of Muslim and al-Bukhari, and look up what an-Nawawi relates from al-Qadi 'Iyad about the seventy thousand.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The hadith must not be used to disparage Jews or anyone else; the account itself displays gentleness toward the questioner. Thawban's shove must not be cited as a justification for harshness. Add nothing to the text in describing the fish or the bull or their size. Affirm what is reported about Allah's attributes without asking how and without likening Him to anything. When planning hospitality, be mindful of anyone with food allergies or financial hardship: the welcome gift can be a kind word or an act of service.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

Remind students not to add anything to the text when describing the unseen, and not to embarrass anyone who cannot afford material hospitality.

## References

[^1]: Sahih Muslim, Book of Menstruation, Chapter on the Description of the Fluid of the Man and the Woman, and That the Child Is Created from Both, hadith 315, narrated by Thawban, the freed servant of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, through Abu Asma' ar-Rahabi; authentic: [sunnah.com/muslim:315a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:315a). The second narration, which follows it (through Yahya ibn Hassan from Mu'awiyah ibn Sallam, with the same chain), has «زَائِدَةُ كَبِدِ النُّونِ» ("the extra lobe of the fish's liver," with *za'idah*) and «كُنْتُ قَاعِدًا» ("I was sitting"). The text given here follows the wording of the first narration; the question about the child and its answer have been omitted, and their place is marked.

The text given here is an excerpt from the wording of 3938, with the omissions marked; the full hadith goes on to tell the story of his acceptance of Islam and how his people reacted to it.
