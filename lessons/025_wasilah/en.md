# Al-Wasilah and the Station of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of the Great Intercession (al-Bukhari 4712) in its proper sequence: mankind gathered on the plain of the Day of Resurrection; their going to Adam, then Nuh, then Ibrahim, then Musa, then Isa, peace be upon them all; each prophet excusing himself; and then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, falling in prostration beneath the Throne and saying, "My ummah, O Lord, my ummah, O Lord."
- Distinguish between the Praised Station (*al-maqam al-mahmud*), which is the station of the Great Intercession on the Day of Resurrection, as Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, and most commentators explained it (al-Isra' 17:79; al-Bukhari 4718), and al-Wasilah, a rank in Jannah fitting for only one servant, which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped would be his (Muslim 384).
- Explain the meaning of his words, peace and blessings be upon him, "I will be the leader of the children of Adam on the Day of Resurrection, the first to intercede and the first whose intercession is accepted" (Muslim 2278), and show that this leadership came to light on a day when Adam and even the greatest of the messengers, the Possessors of Firm Resolve, excused themselves.
- Carry out the after-adhan sequence as the Sunnah teaches it: repeating what the muezzin says, then sending blessings upon the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, then asking Allah to grant him al-Wasilah with the du'a narrated by Jabir (al-Bukhari 614), understanding the meaning of each of its phrases.
- Complete the activity "The After-Adhan Sequence: Learn It, Then Teach It," by practicing the sequence during a real adhan and then teaching it to someone else.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to send blessings upon His Prophet, to grant him al-Wasilah, to bless us with his intercession, and to gather us with him in Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On the Day of Resurrection, the prophets will decline to intercede, one after another, until the intercession finally comes to our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. That is the Praised Station. And in Jannah there is a rank called al-Wasilah, fitting for only one servant, and he, peace and blessings be upon him, taught us to ask for it on his behalf after every adhan. How, then, is that station bound up with a small act we perform five times a day?

### The Hadith of the Great Intercession: "I Will Be the Leader of Mankind on the Day of Resurrection"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: أُتِيَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ بِلَحْمٍ، فَرُفِعَ إِلَيْهِ الذِّرَاعُ، وَكَانَتْ تُعْجِبُهُ، فَنَهَسَ مِنْهَا نَهْسَةً، ثُمَّ قَالَ: **«أَنَا سَيِّدُ النَّاسِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ، وَهَلْ تَدْرُونَ مِمَّ ذَلِكَ؟ يُجْمَعُ النَّاسُ الْأَوَّلِينَ وَالْآخِرِينَ فِي صَعِيدٍ وَاحِدٍ، يُسْمِعُهُمُ الدَّاعِي، وَيَنْفُذُهُمُ الْبَصَرُ، وَتَدْنُو الشَّمْسُ، فَيَبْلُغُ النَّاسَ مِنَ الْغَمِّ وَالْكَرْبِ مَا لَا يُطِيقُونَ وَلَا يَحْتَمِلُونَ، فَيَقُولُ النَّاسُ: أَلَا تَرَوْنَ مَا قَدْ بَلَغَكُمْ؟ أَلَا تَنْظُرُونَ مَنْ يَشْفَعُ لَكُمْ إِلَى رَبِّكُمْ؟ فَيَقُولُ بَعْضُ النَّاسِ لِبَعْضٍ: عَلَيْكُمْ بِآدَمَ. فَيَأْتُونَ آدَمَ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ فَيَقُولُونَ لَهُ: أَنْتَ أَبُو الْبَشَرِ، خَلَقَكَ اللَّهُ بِيَدِهِ، وَنَفَخَ فِيكَ مِنْ رُوحِهِ، وَأَمَرَ الْمَلَائِكَةَ فَسَجَدُوا لَكَ، اشْفَعْ لَنَا إِلَى رَبِّكَ، أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا نَحْنُ فِيهِ؟ أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا قَدْ بَلَغَنَا؟ فَيَقُولُ آدَمُ: إِنَّ رَبِّي قَدْ غَضِبَ الْيَوْمَ غَضَبًا لَمْ يَغْضَبْ قَبْلَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَلَنْ يَغْضَبَ بَعْدَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَإِنَّهُ نَهَانِي عَنِ الشَّجَرَةِ فَعَصَيْتُهُ، نَفْسِي نَفْسِي نَفْسِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى غَيْرِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى نُوحٍ. فَيَأْتُونَ نُوحًا فَيَقُولُونَ: يَا نُوحُ، إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ أَوَّلُ الرُّسُلِ إِلَى أَهْلِ الْأَرْضِ، وَقَدْ سَمَّاكَ اللَّهُ عَبْدًا شَكُورًا، اشْفَعْ لَنَا إِلَى رَبِّكَ، أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا نَحْنُ فِيهِ؟ فَيَقُولُ: إِنَّ رَبِّي عَزَّ وَجَلَّ قَدْ غَضِبَ الْيَوْمَ غَضَبًا لَمْ يَغْضَبْ قَبْلَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَلَنْ يَغْضَبَ بَعْدَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَإِنَّهُ قَدْ كَانَتْ لِي دَعْوَةٌ دَعَوْتُهَا عَلَى قَوْمِي، نَفْسِي نَفْسِي نَفْسِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى غَيْرِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى إِبْرَاهِيمَ. فَيَأْتُونَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ فَيَقُولُونَ: يَا إِبْرَاهِيمُ، أَنْتَ نَبِيُّ اللَّهِ وَخَلِيلُهُ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْأَرْضِ، اشْفَعْ لَنَا إِلَى رَبِّكَ، أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا نَحْنُ فِيهِ؟ فَيَقُولُ لَهُمْ: إِنَّ رَبِّي قَدْ غَضِبَ الْيَوْمَ غَضَبًا لَمْ يَغْضَبْ قَبْلَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَلَنْ يَغْضَبَ بَعْدَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَإِنِّي قَدْ كُنْتُ كَذَبْتُ ثَلَاثَ كَذَبَاتٍ (فَذَكَرَهُنَّ أَبُو حَيَّانَ فِي الْحَدِيثِ)، نَفْسِي نَفْسِي نَفْسِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى غَيْرِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى مُوسَى. فَيَأْتُونَ مُوسَى فَيَقُولُونَ: يَا مُوسَى، أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، فَضَّلَكَ اللَّهُ بِرِسَالَتِهِ وَبِكَلَامِهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ، اشْفَعْ لَنَا إِلَى رَبِّكَ، أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا نَحْنُ فِيهِ؟ فَيَقُولُ: إِنَّ رَبِّي قَدْ غَضِبَ الْيَوْمَ غَضَبًا لَمْ يَغْضَبْ قَبْلَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَلَنْ يَغْضَبَ بَعْدَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَإِنِّي قَدْ قَتَلْتُ نَفْسًا لَمْ أُومَرْ بِقَتْلِهَا، نَفْسِي نَفْسِي نَفْسِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى غَيْرِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى عِيسَى. فَيَأْتُونَ عِيسَى فَيَقُولُونَ: يَا عِيسَى، أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَكَلِمَتُهُ أَلْقَاهَا إِلَى مَرْيَمَ، وَرُوحٌ مِنْهُ، وَكَلَّمْتَ النَّاسَ فِي الْمَهْدِ صَبِيًّا، اشْفَعْ لَنَا، أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا نَحْنُ فِيهِ؟ فَيَقُولُ عِيسَى: إِنَّ رَبِّي قَدْ غَضِبَ الْيَوْمَ غَضَبًا لَمْ يَغْضَبْ قَبْلَهُ مِثْلَهُ، وَلَنْ يَغْضَبَ بَعْدَهُ مِثْلَهُ (وَلَمْ يَذْكُرْ ذَنْبًا)، نَفْسِي نَفْسِي نَفْسِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى غَيْرِي، اذْهَبُوا إِلَى مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ. فَيَأْتُونَ مُحَمَّدًا ﷺ فَيَقُولُونَ: يَا مُحَمَّدُ، أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ وَخَاتَمُ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ، وَقَدْ غَفَرَ اللَّهُ لَكَ مَا تَقَدَّمَ مِنْ ذَنْبِكَ وَمَا تَأَخَّرَ، اشْفَعْ لَنَا إِلَى رَبِّكَ، أَلَا تَرَى إِلَى مَا نَحْنُ فِيهِ؟ فَأَنْطَلِقُ فَآتِي تَحْتَ الْعَرْشِ، فَأَقَعُ سَاجِدًا لِرَبِّي عَزَّ وَجَلَّ، ثُمَّ يَفْتَحُ اللَّهُ عَلَيَّ مِنْ مَحَامِدِهِ وَحُسْنِ الثَّنَاءِ عَلَيْهِ شَيْئًا لَمْ يَفْتَحْهُ عَلَى أَحَدٍ قَبْلِي، ثُمَّ يُقَالُ: يَا مُحَمَّدُ، ارْفَعْ رَأْسَكَ، سَلْ تُعْطَهْ، وَاشْفَعْ تُشَفَّعْ. فَأَرْفَعُ رَأْسِي فَأَقُولُ: أُمَّتِي يَا رَبِّ، أُمَّتِي يَا رَبِّ. فَيُقَالُ: يَا مُحَمَّدُ، أَدْخِلْ مِنْ أُمَّتِكَ مَنْ لَا حِسَابَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ الْبَابِ الْأَيْمَنِ مِنْ أَبْوَابِ الْجَنَّةِ، وَهُمْ شُرَكَاءُ النَّاسِ فِيمَا سِوَى ذَلِكَ مِنَ الْأَبْوَابِ. ثُمَّ قَالَ: وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، إِنَّ مَا بَيْنَ الْمِصْرَاعَيْنِ مِنْ مَصَارِيعِ الْجَنَّةِ كَمَا بَيْنَ مَكَّةَ وَحِمْيَرَ، أَوْ كَمَا بَيْنَ مَكَّةَ وَبُصْرَى»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: Some meat was brought to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and the foreleg, a cut he was fond of, was handed up to him. He took a bite from it, and then he said: **"I will be the leader of mankind on the Day of Resurrection. And do you know why that is? All of mankind, the first of them and the last, will be gathered on a single open plain, where a caller's voice will reach every one of them and a single gaze will take them all in. The sun will draw near, and people will be overcome by more grief and anguish than they can endure or bear. Then people will say: 'Do you not see what has come upon you? Will you not look for someone to intercede for you with your Lord?' And some will say to others: 'Go to Adam.' So they will come to Adam, peace be upon him, and say to him: 'You are the father of mankind. Allah created you with His own Hand, breathed into you of His spirit, and commanded the angels, and they bowed down in prostration to you. Intercede for us with your Lord. Do you not see the state we are in? Do you not see what has come upon us?' Adam will say: 'Today my Lord is angry as He has never been angry before, nor will He ever be angry like this again. He forbade me the tree, and I disobeyed Him. Myself, myself, myself! Go to someone else. Go to Nuh.' So they will come to Nuh and say: 'O Nuh, you were the first of the messengers sent to the people of the earth, and Allah named you a truly grateful servant. Intercede for us with your Lord. Do you not see the state we are in?' He will say: 'Today my Lord, Mighty and Majestic is He, is angry as He has never been angry before, nor will He ever be angry like this again. I had one supplication, and I used it against my own people. Myself, myself, myself! Go to someone else. Go to Ibrahim.' So they will come to Ibrahim and say: 'O Ibrahim, you are the Prophet of Allah and His intimate friend from among the people of the earth. Intercede for us with your Lord. Do you not see the state we are in?' He will say to them: 'Today my Lord is angry as He has never been angry before, nor will He ever be angry like this again. And I once told three lies' (Abu Hayyan, the narrator, listed them in the hadith). 'Myself, myself, myself! Go to someone else. Go to Musa.' So they will come to Musa and say: 'O Musa, you are the Messenger of Allah. Allah favored you above all people with His message and by speaking to you. Intercede for us with your Lord. Do you not see the state we are in?' He will say: 'Today my Lord is angry as He has never been angry before, nor will He ever be angry like this again. And I took a life that I had not been commanded to take. Myself, myself, myself! Go to someone else. Go to Isa.' So they will come to Isa and say: 'O Isa, you are the Messenger of Allah, and His word which He conveyed to Maryam, and a spirit from Him, and you spoke to people as an infant in the cradle. Intercede for us. Do you not see the state we are in?' Isa will say: 'Today my Lord is angry as He has never been angry before, nor will He ever be angry like this again' (and he mentioned no sin). 'Myself, myself, myself! Go to someone else. Go to Muhammad,' peace and blessings be upon him. So they will come to Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, and say: 'O Muhammad, you are the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets, and Allah has forgiven you all your sins, those gone before and those yet to come. Intercede for us with your Lord. Do you not see the state we are in?' Then I will set out and come to the place beneath the Throne, and I will fall down in prostration before my Lord, Mighty and Majestic is He. Then Allah will open up to me such words of praise and such beautiful glorification of Him as He never opened up to anyone before me. Then it will be said: 'O Muhammad, raise your head. Ask, and you will be given. Intercede, and your intercession will be accepted.' So I will raise my head and say: 'My ummah, O Lord! My ummah, O Lord!' And it will be said: 'O Muhammad, admit those of your ummah who face no reckoning through the right-hand gate of Jannah; and they will also share the other gates with the rest of the people.'" Then he said: "By the One in whose Hand my soul rests, the distance between the two door-leaves of one of Jannah's gates is like the distance between Makkah and Himyar, or like the distance between Makkah and Busra."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

What the prophets cite as their reason for excusing themselves is recorded in the Qur'an: Adam's eating from the tree, after which Allah accepted his repentance; Nuh's supplication against his people; and the blow Musa struck that ended a man's life, after which he sought forgiveness and Allah forgave him.[^8] As for Ibrahim's three statements, two of them were made "for the sake of Allah," and the scholars understood all three as *ma'arid*, statements that were literally true but worded so as to be taken another way.[^9] The divine attributes mentioned in the hadith are affirmed without likening them to creation and without asking how.

#### Lesson Explanation

The prophets excuse themselves out of the sheer awe they feel before their Lord on that day, not because their rank is lacking. Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, put prostration and praise before his intercession, and then he asked nothing for himself. Instead he said: "My ummah, O Lord."

### "It May Be That Your Lord Will Raise You to a Praised Station"

> **{وَمِنَ اللَّيْلِ فَتَهَجَّدْ بِهِ نَافِلَةً لَّكَ عَسَىٰ أَن يَبْعَثَكَ رَبُّكَ مَقَامًا مَّحْمُودًا}** [الإسراء: ٧٩][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And rise in the night and pray with it, as an extra act of devotion for you alone. It may be that your Lord will raise you to a praised station."** (al-Isra' 17:79)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, quotes Ibn Jarir as saying: "Most of the scholars of interpretation said that this is the station in which he, peace and blessings be upon him, will stand on the Day of Resurrection to intercede for people, so that their Lord may relieve them of the terrible ordeal they are in on that day." He also mentions that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, will have "the Great Intercession with Allah, so that He may come to judge between His creatures."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

It is called "praised" because its benefit reaches everyone standing on that plain, so the first and the last of mankind alike praise him for it.

### "That Is the Day Allah Raises Him to the Praised Station"

> عَنِ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: **«إِنَّ النَّاسَ يَصِيرُونَ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ جُثًا، كُلُّ أُمَّةٍ تَتْبَعُ نَبِيَّهَا، يَقُولُونَ: يَا فُلَانُ اشْفَعْ، حَتَّى تَنْتَهِيَ الشَّفَاعَةُ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ، فَذَلِكَ يَوْمَ يَبْعَثُهُ اللَّهُ الْمَقَامَ الْمَحْمُودَ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, who said: **"On the Day of Resurrection, people will gather in groups, each nation following its own prophet, saying: 'O so-and-so, intercede!' until the intercession comes at last to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. That is the day Allah raises him to the Praised Station."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Bukhari placed this statement of Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, in his commentary on the ayah of al-Isra'. *Juthan* means groups. The Praised Station, then, is the moment when the intercession finally comes to him, peace and blessings be upon him.[^3]

### Al-Wasilah: A Rank in Jannah Fitting for Only One Servant

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَمْرِو بْنِ الْعَاصِ رضي الله عنهما، أَنَّهُ سَمِعَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: **«إِذَا سَمِعْتُمُ الْمُؤَذِّنَ فَقُولُوا مِثْلَ مَا يَقُولُ، ثُمَّ صَلُّوا عَلَيَّ، فَإِنَّهُ مَنْ صَلَّى عَلَيَّ صَلَاةً صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ بِهَا عَشْرًا، ثُمَّ سَلُوا اللَّهَ لِيَ الْوَسِيلَةَ، فَإِنَّهَا مَنْزِلَةٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ لَا تَنْبَغِي إِلَّا لِعَبْدٍ مِنْ عِبَادِ اللَّهِ، وَأَرْجُو أَنْ أَكُونَ أَنَا هُوَ، فَمَنْ سَأَلَ لِيَ الْوَسِيلَةَ حَلَّتْ لَهُ الشَّفَاعَةُ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, that he heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"When you hear the muezzin, say what he says. Then send blessings upon me, for whoever sends a single blessing upon me, Allah sends ten blessings upon him for it. Then ask Allah to grant me al-Wasilah, for it is a rank in Jannah that befits only one of Allah's servants, and I hope that I will be that one. Whoever asks for al-Wasilah for me, intercession becomes his due."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, explains that in the Arabic language *al-wasilah* means a position of nearness at a king's court, and that "becomes his due" (*hallat lahu*) means that it becomes binding for him, or, according to another view, that it reaches him.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

There are three steps here, in order: answering the muezzin, then sending blessings upon the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, then asking for al-Wasilah for him. And in the words "I hope" there is a lesson in courtesy toward Allah: the very highest of creation speaks in the language of hope.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### The Du'a for Al-Wasilah After the Adhan

> عَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رضي الله عنهما، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«مَنْ قَالَ حِينَ يَسْمَعُ النِّدَاءَ: اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّ هَذِهِ الدَّعْوَةِ التَّامَّةِ، وَالصَّلَاةِ الْقَائِمَةِ، آتِ مُحَمَّدًا الْوَسِيلَةَ وَالْفَضِيلَةَ، وَابْعَثْهُ مَقَامًا مَحْمُودًا الَّذِي وَعَدْتَهُ، حَلَّتْ لَهُ شَفَاعَتِي يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jabir ibn Abdullah, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever says, upon hearing the call to prayer, 'O Allah, Lord of this perfect call and of the prayer about to be established, grant Muhammad al-Wasilah and al-Fadilah, and raise him to the Praised Station that You have promised him,' my intercession becomes his due on the Day of Resurrection."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

"The perfect call" is the adhan, so called because it contains the declaration of Allah's oneness. Ibn Hajar, may Allah have mercy on him, explained *al-fadilah* as a rank raised above that of all other creatures.[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

This du'a brings together al-Wasilah in Jannah and the Praised Station on the Day of Resurrection, and its reward matches the deed: whoever asks that his Prophet be given the station of intercession is himself granted that Prophet's intercession.

<!-- evidence:end -->

His leadership, peace and blessings be upon him, becomes plain to all creation on the Day of Resurrection, when everyone else excuses himself and he alone intercedes.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Imagine the adhan starts just as you are in the middle of a match, or in the middle of a game on a screen. Do you carry on as if you never heard it, or do you stop for one minute? That one small minute is connected to the greatest scene of the Day of Resurrection. <!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-4712" -->

- **`Intercession (ash-shafa'ah)`** — when someone asks Allah for good on behalf of someone else; on the Day of Resurrection, it happens only with Allah's permission.
- **`The Praised Station (al-maqam al-mahmud)`** — the place where the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, will stand on the Day of Resurrection to intercede for people, so that everyone praises him for it.[^2]
- **`Al-Wasilah`** — a rank in Jannah meant for only one servant of Allah, which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped would be his.[^4]

### A True Hadith: "My Ummah, O Lord! My Ummah, O Lord!"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-4712" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and it is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was sitting with his Companions when some meat was brought to him. The foreleg was handed up to him, a cut he liked, and he ate from it. Then he said: "I will be the leader of mankind on the Day of Resurrection. And do you know why that is?"

Then he described that day to them. All of mankind, the first of them and the last, will be gathered on one stretch of land. The sun will come close, and people will be hit by more worry and distress than they can bear. So they will say to one another: "Why don't you find someone to intercede for you with your Lord?"

They will go to Adam, peace be upon him: "You are the father of mankind. Allah created you with His Hand and made the angels bow down to you. Intercede for us!" But Adam will mention that he ate from the tree he had been told not to eat from, and he will say: "Myself, myself! Go to Nuh." Nuh will mention a prayer he made against his people and send them on to Ibrahim. Ibrahim will send them to Musa. Musa will mention that he took a life he had not been commanded to take, and he will send them to Isa. Isa, peace be upon him, will not mention any sin, but he too will say: "Go to someone else. Go to Muhammad," peace and blessings be upon him.

Notice this: these are the greatest of the prophets, and each one of them knows that this moment is bigger than he is, so he honestly points people to someone else.

Then they will come to our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He will set out and bow down in prostration to his Lord beneath the Throne before he intercedes, and Allah will open up to him words of praise and glorification that He never opened up to anyone before him. Then it will be said: "O Muhammad, raise your head. Ask, and you will be given. Intercede, and your intercession will be accepted." He will raise his head and say: "My ummah, O Lord! My ummah, O Lord!" And he will be told: "Bring in those of your ummah who face no reckoning through the right-hand gate of Jannah." He also told us, peace and blessings be upon him, that the space between the two sides of one of Jannah's gates is like the distance between Makkah and Himyar, or between Makkah and Busra.[^1]

This is the "Praised Station" that Allah promised him in the Qur'an.[^3]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-4712" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: on a terribly hard day, people will search for someone to intercede for them. Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, and Isa, peace be upon them, will each excuse himself and point them to someone else, until they come to Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him. He will bow down in prostration and praise his Lord, then intercede for his ummah, and his intercession will be accepted.[^1]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in a world that measures a person's worth by how many people are paying attention to them. Yet the greatest station any human being will ever stand in, "the Praised Station," was not a station of fame. It was a station of prostration and of service to others. And in Jannah there is a rank called "al-Wasilah," fitting for only one servant. He, peace and blessings be upon him, said of it, "And I hope that I will be that one," and then he asked us to request it for him after every adhan.[^4] So what does one minute after the adhan, spent among your friends or in front of a screen, have to do with the greatest scene of the Day of Resurrection?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-4712" -->

- **`The Great Intercession (ash-shafa'ah al-'uzma)`** — the Prophet's intercession, peace and blessings be upon him, for everyone standing on the plain of Judgment, so that the reckoning can begin and the agony of waiting can be lifted from them; according to most commentators, it is the Praised Station.[^2]
- **`"Myself, myself" (nafsi, nafsi)`** — the words of each of the five prophets on that day, meaning that the overwhelming gravity of the moment has turned his attention to his own standing, not that he lacks mercy for people.
- **`Al-Fadilah`** — in the adhan du'a, a rank raised above that of all other creatures.[^10]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: The Day Intercession Came Down to One Man

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-4712" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and it is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

The hadith begins in a completely ordinary moment: a meal among friends, and a cut of meat the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was fond of. Then, out of nowhere, he said: "I will be the leader of mankind on the Day of Resurrection, and do you know why that is?" He did not leave the statement hanging. He explained it by painting an entire scene.

The first and the last of mankind are gathered on a single open plain, where a caller's voice reaches all of them and a single gaze takes them all in. The sun draws near, and people are overcome by more grief and anguish than they can bear. So they go looking for an intercessor. They begin with Adam, peace be upon him, listing his honors, and he excuses himself because he ate from the tree. Then Nuh, the first messenger sent to the people of the earth, who excuses himself because of a prayer he made against his people. Then Ibrahim, the intimate friend of Allah, who excuses himself because of three things he once said. Then Musa, to whom Allah spoke directly, who excuses himself because he took a life he had not been commanded to take. Then Isa, Allah's word and a spirit from Him, who mentions no sin, yet still says what the others said: "Myself, myself, myself! Go to someone else." And then he points them to Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him.

Notice two things. Each prophet knows the limits of his own position and does not claim what is not his. And none of them stops at excusing himself; each one points people to the next.

Then the people come to Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him. He sets out and falls down in prostration beneath the Throne before he intercedes, and Allah opens up to him words of praise and beautiful glorification such as He never opened up to anyone before him. Then it is said: "Raise your head. Ask, and you will be given. Intercede, and your intercession will be accepted." At the very moment he is told "Ask, and you will be given," his words are: "My ummah, O Lord! My ummah, O Lord!" And he is given permission to bring those of his ummah who face no reckoning into Jannah through its right-hand gate.[^1]

Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, said: "That is the day Allah raises him to the Praised Station."[^3]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-4712" audience="13+" -->

To put it another way: on a day when distress reached its peak, Adam and then the greatest of the messengers, the Possessors of Firm Resolve, excused themselves one after another, each pointing to someone else, until the intercession came at last to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He began with prostration and praise, and then, rather than asking anything for himself, he said: "My ummah, O Lord."[^1][^3]

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ صَلِّ وَسَلِّمْ عَلَى نَبِيِّنَا مُحَمَّدٍ، وَآتِهِ الْوَسِيلَةَ وَالْفَضِيلَةَ، وَارْزُقْنَا شَفَاعَتَهُ، وَاجْمَعْنَا بِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ.

What it means: O Allah, send blessings and peace upon our Prophet Muhammad, grant him al-Wasilah and al-Fadilah, make us among those who receive his intercession, and gather us with him in Jannah. This du'a is not the same as the transmitted adhan du'a, which is said upon hearing the call to prayer.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of the Great Intercession, following the sequence of the five prophets; distinguishes between the Praised Station (on the Day of Resurrection) and al-Wasilah (in Jannah), citing al-Isra' 17:79, the statement of Ibn Umar, and Muslim 384 as evidence; explains Jabir's du'a phrase by phrase; and practices the sequence for a week, teaches it to someone else, and writes a note about the experience.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for looking up al-Isra' 17:79 and the ayat referred to in the scholarly explanation (Ta-Ha 20:121-122, Nuh 71:26, al-Qasas 28:15-16); a whiteboard showing a timeline with five stations for the prophets and a sixth station for the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; an activity sheet with a three-column table (phrase, meaning, evidence); an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 4712 in full, aloud, twice, to get a feel for its rhythm, and reviews Ibn Kathir's commentary on al-Isra' 17:79, an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 384, Ibn Hajar's remarks on "al-Fadilah" in his commentary on al-Bukhari 614, and al-Bukhari 3358 on Ibrahim's three statements, so as to be ready for the question that is bound to come up about them. The teacher also bears in mind that the previous lesson in this series (the highest and lowest of the people of Jannah) cited the hadith of al-Wasilah as supporting evidence, and that this lesson gives it a full explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What do you usually do in the minute after the adhan?" and then: "If you were told that this minute is connected to the greatest scene of the Day of Resurrection, would your answer change?" The answers are written down without comment, to be revisited at the close.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Abu Hurayrah in full, while learners fill in the stations of the timeline: with which virtue each prophet was addressed, and with what he excused himself (10 minutes). Then read al-Isra' 17:79 and the statement of Ibn Umar, with the question: "What makes this station praised?" (4 minutes). Then read Muslim 384 and al-Bukhari 614, and draw an arrow on the timeline from "the Praised Station" to "al-Wasilah" in Jannah (4 minutes). Then present Muslim 2278 as a summary (2 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion around three contrasts: "Myself, myself" versus "My ummah, O Lord"; prostration and praise before asking; and the hope expressed in "I hope that I will be that one" despite the loftiness of his rank. The teacher asks: "Why is intercession the reward for asking for al-Wasilah?" and guides learners toward the principle that the reward matches the deed.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners fill in the table of phrases, meanings, and evidence individually (8 minutes). Then pairs practice "teaching": one explains a single phrase to the other in one minute, and then they switch (5 minutes). Each learner then names the two daily adhans they will commit to and the person they will teach (2 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** An exit card with two questions: "State the difference between the Praised Station and al-Wasilah, with evidence for each," and "Whom will you teach the sequence to, and when?" The teacher then returns to the answers from the opening and closes by performing the sequence together if an adhan falls during the session, or otherwise by reciting Jabir's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, Muslim 384, and Jabir's du'a are enough. Advanced learners are asked to consult Chapter 40 of Ibn al-Qayyim's *Hadi al-Arwah*, "On the Highest and the Lowest in Rank Among the People of Jannah," and to compare al-Bukhari's narration of the intercession hadith (4712) with Muslim's (194).

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The prophets, peace be upon them, are spoken of with the utmost reverence. What they cited as their reasons for excusing themselves must never become material for belittling them; make clear that Allah accepted their repentance and that Ibrahim's statements were *ma'arid*. The divine attributes mentioned in the hadith are affirmed without likening them to creation, without asking how, and without delving into their modality. No du'a or formula that is not authentically established is attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and learners are reminded that the bedtime du'a in this lesson is lesson-authored. No particular individual is declared certain to receive intercession.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child can say that the people went to several prophets until they came to our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; can say that our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, bowed down to his Lord and said, "My ummah, O Lord"; puts the three after-adhan cards in order and goes through them once with a parent; and teaches them to someone else or to a toy.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Three blank cards for each child; child-safe coloring pencils; six small numbered chairs or cushions to stand for the stations along the way; a short recording of the adhan; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver lines up the cushions in a row and practices telling the story in a calm voice, moving from one cushion to the next at each prophet. The caregiver also prepares a gentle answer in case the child asks about the sun being close: "It will be a hard day, but Allah is merciful, and He made our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, intercede for us."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver asks: "If you need help with something hard, who do you go to?" and listens, then says: "Today we're going to hear about a day when everyone needed help."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the story and moves with the child from cushion to cushion: Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, and Isa, peace be upon them, and then the last cushion for our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, where the child repeats: "My ummah, O Lord."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver asks: "What did our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, ask for? For himself, or for us?" and explains that our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, remembered us, and that we remember him after the adhan when we ask for "al-Wasilah" for him, the highest place in Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child draws the three cards and puts them in order. Then the adhan recording is played, and the child goes through the steps, holding up each card at its turn, and then "teaches" a brother, sister, or toy.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Observe whether the child can put the cards in order unaided and knows what our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said when he bowed down. Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, two cards (the ear and the heart) and the phrase "O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad" are enough. Older children learn the phrase "grant Muhammad al-Wasilah and al-Fadilah" by repetition.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not go into detail about the terrors of the Day of Resurrection, the Lord's anger, or the prophets' reasons for excusing themselves; it is enough that each of them pointed to someone else. Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, any other prophet, or the angels, and keep the cushions faceless. Keep the focus on love and reassurance.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the hadith of intercession in the order of the five prophets; defines intercession, the Praised Station, and al-Wasilah; writes Jabir's du'a on the sequence strip; and uses it at least three times during the week and teaches it to a family member.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards for the three terms; six cards bearing the names of the stations (Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, and Isa, peace be upon them, and Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him), with no pictures; three-box paper strips; pens; a recording of the adhan.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes Jabir's du'a clearly, with full vowel marks, on the board, prepares a question for each station ("With which virtue did they address him?"), and reviews the difference between the Praised Station and al-Wasilah.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Present the scenario from the introduction: "The adhan starts while you're in the middle of a match." Then ask: "What do most people do? And what could you do in just one minute?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story while the students arrange the six station cards as they listen, writing on the back of each card the virtue people mentioned for that prophet (8 minutes). Then explain the three terms (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, begin with prostration? What did he ask for? How is that connected to what we say after the adhan?" Then the students recite Jabir's du'a together, and the teacher explains the meaning of "the perfect call" and "al-Fadilah."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The students make the sequence strip as described in the activity unit. Then the adhan is played and they go through the sequence once, after which pairs practice teaching the strip to each other.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The Praised Station is..., and al-Wasilah is...." Answers are assessed by how accurately they distinguish time from place. Then the bedtime du'a is read, and everyone agrees to keep up their check marks for the week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are struggling receive a ready-made strip and focus on only the first phrase of the du'a. Advanced students are asked to memorize Muslim 384 and explain the three steps it contains.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the prophets' reasons for excusing themselves briefly and respectfully, exactly as they are reported, making clear that Allah accepted their repentance and that they are the best of creation after our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Do not compare families on how consistently they respond to the adhan, and do not embarrass any student who misses some of the times. Do not depict any prophet.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the scene of intercession in terms of responsibility, knowing one's limits, and pointing others to someone more capable; explains why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, began with prostration and praise; distinguishes between the Praised Station, al-Wasilah, and al-Fadilah; and memorizes Jabir's du'a, practices "the adhan minute" for seven days, and teaches it to someone else.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a personal sheet that will not be collected; small cards for writing anonymous questions; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral examples of situations in which it is hard to pause at the adhan (a group game, a chat, a match), and reviews Ibn Kathir's commentary on al-Isra' 17:79, the statement of Ibn Umar, and al-Bukhari 3358, in order to answer the question about Ibrahim's statements if it comes up.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What makes someone important in people's eyes today?" and then: "What if the greatest station in history was a station of prostration and service?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the scene, pausing at three moments: the opening of the hadith with the meal and the question; the repeated line "Go to someone else"; and the moment of "Ask, and you will be given," with the answer it received.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Small groups discuss questions 2 and 3, followed by brief presentations. The teacher guides students toward seeing that knowing your limits is not weakness, and that returning the Prophet's love, peace and blessings be upon him, means following him, sending blessings upon him, and asking for al-Wasilah for him, not just saying so.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students write out the phrases of the du'a and their meanings in their own words, choose the adhans at which they will practice "the adhan minute" and the person they will teach, and then pairs practice explaining a single phrase.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph on "What do the words 'My ummah, O Lord' teach me?" A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question about the experience, without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private audio recording instead. Advanced students are asked to trace where the five prophets are mentioned in the Qur'an and to identify the virtue that people mentioned for each of them in the hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The standing of the prophets, peace be upon them, must be protected from any belittling or mockery during discussion. No one is asked to disclose their own shortcomings in prayer. "Myself, myself" must not be understood as selfishness; it is awe before the overwhelming gravity of that moment. Intercession is presented together with its condition, Allah's permission and His pleasure, not as a guarantee to rely on while neglecting good deeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
