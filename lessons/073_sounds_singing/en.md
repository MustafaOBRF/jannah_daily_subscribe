# The Sounds of Jannah: In a Garden, Delighted

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the scene in Surah ar-Rum (30:14-16): on the Day the Hour arrives, people will be divided into two groups; those who believed and did righteous deeds will be "in a garden, delighted," while those who denied will be brought into the punishment.
- Explain the meaning of *yuhbarun* ("delighted") through the words of the commentators: "they will be honored" (Ibn 'Abbas), "they will enjoy bliss" (Mujahid and Qatadah), and "listening in Jannah" (Yahya ibn Abi Kathir), together with Ibn Kathir's remark that *al-habrah* is broader than all of this, and the way Ibn al-Qayyim and al-Qurtubi reconcile these views.
- Describe, from the Qur'an and the Sunnah, what the people of Jannah hear and what they never hear: glorification, greetings of peace and praise (Yunus 10:10); no idle talk (al-Ghashiyah 88:11); the sound of Bilal's sandals, which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, heard in Jannah (al-Bukhari 1149); and what no ear has heard (al-Bukhari 3244).
- Present the reports about the singing of the hur al-'ayn together with their disputed gradings, distinguishing what can be relied upon from what cannot, and set out the scholars' positions on musical instruments and singing in this world with integrity (for adults).
- Carry out the "Listen Closely, Then Add a Good Sound" activity: a silent listening session in which every sound is counted and sorted, followed by adding one good sound to the home for a set period and recording who joined in.
- Learn by heart a lesson-authored bedtime du'a asking Allah to guard our hearing from idle talk and to make us among those who are "in a garden, delighted."

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The ear is a wide doorway for both delight and pain. A loved one's voice settles the heart; a cutting word lingers in the soul for days; and noise that never stops wears a person down. The Qur'an tells us that the bliss of the people of Jannah includes what they hear, and that whatever hurts the ear has been lifted from them. This lesson is about the sounds of Jannah and what its people hear; about what has been reported of the singing of the hur al-'ayn, and the grade of each report; and about how all of this connects to guarding our hearing in this world.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-30-14-16" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Be in a Garden, Delighted"

> **{وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ السَّاعَةُ يَوْمَئِذٍ يَتَفَرَّقُونَ ۝ فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ فَهُمْ فِي رَوْضَةٍ يُحْبَرُونَ ۝ وَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا وَكَذَّبُوا بِآيَاتِنَا وَلِقَاءِ الْآخِرَةِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ فِي الْعَذَابِ مُحْضَرُونَ}** [الروم: ١٤-١٦][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And on the Day the Hour arrives, on that Day they will be divided. As for those who believed and did righteous deeds, they will be in a garden, delighted. And as for those who disbelieved and denied Our signs and the meeting of the Hereafter, they will be brought into the punishment."** (ar-Rum 30:14-16)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tabari said that they will be among fragrant plants and dense, intertwined greenery, "made joyful, and given delight through listening and through a good and easy life." He explained why Allah singled out a garden (*rawdah*): "nothing was finer to look at, or sweeter in fragrance, than gardens," and he added: "*Al-habrah*, to the Arabs, means joy and happiness." He then related the views of the early scholars with their chains: Ibn 'Abbas said, "They will be honored"; Mujahid and Qatadah said, "They will enjoy bliss"; and Yahya ibn Abi Kathir said, "*Al-habrah* is delight and listening," and in another wording, "listening in Jannah."[^1]

Ibn Kathir said: "*Al-habrah* is broader than all of this." As-Sa'di counted among the forms of this delight "enrapturing voices and moving sounds to listen to," alongside every other kind of bliss. After listing the views, al-Qurtubi said: "All of this is part of bliss, joy and honor, so there is no contradiction between these views."[^2] And Ibn al-Qayyim said: "The ear's delight in listening is part of *al-habrah* and of bliss."[^3]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-10-10" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Glory Be to You, O Allah"

> **{دَعْوَاهُمْ فِيهَا سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ وَتَحِيَّتُهُمْ فِيهَا سَلَامٌ ۚ وَآخِرُ دَعْوَاهُمْ أَنِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ}** [يونس: ١٠][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Their call in it will be, 'Glory be to You, O Allah,' and their greeting in it will be, 'Peace,' and the last of their call will be, 'All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.'"** (Yunus 10:10)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said that all religious obligations will have fallen away from them, "and what remains for them is the most perfect of pleasures... namely, the remembrance of Allah, by which hearts find rest and souls rejoice. To them it is like breathing, with no burden and no strain," and that their greeting is peace, "that is, speech free of idle talk and sin."[^4]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-88-8-12" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Where They Will Hear No Idle Talk"

> **{وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاعِمَةٌ ۝ لِسَعْيِهَا رَاضِيَةٌ ۝ فِي جَنَّةٍ عَالِيَةٍ ۝ لَا تَسْمَعُ فِيهَا لَاغِيَةً ۝ فِيهَا عَيْنٌ جَارِيَةٌ}** [الغاشية: ٨-١٢][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Faces that Day will glow with bliss, well pleased with their striving, in a lofty garden, where they will hear no idle talk. In it is a flowing spring."** (al-Ghashiyah 88:8-12)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said of *laghiyah* (idle talk): "a word of idle talk and falsehood, let alone forbidden speech. Rather, their speech is good speech... containing the remembrance of Allah Most High and the remembrance of the blessings He showers on them, one after another."[^5]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-1149" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Heard the Sound of Your Sandals"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ قَالَ لِبِلَالٍ عِنْدَ صَلَاةِ الْفَجْرِ: **«يَا بِلَالُ، حَدِّثْنِي بِأَرْجَى عَمَلٍ عَمِلْتَهُ فِي الْإِسْلَامِ، فَإِنِّي سَمِعْتُ دَفَّ نَعْلَيْكَ بَيْنَ يَدَيَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ»**. قَالَ: مَا عَمِلْتُ عَمَلًا أَرْجَى عِنْدِي أَنِّي لَمْ أَتَطَهَّرْ طُهُورًا فِي سَاعَةِ لَيْلٍ أَوْ نَهَارٍ إِلَّا صَلَّيْتُ بِذَلِكَ الطُّهُورِ مَا كُتِبَ لِي أَنْ أُصَلِّيَ.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to Bilal at the time of the Fajr prayer: **"O Bilal, tell me about the deed you have done in Islam that gives you the most hope, for I heard the sound of your sandals ahead of me in Jannah."** He said: "I have done no deed that gives me more hope than this: I have never purified myself, at any hour of the night or the day, without praying with that purification whatever was written for me to pray."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Al-Bukhari explained *daff na'layk* as "movement," that is, the sound sandals make as one walks. So the first sound in Jannah that this hadith tells us about is the sound of a quiet, hidden good deed: wudu' followed by prayer.[^6]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3244" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "No Ear Has Heard"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«قَالَ اللَّهُ: أَعْدَدْتُ لِعِبَادِي الصَّالِحِينَ مَا لَا عَيْنَ رَأَتْ، وَلَا أُذُنَ سَمِعَتْ، وَلَا خَطَرَ عَلَى قَلْبِ بَشَرٍ، فَاقْرَءُوا إِنْ شِئْتُمْ: {فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَا أُخْفِيَ لَهُمْ مِنْ قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ}»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Allah said: I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined. So recite, if you wish: 'No soul knows what comfort of the eyes has been kept hidden for them.'"**[^7]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tabarani-ibn-umar-hur-singing" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Reports of the Singing of the Hur al-'Ayn

> عَنِ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«إِنَّ أَزْوَاجَ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ لَيُغَنِّينَ أَزْوَاجَهُنَّ بِأَحْسَنِ أَصْوَاتٍ مَا سَمِعَهَا أَحَدٌ قَطُّ، إِنَّ مِمَّا يُغَنِّينَ: نَحْنُ الْخَيْرَاتُ الْحِسَانُ، أَزْوَاجُ قَوْمٍ كِرَامٍ، يَنْظُرْنَ بِقُرَّةِ أَعْيَانٍ، وَإِنَّ مِمَّا يُغَنِّينَ بِهِ: نَحْنُ الْخَالِدَاتُ فَلَا يَمُتْنَهْ، نَحْنُ الْآمِنَاتُ فَلَا يَخَفْنَهْ، نَحْنُ الْمُقِيمَاتُ فَلَا يَظْعَنَّهْ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The wives of the people of Jannah will sing to their husbands in the most beautiful voices anyone has ever heard. Among what they will sing is: 'We are the good, the lovely ones, wives of a noble people, gazing with eyes content.' And among what they will sing is: 'We are the everlasting, and never shall we die; we are the secure, and never shall we fear; we are the abiding, and never shall we depart.'"**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The hadith scholars differ over this hadith. Al-Albani graded it authentic, while noting that he could find no biographical entry for the teacher of at-Tabarani in its chain; the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah* judged its chain broken, because Zayd ibn Aslam heard only two hadiths from Ibn 'Umar. Close to it in meaning is the hadith of Anas: «إِنَّ الْحُورَ الْعِينَ لَتُغَنِّينَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ: نَحْنُ الْحُورُ الْحِسَانُ، خُبِّئْنَا لِأَزْوَاجٍ كِرَامٍ» ("The hur al-'ayn sing in Jannah: 'We are the lovely hur, kept hidden for noble husbands'"). Al-Albani graded it authentic and as-Suyuti called its chain good, while the editor graded it weak because of inconsistency in its chain. As for the hadith of 'Ali in at-Tirmidhi (2564), al-Albani and Ahmad Shakir graded it weak, and at-Tirmidhi called it *gharib* (singular in its transmission).[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

For this reason, this lesson does not make these reports a primary proof. The general meaning, that the people of Jannah have something to listen to that delights them, is established by how the early generations explained the ayah of ar-Rum. Ibn Abi ad-Dunya also relates, with a chain the editor graded authentic, from the Successor Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir: "On the Day of Resurrection, a caller will call out: 'Where are those who kept their hearing and their souls clear of gatherings of idle amusement and the flutes of Shaytan? Settle them in gardens of musk.' Then He will say to the angels: 'Let them hear My glorification and My praise.'" This is the statement of a Successor, not a hadith traced back to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^3]

<!-- evidence:end -->

### Musical Instruments in This World

> عَنْ أَبِي عَامِرٍ أَوْ أَبِي مَالِكٍ الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، سَمِعَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: **«لَيَكُونَنَّ مِنْ أُمَّتِي أَقْوَامٌ يَسْتَحِلُّونَ الْحِرَ وَالْحَرِيرَ وَالْخَمْرَ وَالْمَعَازِفَ...»** [وبقية الحديث وعيدٌ لبعضهم بالهلاك والمسخ].[^9]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu 'Amir or Abu Malik al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, that he heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"There will certainly be people among my ummah who will treat as lawful illicit sexual relations, silk, wine and musical instruments (*al-ma'azif*)..."** [The rest of the hadith is a warning that some of them will be destroyed and transformed.][^9]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Bukhari recorded it as a suspended report (*mu'allaq*), introduced in a form that asserts it, from his teacher Hisham ibn 'Ammar. Al-'Ayni said: "The hadith is authentic, even though its form is that of a suspended report." He quoted Ibn Hazm's claim that it is broken, and then said: "Ibn Hazm was mistaken in this." *Al-ma'azif* has been explained as instruments of amusement such as the lute and the tunbur.[^9] And Ibn Mas'ud said about Allah's words "And among the people is he who buys idle talk (*lahw al-hadith*)": "It is singing, by Allah, besides whom there is no god." This is the statement of a Companion.[^11]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-952" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "And This Is Our Eid"

> عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها قَالَتْ: دَخَلَ أَبُو بَكْرٍ وَعِنْدِي جَارِيَتَانِ مِنْ جَوَارِي الْأَنْصَارِ تُغَنِّيَانِ بِمَا تَقَاوَلَتِ الْأَنْصَارُ يَوْمَ بُعَاثَ، قَالَتْ: وَلَيْسَتَا بِمُغَنِّيَتَيْنِ، فَقَالَ أَبُو بَكْرٍ: أَمَزَامِيرُ الشَّيْطَانِ فِي بَيْتِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ؟ وَذَلِكَ فِي يَوْمِ عِيدٍ، فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«يَا أَبَا بَكْرٍ، إِنَّ لِكُلِّ قَوْمٍ عِيدًا، وَهَذَا عِيدُنَا»**.[^10]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, who said: Abu Bakr came in while two young girls of the Ansar were with me, singing the verses the Ansar had traded with one another on the day of Bu'ath. She said: And they were not professional singers. Abu Bakr said, "The flutes of Shaytan in the house of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him?" That was on a day of Eid. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"O Abu Bakr, every people has its Eid, and this is our Eid."**[^10]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Rajab said: "This hadith contains a concession for young girls, on the day of Eid, to play and to sing in the manner of the Bedouin Arabs' singing."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

For the scholars, then, the question does not come down to a single sweeping ruling. There are texts that censure musical instruments and idle talk, and there is an established concession for innocent singing on Eid and similar occasions. The detailed rulings belong in the books of fiqh and with trustworthy scholars. And the description of what is heard in Jannah must never become a pretext for what is forbidden in this world, just as the wine of Jannah does not make the wine of this world lawful.[^9][^10]

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The people of Jannah are in a garden, made joyful and honored, and part of their delight is the delight of the ear. They hear glorification, greetings of peace and praise, and they hear no idle talk. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, heard there the sound of a righteous deed, and in it is what no ear has heard. The details of the singing of the hur, however, rest on reports whose authenticity is disputed. And the practical question is this: what is my ear taking in today, and what good sound will I add to my home?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What are the views on the meaning of *yuhbarun*? And why did al-Qurtubi say that they do not contradict one another?
2. According to the ayat of Yunus and al-Ghashiyah, what do the people of Jannah hear, and what do they not hear?
3. Why did this lesson not make the reports of the singing of the hur a primary proof? And what is the difference between al-Albani's verdict and that of the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah*?
4. How can a learner hold together the hadith on musical instruments and the hadith of the two girls on the day of Eid, without going to either extreme?
5. What connects the statement of Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir with the hadith of Bilal, given that both tie what is heard in Jannah to a deed done in this world?

### Activity: Listen Closely, Then Add a Good Sound

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.073.activity.listen-closely-then-add-a-good-sound" -->

Sit in silence for five minutes in a place where you spend a lot of time, such as your home or your workplace, and write down every sound you hear. Sort the sounds into three columns: a sound of blessing you thank Allah for; remembrance of Allah or good speech; and idle talk, noise or hurtful words. Then choose one good sound to add to your home at a fixed time for seven days: reciting a short surah aloud after Maghrib, for example, or being the first to say salam when you come in, or glorifying Allah together after the prayer. Each day, note down: did I add it, and who joined me? On the seventh day, write three lines: What changed in the atmosphere of the home? Which sound from the third column grew quieter? And how does that remind you of a Jannah where no idle talk is heard?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="3.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Close your eyes for a little while and listen. Can you hear water? Or little birds? Or your mom saying to you, "As-salamu 'alaykum"? Those are lovely sounds, and Allah is the One who gave us ears to hear them.

Jannah has sounds even lovelier than anything we have ever heard. There is a spring of water there, flowing and flowing. The people there say "Subhan Allah" and "Alhamdulillah," and they say "Salam," "Peace," to each other. And nobody there ever hears a single bad word.[^4][^5] We don't know exactly what the sounds of Jannah are like, but Allah has told us they are more beautiful than anything we can imagine.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

### From the Qur'an: "In a Garden, Delighted"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-30-14-16" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In a surah called "ar-Rum," Allah tells us about the Day of Resurrection.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-30-14-16" audience="4-7" -->

On that Day, all the people come together, and then they become two groups. The people who believed in Allah and did good deeds go to a garden. A garden like this is green and beautiful, full of plants and flowers. The scholars say Allah spoke of a garden because people did not know anything lovelier to look at, or sweeter to smell, than a garden. In that garden they are "delighted": happy and joyful, with Allah honoring them and giving them wonderful gifts. As for the people who said Allah's words were not true, they are not with them.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

How did those people get to the garden? They believed in Allah and did good deeds. And some good deeds are kind words we say every day: "As-salamu 'alaykum," "Subhan Allah," and "Alhamdulillah." Those are the very same words the people of Jannah say![^4]

<!-- story:end -->

### Activity: Little Ears and a Good Sound

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.073.activity.listen-closely-then-add-a-good-sound" -->

Sit with your dad or your mom. Both of you close your eyes for one minute and listen. Then say every sound you heard: Water? A bird? A car? Give a thumbs-up for every sound that reminds you of a gift from Allah, and say "Alhamdulillah." Then pick a good sound that you will start at home for three days: saying "As-salamu 'alaykum" when you come in, or "Subhan Allah" when you see something beautiful. Draw a big ear on a piece of paper, and every time you say your good sound, put a star next to it. On the third day, count your stars and ask: Who said it with me?

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On a school trip to the woods, the teacher asked everyone to stay silent for two minutes. At first a few kids giggled, but then everyone settled down, and suddenly they were hearing things they had never noticed: a little stream gurgling, leaves rustling in the trees, a bird singing far away. "Those sounds were here the whole time," the teacher said. "Our noise was just drowning them out."

Jannah has sounds far more beautiful than these, and no noise to drown them out. Allah says about it: "Where they will hear no idle talk. In it is a flowing spring." In other words, you won't hear a single bad or empty word there, and there is a spring of water flowing.[^5] Its people glorify Allah and praise Him, and they greet each other with "Peace."[^4] We don't know exactly what the sounds of Jannah are like. In a hadith qudsi, Allah tells us He has prepared for His righteous servants what "no ear has heard."[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-30-14-16" -->

- **`Rawdah (garden)`** — a garden full of plants and flowers; at-Tabari says Allah mentioned it in particular because, to the Arabs, nothing was finer to look at or sweeter to smell.[^1]
- **`Yuhbarun (delighted)`** — made joyful, honored and given bliss; at-Tabari says that to the Arabs *al-habrah* means joy.[^1]
- **`Laghiyah (idle talk)`** — a word that is empty or false, as as-Sa'di explains.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: The Day People Are Divided

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-30-14-16" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

In Surah ar-Rum, a few short ayat paint a scene from the Day of Resurrection.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-30-14-16" audience="8-12" -->

"And on the Day the Hour arrives, on that Day they will be divided." People who lived side by side in this world, in the same town and maybe even in the same house, split that Day into two groups. And two things decide which group they are in: faith and deeds.

"As for those who believed and did righteous deeds, they will be in a garden, delighted." At-Tabari said that they will be among fragrant plants and flowers, "made joyful, and given delight through listening and through a good life." Ibn 'Abbas said that *yuhbarun* means "they will be honored"; Mujahid and Qatadah said it means "they will enjoy bliss"; and Yahya ibn Abi Kathir said it means they will delight in what they hear in Jannah. All of this is part of the bliss of Jannah.

"And as for those who disbelieved and denied Our signs and the meeting of the Hereafter, they will be brought into the punishment."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice that the ayah does not say only "those who believed." It says those who believed *and* did righteous deeds. And some of our good deeds are things people hear from us every single day. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told Bilal that he had heard the sound of Bilal's sandals ahead of him in Jannah, and asked him what deed he did. Bilal said: whenever he made wudu', he prayed whatever Allah had written for him.[^6] Footsteps heard in Jannah, all because of a small deed kept up faithfully in this world.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. According to Surah ar-Rum, what happens to people on the Day the Hour arrives?
2. What are the two things that make a person one of the people of the garden?
3. Give three meanings of the word *yuhbarun*.
4. What sound did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hear in Jannah? And what deed was behind it?
5. What sounds did the students discover when they went quiet? And what had been drowning them out?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Sound Map

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.073.activity.listen-closely-then-add-a-good-sound" -->

Sit in silence for three minutes somewhere in your home or yard, and write down every sound you hear. Then put a symbol next to each sound: a star for a sound of blessing you thank Allah for, a heart for a kind word or remembrance of Allah, and a circle for noise or bad words. After that, choose one good sound to add to your home for four days at a fixed time: saying salam when you come in, saying "Subhan Allah" three times with your family after 'Isha, or reading a short surah aloud. Each day, write down: Did I do it? Who joined me? On the fourth day, write two sentences: What changed? And what did it remind me of from the sounds of Jannah?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.073.dua.guard-our-hearing-among-those-who-rejoice" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ احْفَظْ أَسْمَاعَنَا مِنَ اللَّغْوِ، وَامْلَأْ قُلُوبَنَا بِذِكْرِكَ، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِنَ الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي رَوْضَةٍ يُحْبَرُونَ.
>
> What it means: O Allah, keep our ears safe from empty and hurtful words, fill our hearts with Your remembrance, and make us among the people of the garden who are joyful and honored in Jannah.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Your earbuds are in for hours every day: clips, songs, podcasts and chats, the constant buzz of group chats. How often do you stop and ask: What is actually getting into my ears? And what is it doing to me? The ear is not a neutral doorway. Whatever comes in through it reaches the heart.

The Qur'an describes Jannah as a place "where they will hear no idle talk," not one empty or false word.[^5] Its people glorify Allah and praise Him, and their greeting is "Peace." As-Sa'di says that for them, remembering Allah is "like breathing, with no burden and no strain."[^4] Jannah also has sounds its people delight in listening to, as some of the early scholars explained the ayah of ar-Rum. There are reports that the hur al-'ayn sing to their husbands in the most beautiful voices, though the hadith scholars differ over whether those reports are authentic.[^3][^8] What is certain is that Jannah holds what "no ear has heard."[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-30-14-16" -->

- **`Yatafarraqun (they will be divided)`** — they become two groups that never meet again: one in the garden, the other in the punishment.[^1]
- **`Yuhbarun (delighted)`** — made joyful, honored and given bliss; for Yahya ibn Abi Kathir this includes listening in Jannah, and Ibn Kathir said that *al-habrah* is broader than all of this.[^1][^2]
- **`Muhdarun (brought in)`** — brought into the punishment, and never absent from it.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: A Parting With No Reunion

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-30-14-16" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

In Surah ar-Rum, these ayat come right after the description of the wrongdoers who are struck with despair on the Day the Hour arrives.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-30-14-16" audience="13+" -->

The scene: "And on the Day the Hour arrives, on that Day they will be divided." Classmates, people from the same friend group, members of the same family, all go their separate ways. And the dividing line is not popularity, and not follower counts: "As for those who believed and did righteous deeds, they will be in a garden, delighted. And as for those who disbelieved and denied Our signs and the meeting of the Hereafter, they will be brought into the punishment."

At-Tabari said that to the Arabs, *al-habrah* meant "joy and happiness," and that in the garden they will be "made joyful, and given delight through listening and through a good and easy life." Al-Qurtubi brought the commentators' views together (honored, given bliss, delighting in what they hear) and then said: "All of this is part of bliss, joy and honor, so there is no contradiction between these views."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice that the ayah does not simply say "they will be happy." It says *yuhbarun*, a word in which the commentators found joy, honor and bliss all at once. In this world, you might feel alone when you turn a song down, or when you leave a group where all you ever hear is mockery and crude talk. Ibn Abi ad-Dunya relates from the Successor Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir that on the Day of Resurrection a call will go out: Where are those who kept their hearing clear of gatherings of idle amusement? "Let them hear My glorification and My praise."[^3] Whatever you give up today for Allah is never lost.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. According to the ayat, on what basis will people be divided on the Day of Resurrection? And why is faith without deeds not enough?
2. Why did al-Qurtubi say that the commentators' views on *yuhbarun* do not contradict one another?
3. What is the difference between an authentic report that the scholars agree on and a report whose authenticity the hadith scholars dispute? And how should we deal with the second kind?
4. What fills your ears most during your day? And which part of it brings you closer to "where they will hear no idle talk"?
5. What good sound could you add to your home or your friend group this week?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Five Minutes Without Earbuds

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.073.activity.listen-closely-then-add-a-good-sound" -->

Take your earbuds out and sit in silence for five minutes, in your room or in a quiet public place. In a private note, write down every sound you hear and sort it: a sound of blessing, good speech and remembrance of Allah, or idle talk and noise. Then choose one good sound that you will add yourself for five days: reciting a page of Qur'an aloud, being the first to say salam at home, sending a friend a kind voice message, or glorifying Allah after the prayer. Each day, note: Did I add it? Did anyone respond to it? On the fifth day, write a private paragraph: What did I discover about the sounds that fill my day? And what do I want to hear in Jannah?

<!-- activity:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.073.dua.guard-our-hearing-among-those-who-rejoice" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ احْفَظْ أَسْمَاعَنَا مِنَ اللَّغْوِ، وَامْلَأْ قُلُوبَنَا بِذِكْرِكَ، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِنَ الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي رَوْضَةٍ يُحْبَرُونَ.
>
> What it means: O Allah, guard our hearing from every empty and false word, and fill our hearts with Your remembrance until it is dearer to us than any other sound. Make us among those who believed and did righteous deeds, and so are made joyful and honored in the garden of Jannah.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene of ar-Rum 30:14-16; presents the commentators' views on *yuhbarun* and how al-Qurtubi and Ibn al-Qayyim reconcile them; uses Yunus 10:10, al-Ghashiyah 88:8-12 and al-Bukhari 1149 and 3244 as evidence for what the people of Jannah hear and what they do not hear; explains the gradings of the reports about the singing of the hur al-'ayn and the disagreement over them; presents the texts on musical instruments and the concession on Eid with integrity; and begins the seven-day "Listen Closely, Then Add a Good Sound" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "What the people of Jannah hear," "What they do not hear" and "What is disputed in authenticity"; the three-column activity sheet; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and al-Qurtubi on ar-Rum 30:15; as-Sa'di's tafsir of Yunus 10:10 and al-Ghashiyah 88:11; chapter fifty-seven of *Hadi al-Arwah* together with its editor's notes; al-'Ayni's commentary on al-Bukhari 5590 and Ibn Rajab's on al-Bukhari 952; and Ibn Kathir's tafsir of Luqman 31:6. The teacher decides in advance to present the disagreement over musical instruments as a brief scholarly overview without issuing a fatwa, and to refer the details to the people of knowledge.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks for one full minute of silence, then asks: "What sounds did you hear? Which of them do you usually not notice?" And then: "What is your favorite sound in this world?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of ar-Rum in full, with the commentators' views on *yuhbarun* (6 minutes). Then Yunus 10:10, al-Ghashiyah 88:8-12 and al-Bukhari 1149 and 3244, filling in the first and second columns (5 minutes). Then the report of Ibn 'Umar on the singing of the hur and the hadith scholars' verdicts on it, written in the third column, followed by the statement of Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir, making clear that it is *maqtu'* (the statement of a Successor) (4 minutes). Then the hadith on musical instruments with al-'Ayni's comments and Ibn Mas'ud's statement, and the hadith of the two girls with Ibn Rajab's comment (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the third and fourth questions. The teacher explains the difference between a primary proof and a disputed report; that disagreement over whether a hadith is authentic does not mean denying its general meaning when that meaning is established another way; and that the bliss of Jannah is not a basis for deriving rulings in this world, just as the wine of Jannah does not make the wine of this world lawful.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Five minutes of silence and counting sounds in the room itself; then each learner sorts them into the three columns and chooses the good sound they will add to their home, and its fixed time.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give two views on the meaning of *yuhbarun*," "What is not heard in Jannah?" and "What is the grade of the reports about the singing of the hur, and why were they not made a primary proof?" Then read the bedtime du'a, pointing out that it is lesson-authored.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only ar-Rum 30:14-16, Yunus 10:10 and the hadith of Bilal. Ask advanced learners to trace the chains of the reports in this chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* and its notes, and to write a paragraph on how to approach a hadith that one scholar graded authentic and another graded weak.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not let the lesson become a platform for heated argument about musical instruments, or for accusing anyone present; present the texts and views faithfully, and refer detailed rulings to the people of knowledge. Speak of the hur al-'ayn with dignity, and do not digress into descriptions that no text establishes. Mention that Allah gives every believing man and woman whatever completes their joy. Cite weak reports only to point out their weakness. Be mindful of anyone who is hard of hearing or has lost their hearing: no one is denied the delights of Jannah because of an impairment in this world.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that those who believed and did good deeds are in a garden, happy; names the words of the people of Jannah: "Subhan Allah," "Alhamdulillah" and "Salam"; knows that there are no bad words in Jannah; chooses a good sound to start at home; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A short recording of running water or birdsong (optional); a picture of a garden in bloom; a sheet of paper for each child with a big ear drawn on it; star stickers; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the scene in a calm voice, prepares a quiet spot for the listening minute, and sends families a short note explaining the three-day activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The children close their eyes for a minute and listen to the recording or to the sounds around them. Then the caregiver asks: "What did you hear? Who gave you your ears?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the Qur'anic scene while showing the picture of the garden. The children repeat: "فَهُمْ فِي رَوْضَةٍ يُحْبَرُونَ" ("they will be in a garden, delighted"), and then repeat the words of the people of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions. The children practice saying "As-salamu 'alaykum" in a gentle voice, and "Subhan Allah" when they see a beautiful picture.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** A second listening minute, after which each child names one sound they heard and gives a thumbs-up if it reminded them of a gift from Allah. Then each child chooses their good sound and takes the ear sheet home to add stars for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver notices whether the child can name one of the words of the people of Jannah and the sound they will start with. Then the du'a is read one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the word "Salam" and the garden are enough. Ask older children to tell the scene in their own words: people become two groups, and the believers are happy in the garden.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Say nothing at all to the children about the hur al-'ayn, singing or musical instruments. Present the sounds of Jannah as remembrance of Allah, greetings of peace, water and the joy of its people, and say that Allah knows best what they are truly like and that they are more beautiful than we can imagine, without inventing any descriptions. Mention the other group in calm words that do not frighten. Be mindful of a child who is hard of hearing: replace listening with noticing movement and touch, and reassure the child that Jannah is for every believer.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the scene of ar-Rum 30:14-16; defines the three terms; gives three meanings of *yuhbarun*; narrates the hadith of Bilal; and draws a sound map of their surroundings and adds a good sound for four days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a "Sound Map" sheet with the star, heart and circle symbols; a mushaf; a clock to time the silence.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on ar-Rum 30:15, as-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Ghashiyah 88:11 and Yunus 10:10, and the hadith of al-Bukhari 1149, and prepares a written example of a sound map from their own home.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the story of the trip to the woods, then asks for two minutes of silence in the classroom, and asks: "What did you hear that you hadn't noticed before?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the Qur'anic scene in its three paragraphs, pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of the commentators (6 minutes), then the hadith of Bilal (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss the second and fourth questions. The teacher makes clear that faith and deeds together are the reason for entering the garden, and that the sounds of Jannah are remembrance of Allah, greetings of peace, water and joy, with no idle talk in it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The teacher shows their example; then, after three minutes of silence, each student draws a sound map of the classroom, marks each sound with the symbols, and chooses the good sound they will add at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The people of Jannah are in a garden, *yuhbarun*, which means..." and "The good sound I will add is...". Then read the du'a. After four days, anyone who wishes can share what they noticed.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, five sounds and two days are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize ar-Rum 30:14-16 and to explain the difference between people being divided ("*yatafarraqun*") in the Hereafter and people being together in this world.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Say nothing to the students about the hur al-'ayn, about singing in Jannah, or about the disagreement over musical instruments; present Yahya ibn Abi Kathir's statement only as "they delight in what they hear." Do not frighten the students with ayah 16; read it calmly. Do not ask anyone to reveal what they hear at home, such as arguments or shouting; if a student shows signs of being hurt, listen to them privately and inform the responsible person when necessary.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the scene of ar-Rum 30:14-16 and the basis of the division in it; explains *yatafarraqun*, *yuhbarun* and *muhdarun*; distinguishes an established report from a disputed one; evaluates what enters their ears each day; and begins the five-day "Five Minutes Without Earbuds" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; cards for anonymous answers; a "My Ear's Day" sheet on which the student estimates how many hours they spend listening to different things.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi on ar-Rum 30:15, as-Sa'di's tafsir of Yunus 10:10 and al-Ghashiyah 88:11, and the statement of Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir with the notes of the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah*, and decides how to answer a question about music briefly: by saying that the matter involves texts and the views of the scholars, and referring the student to their parents and to a trustworthy scholar.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Students write on anonymous cards: "How many hours a day do you spend with earbuds in? And what do you usually listen to?" The teacher shares the results without names.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the Qur'anic scene with three pauses: the division, the garden and its delight, and the other group, together with al-Qurtubi's reconciliation of the views.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 3 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher explains that Islam does not ask for a deaf ear but for an ear that chooses; that whoever gives something up for Allah never loses it; and that the right way to handle a disputed report is to mention the disagreement and not to build on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Five minutes of silence with no earbuds and no phones; then each student writes their note, sorts the sounds and chooses the good sound they will add.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: the meaning of *yuhbarun*, the basis of the division on the Day of Resurrection, and a good sound they will add. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see anyone's notes. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may keep private voice notes instead. Ask advanced students to do a short study of the meaning of "*laghw*" (idle talk) in the Qur'an through al-Ghashiyah 88:11 and as-Sa'di's tafsir.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Keep any mention of the hur al-'ayn brief and modest, as in the unit, without the details of the reports or any descriptions. Do not open a fiqh debate about music, and do not accuse any student over what they listen to; the aim is awareness and choice, not reproach. Be mindful of a student who uses earbuds to escape noise, conflict at home, or anxiety: listen to them in confidence, encourage them to seek help from their parents or a trusted professional, and inform the responsible person in line with the child-protection policy when necessary.
