# The Buildings of Jannah: Its Bricks and Its Walls

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him (al-Bukhari 450 and Muslim 533), in its proper sequence: the people's dislike of his changing the building of the Prophet's Mosque and how much they talked about it, followed by Uthman's answer with the hadith: "Whoever builds a mosque seeking by it the Face of Allah, Allah will build for him the like of it in Jannah."
- Describe how the Prophet's Mosque was built in the lifetime of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and after him, as recorded in the hadith of Ibn Umar (al-Bukhari 446): mud brick, palm fronds and palm trunks at first, then carved stone, plaster and teak.
- Affirm what the Qur'an and authentic hadith establish about the buildings of Jannah: `chambers, above them chambers built high` (az-Zumar 39:20), and two gardens of silver and two gardens of gold, "their vessels and all that is in them" (al-Bukhari 4878).
- Identify the report "one brick of silver and one brick of gold, and its mortar is richly fragrant musk" (at-Tirmidhi 2526) as a report whose authenticity is disputed, and so not to be used as primary evidence.
- Explain an-Nawawi's two readings of the meaning of "the like of it," and explain that the real building in Jannah is done by Allah, while its foundation in this world is a sincere intention.
- Carry out the "Intention Before the First Stone" activity: writing down one's intention before doing hands-on service for a place of prayer, then reviewing one's motives once the work is done.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for sincerity in our deeds and for lofty chambers built in Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We all know building from experience: a foundation, then bricks, then walls, then a roof, and finally the finishing touches. We also know that every building in this world wears out, that its owners may disagree about its design, and that people will talk about it. Coming after the lesson on the ground and soil of Jannah, this lesson asks: What do we know with certainty about the buildings of Jannah? What can we not state definitively? And how does a servant of Allah come to have something built for him there?

The lesson rests on an authentic hadith that brings both kinds of building together: a man builds a house for Allah in this world, people criticize his work, and he answers them with a Prophetic promise: for whoever builds for Allah, Allah builds the like of it in Jannah. The builder in Jannah, then, is Allah, and the material the servant supplies in this world is sincere work. After that, we read what has been authentically reported about the buildings themselves, and we put the report whose authenticity is disputed in its proper place.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-450" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whoever Builds a Mosque Seeking by It the Face of Allah"

> عَنْ عُبَيْدِ اللَّهِ الْخَوْلَانِيِّ أَنَّهُ سَمِعَ عُثْمَانَ بْنَ عَفَّانَ رضي الله عنه يَقُولُ عِنْدَ قَوْلِ النَّاسِ فِيهِ حِينَ بَنَى مَسْجِدَ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ: **«إِنَّكُمْ أَكْثَرْتُمْ، وَإِنِّي سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: مَنْ بَنَى مَسْجِدًا -قَالَ بُكَيْرٌ: حَسِبْتُ أَنَّهُ قَالَ:- يَبْتَغِي بِهِ وَجْهَ اللَّهِ، بَنَى اللَّهُ لَهُ مِثْلَهُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ»**.[^1]
>
> وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ عِنْدَ مُسْلِمٍ عَنْ مَحْمُودِ بْنِ لَبِيدٍ: **أَنَّ عُثْمَانَ بْنَ عَفَّانَ أَرَادَ بِنَاءَ الْمَسْجِدِ، فَكَرِهَ النَّاسُ ذَلِكَ، فَأَحَبُّوا أَنْ يَدَعَهُ عَلَى هَيْئَتِهِ، فَقَالَ: سَمِعْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ يَقُولُ: «مَنْ بَنَى مَسْجِدًا لِلَّهِ بَنَى اللَّهُ لَهُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِثْلَهُ»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Ubaydullah al-Khawlani reported that he heard Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him, say, when people were talking about him as he rebuilt the Mosque of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him: **"You have said a great deal. But I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: 'Whoever builds a mosque (Bukayr said: I think he said: seeking by it the Face of Allah), Allah will build for him the like of it in Jannah.'"**[^1]
>
> And in a narration in Muslim from Mahmud ibn Labid: **Uthman ibn Affan wanted to rebuild the mosque, and the people disliked the idea; they would have preferred him to leave it as it was. So he said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, say: "Whoever builds a mosque for Allah, Allah will build for him in Jannah the like of it."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Bukayr's words "I think he said" record one narrator's uncertainty about the exact wording "seeking by it the Face of Allah." The same meaning, however, comes in the words "for Allah" in the narration of Mahmud ibn Labid, so the condition of sincerity is firmly established.[^1] An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, mentioned two possible meanings of "the like of it": that the likeness lies simply in its being a house, while its size and qualities belong to what no eye has seen and no ear has heard; or that the excellence of that house over the other houses of Jannah is like the excellence of a mosque over the houses of this world.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this promise, the one who builds is Allah: "Allah will build for him." The building of Jannah, then, is not human craftsmanship that we can measure by our own; it is the gift of a generous Lord. And Uthman's answer, may Allah be pleased with him, is a model for anyone criticized over a good deed: he did not argue in his own defense; he referred the matter back to the text and to the intention.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### From Mud Brick and Palm Fronds to Carved Stone

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما: **أَنَّ الْمَسْجِدَ كَانَ عَلَى عَهْدِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ مَبْنِيًّا بِاللَّبِنِ، وَسَقْفُهُ الْجَرِيدُ، وَعُمُدُهُ خَشَبُ النَّخْلِ، فَلَمْ يَزِدْ فِيهِ أَبُو بَكْرٍ شَيْئًا، وَزَادَ فِيهِ عُمَرُ وَبَنَاهُ عَلَى بُنْيَانِهِ فِي عَهْدِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ بِاللَّبِنِ وَالْجَرِيدِ، وَأَعَادَ عُمُدَهُ خَشَبًا، ثُمَّ غَيَّرَهُ عُثْمَانُ، فَزَادَ فِيهِ زِيَادَةً كَثِيرَةً، وَبَنَى جِدَارَهُ بِالْحِجَارَةِ الْمَنْقُوشَةِ وَالْقَصَّةِ، وَجَعَلَ عُمُدَهُ مِنْ حِجَارَةٍ مَنْقُوشَةٍ، وَسَقَفَهُ بِالسَّاجِ**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both: **In the time of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, the mosque was built of mud brick, its roof was of palm fronds, and its pillars were of palm wood. Abu Bakr added nothing to it. Umar enlarged it, rebuilding it just as it had been built in the time of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, with mud brick and palm fronds, and he put in new pillars of wood. Then Uthman changed it, enlarging it a great deal. He built its wall of carved stone and plaster, made its pillars of carved stone, and roofed it with teak.**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-labin* is brick made of sun-dried mud; *al-jarid* is the stripped fronds of the date palm; *al-qassah* is plaster (gypsum); and *as-saj* (teak) is a well-known wood brought from India.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith shows what people were talking about: a mosque they had known as mud brick and palm fronds in the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and his two Companions, whose building then changed and grew larger. Their dislike of the change had an understandable reason behind it, and Uthman answered with the text, not with force.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-20" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Chambers, Above Them Chambers Built High"

> **{لَٰكِنِ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ لَهُمْ غُرَفٌ مِنْ فَوْقِهَا غُرَفٌ مَبْنِيَّةٌ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ وَعْدَ اللَّهِ ۖ لَا يُخْلِفُ اللَّهُ الْمِيعَادَ}** [الزمر: ٢٠][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"But those who are mindful of their Lord will have chambers, above them chambers built high, beneath which rivers flow. It is the promise of Allah, and Allah never fails His promise."** (az-Zumar 39:20)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "Tier upon tier, built, firmly made, adorned and lofty."[^5] As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, described them as lofty, adorned dwellings, one above another, "built of gold and silver, with mortar of richly fragrant musk," and he concluded that Allah has promised this reward to those who are mindful of Him, "so let them fulfill the qualities of taqwa, that He may fulfill their rewards to them in full."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The word `built` (*mabniyyah*) is a Qur'anic statement that Jannah holds real buildings, not merely a symbolic meaning. The ayah ties these buildings to taqwa, mindfulness of Allah, and seals them with a promise.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-4878" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### Two Gardens of Silver and Two Gardens of Gold

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«جَنَّتَانِ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ آنِيَتُهُمَا وَمَا فِيهِمَا، وَجَنَّتَانِ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ آنِيَتُهُمَا وَمَا فِيهِمَا، وَمَا بَيْنَ الْقَوْمِ وَبَيْنَ أَنْ يَنْظُرُوا إِلَى رَبِّهِمْ إِلَّا رِدَاءُ الْكِبْرِ عَلَى وَجْهِهِ فِي جَنَّةِ عَدْنٍ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Two gardens of silver, their vessels and all that is in them; and two gardens of gold, their vessels and all that is in them. And nothing stands between the people and their looking upon their Lord except the cloak of Grandeur over His Face, in Jannat 'Adn."**[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

"And all that is in them" takes in everything in the two gardens, their buildings included, so gold and silver are among the materials of Jannah by an authentic text. The end of the hadith, which concerns an attribute of Allah the Exalted, is affirmed just as it has come, without likening it to anything created and without asking how.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2526" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Report of the Bricks: Its Authenticity Is Disputed

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: [...] قُلْنَا: الْجَنَّةُ مَا بِنَاؤُهَا؟ قَالَ: **«لَبِنَةٌ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَلَبِنَةٌ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ، وَمِلَاطُهَا الْمِسْكُ الْأَذْفَرُ، وَحَصْبَاؤُهَا اللُّؤْلُؤُ وَالْيَاقُوتُ، وَتُرْبَتُهَا الزَّعْفَرَانُ، مَنْ يَدْخُلْهَا يَنْعَمْ وَلَا يَبْأَسْ، وَيُخَلَّدْ وَلَا يَمُوتْ، لَا تَبْلَى ثِيَابُهُمْ، وَلَا يَفْنَى شَبَابُهُمْ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: [...] We said, "Jannah: what is it built of?" He said: **"One brick of silver and one brick of gold; its mortar is richly fragrant musk, its pebbles are pearls and rubies, and its soil is saffron. Whoever enters it will live in bliss and never know misery, will live forever and never die. Their clothes will never wear out, and their youth will never fade."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tirmidhi said: "Its chain is not that strong, and in my view it is not connected." Ahmad Shakir and al-Albani graded it authentic, while others graded it weak.[^7] *Al-milat* (mortar) is what is laid between bricks to hold them together.

#### Lesson Explanation

That is why the report is mentioned here only so the reader knows what the books describing Jannah contain, not as primary evidence, and nothing in the children's stories is built on it. What is firmly established is enough: real buildings, chambers above chambers, gold and silver. The detailed descriptions of the walls of Jannah reported in those same books have not been relied on here.

<!-- evidence:end -->

Have you ever built a house out of blocks? You put one block on top of another until you have a wall, and then a whole house. In Jannah there are real houses, and Allah built them. They have rooms on top of rooms, and rivers flowing underneath them.[^3] There is shining gold and silver there, too.[^6] We don't know exactly what they look like, because they are more beautiful than anything we have ever seen. But our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught us how we can have a house built for us there.

### An Authentic Hadith: Uthman and the New Mosque

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-450" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. It is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, and it is not a made-up story.**

In the city of Madinah, the mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was built of mud bricks. Its roof was made of palm leaves, and its pillars were made of palm-tree trunks.[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-450" audience="4-7" -->

A long time later, Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him, became the leader of the Muslims. He wanted to build the mosque all over again, bigger and stronger.

But some people didn't like that idea. They wanted the mosque to stay just the way it was. And they talked about it a lot.

So what did Uthman do? He reminded them of what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had said. He told them: "You have talked a lot. And I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: 'Whoever builds a mosque for Allah, Allah will build the same for him in Jannah.'"[^1]

Then Uthman built the mosque out of stone, and he made it bigger.[^2]

See? Uthman built a house for Allah here in this world, and Allah is the One who builds in Jannah. And the most important thing about building is doing it for Allah, not so that people will say, "Wow, how beautiful!"[^1]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

When you see a huge building, you might wonder: Who built it? What is it made of? How long will it last? Every building in this world has a builder and materials, and one day it will come to an end. But Allah has told us that in Jannah there are `chambers, above them chambers built high, beneath which rivers flow`.[^3] And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that there are two gardens of silver and two gardens of gold, "their vessels and all that is in them."[^6] Some books also contain a report about bricks of gold and silver, but the scholars disagree about whether it is authentic, so we don't make it the foundation of what we say.[^7] The important question is this: how can something be built for you there?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-446" -->

- **`Mud brick (al-labin)`** — brick made of sun-dried mud; the mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was built from it.[^9]
- **`Plaster (al-qassah)`** — gypsum, a white material used to stick stones together and to coat walls.[^9]
- **`Teak (as-saj)`** — a kind of strong wood; the mosque's roof was made of it in the time of Uthman.[^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-450" -->

- **`Seeking by it the Face of Allah (yabtaghi bihi wajh Allah)`** — wanting only Allah's pleasure through what you do, not people's praise.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: "You Have Said a Great Deal"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-450" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated from Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not a made-up story.**

Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, tells us that the mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Madinah was built of mud brick, with a roof of palm fronds and pillars of palm wood. Abu Bakr added nothing to it. Umar made it bigger, but he rebuilt it in exactly the same style: mud brick, palm fronds and wooden pillars.[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-450" audience="8-12" -->

Then came the time of Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him, and he wanted to rebuild the mosque. That is where the problem started: people disliked the idea, and they wanted him to leave the mosque the way they had always known it. And there was a lot of talk about it.[^1]

Some people would drop a project because they were afraid of the talk. Others would push ahead just to prove they were right. But Uthman took the matter back to the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "You have said a great deal. But I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: 'Whoever builds a mosque seeking by it the Face of Allah, Allah will build for him the like of it in Jannah.'"[^1]

So Uthman went ahead with the building. He enlarged the mosque a great deal, built its wall of carved stone and plaster, made its pillars of carved stone, and gave it a roof of teak.[^2]

Notice two things. First, the promise says "Allah will build for him," so Allah is the One who builds in Jannah. Second, the condition is "seeking by it the Face of Allah," so the value of the work lies in its intention before it lies in its stones. And whoever cannot build a mosque can still serve the houses of Allah with a sincere intention.[^1]

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.037.dua.sincere-work-built-chambers" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ عَمَلَنَا خَالِصًا لِوَجْهِكَ، وَاجْعَلْ لَنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ غُرَفًا مَبْنِيَّةً تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ.

Meaning: O Allah, make our deeds for You alone and not for people's praise, and give us in Your Jannah the lofty chambers You have promised to those who are mindful of You, with rivers flowing beneath them.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every public project, whether it's a fundraising drive, a volunteer initiative or a useful page online, is going to get people talking. Some will criticize fairly, some will cling to the way things used to be, and some will just talk. Under that kind of pressure, two questions come up: Do I stop because I'm afraid of what people will say? Or do I keep going just to prove I'm right? In an authentic hadith, Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him, faced exactly this situation when he rebuilt the Mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and his answer became a lesson in the kind of building that lasts. The Qur'an tells us that those who are mindful of Allah will have in Jannah `chambers, above them chambers built high`.[^3] So how can you be one of those for whom something is built?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-450" -->

- **`Seeking by it the Face of Allah (yabtaghi bihi wajh Allah)`** — when the real motive behind what you do is Allah's pleasure, not fame and not proving yourself; it is the condition of the promise in the hadith.[^1]
- **`The like of it (mithlahu)`** — an-Nawawi said: like it in being a house, while its qualities are beyond anything we can imagine; or its excellence over the other houses of Jannah is like the excellence of a mosque over the houses of this world.[^8]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-39-20" -->

- **`Chambers built high (ghurafun mabniyyah)`** — lofty dwellings, one above another, solidly built; in the ayah they are the reward of those who are mindful of Allah.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: The Builder Who Answered the Talk with the Text

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-450" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

Start with the place. The Prophet's Mosque in Madinah was built by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions out of mud brick, with a roof of palm fronds and pillars of palm trunks. Abu Bakr changed nothing in it. Umar enlarged it but kept the same materials. That simple building had become part of people's memory.[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-450" audience="13+" -->

Then Uthman ibn Affan, may Allah be pleased with him, now the caliph, wanted to rebuild it. Mahmud ibn Labid says the people disliked the idea and would have preferred him to leave it as it was. Ubaydullah al-Khawlani says they talked about it a great deal.[^1]

Notice that the objection did not come from enemies. It came from Muslims who loved the mosque the way they had always known it. And that is the hardest kind of pressure: being criticized by sincere people.

So what did Uthman do? The hadith does not say that he pulled rank, or that he defended his own taste in architecture. He said: "You have said a great deal. But I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: 'Whoever builds a mosque seeking by it the Face of Allah, Allah will build for him the like of it in Jannah.'"[^1] He shifted the conversation from "What does Uthman want?" to "What did the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, say? And what is the intention?"

Then he finished the building: he enlarged the mosque a great deal, made its wall of carved stone and plaster, its pillars of stone, and its roof of teak.[^2]

And here is the lesson for you: the promise depends on intention, not applause. People see the stones; Allah sees why you laid them. Whoever builds for Allah in this world, it is Allah who builds for him in Jannah, and what He builds cannot be measured against anything humans build. As for details like "one brick of gold and one brick of silver," they come in a report whose authenticity is disputed, so we don't make them our foundation.[^7] What is firmly established is enough for us: chambers built high, and gold and silver.[^3][^6]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why was the people's objection to Uthman harder to deal with than an objection from opponents?
2. What did Uthman gain by taking the discussion back to the text instead of to his position or his personal taste?
3. How do you tell the difference between criticism you should learn from and talk that shouldn't stop you?
4. What project are you working on right now, and who are you really doing it for?
5. Why is this lesson so careful to separate what is firmly established from what is disputed in describing Jannah?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Intention Before the First Stone

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.037.activity.intention-before-the-first-stone" -->

Within the next week, choose a real act of service for a place of prayer: helping to clean the mosque, setting up your school's prayer room, or organizing its library. Before you start, write your intention in one line in a private note. Once you're done, answer: Did I think about taking photos of the work or posting it? Why? Did anyone say something about my work, and how did it affect me? Would I have done it if no one had known? Then finish this sentence: "Next time, I will correct my intention by..."

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of Uthman in both of its narrations and draws out from it the condition of sincerity; describes how the mosque's building developed, from the hadith of Ibn Umar; affirms what az-Zumar 39:20 and al-Bukhari 4878 establish about the buildings of Jannah; explains why the report in at-Tirmidhi 2526 is not relied on as primary evidence; explains an-Nawawi's two readings of "the like of it"; and identifies one hands-on act of service for a place of prayer, with a written line of intention.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with two columns, "Established by text" and "Authenticity disputed"; an activity sheet with the line of intention and the three questions; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 446 and 450 and Muslim 533 in both its narrations, al-Bukhari 4878, and at-Tirmidhi 2526 together with at-Tirmidhi's remarks on its chain; the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on az-Zumar 39:20; and an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 533. The teacher should note that as-Sa'di's tafsir mentions the description of gold, silver and musk, and should make clear that he was drawing on the report for support, which does not settle its grading. The teacher should also keep in mind that the previous lesson, on the ground of Jannah, cited the same report as supporting evidence.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Think of a public project you carried out or took part in that people talked about. How did you feel? And what did you do?" Then the teacher poses the lesson's question: Who builds in Jannah? And on what foundation?

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Ibn Umar first, to set the scene (4 minutes). Then read the hadith of Uthman in both narrations, and discuss Bukayr's uncertainty and the narration with "for Allah" (6 minutes). Then read the ayah of az-Zumar with the two tafsirs and the hadith of Abu Musa, and fill in the "Established by text" column (6 minutes). Then read the report in at-Tirmidhi and his remarks on its chain, and place it in the second column (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Explain an-Nawawi's two readings of "the like of it," then ask: "Why is the building in Jannah attributed to Allah?" and "How did Uthman answer the criticism?" Steer the discussion toward the point that answering with the text and the intention protects the work from becoming a matter of personal vindication, without diminishing people's right to give sincere advice.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner decides on their act of service for a place of prayer and when they will do it (5 minutes), writes their line of intention alone without sharing it (3 minutes), and then discusses in pairs: What might spoil the intention in this particular act of service? And how can it be guarded against? (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give two firmly established proofs of buildings in Jannah, and one report that is not relied on as primary evidence, and say why." Then read the hadith of Uthman one last time, and agree to review the three questions after the act of service.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith of Uthman and az-Zumar 39:20 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations of "Whoever builds a mosque for Allah" and compare their wording, and to consult the chapter on the buildings of Jannah in *Hadi al-Arwah* along with its editor's footnotes.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not let the hadith become an excuse for boasting about donations or for putting names on buildings, nor for belittling those who objected to Uthman with good intentions, nor for disparaging the Companions. Do not declare with certainty that any specific person has a house in Jannah, and do not measure the buildings of Jannah against the architecture of this world. Affirm the attribute of Allah in the hadith of Abu Musa just as it has come, without likening it to anything created and without asking how.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Allah builds houses in Jannah with rooms on top of rooms; mentions that Uthman built the mosque and that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever builds a mosque for Allah, Allah will build the same for him in Jannah"; and does a small service for the prayer corner after saying, "I'm doing this for Allah."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Building blocks; a picture of palm fronds, a palm trunk and mud bricks, if possible; a cloth for wiping the shelf; paper and crayons; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver deliberately leaves the prayer corner a little untidy (a folded prayer rug, a mushaf out of its place) for the child to put right, and practices telling the account without attributing to Uthman any words or feelings that are not in the report.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The child builds a small tower out of blocks, and the caregiver asks: "Who built this? And who builds the houses in Jannah?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the explanation and then the account, showing the child the pictures of the palm and the bricks when the first mosque is mentioned, and repeats with the child: "Allah will build the same for him in Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What did Uthman say when people talked?" and "Why do we build for Allah and not so that someone will praise us?" Accept simple answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child puts a hand on their heart and says, "I'm doing this for Allah," then tidies the corner, draws it all neat, and answers the question about intention, with gentleness.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Notice whether the child can say who builds in Jannah and what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said. Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the sentence "Allah builds in Jannah for whoever works for Him" and a single task are enough. Ask older children to tell the account to a brother or sister in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not present the details about "bricks of gold and silver" to the child as certain. Do not draw or act out the Companions. Do not embarrass a child who says they wanted to be praised; thank them for their honesty and teach them gently.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith in sequence, with a description of the mosque before and after Uthman; explains the four terms; distinguishes between what is firmly established and what is disputed in describing the buildings of Jannah; and carries out an act of service for a place of prayer, with an intention card and a before-and-after drawing.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a two-column chart, "Before Uthman" and "After Uthman"; small intention cards; drawing paper; simple cleaning supplies; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher arranges in advance with the mosque or school administration where the service will take place and what it will involve, and writes az-Zumar 39:20 on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show a picture of a huge building, with no writing on it, and ask: "Who built it? What is it made of? How long will it last?" Then read the ayah.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story while the students fill in the "Before Uthman / After Uthman" chart as they listen (8 minutes), then explain the terms (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 2, 3 and 4, and make clear that Uthman neither abandoned the work nor tried to vindicate himself, but took the matter back to the text; and explain why we mention the report of the bricks with caution.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes their intention card and folds it up, and draws the place before the service. The service is then carried out at the agreed time (during the session or afterward), and the drawing and answers are completed after it.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes: "Who builds in Jannah? And what is the condition of the promise in the hadith?" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, offer a ready-made intention card they can choose from. Ask advanced students to memorize az-Zumar 39:20 and to compare the narrations of al-Bukhari and Muslim.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Intention cards are not read aloud or compared. Keep cleaning tasks safe, under adult supervision. Do not speak of the Companions in any unbecoming way, and make clear that people disliked the change because they loved the mosque.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes Uthman's stance under the pressure of sincere criticism; distinguishes between useful criticism and talk that should not stop the work; explains the condition "seeking by it the Face of Allah" and the two readings of "the like of it"; justifies why the report of the bricks is treated with caution; and carries out an act of service for a place of prayer with a private review of their motives.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; cards for anonymous questions; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral examples of public projects that faced criticism (a volunteer drive, a useful web page), reviews an-Nawawi's commentary on the hadith, and is ready for the question: "Does caring about sincerity mean I should never make any good deed public?"

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "You have a good project, and people you respect criticize it. What do you do?" The teacher listens without settling the question.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the account, pausing at three moments: the first picture of the mosque, the people's objection, and Uthman's answer. Then read the terms and the ayah of az-Zumar.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 2 and 3, followed by brief presentations. The teacher guides students to see that sincerity does not forbid making good deeds public when there is a need, but it changes the reason for doing so, and that the text is the reference point when people disagree.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student decides on their act of service and when they will do it, writes their line of intention on their private sheet, anticipates one situation that might test their intention (taking photos, a comment, a comparison), and writes how they will handle it.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "What is the difference between a building people see and a building Allah builds?" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about the experience, without asking for details of anyone's intention.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on what at-Tirmidhi and al-Albani said about hadith 2526 and why they differed.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to reveal their intention. Do not let the lesson turn into accusing people's intentions, or into constant self-doubt that stops people from working. Do not use Uthman's stance to disparage those who objected, and do not draw the Companions or have anyone act out their roles.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
