# Seeing Allah, Exalted Is He, and "More Besides"

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Show from the Qur'an that the believers will see their Lord in the Hereafter, citing "Some faces that Day will be radiant, looking at their Lord" (al-Qiyamah 75:22-23) and "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides" (Yunus 10:26), together with the Prophet's own explanation, peace and blessings be upon him, of "more besides" in the hadith of Suhayb.
- Narrate the hadith of Suhayb: Allah's question to the people of Jannah, "Do you want anything more, that I may give you?", their answer, the lifting of the veil, and the fact that they were given nothing dearer to them than looking at their Lord.
- Explain the hadith of Jarir about seeing the moon on the night it is full, and show that the comparison is between one seeing and another in its clarity, not between Allah and the moon, for "There is nothing like Him."
- Set out the creed of Ahl as-Sunnah on the seeing of Allah: affirmation without likening (*tashbih*), without asking how (*takyif*) and without denial (*ta'til*), together with what al-Mutaffifin 83:15 indicates, as ash-Shafi'i understood it.
- Connect the reward to the deed: those who did what is excellent receive "more besides," and *ihsan*, excellence, is "that you worship Allah as though you see Him."
- Carry out "The Same Deed, Twice" activity: doing one deed in the usual way, then again after a pause for *ihsan*, comparing the difference, and keeping up the pause for a set number of days.
- Learn by heart a passage from an authentic Prophetic du'a: "And I ask You for the delight of looking upon Your Face, and for longing to meet You."

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Earlier lessons introduced us to the rivers of Jannah, its palaces, its food and its people. This lesson concerns a bliss above all of that: the believers looking upon their Lord. Ahl as-Sunnah wa al-Jama'ah affirm that the believers will see their Lord in the Hereafter, a real seeing with their own eyes, as the Qur'an and the mass-transmitted (*mutawatir*) Sunnah indicate. They affirm it without likening Allah to His creation, without asking how He will be seen, and without denying what He has told us. This seeing belongs to the Hereafter. In this world, the believer's station is to worship Allah "as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you."[^7]

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-75-22-25" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Some Faces That Day Will Be Radiant"

> **{وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاضِرَةٌ ۝ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ ۝ وَوُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ بَاسِرَةٌ ۝ تَظُنُّ أَنْ يُفْعَلَ بِهَا فَاقِرَةٌ}** [القيامة: ٢٢-٢٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Some faces that Day will be radiant, looking at their Lord. And some faces that Day will be grim, sensing that a backbreaking calamity is about to strike them."** (al-Qiyamah 75:22-25)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says of *nadirah*, "radiant": "From *nadarah*, that is, beautiful, splendid, shining, joyful." Of "looking at their Lord" he says: "That is, seeing Him with their own eyes." He states that the seeing is established "in the authentic hadiths, through mass-transmitted chains, according to the imams of hadith," and relates from al-Hasan: "They look at the Creator, and well may they be radiant when they are looking at the Creator." He rejects the reading that turns the ayah into waiting for the reward, saying: "Whoever says this has gone far afield indeed." As-Sa'di says of *nadirah*: "Beautiful and splendid, with a luster and a light."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayat describe two kinds of faces on the Day of Resurrection: faces shining as they look at their Lord, and faces scowling as they brace for disaster. The radiance is the mark of bliss, and looking at the Lord is its summit.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-10-26" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "For Those Who Did What Is Excellent Is the Most Excellent Reward, and More Besides"

> **{لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَىٰ وَزِيَادَةٌ ۖ وَلَا يَرْهَقُ وُجُوهَهُمْ قَتَرٌ وَلَا ذِلَّةٌ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ الْجَنَّةِ ۖ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [يونس: ٢٦][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides. No darkness and no disgrace will cover their faces. Those are the companions of Jannah; they will abide in it forever."** (Yunus 10:26)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that "more besides" includes the multiplying of the reward and the bliss that Jannah holds, "and better and higher than that is looking upon His Noble Face, for that is an increase greater than everything they have been given, which they do not deserve by their deeds, but only by His grace and mercy." He notes that interpreting it as looking upon Allah is reported from Abu Bakr as-Siddiq, Hudhayfah, Ibn 'Abbas and a number of the Salaf. As-Sa'di explains that they "did what is excellent" "in worshipping the Creator, by worshipping Him as one who knows he is watched (*muraqabah*)," and did good to the servants of Allah; that "the most excellent reward" (*al-husna*) is "Jannah, perfect in its beauty"; and that "more besides" (*ziyadah*) is "looking upon the Noble Face of Allah, hearing His speech, attaining His good pleasure, and the joy of being near Him."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The reward is of the same kind as the deed. Whoever worshipped Allah in constant awareness of Him, as though seeing Him, is rewarded with the most excellent reward, and then with "more besides," which is pure grace.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-181" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of Suhayb: "Then He Will Lift the Veil"

> عَنْ صُهَيْبٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «إِذَا دَخَلَ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ الْجَنَّةَ، يَقُولُ اللَّهُ تَبَارَكَ وَتَعَالَى: تُرِيدُونَ شَيْئًا أَزِيدُكُمْ؟ فَيَقُولُونَ: أَلَمْ تُبَيِّضْ وُجُوهَنَا؟ أَلَمْ تُدْخِلْنَا الْجَنَّةَ وَتُنَجِّنَا مِنَ النَّارِ؟ فَيَكْشِفُ الْحِجَابَ، فَمَا أُعْطُوا شَيْئًا أَحَبَّ إِلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّظَرِ إِلَى رَبِّهِمْ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ». وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ: ثُمَّ تَلَا هَذِهِ الْآيَةَ: {لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَىٰ وَزِيَادَةٌ}.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Suhayb, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"When the people of Jannah have entered Jannah, Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He, will say: 'Do you want anything more, that I may give you?' They will say: 'Have You not made our faces bright? Have You not brought us into Jannah and saved us from the Fire?' Then He will lift the veil, and they will not have been given anything dearer to them than looking at their Lord, Mighty and Majestic is He."** In another narration: Then he recited this ayah: **"For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites this hadith in his tafsir of the ayah of Yunus and makes it the foundation for interpreting "more besides" as looking upon the Noble Face of Allah.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The people of Jannah thought they had reached the very end of their hopes: bright faces, Jannah, and rescue from the Fire. Then came something that had never crossed their minds, and it was dearer to them than everything that had come before.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-554" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of Jarir: "As You See This Moon"

> عَنْ جَرِيرٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: كُنَّا عِنْدَ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ فَنَظَرَ إِلَى الْقَمَرِ لَيْلَةً، يَعْنِي الْبَدْرَ، فَقَالَ: «إِنَّكُمْ سَتَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ كَمَا تَرَوْنَ هَذَا الْقَمَرَ، لَا تُضَامُّونَ فِي رُؤْيَتِهِ، فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَنْ لَا تُغْلَبُوا عَلَى صَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا فَافْعَلُوا»، ثُمَّ قَرَأَ: {وَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَقَبْلَ الْغُرُوبِ}.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jarir, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: We were with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and he looked at the moon one night, meaning the full moon, and said: **"You will see your Lord as you see this moon, and you will not crowd one another to see Him. So if you are able not to let anything get the better of you in praying a prayer before the sun rises and before it sets, then do so."** Then he recited: **"And glorify your Lord with His praise before the rising of the sun and before its setting."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Rajab says: "He likened the seeing to the seeing, not the One seen to the thing seen, glorified and exalted is He." He gives two meanings for the comparison: seeing the moon on the night it is full "is not doubted or disputed," and "all people are equal in it, without any hardship." Al-'Ayni says: "The point of the comparison is that you will see Him with a seeing that is certain, in which there is no doubt, no hardship and nothing hidden."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith does not liken Allah to the moon. It likens how clear the seeing will be to how clearly the full moon is seen. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tied the promise to a deed: guarding the Fajr and 'Asr prayers.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-42-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "There Is Nothing Like Him"

> **{فَاطِرُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۚ جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا وَمِنَ الْأَنْعَامِ أَزْوَاجًا ۖ يَذْرَؤُكُمْ فِيهِ ۚ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ}** [الشورى: ١١][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"The Originator of the heavens and the earth. He has made for you mates from among yourselves, and mates among the livestock, multiplying you thereby. There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."** (ash-Shura 42:11)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tahawi says in his creed: "The seeing is true for the people of Jannah, without their encompassing Him and without any 'how,' just as the Book of our Lord has declared."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

We affirm what Allah has affirmed of Himself, we deny that He resembles His creation in any way, and we leave the "how" to Him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-83-15" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "No Indeed! From Their Lord, That Day, They Will Be Veiled"

> **{كَلَّا إِنَّهُمْ عَنْ رَبِّهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَمَحْجُوبُونَ}** [المطففين: ١٥][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"No indeed! From their Lord, that Day, they will be veiled."** (al-Mutaffifin 83:15)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir relates from ash-Shafi'i: "He did not veil the wicked except in the knowledge that the righteous will see Him, Mighty and Majestic is He."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

That those who denied the truth are barred from seeing Him shows that the believers will be honored with it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-50" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### Ihsan: "As Though You See Him"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه فِي سُؤَالِ جِبْرِيلَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ: قَالَ: مَا الْإِحْسَانُ؟ قَالَ: «أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ».[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, concerning Jibril's questioning of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: He said, "What is *ihsan*?" He said: **"That you worship Allah as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you."**[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

*Ihsan* in this world is worship with a heart that is fully present, as though it were seeing; its reward in the Hereafter is "more besides": looking upon the Noble Face of Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

Three things are established. The believers will see their Lord in the Hereafter, a real and clear seeing that costs them no strain. It is the dearest and highest of the joys of Jannah to them, a grace from Allah and not the price of any deed. And those who denied the truth will be veiled from it. Two principles keep all of this in its place: "There is nothing like Him," so there is no likening; and "without their encompassing Him and without any 'how,'" so there is no asking how. The path to it in this world is *ihsan*, and the hadith of Jarir names its practical sign: guarding Fajr and 'Asr.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. How do the ayat "Some faces that Day will be radiant, looking at their Lord" point to the seeing of Allah? And why did Ibn Kathir reject the reading that turns them into waiting for the reward?
2. What is "the most excellent reward," and what is "more besides"? And who interpreted "more besides" as looking upon the Face of Allah?
3. What does the answer of the people of Jannah, "Have You not made our faces bright?", teach us about contentment, and about the greatness of what they never expected?
4. What exactly is being compared in "as you see this moon"? And how would you explain it to someone who thought it likened Allah to the moon?
5. What is the link between the definition of *ihsan* in the hadith of Jibril and the reward promised to "those who did what is excellent"?

### Activity: The Same Deed, Twice

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.078.activity.as-though-you-see-him-same-deed-twice" -->

Choose one deed that recurs in your day: a particular prayer, your wudu', or a service you do for someone in your family. On the first day, do it as you always do, then write three notes: How long did it take? Where was my heart? What did I rush through? On the second day, stop for one full minute before it and bring to mind: "as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you." Then do it, and answer the same three questions. Compare the two sets of answers in a two-column table headed "My usual way" and "After the pause for *ihsan*." Then keep up the pause before the same deed for seven days, writing one word each day that describes how present you were. On the last day, write a paragraph: which half helped you more, "as though you see Him" or "He surely sees you"?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="3.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah is full of gifts from Allah: rivers, trees, beautiful homes, and happiness that never ends. But there is one gift in Jannah that is the most beautiful gift of all: the believers will see their Lord.

We don't see Allah in this world, but we believe in Him and we love Him, and He always sees us. And Allah is not like anything at all. He is not like people, not like the sun, not like the moon, not like anything we know. Allah says: "There is nothing like Him."[^5] We don't know how the seeing will be, and we don't try to picture it. We only know that it is true, and that it is the thing the people of Jannah love most of all.

<!-- unit:end -->

### From the Qur'an: A Gift "and More Besides"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-10-26" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an and the hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-10-26" audience="4-7" -->

There is a beautiful ayah in the Qur'an where Allah says: "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides." The people who "did what is excellent" are the people who did good things carefully and beautifully, with hearts that love Allah. They get "the most excellent reward," and that is Jannah. And they get "more besides," which means an even bigger gift after Jannah.[^2]

So what is this "more besides"? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us. When the believers go into Jannah, Allah will say to them: "Do you want anything more, that I may give you?" That means: "Is there something else you would like?" They will be amazed and say: "Our Lord, You have given us everything! You made our faces bright and shining, You brought us into Jannah, and You saved us from the Fire." Then Allah will lift the veil, and they will look at their Lord. And in all of Jannah they will find nothing they love more than looking at their Lord. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recited the ayah: "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides."[^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

This is the greatest gift of all. We long for it, and we get ready for it starting now: we pray calmly, we do good things carefully, and we remember that Allah sees us.

### Activity: A Quick Way and a Beautiful Way

### Bedtime Du'a

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

After the 'Isha prayer, Maryam asked her father, "Dad, what's the best thing in Jannah? The rivers? The palaces?" He said, "Everything Jannah holds is wonderful, but the best of it all is that the believers will see their Lord."

Allah says: "Some faces that Day will be radiant, looking at their Lord." In other words, faces glowing with joy, looking at their Lord.[^1]

One night, when the moon was perfectly full, the Companion Jarir, may Allah be pleased with him, was sitting with his friends in the company of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. The Prophet looked at the moon and said: "You will see your Lord as you see this moon, and you will not crowd one another to see Him." Then he told them not to let anything get the better of them when it came to the Fajr prayer and the 'Asr prayer.[^4]

Notice something important: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not say that Allah is like the moon. Allah says: "There is nothing like Him."[^5] What he compared was the seeing with the seeing. Just as everyone sees the full moon clearly, without any doubt and without pushing and shoving, the believers will see their Lord clearly. As for how? That we do not know, and we do not ask about it. We believe what we have been told.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-10-26" -->

- **`The most excellent reward (al-husna)`** — Jannah, as as-Sa'di explained it: "Jannah, perfect in its beauty."[^2]
- **`More besides (ziyadah)`** — looking upon the Noble Face of Allah, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained it in the hadith of Suhayb.[^3]
- **`You will not crowd one another (la tudammun)`** — you will not have to push against one another, and it will not be hard for you to see Him.[^4]
- **`Excellence (ihsan)`** — to worship Allah as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you.[^7]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: The Question the People of Jannah Never Expected

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-10-26" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an and from the hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that explains the ayah. The explanations within it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-10-26" audience="8-12" -->

In Surah Yunus, Allah makes a promise: "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides. No darkness and no disgrace will cover their faces." That means their faces will never be darkened or shamed. As-Sa'di said that those who "did what is excellent" are those who worshipped Allah "as one who knows he is watched," and who treated people with excellence too.[^2]

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained what "more besides" is. He said that when the people of Jannah have entered Jannah, Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He, will say: "Do you want anything more, that I may give you?" Picture the moment. They are in Jannah, their faces are shining, and they have been saved from the Fire. What could possibly be left? So they say: "Have You not made our faces bright? Have You not brought us into Jannah and saved us from the Fire?" Then Allah will lift the veil, "and they will not have been given anything dearer to them than looking at their Lord, Mighty and Majestic is He." Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recited the ayah.[^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

The people of Jannah thought they had already been given everything, and then Allah gave them what they would love more than everything. This "more besides" is a gift of Allah's grace, and the road to it is *ihsan*: praying and acting as though you see Allah, for if you do not see Him, He sees you.

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.078.dua.pleasure-of-gazing-longing-to-meet" provenance="sunnah" source_id="nasai-1305" attribution="prophetic" -->

This is part of a Prophetic du'a that the Companion 'Ammar ibn Yasir, may Allah be pleased with him, used to make in his prayer:

> **«... وَأَسْأَلُكَ لَذَّةَ النَّظَرِ إِلَى وَجْهِكَ، وَالشَّوْقَ إِلَى لِقَائِكَ، فِي غَيْرِ ضَرَّاءَ مُضِرَّةٍ، وَلَا فِتْنَةٍ مُضِلَّةٍ».**[^8]

>

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in a world that promises us "more" every single day: a newer phone, more followers, a bigger thrill. And every "more" loses its shine within days. The Qur'an promises those who do what is excellent a "more" that never fades: "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides." And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained that "more" as looking upon Allah.[^2][^3]

A friend might ask you, "How can we see Allah? Doesn't that mean He looks like something?" Ahl as-Sunnah have a clear answer. We affirm the seeing, because Allah and His Messenger told us about it: "Some faces that Day will be radiant, looking at their Lord."[^1] We deny any likeness, because "There is nothing like Him."[^5] And we do not ask how, because the seeing is, in at-Tahawi's words, "without their encompassing Him and without any 'how.'"[^5]

When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, looked at the full moon and said, "You will see your Lord as you see this moon, and you will not crowd one another to see Him," the comparison was, as Ibn Rajab put it, "the seeing to the seeing, not the One seen to the thing seen": a certain, unmistakable seeing in which everyone is equal. Then he tied it straight to a deed: "So if you are able not to let anything get the better of you in praying a prayer before the sun rises and before it sets, then do so."[^4]

On the other side, Allah says of those who denied the truth: "No indeed! From their Lord, that Day, they will be veiled." Ash-Shafi'i drew from their being veiled the proof that the righteous will see Him.[^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-10-26" -->

- **`More besides (ziyadah)`** — looking upon the Noble Face of Allah, which Ibn Kathir calls "an increase greater than everything they have been given," granted by Allah's grace and not earned by deeds.[^2]
- **`Likening (tashbih)`** — making Allah like something He created, which is ruled out by "There is nothing like Him."[^5]
- **`Asking how (takyif)`** — asking about the "how" of one of Allah's attributes, or trying to picture it; the Salaf affirmed the seeing "without their encompassing Him and without any 'how.'"[^5]
- **`Denial (ta'til)`** — denying what Allah has affirmed of Himself, or reinterpreting it until its meaning is gone, such as reading "looking at their Lord" as merely waiting for the reward.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: "And More Besides"

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-10-26" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an and from the hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that explains the ayah. The explanations within it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-10-26" audience="13+" -->

In Surah Yunus, right after Allah's invitation to the Home of Peace, the promise arrives: "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides. No darkness and no disgrace will cover their faces. Those are the companions of Jannah; they will abide in it forever." As-Sa'di said they "did what is excellent in worshipping the Creator, by worshipping Him as one who knows he is watched," and did what is excellent toward His creation as well.[^2]

The hadith of Suhayb opens up the scene. The people of Jannah have already entered it. Then Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He, says: "Do you want anything more, that I may give you?" They answer by counting three blessings: "Have You not made our faces bright? Have You not brought us into Jannah and saved us from the Fire?" It is the answer of hearts so full of contentment that they cannot imagine what could be added. "Then He will lift the veil, and they will not have been given anything dearer to them than looking at their Lord, Mighty and Majestic is He." Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recited the ayah.[^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Reflect on three things. First, the people of Jannah are grateful before the "more" ever comes; they are not asking for extra. Contentment is the character of the people of Jannah. Second, the "more" is pure grace from Allah, as Ibn Kathir said: "which they do not deserve by their deeds, but only by His grace and mercy."[^2] Third, the ayah opens with "those who did what is excellent," and *ihsan* is "that you worship Allah as though you see Him."[^7] Whoever lived conscious of Allah without seeing Him, Allah honors with seeing Him in the Hereafter. So the real question is not "How?" It is this: Am I living today like someone who is waiting for that meeting?

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What is the difference between the "more" the world keeps promising you and the "more besides" that Allah has promised?
2. How would you answer a friend who says, "If we're going to see Allah, He must look like something"?
3. Why did the people of Jannah answer by listing their blessings instead of asking for more? And what does that teach you?
4. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tie the promise of the seeing to the Fajr and 'Asr prayers?
5. In which situation, when nobody can see you, is it hardest for you to remember "He surely sees you"?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: The Same Deed, Twice

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.078.activity.as-though-you-see-him-same-deed-twice" -->

Choose something you do that no one else sees: a prayer you pray alone, a chore you do with no one checking, or your time alone with your phone. Do it today the way you usually do, then write three answers in a private note: How long did it take? Where was my heart? What did I do because nobody was watching? The next day, stop for a minute before it and read: "as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you." Then do it, and answer the same questions. Keep up the pause for seven days, with one word each day. On the last day, write a paragraph: How is what I do different when I remember the One who truly sees me, not the people who follow me?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.078.dua.pleasure-of-gazing-longing-to-meet" provenance="sunnah" source_id="nasai-1305" attribution="prophetic" -->

'Ammar ibn Yasir, may Allah be pleased with him, once led a short, light prayer. When he was taken to task for it, he said that he had made du'as in it that he had heard from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. Among them:

> **«... وَأَسْأَلُكَ لَذَّةَ النَّظَرِ إِلَى وَجْهِكَ، وَالشَّوْقَ إِلَى لِقَائِكَ، فِي غَيْرِ ضَرَّاءَ مُضِرَّةٍ، وَلَا فِتْنَةٍ مُضِلَّةٍ».**[^8]
>
> *"... And I ask You for the delight of looking upon Your Face, and for longing to meet You, without any harmful hardship and without any trial that leads astray."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, I ask You for the delight of looking upon Your Noble Face, and for a longing to meet You that fills my heart, without any affliction that harms me and without any trial that leads me astray.[^8]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner uses al-Qiyamah 75:22-23 and Yunus 10:26 as evidence for the seeing of Allah; narrates the hadith of Suhayb and the hadith of Jarir; explains that the comparison in the hadith of Jarir is between one seeing and another; sets out the rule of affirmation without likening, without asking how and without denial, with ash-Shura 42:11 and al-Mutaffifin 83:15; connects "more besides" to *ihsan*; and begins "The Same Deed, Twice" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "We affirm," "We deny" and "We do not ask"; the printed activity table with two columns and seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Qiyamah 75:22-23 and Yunus 10:26, the commentary of Ibn Rajab and al-'Ayni on the hadith of Jarir, and at-Tahawi's words on the seeing. The teacher prepares one fixed answer to the question "How?": we believe what Allah has told us, and we neither picture it nor ask how.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "If you were offered a choice between all the bliss of Jannah we have studied and one single thing above it, what do you expect that one thing would be?" Listen to the answers without correcting them.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of al-Qiyamah with the words of Ibn Kathir and al-Hasan (5 minutes); then the ayah of Yunus and the hadith of Suhayb together, pausing on the answer of the people of Jannah (6 minutes); then the hadith of Jarir and Ibn Rajab's words (5 minutes); then ash-Shura 42:11, al-Mutaffifin 83:15 and the hadith of *ihsan* (4 minutes). Learners fill in the three columns as they go.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the fourth and fifth questions. In pairs, learners practice explaining "the seeing to the seeing, not the One seen to the thing seen" in two sentences to someone hearing the hadith for the first time.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses a deed, writes the "My usual way" answers about the last time they did it, drafts the words they will use for their pause, and sets a time for the second day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What is 'more besides,' and who explained it?"; "What exactly is being compared in the hadith of the moon?"; and "Name the three principles, no likening, no asking how and no denial, with an example of each." Then read the passage of the Prophetic du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the ayat of al-Qiyamah, the hadith of Suhayb and the hadith of Jarir. Ask advanced learners to summarize the evidence Ibn Kathir gives for the seeing in his tafsir of al-Qiyamah 75:23, and to distinguish the authentic reports he cites from those the lesson does not rely on.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Use no drawing, image or sensory comparison of Allah, exalted is He, and do not ask learners to imagine the seeing. Do not present the hadith of Ibn 'Umar about looking "twice every day" as evidence: Ibn Kathir mentions the disagreement over whether it goes back to the Prophet or stops at Ibn 'Umar, and its chain includes Thuwayr ibn Abi Fakhitah. Engage anyone who reinterprets the seeing courteously and with evidence, without declaring anyone a disbeliever or attacking anyone in the room. Remind learners that the seeing is a grace from Allah: no one should despair of it, and no one should claim it is guaranteed for any particular person.[^1]

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the most beautiful gift in Jannah is that the believers will see their Lord; retells, in his or her own words, Allah's question to the people of Jannah and their answer; says that Allah is not like anything at all; does a small job twice and describes the difference; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A sheet of paper divided in half for each child; coloring pencils; a five-star card; a picture of a peaceful garden with no people, no sun and no moon; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the story in a calm voice full of longing, prepares a small job to do twice in front of the children, and sends parents a note explaining the five-star activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The educator asks: "What is the most beautiful present you've ever been given? Who gave it to you?" Then says: "In Jannah there is a gift more beautiful than every present."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The educator reads the introduction and then the story, stopping at Allah's question, "Do you want anything more, that I may give you?", to ask the children, "What do you think they will say?", and then continues. The children repeat: "For those who did what is excellent is the most excellent reward, and more besides."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator asks the unit's questions and reinforces one fixed sentence: "Allah is not like anything at all. We don't try to picture Him. We love Him, and we long to meet Him."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The educator lines up the shoes once the quick way, and then again after a pause and saying, "Allah sees me." Each child then draws the two pictures and chooses a job to do at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The educator notes whether each child can say what the people of Jannah answered. Then the du'a is read one phrase at a time, together with its meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, three stars and the sentence "Allah sees me" are enough. Older children are asked to retell the story to a brother or sister at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Allah is never drawn, and no child is asked to draw Him or imagine what He looks like. The moon, the sun or any light is never used as an example of Him. If a child asks, "What does Allah look like?", the educator answers calmly: "Allah is not like anything at all, and in Jannah we will know whatever Allah wants us to know." Do not frighten children with the thought of being kept from seeing Him; make them long for it instead.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains "looking at their Lord"; narrates the hadith of Jarir and the hadith of Suhayb; explains that the comparison in the hadith of the moon concerns the seeing, not Allah; defines the four terms; and begins "The Same Deed, Twice" card for the Fajr or 'Asr prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a mushaf; the activity card with two columns and four boxes; a small whiteboard for the "Yes or No" exercise.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads as-Sa'di's tafsir of Yunus 10:26 and al-Qiyamah 75:22 and Ibn Rajab's commentary on the hadith of Jarir, and prepares a completed activity card as a model.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads Maryam's question to her father and asks: "What would you have answered?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Qiyamah and then the hadith of Jarir (4 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes), then the story, pausing at Allah's question and the answer of the people of Jannah (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** "Yes or No" exercise: the teacher reads statements such as "We will see Allah clearly" (yes), "Allah is like the moon" (no), "We know how the seeing will be" (no) and "The seeing is the joy of Jannah its people love most" (yes), and the students back up each answer with evidence from the lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The teacher shows the model card, then each student fills in the "My usual way" column about the last Fajr or 'Asr prayer they prayed and writes the sentence for their pause.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "'More besides' is..." and "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, compared the seeing to the seeing because...". Then the du'a is read.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who are struggling, two boxes on the card and five days are enough. Advanced students are asked to memorize all of Yunus 10:26 and explain "No darkness and no disgrace will cover their faces."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask students to imagine or draw the seeing, and do not draw the moon in the lesson as a symbol of Allah. Be considerate of a student who misses Fajr because of circumstances at home: let him or her choose 'Asr instead, and never embarrass anyone in front of classmates. Answer "How?" questions with the rule: we believe, and we do not ask how.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen compares the world's "more" with the "more besides" of the Hereafter; analyzes the hadith of Suhayb and the answer of the people of Jannah; explains the comparison in the hadith of Jarir using Ibn Rajab's words; distinguishes between likening, asking how and denial; uses al-Mutaffifin 83:15 as evidence; and begins the activity of doing an unseen deed twice.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; three cards with questions posed by a skeptical friend; a private notebook for each student.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews Ibn Kathir's tafsir of al-Qiyamah 75:23 and Yunus 10:26 and the words of Ibn Rajab and at-Tahawi, and writes the three cards: "Is Allah like the moon?", "If we can't see Him now, how can we believe?" and "Isn't the seeing just a figure of speech?"

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What's the last thing you waited for eagerly that lost its shine fast?" and links the answers to the idea of a "more" that fades.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the story, with three pauses: the answer of the people of Jannah, Ibn Kathir's words about grace, and the definition of *ihsan*.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** In small groups, each with one question card, students write a three-sentence answer supported by evidence from the lesson and then present it. The teacher corrects any wording that suggests likening or asking how.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student chooses an unseen deed, writes the first day's answers about the last time they did it, and drafts their pause, without being asked to share what they wrote.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence on each of: the meaning of "more besides," what is compared in the hadith of the moon, and the difference between affirming and asking how. Then the du'a is read.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may record a private voice note instead. Advanced students are asked to trace what is transmitted from al-Hasan, Malik and ash-Shafi'i on the seeing, and to distinguish what was quoted directly from what reached us through an intermediary.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Use no images or clips that claim to depict the seeing or that symbolize Allah with a light or a shape. Respect the student who asks the skeptical question: never mock or accuse him or her. Do not turn the ayah of the veiling into a verdict on particular people. The activity notebook remains the student's own private property.
