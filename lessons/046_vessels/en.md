# The Vessels and Platters of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the ayah "Platters of gold and cups will be passed around among them" (az-Zukhruf 43:71) and the ayat "And there will be passed around among them vessels of silver and cups of crystal, crystal made of silver, measured out to the exact measure" (al-Insan 76:15-16), and explain the meaning of platters (*sihaf*), cups (*akwab*), crystal (*qawarir*) and exact measure (*taqdir*) using the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di.
- Narrate the account of Hudhayfah, may Allah be pleased with him, in al-Mada'in, when he was brought a silver cup (Sahih al-Bukhari 5632), together with the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "They are theirs in this world, and they are yours in the Hereafter."
- Explain that eating and drinking from vessels of gold and silver is forbidden to men and women alike, by a consensus that an-Nawawi reports; that the difference between men and women lies in jewelry, not in vessels; and that "theirs in this world" describes who uses them, not a permission to use them.
- Cite the hadith in al-Bukhari (4878) of two gardens whose vessels are of silver and two gardens whose vessels are of gold, and accept the matter of the unseen it describes without asking how.
- Act with good manners when served food or drink in a vessel of gold or silver, by moving it into another vessel as the scholars advise, without embarrassing the host.
- Carry out the "Ask, Then Serve" activity: serving food or drink to others after asking each person how much they need, recording what is left in the dishes over a set period, and then reflecting on the meaning of "measured out to the exact measure."
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for lawful food, for help in leaving what He has forbidden, and to make us among those in Jannah who are served platters of gold and cups of silver.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Many homes keep a set of dishes that comes out only for an honored guest: washed with care, then put carefully away again. In every culture, the dish itself carries a message. It tells the guest, "You are honored here." The Qur'an speaks of the vessels of the people of Jannah in more than one place and presents them as a sign of honor: vessels that are brought around to them, so that they never have to get up and fetch them.

This lesson brings together four texts, and three truths emerge from them:

1. **The vessels of Jannah are gold and silver, of a kind that has no equal:** platters of gold, and cups of silver as clear as glass, each one measured to the need of the one who drinks from it.
2. **Bringing them around is an act of honor:** they are passed around among the people of Jannah, who are served and honored.
3. **What is given up here is stored up there:** the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade eating and drinking from vessels of gold and silver in this world and said that they are ours in the Hereafter. So leaving these vessels today becomes an act of obedience, and the one who leaves them waits for what he has been promised.

The food and drink of the people of Jannah have lessons of their own later on; the servants who go around among them are the subject of the next lesson; and the clothing of silk was covered in the lesson on clothing.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-43-71" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Platters of Gold and Cups Will Be Passed Around Among Them"

> **{يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِصِحَافٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ ۖ وَفِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِيهِ الْأَنْفُسُ وَتَلَذُّ الْأَعْيُنُ ۖ وَأَنْتُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [الزخرف: ٧١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Platters of gold and cups will be passed around among them, and in it will be whatever souls desire and eyes delight in, and you will abide in it forever."** (az-Zukhruf 43:71)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explains "platters of gold" as "bowls, that is, vessels for food," and "cups" as "vessels for drinking, that is, of gold, without spouts or handles." As-Sa'di says: "Their servants, the immortal youths, go around among them with their food, in the finest and most splendid of vessels, which are the platters of gold." And on "whatever souls desire and eyes delight in" he says: "This is an all-embracing phrase that takes in every bliss and joy, every delight of the eye and gladness of the heart."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The verb is in the passive voice: "will be passed around among them." The attention falls on those who are served, not on whoever does the serving. And the ayah gathers three things together: the beauty of the vessel, the pleasure of what it holds, and the permanence of the bliss: "and you will abide in it forever."

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-15-16" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Crystal Made of Silver, Measured Out to the Exact Measure"

> **{وَيُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِآنِيَةٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ كَانَتْ قَوَارِيرَا * قَوَارِيرَ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ قَدَّرُوهَا تَقْدِيرًا}** [الإنسان: ١٥-١٦][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And there will be passed around among them vessels of silver and cups of crystal, crystal made of silver, which they have measured out to the exact measure."** (al-Insan 76:15-16)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "The servants go around among them with vessels for food, which are of silver, and cups for drinking, which are jugs without handles or spouts." On "crystal made of silver," he reports from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and al-Hasan: "the whiteness of silver with the clarity of glass," and then adds: "This is something that has no equal in this world." On "measured out to the exact measure," he says: "according to their thirst, neither more nor less... and this shows the utmost care, honor and generosity."[^4] As-Sa'di says: "This is one of the most wondrous of things: that dense silver, through the purity of its substance and the excellence of its metal, should have the clarity of crystal." And he gives the reason for the exact measure: "because if it were more, its pleasure would be less, and if it were less, it would not quench their thirst."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world, silver is a metal you cannot see through, and glass is clear but is not silver. The cups of Jannah are both at once. And measuring a cup exactly is itself a form of care: no overflow to be wasted, no shortfall that leaves the drinker wanting. These two ayat come in the setting of the reward of the righteous (*al-abrar*) in Surah al-Insan, which is the subject of the next lesson.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5632" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Are Theirs in This World, and They Are Yours in the Hereafter"

> عَنِ ابْنِ أَبِي لَيْلَى قَالَ: **كَانَ حُذَيْفَةُ بِالْمَدَايِنِ فَاسْتَسْقَى، فَأَتَاهُ دِهْقَانٌ بِقَدَحِ فِضَّةٍ، فَرَمَاهُ بِهِ، فَقَالَ: إِنِّي لَمْ أَرْمِهِ إِلَّا أَنِّي نَهَيْتُهُ فَلَمْ يَنْتَهِ، وَإِنَّ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم نَهَانَا عَنِ الْحَرِيرِ وَالدِّيبَاجِ وَالشُّرْبِ فِي آنِيَةِ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ، وَقَالَ: «هُنَّ لَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا، وَهِيَ لَكُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ»**.[^6]
>
> وفي رواية عند البخاري: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقُولُ: **«لَا تَلْبَسُوا الْحَرِيرَ وَلَا الدِّيبَاجَ، وَلَا تَشْرَبُوا فِي آنِيَةِ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ، وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا فِي صِحَافِهَا، فَإِنَّهَا لَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَلَنَا فِي الْآخِرَةِ»**.[^6]
>
> وفي رواية مسلم عن عبد الله بن عُكَيم: **كُنَّا مَعَ حُذَيْفَةَ بِالْمَدَائِنِ فَاسْتَسْقَى حُذَيْفَةُ، فَجَاءَهُ دِهْقَانٌ بِشَرَابٍ فِي إِنَاءٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ، فَرَمَاهُ بِهِ، وَقَالَ: إِنِّي أُخْبِرُكُمْ أَنِّي قَدْ أَمَرْتُهُ أَنْ لَا يَسْقِيَنِي فِيهِ، فَإِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: «لَا تَشْرَبُوا فِي إِنَاءِ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ، وَلَا تَلْبَسُوا الدِّيبَاجَ وَالْحَرِيرَ، فَإِنَّهُ لَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا، وَهُوَ لَكُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Ibn Abi Layla said: **Hudhayfah was in al-Mada'in and asked for a drink. A *dihqan* brought him a silver cup, and he threw it at him, and said: "I would not have thrown it, except that I had told him not to, and he did not stop. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade us silk and brocade and drinking from vessels of gold and silver, and he said: 'They are theirs in this world, and they are yours in the Hereafter.'"**[^6]
>
> In another narration in al-Bukhari: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"Do not wear silk or brocade, do not drink from vessels of gold and silver, and do not eat from their platters, for they are theirs in this world and ours in the Hereafter."**[^6]
>
> And in the narration of Muslim, from 'Abdullah ibn 'Ukaym: **We were with Hudhayfah in al-Mada'in, and Hudhayfah asked for a drink. A *dihqan* brought him a drink in a silver vessel, and he threw it at him, and said: "I am telling you that I had instructed him not to give me drink in it. For the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Do not drink from vessels of gold and silver, and do not wear brocade or silk, for it is theirs in this world, and it is yours in the Hereafter, on the Day of Resurrection.'"**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi says: "The Muslims are agreed by consensus that eating and drinking from a vessel of gold, and a vessel of silver, is forbidden to the man and to the woman." He also says: "Men and women are equal in this prohibition, without disagreement; the distinction between men and women is made only in wearing jewelry." And he reports from the scholars of his school: "If someone is faced with food in a vessel of gold or silver, let him take the food out into another vessel made of neither." He reports from al-Qadi 'Iyad that the words "theirs in this world" are a statement about the disbelievers, "whose habit it is to do that," meaning: "they are the ones who use them in this world."[^7] Al-'Ayni reports from al-Isma'ili: "What is meant is not... that their use of it is permitted," and that "yours in the Hereafter" means: "you will use it as a reward to you for having left it in this world." A *dihqan* is, in Persian, "the leader of the people and the chief of the village," and al-Mada'in was a city on the Tigris that "was the residence of the Persian kings."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

Look closely at the word itself: "do not eat from their platters" in the hadith, and "platters of gold" in the ayah. The very platters the believer leaves here are the ones promised to him there. The prohibition is a deferral, not a denial. Hudhayfah had asked for water so that he could drink, and still he sent the cup back. He threw it only after he had already told the man not to, and the man had ignored him, as Hudhayfah himself explained by way of apology; then he made the ruling and its proof clear to those present. As for a vessel that is plated with gold or silver, or mended with a band of it, the jurists discuss it in detail, and the matter should be referred to people of knowledge.[^8]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-4878" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Two Gardens of Silver, Their Vessels and All That Is in Them"

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«جَنَّتَانِ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ، آنِيَتُهُمَا وَمَا فِيهِمَا، وَجَنَّتَانِ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ، آنِيَتُهُمَا وَمَا فِيهِمَا، وَمَا بَيْنَ الْقَوْمِ وَبَيْنَ أَنْ يَنْظُرُوا إِلَى رَبِّهِمْ إِلَّا رِدَاءُ الْكِبْرِ عَلَى وَجْهِهِ فِي جَنَّةِ عَدْنٍ»**.[^9]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Two gardens of silver, their vessels and all that is in them; and two gardens of gold, their vessels and all that is in them; and nothing stands between the people and their gazing upon their Lord except the cloak of Majesty upon His Face, in Jannat 'Adn."**[^9]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Bukhari records it in the Book of Qur'an Commentary, under Surah ar-Rahman, which mentions the two gardens. The hadith shows that the vessels of each garden are of the same material as the garden itself: silver in two gardens, and gold in two gardens.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith mentions the vessels alongside something far greater: gazing upon Allah, exalted is He. We believe in what it says just as it has come to us, without asking how and without likening Allah to His creation; the vision of Allah has a lesson of its own. It is enough for us here to know that the vessels, for all their beauty, are not the summit of the bliss.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of az-Zukhruf mentions the platters of gold and the cups; the two ayat of al-Insan describe the clear, exactly measured vessels of silver; the hadith of Abu Musa makes the vessels of the gardens gold and silver and reminds us that above them lies a far greater bliss; and the hadith of Hudhayfah ties this world to the next: what we leave today in obedience to Allah is what will be brought around to us tomorrow, by His mercy. So when the believer sees a luxurious vessel in this world, he does not envy its owner; instead he remembers a promise that is truer and more lasting.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What do platters and cups mean according to Ibn Kathir? And why does the verb "will be passed around" come in the passive voice?
2. What is so wondrous about "crystal made of silver," according to as-Sa'di? And how did he explain why the cups are measured to the drinker's thirst?
3. Why do the words "They are theirs in this world" not mean that these vessels are permitted to non-Muslims? And to whom does the prohibition apply?
4. Why did the Shari'ah distinguish between men and women in jewelry but not in vessels, as an-Nawawi reports?
5. How would you respond, without embarrassing anyone, if you were served a drink in a silver vessel at the home of a relative or a friend?

### Activity: Ask, Then Serve

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.046.activity.ask-then-serve-to-need" -->

At the first family meal, serve the food and drink as you normally would. Afterward, estimate what is left on the plates and in the cups, in spoonfuls or cupfuls, and write it down. For the next five days, take charge of serving one meal a day yourself: before you pour or ladle, ask each person, "How much would you like?" Serve according to the answer, and give more to anyone who asks. Each day, record what is left in the dishes. At the end, compare the numbers with your family and agree together on one rule for your table. Then write three lines: What changed in the leftovers? What did you learn from "measured out to the exact measure"? And what rule did you agree on?

At home, we eat and drink from plates and cups. In Jannah, the dishes are more beautiful than anything we have seen: plates made of gold, and cups made of silver that shine and are clear like glass. The people of Jannah don't get up to fetch them; they are brought around to them. ### An Imagined Teaching Story: Ilyas and the Big Spoon

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.046.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

On Friday, Grandpa and Grandma came over. Mom put the big pot of rice on the table, and Dad handed Ilyas the big serving spoon. "Today you serve the rice for everybody!"

Ilyas was thrilled. He wanted to be the most generous person in the house! He piled a mountain of rice on Grandpa's plate, so high that some of it tumbled onto the tablecloth. Then he filled Grandma's plate all the way to the edge.

Grandma said gently, "Sweetheart, I only eat a little." After lunch, lots of rice was left on both plates.

Ilyas felt sad. "I wanted to give you a lot!" he said.

Dad said calmly, "Your heart was in the right place, Ilyas. But being generous means giving each person what they need. Ask them first."

At dinner, Ilyas tried again. He asked his little sister, "How many spoonfuls do you want?" "One," she said. So he gave her one. He asked Grandpa, "Two spoonfuls?" "Yes, please," said Grandpa. Grandma said, "Just a small spoonful," and that's what she got. After dinner, every plate was clean, and not a single grain of rice was wasted.

Dad said, "Do you know what? In Jannah, cups are brought around, and every cup holds exactly what its drinker wants: not a drop more, not a drop less."

Ilyas asked, "And there are plates of gold in Jannah? So why don't we eat from a gold plate now?"

Dad said, "Because the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us not to eat or drink from dishes of gold and silver in this world, and he said that they are for us in the next life.[^6] So we leave them here, and we wait for them there."

Grandma smiled and said, "May Allah give you the golden plates of Jannah, Ilyas."

### A True Account: Hudhayfah and the Silver Cup

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-5632" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-5632" audience="4-7" -->

**This really happened, and Imam al-Bukhari recorded it. It is not a made-up story.**

Hudhayfah was a Companion of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. One day, when he was in a city called al-Mada'in, he asked for something to drink.

A man brought him the drink in a cup made of silver! Hudhayfah had already told him, "Don't give me drinks in silver," but the man didn't listen.[^6]

Hudhayfah didn't drink, even though he was thirsty. He threw the cup toward the man. Then he told the people there that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had told us not to drink from dishes of gold and silver, and had said: "They are theirs in this world, and they are yours in the Hereafter."[^6]

That means: these cups aren't for us now. They are for us in Jannah.

And we don't throw things at anyone. If we are given a silver cup, we say politely, "Could I have a different cup, please?"

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.046.dua.leave-it-here-served-there" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ طَعَامَنَا حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا، وَأَعِنَّا عَلَى تَرْكِ مَا نَهَيْتَنَا عَنْهُ، وَاجْعَلْنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مِمَّنْ يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِصِحَافٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ.
>
> *"O Allah, make our food lawful and good, help us to leave what You have forbidden us, and make us, in Your Jannah, among those around whom platters of gold and cups of silver are passed."*

What it means, in simple words: O Allah, give us good food, help us leave what You told us to leave, and let us into Jannah, where there are golden plates and silver cups.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Qur'an describes the dishes of Jannah: platters of gold and cups of silver as clear as glass, carried around to its people, each cup holding exactly what its drinker needs. Allah says: "Platters of gold and cups will be passed around among them,"[^1] and: "crystal made of silver, which they have measured out to the exact measure."[^3] In this world, though, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade eating and drinking from vessels of gold and silver, for men and women alike, even though women may wear gold and silver jewelry.[^7] Whoever leaves these vessels here, obeying Allah, finds them kept safe for him there.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Ruqayyah and Grandma's Cups

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.046.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

At her friend Jana's birthday party, eleven-year-old Ruqayyah watched Jana's grandmother bring in an old box for the juice. "Pure silver cups," she announced proudly, "inherited from my mother. I only bring them out for very special guests!"

She poured juice into the gleaming cups, and the girls started snapping pictures. But Ruqayyah froze. Just last week, her dad had told her about the Companion Hudhayfah and the silver cup.

"If I refuse in front of everyone, Grandma might be hurt," she thought. "But if I drink, I'll be disobeying Allah." She wanted neither to embarrass anyone nor to lecture.

She slipped over to Jana's mom and whispered, "Auntie, could I have a regular cup, please?" She got a glass one, poured her juice into it from the silver cup, and smiled at Grandma. "Delicious juice. Thank you!"

But Lama called out, "Why did you switch cups? Do you think you're better than us?"

Ruqayyah blushed, then said simply, "No, by Allah, not at all. We just don't drink from gold or silver, because the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said they're for us in the Hereafter."

Grandma paused. "I didn't know that. I'll ask about it."

At home, Ruqayyah told her dad everything, worried she had done something wrong. He smiled and read her the scholars' advice: whoever is served food in a gold or silver vessel should move it into another vessel.[^7] "That's exactly what I did!" said Ruqayyah. "And politely," said her dad, "so you didn't embarrass Grandma. Even Hudhayfah threw the cup only after he had already told the man not to."

A week later, Jana's mom texted Ruqayyah's mom: "My mother asked about the hadith. She's selling the cups and giving the money in charity." The scholars say selling such vessels is valid, because the silver can be put to use once melted down.[^7]

Ruqayyah wrote in her notebook: "I didn't lose the silver cup. I just saved it for later."

<!-- story:end -->

### A True Account: Hudhayfah in al-Mada'in

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-5632" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-5632" audience="8-12" -->

**This true account of the Companion Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman, may Allah be pleased with him, is narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

Hudhayfah was in al-Mada'in, a city on the Tigris that had been home to the kings of Persia.[^8] With his companions around him, he asked for a drink.

A *dihqan*, one of the chiefs of that land, brought him a drink in a silver vessel. Hudhayfah had already told him not to, but the man had not listened.

So Hudhayfah threw the vessel toward him. Then he turned to the people and explained: "I am telling you that I had instructed him not to give me drink in it." He told them that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had said: "Do not drink from vessels of gold and silver... for it is theirs in this world, and it is yours in the Hereafter, on the Day of Resurrection."[^6]

In another narration: "and do not eat from their platters."[^6] "Platters" is the same word as in the ayah: "Platters of gold... will be passed around among them."

Notice three things: Hudhayfah gave up a drink he had asked for himself; he had warned the man before instead of losing his temper the first time; and afterward he taught the people the ruling and its proof.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-5632" -->

- **`Platters (sihaf)`** — the plural of *sahfah*, a vessel for food, like a wide dish.[^2]
- **`Cups (akwab)`** — drinking vessels without handles (*'ura*) or spouts.[^4]
- **`Crystal (qawarir)`** — clear glass; the cups of Jannah are silver, yet as clear as glass.[^4]
- **`Measured out to the exact measure (qaddaruha taqdira)`** — holding just enough to quench the drinker's thirst, neither more nor less.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

2. How did Ruqayyah avoid embarrassing Grandma? What scholarly advice matches what she did?
3. What had Hudhayfah done before he threw the vessel? And what did he do afterward?
4. What does "it is theirs in this world, and it is yours in the Hereafter" mean?
5. Why were the cups of Jannah "measured out to the exact measure"? How can that help at your dinner table?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.046.dua.leave-it-here-served-there" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ طَعَامَنَا حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا، وَأَعِنَّا عَلَى تَرْكِ مَا نَهَيْتَنَا عَنْهُ، وَاجْعَلْنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مِمَّنْ يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِصِحَافٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ.

> *"O Allah, make our food lawful and good, help us to leave what You have forbidden us, and make us, in Your Jannah, among those around whom platters of gold and cups of silver are passed."*

What it means: O Allah, give us lawful, good food; help us leave what You have forbidden us, including eating and drinking from gold and silver vessels; and let us into Jannah, where platters of gold and cups of silver are carried around to its people.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a world of images, the vessel has become the message: a gold-rimmed cup in a luxury café, a silver tea set in a styled table shot, comments that measure people's worth by what they eat from. Islam sees differently. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade eating and drinking from vessels of gold and silver, and said: "They are theirs in this world, and they are yours in the Hereafter."[^6] The prohibition covers men and women alike, without disagreement; they differ only in jewelry.[^7] And in Jannah, cups of "crystal made of silver" are carried around to its people: silver with the clarity of glass, which Ibn Kathir calls "something that has no equal in this world."[^4] So the question is not "What vessel will I be seen with?" but "What am I waiting for?"

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Sumayyah and the Glass Cups

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.046.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Sixteen-year-old Sumayyah ran a small account of photos of the tea and desserts she made, with about three thousand followers. At an antiques market, she found an old set of six pure-silver tea glasses. It cost everything she had saved in a year. Her friend Lina said, "This is it! Tea in silver is going to blow up. All the big accounts do this."

Every night before sleep, Sumayyah bought that set again in her imagination. But at her Friday Qur'an circle, the teacher read how Hudhayfah, in al-Mada'in, said the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had forbidden drinking from vessels of gold and silver.

After class, Sumayyah asked, "But I'm a girl, and silver is allowed for women, isn't it?" The teacher said, "Jewelry is allowed for women. But as for vessels, an-Nawawi reported a consensus that eating and drinking from them is forbidden for both men and women.[^7] Beyond that, such as keeping them for display, the scholars have discussed it, so ask people of knowledge."

Sumayyah said, "And if I don't drink from them? If I just photograph them?"

"Ask people of knowledge about the ruling," said the teacher. "And ask yourself: what will that picture say to three thousand people?"

Sumayyah barely slept. The photo was composed in her head, and Lina was waiting. In the morning, she went back to the market and, for half the price, bought six cups of clear, delicate glass that light passed straight through. She remembered that the scholars had said a vessel of precious glass is not forbidden, by consensus.[^7]

She set the cups by the window and poured, and the light came through the glass turned to gold. Under the photo she wrote no sermon, just: "Afternoon tea."

Lina said, "Nice, but kind of ordinary. It could have gone much further." She was right: it got fewer likes than usual.

But in her private journal, Sumayyah wrote: "Today I read that the cups of Jannah are 'crystal made of silver.' I chose glass here, and I'm hoping for clear silver there. And I'm going to give the money I have left in charity."

### A True Account: A Cup Sent Back in al-Mada'in

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-5632" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-5632" audience="13+" -->

**This true account of Hudhayfah ibn al-Yaman, may Allah be pleased with him, is in the Sahih collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

In al-Mada'in, once the residence of the Persian kings,[^8] Hudhayfah asked for a drink. A *dihqan*, one of the local notables, brought him a drink in a silver cup. Hudhayfah had already told him not to, and the man had not stopped.

So Hudhayfah threw the cup toward him. Then he apologized to those present and explained: "I threw it only because I had told him not to, and he would not stop." He told them that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had forbidden them silk, brocade, and drinking from vessels of gold and silver, and had said: "They are theirs in this world, and they are yours in the Hereafter."[^6]

Picture it: a thirsty man, in the land of kings and palaces, is offered a drink in the most splendid vessel there is, and he sends it back. He was not refusing the drink, but the vessel he had been forbidden. Al-'Ayni reports from al-Isma'ili that "yours in the Hereafter" means you will use it "as a reward to you for having left it in this world."[^8]

Notice, too, what he did not do: he did not spring his anger on the man the first time, and he did not leave the people untaught.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-5632" -->

- **`Dihqan`** — a Persian word meaning the leader of a people and the chief of a village.[^8]
- **`Their platters (sihafiha)`** — dishes for food made of gold and silver; it is the very word used in the promise to the people of Jannah: "platters of gold."[^2]
- **`Crystal made of silver (qawariru min fiddah)`** — the whiteness of silver with the clarity of glass, something that, as Ibn Kathir said, has no equal in this world.[^4]

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did Lina's words sound convincing? And what made Sumayyah change her mind?
2. The teacher knew the ruling on drinking. So why didn't she answer everything, and why did she send Sumayyah to people of knowledge on some of the questions?
3. What did Sumayyah lose by her decision? And what did she gain?
4. Why did Hudhayfah send the cup back when he had asked for the drink himself? And what shows that he had not acted rashly?
5. How do you see the images of luxurious tables on your screens after hearing his words, peace and blessings be upon him: "They are theirs in this world, and they are yours in the Hereafter"?

### Activity: Ask, Then Serve

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.046.dua.leave-it-here-served-there" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ طَعَامَنَا حَلَالًا طَيِّبًا، وَأَعِنَّا عَلَى تَرْكِ مَا نَهَيْتَنَا عَنْهُ، وَاجْعَلْنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مِمَّنْ يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِصِحَافٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ.
>
> *"O Allah, make our food lawful and good, help us to leave what You have forbidden us, and make us, in Your Jannah, among those around whom platters of gold and cups of silver are passed."*

This du'a joins three steps: keeping what enters your home good and lawful, patience with what you give up for Allah, and hope for what He has stored up for those who left gold and silver vessels in this world.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites the ayah of az-Zukhruf (43:71) and the two ayat of al-Insan (76:15-16) and explains platters, cups, crystal and exact measure using the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; narrates the account of Hudhayfah (al-Bukhari 5632 and Muslim 2067) and explains, using an-Nawawi's words, the ruling on eating and drinking from vessels of gold and silver for men and women; explains the meaning of "theirs in this world" without taking it as a permission; formulates a courteous way of moving a drink into another vessel; and begins the "Ask, Then Serve" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "What the vessels are like," "What the honor means," and "What we leave here"; a printed activity chart for each learner (one observation day, then five days).

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on az-Zukhruf 43:71 and al-Insan 76:15-16, an-Nawawi's commentary on the chapter on the prohibition of gold and silver vessels in Sahih Muslim, and al-'Ayni's commentary on al-Bukhari 5632; and prepares two cups: one filled until it overflows, and one filled just to what is needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the two cups and asks: "Which of these honors a guest more? And why?" Then reads "measured out to the exact measure" without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of az-Zukhruf and its tafsir, then the two ayat of al-Insan with the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on the crystal and the exact measure, then the hadith of Abu Musa. Then read the account of Hudhayfah in al-Bukhari's two narrations and Muslim's narration, pausing at Hudhayfah's apology and his explanation to those present. Learners fill in the three columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion of three questions: Why does "They are theirs in this world" not mean permission, as al-Qadi 'Iyad and al-Isma'ili explained? Why did the Shari'ah distinguish between women and men in jewelry but not in vessels? And how do we apply the scholars' advice to move food into another vessel when we are at the home of a friend or relative, without embarrassing anyone?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains the "Ask, Then Serve" activity as described in the activity unit, and each learner decides which meal they will take charge of, how they will measure the leftovers, and which family members they will agree with on a rule for the table.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes: the meaning of "measured out to the exact measure" according to as-Sa'di; the ruling on eating and drinking from vessels of gold and silver, with its evidence; and a sentence they would say if served a drink in a silver vessel. Agree that anyone who wishes will share their three lines a week later.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayah of az-Zukhruf and the narration of al-Bukhari 5632 are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare the three narrations and what each one adds (eating from the platters, and "on the Day of Resurrection"), and to read an-Nawawi's words on a vessel in which food is served.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Hudhayfah's throwing of the cup is not to be taken as a model for harshness with people; he made clear that he had told the man before and the man had not stopped, and the default is wisdom and gentleness. The details of vessels plated with, or mended with a band of, gold or silver, and of keeping such vessels without using them, are matters of disagreement and detailed discussion that should be referred to people of knowledge; no fatwa is given on them in the lesson. Under the ayah of az-Zukhruf, Ibn Kathir cites reports about the number of platters (seventy thousand platters, and three hundred platters), among them a report that 'Ikrimah attributes directly to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, without naming a Companion; nothing from these reports is relied upon in this lesson.[^10] The vision of Allah, exalted is He, in the hadith of Abu Musa is affirmed just as it has come, without asking how, and it has its own lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that in Jannah there are plates of gold and cups of silver that are brought around to its people, and that we do not eat or drink from gold and silver in this world; asks "How much would you like?" before serving; politely asks for a different cup; and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A plastic bowl filled with dried beans or small blocks in place of rice, for role-play; a serving spoon; plastic plates and cups; a sheet with three plates drawn on it for coloring; gold and silver crayons; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the story of Ilyas and the account of Hudhayfah in a calm voice, and sets up the role-play on a small table.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What's the most beautiful dish in our house? And who do we bring it out for?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell the story of Ilyas, then say, "And this one really happened," and tell the account of Hudhayfah. The child repeats: "Platters of gold and cups will be passed around among them."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver first acts out the mistake, filling a plate until it overflows, then asks the child: "What should I have done?" and repeats with the child: "First I ask, then I serve." Then the child practices the sentence: "Could I have a different cup, please?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The child serves family members after asking them, as described in the activity unit; colors a smiling plate if no food is left; and colors the plate of Jannah gold and its cup silver.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What do you say before you serve?" and "Why didn't Hudhayfah drink from the silver cup?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the caregiver holds the spoon together with the child, and two plates are enough. Older children serve three people and remember their answers without asking again.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not use hot food or glass dishes. Do not scold the child if food spills; the story praises Ilyas's intention before correcting what he did. Stress that we do not throw things at anyone, and that Hudhayfah had told the man before. Do not tell the child that a relative who owns gold or silver dishes is a bad person; it is enough to say that we leave eating and drinking from them in obedience to Allah. And make sure the child does not come away thinking that gold-colored or silver-colored dishes made of plastic or other metals are forbidden.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains platters, cups, crystal and exact measure; narrates the account of Hudhayfah in al-Mada'in and states what he did before throwing the vessel and after it; states that the prohibition applies to men and women; acts politely when served a drink in a vessel of gold or silver; and carries out the activity with their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the ayat and the hadith written out; cards for the four terms; the activity chart; three scenario cards: "Hospitality at a friend's home with silver cups," "A gift of a silver cup," and "A restaurant that serves a shiny cup made of an unknown metal."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes two questions on the board, "What do I do?" and "How do I say it politely?", and reviews an-Nawawi's words on moving food into another vessel.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Have you ever turned down something you were offered and worried about embarrassing the person who offered it? What did you do?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the ayah of az-Zukhruf and the two ayat of al-Insan, then tell the account of Hudhayfah. Students read the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, exactly as he said them, and the terms are explained.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read the story of Ruqayyah, with the teacher pausing at her moment of hesitation. Then students work in pairs on the scenario cards and write a polite response for each one; on the third card, point out that shininess alone does not mean a cup is made of silver.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students fill in the "Ask, Then Serve" chart as described in the activity unit, and each student decides which meal they will take charge of with their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentences: "The cups of Jannah are 'measured out to the exact measure,' which means..." and "If I am served a drink in a silver cup, I will...". Then read the du'a. A week later, students share their charts.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who struggle ready-made sentences for a polite reply. Advanced students write a short dialogue between Ruqayyah and Lama in which the hadith is explained kindly.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not encourage students to correct adults publicly or to shame them, or to throw anything; the story teaches good manners, not confrontation. Distinguish between vessels actually made of gold and silver and gold-colored or silver-colored vessels made of other metals, and give no fatwa on plated vessels or those mended with a band of gold or silver.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the on-screen culture of the "luxury vessel" in light of the account of Hudhayfah; explains the ruling on vessels for men and women and how it differs from the ruling on jewelry; describes the vessels of Jannah using the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; distinguishes between what has been reported as consensus and what should be asked of people of knowledge; and carries out the activity over a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; three printed photos of luxurious tables from social media, with no names or logos; a clear glass cup; the activity chart; anonymous cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher removes names and logos from the photos, and reviews an-Nawawi's words on the consensus and on vessels of precious glass, and Ibn Kathir's words on "crystal made of silver."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the photos and ask: "What does the vessel in this picture say about its owner? And who is the audience?" Then hold the glass cup up to the light: "Imagine silver that light passes through like this."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the account of Hudhayfah in al-Bukhari's two narrations and Muslim's narration, then the ayah of az-Zukhruf and the two ayat of al-Insan, then the statements of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and an-Nawawi; the terms are explained.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read the story of Sumayyah, and have groups discuss: "What arguments did Lina make? Which of them need to be taken to people of knowledge? And what made Sumayyah's decision possible?" Then ask: "Why didn't Sumayyah write a sermon under her photo?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students begin the "Ask, Then Serve" chart as described in the activity unit; each student privately writes down the meal they will take charge of and commits to not photographing the table for a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes on an anonymous card: "The difference between a vessel photographed for an audience and a vessel passed around in Jannah is...". Then read the du'a. A week later, anyone who wishes shares their paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to compare the narrations of the account of Hudhayfah and set out what each narration adds.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not look down on anyone who owns luxurious things, and do not accuse anyone of eating from forbidden vessels on the strength of a photo alone. Questions of keeping such vessels, photographing them, and plated vessels are referred to people of knowledge; no fatwa is given on them in the lesson. A student whose family is going through financial hardship is never asked to talk about it, and no one is required to buy anything for the activity.
