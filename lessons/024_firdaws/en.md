# Al-Firdaws al-A'la: The Highest Garden

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Relate the instruction with which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, closed the hadith of the hundred degrees, "So when you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws" (al-Bukhari 2790), and name the three things he said about al-Firdaws: it is the middle of Jannah and its highest part, above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful, and from it the rivers of Jannah gush forth.
- Explain, as the commentators have, how al-Firdaws can be both the "middle" and the "highest" part of Jannah, and show that the name al-Firdaws is used both for Jannah as a whole and for its finest, highest part.
- Draw on the two ayat of al-Kahf (18:107-108) to show that the Gardens of al-Firdaws are the lodging prepared for believers who act on their faith, and on the opening ayat of al-Mu'minun (23:1-11) to identify the concrete qualities of those who inherit al-Firdaws.
- Relate the account of Umm Harithah, may Allah be pleased with her, who asked about her son killed at Badr, and the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him: "There are gardens within Jannah, and your son has attained al-Firdaws al-A'la" (al-Bukhari 2809), and draw from it that al-Firdaws is a gift of Allah's grace that a sincere believer can reach, even one who dies young.
- Distinguish the praiseworthy humility of thinking little of one's own deeds from the mistake of lowering what one asks of Allah, and explain that aiming high by asking for al-Firdaws is obedience to the Prophet's command, peace and blessings be upon him, not arrogance.
- Carry out the "Spring and Streams" activity: choose one quality of the inheritors of al-Firdaws as a spring, map the good deeds that branch out from it, keep it up for a set period, and then observe its effect.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for al-Firdaws al-A'la and for help in praying with a humble, present heart and guarding the prayers.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In the lesson on the degrees of Jannah, we learned that Jannah has a hundred degrees, and that the distance between any two of them is like the distance between heaven and earth. This lesson dwells on the words with which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, closed that hadith: "So when you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws." He did not say, "Ask Him for the lowest part of Jannah," nor, "Ask Him for whatever matches your deeds." He pointed the entire ummah to the very highest thing Jannah holds, and then described it in three ways: it is the middle of Jannah and its highest part, above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful, and from it the rivers of Jannah gush forth.

This guidance corrects two opposite mistakes:

1. **Lowering one's request in the name of humility:** saying, "A little corner of Jannah is enough for me," on the assumption that asking for the highest is a presumption unbecoming of someone who falls short. True humility means a servant thinks little of his own deeds and relies on his Lord's mercy; it does not mean thinking little of what the Most Generous can give.
2. **Raising one's request without lifting a finger:** asking for al-Firdaws again and again while turning away from the very means of reaching it, even though the Qur'an describes the inheritors of al-Firdaws by specific deeds: humility in prayer, turning away from idle talk, giving zakah, guarding one's chastity, honoring trusts and covenants, and guarding the prayers.

Between these two stands the account of Umm Harithah: a mother stricken by the loss of her young son, who asked neither for wealth nor for revenge, but only about where he had ended up. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave her the glad tidings that Jannah is made up of many gardens, and that her son had attained al-Firdaws al-A'la. Al-Firdaws, then, is not reserved for those who live long lives or whose names are on everyone's lips. It is a gift of Allah's grace, which He grants to whomever He wills among the sincere.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2790" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "So When You Ask Allah, Ask Him for al-Firdaws"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَبِرَسُولِهِ، وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَصَامَ رَمَضَانَ، كَانَ حَقًّا عَلَى اللَّهِ أَنْ يُدْخِلَهُ الْجَنَّةَ، جَاهَدَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، أَوْ جَلَسَ فِي أَرْضِهِ الَّتِي وُلِدَ فِيهَا»**. فَقَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَفَلَا نُبَشِّرُ النَّاسَ؟ قَالَ: **«إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِائَةَ دَرَجَةٍ أَعَدَّهَا اللَّهُ لِلْمُجَاهِدِينَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، مَا بَيْنَ الدَّرَجَتَيْنِ كَمَا بَيْنَ السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ، فَإِذَا سَأَلْتُمُ اللَّهَ فَاسْأَلُوهُ الْفِرْدَوْسَ، فَإِنَّهُ أَوْسَطُ الْجَنَّةِ وَأَعْلَى الْجَنَّةِ - أُرَاهُ - فَوْقَهُ عَرْشُ الرَّحْمَنِ، وَمِنْهُ تَفَجَّرُ أَنْهَارُ الْجَنَّةِ»**. قَالَ مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ فُلَيْحٍ عَنْ أَبِيهِ: «وَفَوْقَهُ عَرْشُ الرَّحْمَنِ».[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever believes in Allah and His Messenger, establishes the prayer, and fasts Ramadan, it is a right upon Allah to admit him into Jannah, whether he strives in the path of Allah or stays in the land where he was born."** They said, "O Messenger of Allah, shall we not give the people this good news?" He said: **"In Jannah there are a hundred degrees that Allah has prepared for those who strive in His path; the distance between two degrees is like the distance between heaven and earth. So when you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws, for it is the middle of Jannah and the highest part of Jannah. (I think he said:) Above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful, and from it the rivers of Jannah gush forth."** Muhammad ibn Fulayh reported from his father: **"And above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The words "I think he said" (*urahu*) record one narrator's uncertainty over the phrase "above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful." The phrase appears without any such hesitation in the narration of Muhammad ibn Fulayh from his father, quoted at the end of the hadith. Al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar cites Ibn Hibban's view that "middle" refers to breadth and "highest" to elevation, and he notes that "middle" here carries the sense of most balanced and most excellent.[^6] Ibn Battal points out that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded his entire ummah to ask for al-Firdaws, including those who never went out in jihad, which shows that Allah may, by His grace, bring someone who did not fight in His path to al-Firdaws.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The command to ask for al-Firdaws comes right after a general promise of entry to everyone who believes and keeps the obligations. It is as if the hadith were saying: do not let your aspiration stop at merely being saved. And because the command is addressed to everyone, asking for al-Firdaws does not require a servant to consider himself worthy of it. It is enough that he obeys.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-18-107-108" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Gardens of al-Firdaws Will Be Their Lodging"

> **{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ كَانَتْ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتُ الْفِرْدَوْسِ نُزُلًا * خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا لَا يَبْغُونَ عَنْهَا حِوَلًا}** [الكهف: ١٠٧-١٠٨][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, those who believe and do righteous deeds will have the Gardens of al-Firdaws as a lodging, abiding therein forever, never wishing to be moved from it."** (al-Kahf 18:107-108)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

A *nuzul* (lodging) is the honor made ready for a guest upon arrival, and "never wishing to be moved from it" means they will never seek to leave it or prefer anything else. Ibn Kathir cites at this ayah the hadith "When you ask Allah for Jannah, ask Him for al-Firdaws." Ibn al-Qayyim explains that the name al-Firdaws is used for Jannah as a whole, and also for its finest and highest part, as though that part has the greatest claim to the name. He adds that the word originally means an orchard, and cites al-Zajjaj's view that its real sense is an orchard that gathers within it everything orchards can hold.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah makes faith and righteous deeds together the condition, and it describes a bliss of which its people never tire. Every pleasure in this world eventually leaves a person wanting a change; al-Firdaws is the one place for which no one will ever want an alternative.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-23-1-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Those Who Will Inherit al-Firdaws"

> **{قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ * الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي صَلَاتِهِمْ خَاشِعُونَ * وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ عَنِ اللَّغْوِ مُعْرِضُونَ * وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِلزَّكَاةِ فَاعِلُونَ * وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِفُرُوجِهِمْ حَافِظُونَ * إِلَّا عَلَىٰ أَزْوَاجِهِمْ أَوْ مَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُهُمْ فَإِنَّهُمْ غَيْرُ مَلُومِينَ * فَمَنِ ابْتَغَىٰ وَرَاءَ ذَٰلِكَ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْعَادُونَ * وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ لِأَمَانَاتِهِمْ وَعَهْدِهِمْ رَاعُونَ * وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ عَلَىٰ صَلَوَاتِهِمْ يُحَافِظُونَ * أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْوَارِثُونَ * الَّذِينَ يَرِثُونَ الْفِرْدَوْسَ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [المؤمنون: ١-١١][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Successful indeed are the believers: those who are humble in their prayer; who turn away from idle talk; who are active in giving zakah; who guard their chastity, except with their spouses or those their right hands possess, for then they are free from blame, but whoever seeks anything beyond that, it is they who are the transgressors; who are faithful to their trusts and their covenants; and who carefully guard their prayers. It is they who are the inheritors, who will inherit al-Firdaws, abiding therein forever."** (al-Mu'minun 23:1-11)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explains that these are the qualities of the successful believers, and that Allah closes the list by declaring them the inheritors who will inherit al-Firdaws. *Al-laghw* (idle talk) is falsehood and every word or deed that serves no good purpose; *al-khushu'* (humility in prayer) is the heart's stillness and presence during the prayer.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The list of qualities opens with prayer and closes with prayer: humility within it first, and faithfully guarding it last. Prayer is like the spring from which all the other qualities flow. This image belongs to the lesson's explanation, not to the tafsir, and it is the basis of the "Spring and Streams" activity.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2809" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Your Son Has Attained al-Firdaws al-A'la"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه: أَنَّ أُمَّ الرُّبَيِّعِ بِنْتَ الْبَرَاءِ، وَهِيَ أُمُّ حَارِثَةَ بْنِ سُرَاقَةَ، أَتَتِ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَقَالَتْ: يَا نَبِيَّ اللَّهِ، أَلَا تُحَدِّثُنِي عَنْ حَارِثَةَ - وَكَانَ قُتِلَ يَوْمَ بَدْرٍ، أَصَابَهُ سَهْمٌ غَرْبٌ - فَإِنْ كَانَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ صَبَرْتُ، وَإِنْ كَانَ غَيْرَ ذَلِكَ اجْتَهَدْتُ عَلَيْهِ فِي الْبُكَاءِ. قَالَ: **«يَا أُمَّ حَارِثَةَ، إِنَّهَا جِنَانٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَإِنَّ ابْنَكِ أَصَابَ الْفِرْدَوْسَ الْأَعْلَى»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him: Umm ar-Rubayyi' bint al-Bara', the mother of Harithah ibn Suraqah, came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "O Prophet of Allah, will you not tell me about Harithah?" He had been killed on the day of Badr, struck by a stray arrow. "If he is in Jannah, I will be patient; but if it is otherwise, I will pour all my strength into weeping for him." He said: **"O Umm Harithah, there are gardens within Jannah, and your son has attained al-Firdaws al-A'la, the highest of them."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

A *sahm gharb* (stray arrow) is one whose archer is unknown. The commentators have noted that the name given to the mother in this narration reflects an error by one of the narrators, and that she was in fact ar-Rubayyi' bint an-Nadr, the paternal aunt of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with them both.[^2] The account also shows that the mother tied her patience to her son's fate, and the answer she received gave her more than she had asked for: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not stop at telling her that her son was in Jannah, but told her he was in its highest part.

#### Lesson Explanation

This account mentions no long record of deeds for Harithah and no long life. He was killed by an arrow whose archer no one knew, in the first great battle of Islam. And yet he attained al-Firdaws al-A'la. Al-Firdaws is a gift that Allah grants to whoever is sincere, not a reward tallied by the length of one's years.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3982" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Is It Only One Garden?"

> عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: أُصِيبَ حَارِثَةُ يَوْمَ بَدْرٍ وَهُوَ غُلَامٌ، فَجَاءَتْ أُمُّهُ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَقَالَتْ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، قَدْ عَرَفْتَ مَنْزِلَةَ حَارِثَةَ مِنِّي، فَإِنْ يَكُنْ فِي الْجَنَّةِ أَصْبِرْ وَأَحْتَسِبْ، وَإِنْ تَكُ الْأُخْرَى تَرَى مَا أَصْنَعُ. فَقَالَ: **«وَيْحَكِ، أَوَهَبِلْتِ؟ أَوَجَنَّةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ هِيَ؟ إِنَّهَا جِنَانٌ كَثِيرَةٌ، وَإِنَّهُ فِي جَنَّةِ الْفِرْدَوْسِ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: Harithah was struck down on the day of Badr while he was still a youth. His mother came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "O Messenger of Allah, you know how dear Harithah is to me. If he is in Jannah, I will be patient and seek my reward from Allah; but if it is the other, you will see what I do." He said: **"Oh, poor woman (*wayhaki*)! Have you lost your senses? Is it only one garden? There are many gardens, and he is in the Garden of al-Firdaws."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Hubilti* comes from *habal*, the loss of a child, and the commentators explain the meaning as: has the shock of losing your son so stunned you that you imagine Jannah is a single garden? It is a merciful rhetorical question that corrects her picture of things, not a curse upon her.[^3] *Ghulam* means a young man, a youth.

#### Lesson Explanation

This narration adds two things: that Harithah was a youth, and that Jannah is "many gardens" of differing rank, so its bliss must not be pictured as one single station. This ties the present lesson to the lesson on the degrees that came before it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The hadith of Abu Hurayrah commands us to ask for al-Firdaws and describes where it lies; the two ayat of al-Kahf make it the lodging of those who combine faith with deeds; the ayat of al-Mu'minun spell those deeds out as specific qualities; and the account of Umm Harithah shows the promise fulfilled for a sincere young man whose life was short. The request is lofty, the road is clearly marked, and the gift is Allah's grace.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why does the command to ask for al-Firdaws come after the general promise of entry in the hadith of the hundred degrees?
2. How did the commentators reconcile the description of al-Firdaws as both "the middle of Jannah" and "the highest part of Jannah"?
3. What is the difference between humility in how you view your own deeds and lowering what you ask of Allah?
4. Why do the qualities of the inheritors of al-Firdaws in Surah al-Mu'minun begin with prayer and end with it?
5. What does it teach you that Harithah attained al-Firdaws al-A'la as a youth killed by an arrow whose archer no one knew?

### Activity: The Spring and the Streams

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.024.activity.spring-and-streams" -->

At the top of a sheet of paper, write: "O Allah, I ask You for al-Firdaws." Then choose, from the qualities in Surah al-Mu'minun (23:1-11), the one you consider your weakest, and draw it as a spring. From it, draw at least three streams, each carrying a good deed you expect to improve as that spring grows stronger (for example: humility in prayer → greater honesty in speech → more patience with family). Keep up the spring for one full week with a specific daily step, then check off the streams that actually began to flow, and write two lines about what you noticed.

Jannah has lots and lots of places, and some are higher up than others. The very highest place in Jannah is called `al-Firdaws`. The beautiful rivers of Jannah flow out from al-Firdaws. ### An Imagined Teaching Story: Where Does the Water Come From?

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.024.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Yusuf and his sister Maryam went on a trip to the mountain with their dad. Yusuf spotted a little stream trickling between the rocks. Its water was so clear it sparkled in the sun. "Dad," Yusuf asked, "where does this water come from?" "Let's climb up and find out!" said Dad.

They climbed for a little while, and Yusuf got tired. He saw a small pool with just a little water in it, so he sat down and said, "This is far enough. I don't want to climb anymore." Maryam took his hand and said, "It's not much farther, Yusuf. Come with us!" So Yusuf decided to keep going. Up he went, one step at a time, with Dad holding his other hand.

At the very top of the mountain, they found a spring with water bubbling out of it, cool and clean, and all the little streams ran down the mountain from there. Yusuf took a drink, laughed, and said, "The water up here is much nicer than the water in the pool!"

Dad sat down and said, "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the highest part of Jannah is called al-Firdaws, and that the rivers of Jannah flow out from it. And he told us: when you make du'a to Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws." "Then I won't ask for a little place," said Yusuf. "I'm going to ask Allah for al-Firdaws!" "Well done," said Dad. "And we'll pray well, so that we can be among its people."

That night, Yusuf prayed Isha calmly next to his dad. Then he raised his hands and said, "O Allah, please give me al-Firdaws."

### A True Account: Umm Harithah Asks About Her Son

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2809" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2809" audience="4-7" -->

**This really happened. Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, told us about it. It is not a made-up story.**

There was once a young man named Harithah. He went out with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, on the day of Badr, and an arrow hit him, and he died there.[^3] His mother was very, very sad. She came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "Tell me about Harithah. If he is in Jannah, I will be patient. But if he isn't there, I will cry and cry for him." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to her, "O Umm Harithah, Jannah has lots of gardens, and your son has reached al-Firdaws, the highest one."[^2]

His mother was so happy to hear this good news, and she knew that her son was in the very highest part of Jannah. This shows us that al-Firdaws isn't only for grown-ups. Allah gives it to the truthful people He loves, even if they are young.

### Bedtime Du'a

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah has many, many degrees, and the highest, the middle, and the very best of them all is `al-Firdaws`. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful and that the rivers of Jannah gush out from it, and he gave us a clear instruction: "So when you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws."[^1] Asking for al-Firdaws isn't showing off. It is obeying the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. In Surah al-Mu'minun, Allah describes the people who will inherit al-Firdaws. Some of their qualities are: being humble and focused in prayer, staying away from empty, pointless talk, taking good care of what they are trusted with and keeping their promises, and praying all their prayers faithfully.[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Omar's Grandma and Her Du'a

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.024.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Omar loved sitting next to his grandma after the Maghrib prayer. Every day she made the same quiet du'a: "O Lord, a little spot by the gate of Jannah is enough for me." And every day Omar said "Ameen" without really thinking about it.

On Friday, at the study circle in the masjid, the teacher read out the hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "So when you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws, for it is the middle of Jannah and the highest part of Jannah." Omar raised his hand. "Teacher, isn't it more humble to ask for a small place? That's what my grandma does." The teacher smiled. "Humility means seeing your own deeds as small, not seeing Allah's gifts as small. And it was the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, himself who told us to ask for the highest."

Omar walked home with a knot in his stomach. Should he tell his grandma that her du'a was wrong? He was afraid of hurting her feelings, or of sounding like he was lecturing someone much older than he was. So he decided to ask her politely instead of correcting her. He sat down beside her and said, "Grandma, I learned a beautiful hadith today. Would you like me to read it to you?" And he read it from his notebook.

His grandma was quiet for a moment. Then she laughed and said, "SubhanAllah! I always thought asking for the highest place wasn't fitting for someone like me. Then we'll ask for al-Firdaws together." After a pause she added, "But, Omar, whoever asks for the highest has to work for it." So the two of them agreed on one thing: Omar would pray all five prayers on time that week, and his grandma would gently remind him. By the end of the week, their du'a after Maghrib had become: "O Allah, we ask You for al-Firdaws al-A'la."

### A True Account: The Glad Tidings Given to Umm Harithah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2809" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2809" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true account that Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

At the Battle of Badr, the first great battle in Islam, there was a young man among the Muslims named Harithah ibn Suraqah. He was hit by an arrow, and no one knew who had shot it, and he was killed.[^3] His mother was overcome with grief. She came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "O Prophet of Allah, will you not tell me about Harithah? If he is in Jannah, I will be patient; but if it is otherwise, I will pour all my strength into weeping for him."

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to her: "O Umm Harithah, there are gardens within Jannah, and your son has attained al-Firdaws al-A'la, the highest of them."[^2]

Notice that the mother asked, "Is he in Jannah?" and the answer gave her more than she asked for: he is in its very highest part. And notice that Harithah was a young man who did not live long. Al-Firdaws is not only for the old.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2790" -->

- **`Al-Firdaws`** — the highest, middle, and finest part of Jannah, from which the rivers of Jannah gush forth; the word originally means an orchard that gathers every kind of good within it.[^8]
- **`Al-laghw (idle talk)`** — words and actions that are pointless or false; staying away from them is one of the qualities of those who will inherit al-Firdaws.
- **`Sahm gharb (a stray arrow)`** — an arrow whose archer no one knows.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

If you take your faith seriously, you have probably heard it: "Don't overdo it. Just be a normal Muslim." As if ambition in religion were extremism, and as if the highest part of Jannah were booked in advance for scholars, martyrs, and the elderly. But the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, addressed the whole ummah, not some elite within it: "So when you ask Allah, ask Him for al-Firdaws, for it is the middle of Jannah and the highest part of Jannah."[^1] At the same time, the Qur'an describes those who will inherit al-Firdaws by their everyday actions, not their slogans: humility in prayer, turning away from idle talk, giving zakah, chastity, honoring trusts and commitments, and guarding the prayers.[^5] Genuine ambition holds both together: a lofty request you are not embarrassed to make, and private work you do not show off.

<!-- unit:end -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Rana and the Class Group Chat

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.024.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Rana was fifteen, and she loved her class group chat: jokes, photos, gossip. Last Ramadan she had written a goal in her private journal and told no one about it: "I want al-Firdaws al-A'la." Then, after Eid, she forgot all about the journal.

One evening the group started making fun of a classmate who had been absent from school, passing around a photo of her with cutting comments. One of Rana's friends wrote, "Rana, where's your laugh? Don't be such a buzzkill!" And in that same moment, Rana remembered what another friend had told her a week earlier, after seeing her pray at school: "Al-Firdaws is for the sheikhs. Just be normal."

Rana faced two choices, and neither was easy. If she laughed along, she would stay accepted in the group, but she would be taking part in the cruelty. If she pushed back hard, she might be accused of being stuck-up and holier-than-thou. And deep down she felt that asking for al-Firdaws was too big for her anyway, so why put herself through this?

She opened her old journal and found, under her goal, an ayah she had copied from Surah al-Mu'minun: {وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ عَنِ اللَّغْوِ مُعْرِضُونَ}, "and who turn away from idle talk." She realized that the road to that big request ran through a small decision, right now. Calmly, she wrote in the group: "I think this photo is hurting her. I'm not going to join in." Then she sent the absent classmate a private message to check on her.

Two of her friends froze her out for several days, and Rana felt lonely. But the absent classmate thanked her, and two days later one of Rana's friends deleted the photo. That night Rana did not write anything to show anyone. Instead, she wrote in her journal: "My request is high. My step this week: turn away from idle talk, and pray every prayer on time."

### A True Report: A Young Man Who Attained al-Firdaws al-A'la

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2809" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2809" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report that Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, relates that Harithah ibn Suraqah was struck down on the day of Badr while still a *ghulam*, a youth, by an arrow whose archer no one knew.[^3] His mother came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "O Prophet of Allah, will you not tell me about Harithah? ... If he is in Jannah, I will be patient; but if it is otherwise, I will pour all my strength into weeping for him." He said to her: "O Umm Harithah, there are gardens within Jannah, and your son has attained al-Firdaws al-A'la."[^2] In another narration in al-Bukhari, he said to her: "Is it only one garden? There are many gardens."[^3]

Reflect on three things. The mother tied her patience to her son's fate, so the Hereafter was the first thing she asked about. The answer reached higher than her question. And the one who received these glad tidings was a young man close to your own age, with no long list of deeds mentioned in the report, and nothing to suggest that anyone had heard of him before his death. Al-Firdaws does not require you to be famous. It requires you to be sincere.

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2790" -->

- **`Awsat al-jannah wa a'la al-jannah (the middle of Jannah and the highest part of Jannah)`** — Ibn Hajar cites Ibn Hibban's view that "middle" refers to breadth and "highest" to elevation, and notes that "middle" here means the most excellent.[^6]
- **`Al-warithun (the inheritors)`** — those who receive al-Firdaws as a secure gift from Allah, as firmly theirs as an inheritance is its heir's; Allah describes them by practical qualities in Surah al-Mu'minun.
- **`Al-laghw (idle talk)`** — any false or pointless word or deed, including mockery and hurtful talk.

### Bedtime Du'a

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّا نَسْأَلُكَ الْفِرْدَوْسَ الْأَعْلَى، فَاجْعَلْنَا مِنَ الْوَارِثِينَ الَّذِينَ يَرِثُونَهُ، وَأَعِنَّا عَلَى الْخُشُوعِ فِي صَلَاتِنَا وَالْمُحَافَظَةِ عَلَيْهَا.

This du'a brings together obedience to the Prophet's command, peace and blessings be upon him, to ask for al-Firdaws, and a request for help with the first and the last of the qualities of its inheritors in Surah al-Mu'minun: humility in prayer, and faithfully guarding it.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the three descriptions of al-Firdaws in al-Bukhari 2790, reconciles its description as both the "middle" and the "highest" part of Jannah using the commentators' statements, identifies the qualities of its inheritors in al-Mu'minun (23:1-11), relates the account of Umm Harithah and draws from it that al-Firdaws is a gift of Allah's grace, and chooses a practical spring to keep up for a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; a whiteboard or large sheet for drawing a model spring and streams; an activity sheet for each learner; colored pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the five texts and their notes, especially the commentators' remarks on the name of Umm Harithah and the meaning of *hubilti*. The teacher draws a model spring and streams on the board, using the quality "guarding the prayers," and prepares an example of the difference between humility in viewing one's deeds and lowering what one asks of Allah.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher writes a common saying on the board, "A little corner of Jannah is enough for me," and asks: "What do we like about this saying? And what about it might need rethinking?" The teacher listens to a few brief opinions without settling the question.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Bukhari 2790 in full, pausing on where the command falls (right after the promise of entry), on the words "I think he said" (*urahu*), and on the narration of Muhammad ibn Fulayh. Then read the two ayat of al-Kahf and the meaning of "as a lodging" (*nuzulan*) and "never wishing to be moved from it." Then read the ayat of al-Mu'minun, asking the learners to count the qualities and notice how the list opens and closes with prayer. Then read the account of Umm Harithah in both narrations (2809 and 3982), asking: "In what way did the answer go beyond the question?"

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher returns to the opening saying and discusses: the difference between thinking little of one's deeds and thinking little of Allah's giving; Ibn Battal's point that the command is addressed to the whole ummah; and the danger of asking without acting. Key question: "If you asked for al-Firdaws every day, which quality would you need to see in your life?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner draws their spring and streams as described in the activity unit, and sets a daily step for the spring. In pairs, learners share only their streams, not the spring itself, if they prefer to keep it private.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes two sentences: the first explaining the meaning of "the middle of Jannah and the highest part of Jannah," and the second explaining why asking for al-Firdaws does not conflict with humility. The teacher closes with a reminder to follow up on the activity in a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Beginners work only with hadith 2790, the account of Umm Harithah, and two qualities from Surah al-Mu'minun; advanced learners compare the commentators' views on "the middle" and connect this lesson with the lesson on the degrees.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Present the account of Harithah without dwelling on the details of his killing, and be mindful that some present may have lost a loved one; frame the account as glad tidings and mercy. Do not declare with certainty that any particular person of our time is in al-Firdaws; the glad tidings for Harithah were established by the word of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Mention "above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful" as it was reported, affirming what the text affirms, without speculating about how it is or attempting to picture it.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
