# The Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recount what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw at the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary on the night of the Mi'raj, from the hadith of Malik ibn Sa'sa'ah (al-Bukhari 3207): fruits like the clay jars of Hajar, leaves like the ears of elephants, four rivers at its base, and then the obligation of the prayer.
- Locate the Lote Tree from the ayat of an-Najm 53:13-18 and from the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud (Muslim 173), explain that the Garden of Refuge (*Jannat al-Ma'wa*) is beside it, and give an-Nawawi's reconciliation of the reports that place it in the sixth heaven and in the seventh.
- Explain why it is called "al-Muntaha," the Utmost Boundary, as in the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud and the tafsir of as-Sa'di, and that what covered it by the command of Allah is something no created being is able to describe (Muslim 162).
- Identify whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw there, according to the explicit words of the hadith of Aishah (Muslim 177): Jibril, peace be upon him, in the form in which he was created; and note the disagreement among the Companions over whether the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw his Lord, as Ibn Kathir reports it, without delving into how.
- Name the three gifts the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was given there: the five prayers, the closing verses of Surah al-Baqarah, and forgiveness of the grave sins (*al-muqhimat*) for whoever associates nothing with Allah.
- Carry out the "A Phrase a Night from the Gift" activity: reciting the last two ayat of Surah al-Baqarah before sleep for a set number of nights, pausing each night at one phrase from them and linking it to something that happened that day.
- Recite the two ayat before sleep, an established Sunnah (al-Bukhari 5009), as the night's du'a.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On the night of the Mi'raj, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, reached a tremendous tree that the Qur'an calls *Sidrat al-Muntaha*, the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, and beside which it places the Garden of Refuge. This lesson approaches the account of the Mi'raj from the vantage point of the tree itself. Where is it? Why is it called the Utmost Boundary? What is it like? What did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, see there? And what came down from it to his Ummah?

The lesson stops where the authentic texts stop. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that no one among Allah's creation is able to describe it, so great is its beauty. The tree of Tuba, and the trees of Jannah in general, have lessons of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-53-13-18" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "At the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary"

> **{وَلَقَدْ رَآهُ نَزْلَةً أُخْرَىٰ ۝ عِنْدَ سِدْرَةِ الْمُنْتَهَىٰ ۝ عِنْدَهَا جَنَّةُ الْمَأْوَىٰ ۝ إِذْ يَغْشَى السِّدْرَةَ مَا يَغْشَىٰ ۝ مَا زَاغَ الْبَصَرُ وَمَا طَغَىٰ ۝ لَقَدْ رَأَىٰ مِنْ آيَاتِ رَبِّهِ الْكُبْرَىٰ}** [النجم: ١٣-١٨][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And he certainly saw him in another descent, at the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, beside which is the Garden of Refuge, when there covered the Lote Tree that which covered it. His sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep. He truly saw some of the greatest signs of his Lord."** (an-Najm 53:13-18)[^1]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, said of the Lote Tree: "It is a very great tree above the seventh heaven. It was named the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary because whatever ascends from the earth ends at it, and whatever descends from Allah, of revelation and other things, comes down to it; or because the knowledge of created beings ends at it, that is, because it lies above the heavens and the earth, so it is the utmost boundary in its height; or for some other reason. And Allah knows best."[^6] On "the Garden of Refuge" he said: "This is proof that Jannah is in the highest of places, above the seventh heaven."[^7] On "that which covered it": "There covers it, of the command of Allah, something tremendous whose description no one knows except Allah, the Mighty and Majestic."[^8] And on "His sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep": "This is the perfection of his courtesy, may Allah's blessings and peace be upon him: he stood in the station in which Allah placed him, and he neither fell short of it, nor went beyond it, nor turned aside from it."[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayat sketch a place of great height, with the Garden of Refuge beside it; something tremendous covering the tree, named but left undescribed; and a gaze that held steady, neither wandering nor overreaching. Leaving it undescribed is itself a way of magnifying it, so we do not fill that silence with details of our own.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "And the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary Was Raised Up for Me"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ، عَنْ مَالِكِ بْنِ صَعْصَعَةَ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: **«بَيْنَا أَنَا عِنْدَ الْبَيْتِ بَيْنَ النَّائِمِ وَالْيَقْظَانِ...»** [ثم ذكر شقّ صدره وغسله بماء زمزم وملأه حكمةً وإيمانًا، والبراق، وصعوده مع جبريل، واستفتاح السماوات واحدة بعد أخرى، ولقاءه فيها آدم ويحيى وعيسى ويوسف وإدريس وهارون وموسى، ثم إبراهيم عليهم السلام في السماء السابعة، ورفع البيت المعمور له وسؤاله جبريل عنه، ثم قال:] **«وَرُفِعَتْ لِي سِدْرَةُ الْمُنْتَهَى، فَإِذَا نَبِقُهَا كَأَنَّهُ قِلَالُ هَجَرٍ، وَوَرَقُهَا كَأَنَّهُ آذَانُ الْفُيُولِ، فِي أَصْلِهَا أَرْبَعَةُ أَنْهَارٍ: نَهْرَانِ بَاطِنَانِ وَنَهْرَانِ ظَاهِرَانِ، فَسَأَلْتُ جِبْرِيلَ فَقَالَ: أَمَّا الْبَاطِنَانِ فَفِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَأَمَّا الظَّاهِرَانِ النِّيلُ وَالْفُرَاتُ. ثُمَّ فُرِضَتْ عَلَيَّ خَمْسُونَ صَلَاةً»** [ثم ذكر رجوعه إلى موسى عليه السلام ومراجعته ربه مرة بعد مرة حتى جُعلت خمسًا، ثم قال:] **«فَنُودِيَ: إِنِّي قَدْ أَمْضَيْتُ فَرِيضَتِي، وَخَفَّفْتُ عَنْ عِبَادِي، وَأَجْزِي الْحَسَنَةَ عَشْرًا»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, from Malik ibn Sa'sa'ah, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"While I was at the House, between sleep and wakefulness..."** [He then described how his chest was opened, washed with Zamzam water and filled with wisdom and faith; al-Buraq; his ascent with Jibril; the gates of the heavens being asked to open, one after another; his meeting there with Adam, Yahya, 'Isa, Yusuf, Idris, Harun and Musa, and then Ibrahim, peace be upon them all, in the seventh heaven; and how al-Bayt al-Ma'mur was raised up for him and he asked Jibril about it. Then he said:] **"And the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary was raised up for me. Its fruits were like the clay jars of Hajar, and its leaves were like the ears of elephants. At its base were four rivers: two hidden and two visible. I asked Jibril, and he said, 'The two hidden ones are in Jannah, and the two visible ones are the Nile and the Euphrates.' Then fifty prayers were made obligatory upon me."** [He then described how he went back to Musa, peace be upon him, and returned to his Lord again and again until they were made five. Then he said:] **"Then a voice called out: 'I have enacted My obligation, I have lightened the load for My servants, and I reward each good deed tenfold.'"**[^2]

#### Scholarly Commentary

Ibn Hajar quotes al-Khattabi as saying that *qilal* are "jars, well known to those being addressed and of a familiar size," and that *nabiq* is "the fruit of the lote tree."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described what no one else could see in terms his listeners already knew: fruit the size of great jars, leaves like the ears of elephants. The account also teaches the etiquette of asking: he saw the rivers and asked Jibril about them rather than offering an opinion of his own. And it was at the Lote Tree that the prayer was made obligatory. As for how the Nile and the Euphrates can issue from its base, we do not delve into that.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "None of Allah's Creation Is Able to Describe It"

> عَنْ ثَابِتٍ الْبُنَانِيِّ، عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ فِي خَبَرِ الْإِسْرَاءِ [بعد ذكر السماء السابعة وإبراهيم عليه السلام مسندًا ظهره إلى البيت المعمور]: **«ثُمَّ ذَهَبَ بِي إِلَى السِّدْرَةِ الْمُنْتَهَى، وَإِذَا وَرَقُهَا كَآذَانِ الْفِيَلَةِ، وَإِذَا ثَمَرُهَا كَالْقِلَالِ»**، قَالَ: **«فَلَمَّا غَشِيَهَا مِنْ أَمْرِ اللَّهِ مَا غَشِيَ تَغَيَّرَتْ، فَمَا أَحَدٌ مِنْ خَلْقِ اللَّهِ يَسْتَطِيعُ أَنْ يَنْعَتَهَا مِنْ حُسْنِهَا. فَأَوْحَى اللَّهُ إِلَيَّ مَا أَوْحَى، فَفَرَضَ عَلَيَّ خَمْسِينَ صَلَاةً فِي كُلِّ يَوْمٍ وَلَيْلَةٍ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Thabit al-Bunani, from Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said in the account of the Night Journey [after mentioning the seventh heaven, and Ibrahim, peace be upon him, leaning his back against al-Bayt al-Ma'mur]: **"Then he took me to the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, and its leaves were like the ears of elephants, and its fruits were like great jars."** He said: **"Then, when there covered it, by the command of Allah, what covered it, it was transformed, and none of Allah's creation is able to describe it, so great is its beauty. Then Allah revealed to me what He revealed, and He made fifty prayers obligatory upon me in every day and night."**[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

What covered the tree was "by the command of Allah," and it was transformed into a beauty beyond any creature's power to describe. This is the measure for everything we say about the unseen: we describe what has been described, and we fall silent about what cannot be described.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-173" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "To It Ends Whatever Is Taken Up from the Earth"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ مَسْعُودٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **لَمَّا أُسْرِيَ بِرَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ انْتُهِيَ بِهِ إِلَى سِدْرَةِ الْمُنْتَهَى، وَهِيَ فِي السَّمَاءِ السَّادِسَةِ، إِلَيْهَا يَنْتَهِي مَا يُعْرَجُ بِهِ مِنَ الْأَرْضِ فَيُقْبَضُ مِنْهَا، وَإِلَيْهَا يَنْتَهِي مَا يُهْبَطُ بِهِ مِنْ فَوْقِهَا فَيُقْبَضُ مِنْهَا. قَالَ: {إِذْ يَغْشَى السِّدْرَةَ مَا يَغْشَى} قَالَ: فَرَاشٌ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ. قَالَ: فَأُعْطِيَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ ثَلَاثًا: أُعْطِيَ الصَّلَوَاتِ الْخَمْسَ، وَأُعْطِيَ خَوَاتِيمَ سُورَةِ الْبَقَرَةِ، وَغُفِرَ لِمَنْ لَمْ يُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ مِنْ أُمَّتِهِ شَيْئًا الْمُقْحِمَاتُ.**[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of 'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: **When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was taken on the Night Journey, he was brought as far as the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, which is in the sixth heaven. Whatever is taken up from the earth ends there and is received from it, and whatever is brought down from above it ends there and is received from it. He said: "When there covered the Lote Tree that which covered it": he said, butterflies of gold. He said: And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was given three things. He was given the five prayers; he was given the closing verses of Surah al-Baqarah; and forgiveness of the grave sins (al-muqhimat) was granted to those of his Ummah who associate nothing with Allah.**[^4]

#### Scholarly Commentary

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, reconciling this report with those that place the tree in the seventh heaven, said: "The two can be reconciled: its root is in the sixth heaven, and the greater part of it in the seventh." He explained *al-muqhimat* as "the great sins, the major sins that destroy those who commit them and bring them into the Fire."[^11]

#### Lesson Explanation

Ibn Mas'ud's report, may Allah be pleased with him, gives the reason for the name: the Lote Tree is the boundary at which whatever rises from the earth, and whatever descends from above it, comes to an end. The explanation of what covered it as "butterflies of gold" comes in the course of his own words, so we mention it as it has been reported. And the forgiveness of grave sins is glad tidings for the people of tawhid, by Allah's grace, not a license to take major sins lightly.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-177a" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "That Was Only Jibril"

> عَنْ مَسْرُوقٍ قَالَ: كُنْتُ مُتَّكِئًا عِنْدَ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها، فَقَالَتْ: **مَنْ زَعَمَ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا ﷺ رَأَى رَبَّهُ فَقَدْ أَعْظَمَ عَلَى اللَّهِ الْفِرْيَةَ.** قَالَ: فَجَلَسْتُ فَقُلْتُ: يَا أُمَّ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ... أَلَمْ يَقُلِ اللَّهُ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: {وَلَقَدْ رَآهُ بِالْأُفُقِ الْمُبِينِ}، {وَلَقَدْ رَآهُ نَزْلَةً أُخْرَى}؟ فَقَالَتْ: **أَنَا أَوَّلُ هَذِهِ الْأُمَّةِ سَأَلَ عَنْ ذَلِكَ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ، فَقَالَ: «إِنَّمَا هُوَ جِبْرِيلُ، لَمْ أَرَهُ عَلَى صُورَتِهِ الَّتِي خُلِقَ عَلَيْهَا غَيْرَ هَاتَيْنِ الْمَرَّتَيْنِ، رَأَيْتُهُ مُنْهَبِطًا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ، سَادًّا عِظَمُ خَلْقِهِ مَا بَيْنَ السَّمَاءِ إِلَى الْأَرْضِ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Masruq, who said: I was reclining in the company of Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, and she said: **"Whoever claims that Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, saw his Lord has invented a tremendous lie against Allah."** He said: I sat up and said, "O Mother of the Believers... did Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, not say, 'And he certainly saw him on the clear horizon,' and, 'And he certainly saw him in another descent'?" She said: **"I was the first of this Ummah to ask the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, about that, and he said: 'That was only Jibril. I have not seen him in the form in which he was created except on these two occasions. I saw him descending from the sky, the sheer vastness of his form filling all that lies between the sky and the earth.'"**[^5]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said on the words "And he certainly saw him in another descent": "This is the second time the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, saw Jibril in the form in which Allah created him, and it was on the night of the Isra'." He mentions that Ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, "used to affirm the vision on the night of the Isra'," and that "groups of the Companions, may Allah be pleased with them, the Successors and others disagreed with him."[^12]

#### Lesson Explanation

The one seen in the words "And he certainly saw him" is Jibril, peace be upon him, according to the Prophet's own answer, peace and blessings be upon him. As for whether the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw his Lord, the Companions differed on the question, as Ibn Kathir reports, and for our purposes here the wording of the hadith is enough.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What explanations does as-Sa'di give for why the Lote Tree is called "the Utmost Boundary"? Which of them appears in the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud?

2. How did an-Nawawi reconcile the report of "the sixth heaven" with the reports of the seventh?

3. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, compare the Lote Tree to familiar things? And what does "none of Allah's creation is able to describe it" tell us?

4. Whom did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, see "in another descent," according to the explicit words of the hadith of Aishah? And how should we handle the disagreement over the vision?

5. What were the three gifts the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was given at the Lote Tree? And how should the forgiveness of grave sins be understood without making light of major sins?

6. What does Allah's description "His sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep" teach you about your prayer, and about the way you speak of matters of the unseen?

### Activity: A Phrase a Night from the Gift

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.055.activity.a-phrase-a-night-from-the-gift" -->

The closing verses of Surah al-Baqarah are among the gifts of the Lote Tree. Recite its last two ayat before sleep for seven nights, and each night pause at one phrase, in this order:

- {آمَنَ الرَّسُولُ بِمَا أُنْزِلَ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ رَبِّهِ وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ} "The Messenger believes in what has been sent down to him from his Lord, and so do the believers"
- {لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِنْ رُسُلِهِ} "We make no distinction between any of His messengers"
- {سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا} "We hear and we obey"
- {غُفْرَانَكَ رَبَّنَا وَإِلَيْكَ الْمَصِيرُ} "Your forgiveness, our Lord; and to You is the final return"
- {لَا يُكَلِّفُ اللَّهُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا} "Allah does not burden any soul beyond what it can bear"
- {رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذْنَا إِنْ نَسِينَا أَوْ أَخْطَأْنَا} "Our Lord, do not take us to task if we forget or make a mistake"
- {وَاعْفُ عَنَّا وَاغْفِرْ لَنَا وَارْحَمْنَا} "Pardon us, forgive us, and have mercy on us"

Under each phrase, write a single line: where did I meet it in my day? On the seventh night, write a paragraph: which phrase did I need most, and why?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Have you ever seen a really big tree? Did you sit in its shade and look up at its high branches?

Allah created a tree that is greater than every tree in this world, in a place very, very high, above the heavens. Its name is *Sidrat al-Muntaha*, the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary. And Jannah is right beside it.[^1][^7]

No person has ever seen this tree except our Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, on an amazing night called the night of the Mi'raj.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Great Tree in the Highest Place

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3207" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told it, and the Companion Malik ibn Sa'sa'ah, may Allah be pleased with him, passed it on. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3207" audience="4-7" -->

One night, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was in Makkah, by the Ka'bah.

The angel Jibril, peace be upon him, came to him and went up with him into the sky. Up through one heaven, then the next, and at every heaven the door was opened for them, and someone said about the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Welcome to him!"

In the heavens, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, met noble prophets. He greeted them with salam, and they welcomed him.

Then Allah raised up before him a great tree: the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary.

Its fruits were so big, like great big water jars. And its leaves were so big, like the ears of elephants!

At the bottom of the tree were four rivers. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked Jibril, "What are these rivers?" And Jibril told him: two of them are in Jannah, and two are in this world: the Nile and the Euphrates.[^2]

Then something tremendous, by the command of Allah, covered the tree, and it became so beautiful that no one is able to describe how beautiful it is.[^3]

And there, Allah gave His Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, a gift for us: the prayer. At first it was fifty prayers. Then Allah made it lighter, until it became five prayers a day, and we get the reward of fifty.[^2]

So every time you pray, remember: this prayer is a gift from Allah. Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was given it in the highest place, by the great tree.

### A True Account: "And the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary Was Raised Up for Me"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3207" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account told by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and narrated by the Companion Malik ibn Sa'sa'ah, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3207" audience="8-12" -->

The Prophet was by the Ka'bah in Makkah, between sleep and wakefulness, when the most amazing journey in history began. His chest was opened, washed with Zamzam water and filled with wisdom and faith, and he rose into the sky with Jibril, peace be upon him.

At each heaven, the same exchange: "Who is this?" "Jibril." "Who is with you?" "Muhammad." "Welcome to him! What an excellent arrival!" There he met Adam, Yahya, 'Isa, Yusuf, Idris, Harun and Musa, then Ibrahim, peace be upon them all, in the seventh heaven.

Then the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary was raised up for him: "Its fruits were like the clay jars of Hajar, and its leaves were like the ears of elephants." One fruit the size of a huge jar, one leaf the size of an elephant's ear!

At its base were four rivers, two hidden and two visible. Not knowing what they were, he didn't guess; he asked Jibril, who answered: "The two hidden ones are in Jannah, and the two visible ones are the Nile and the Euphrates."[^2]

In the narration of Anas, when what covered the tree by Allah's command covered it, it was transformed into a beauty no creature can describe.[^3]

There fifty prayers were made obligatory, then lightened to five, and a voice called out: "I have enacted My obligation, I have lightened the load for My servants, and I reward each good deed tenfold."[^2] In Ibn Mas'ud's narration, he received three gifts there: the five prayers, the closing verses of Surah al-Baqarah, and forgiveness of the gravest sins for those of his Ummah who associate nothing with Allah.[^4]

He described what he saw with familiar things, asked about what he didn't know, and kept silent about what cannot be described. That is exactly what Lina needs now.

### A True Account: At the Tree Where Everything Reaches Its Limit

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3207" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by Anas ibn Malik from Malik ibn Sa'sa'ah, may Allah be pleased with them both, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih al-Bukhari, not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3207" audience="13+" -->

It opens somewhere familiar: by the Ka'bah, the Prophet between sleep and wakefulness. His chest is opened, washed with Zamzam and filled with wisdom and faith; he rises with Jibril, peace be upon him. At every heaven the same exchange: "Who is this? Who is with you? Has he been sent for?" Then: "Welcome to him! What an excellent arrival!" Through seven heavens, prophets greet him, up to Ibrahim, peace be upon him, in the seventh; then al-Bayt al-Ma'mur is raised for him.

Then the peak: "And the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary was raised up for me." In Ibn Mas'ud's narration, it is where all that rises from the earth or descends from above ends.[^4] He describes it in images his listeners knew: fruit like the jars of Hajar, leaves like elephants' ears. At its base are four rivers, two hidden and two visible. He asks Jibril, who says the hidden ones are in Jannah and the visible ones are the Nile and Euphrates.[^2]

Pause there. At the highest point any human has reached, before the most astonishing sight any eye has seen, he did not speak without knowledge; he asked. Then, covered by what covered it at Allah's command, the tree was transformed beyond any creature's power to describe, so great was its beauty.[^3] The Qur'an describes his gaze there: "His sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep."[^1]

From that station came the gifts: fifty prayers that became five with the reward of fifty, "I have enacted My obligation, I have lightened the load for My servants, and I reward each good deed tenfold";[^2] the closing verses of Surah al-Baqarah; and forgiveness of the gravest sins for whoever associates nothing with Allah.[^4]

So the account gives us a standard, not a picture to amuse ourselves with: the most accurate description is revelation's, the most honest answer to the unknown is to ask or stay silent, and the most precious thing he brought back was not a description of the tree but a daily prayer.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner locates the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary from an-Najm 53:13-15 and Muslim 173, together with an-Nawawi's reconciliation of the sixth and seventh heavens; explains the reason for its name using as-Sa'di's explanations and the text of Ibn Mas'ud's report; recounts its description from al-Bukhari 3207 and Muslim 162; shows that the one seen "in another descent" was Jibril, according to the explicit text of Muslim 177, and presents the Companions' disagreement over the vision as Ibn Kathir reports it, without forcing a verdict; lists the three gifts; and plans a week of "A Phrase a Night."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with five columns headed "The Place," "The Name," "The Description," "What He Saw There," and "What Came Down from It"; the activity sheet with the seven phrases; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 3207 in full along with its other narration, 3887, as well as Muslim 162, 173 and 177, and al-Bukhari 5009; reviews the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on an-Najm 53:13-18, an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 173, and Ibn Hajar's remarks on the jars of Hajar and on *nabiq*. The teacher also prepares a calm answer for anyone who asks whether the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw his Lord: present the view of Aishah and the view of Ibn 'Abbas as Ibn Kathir reports them, and refer anyone who wants the details to works of creed.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What is the highest place you have ever stood? What did you see from up there?" Then: "What if there were a place where the knowledge of all creation comes to an end?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of an-Najm with as-Sa'di's tafsir, and fill in the columns "The Place" and "The Name" (5 minutes); then al-Bukhari 3207, explaining what the bracketed summaries stand in for and glossing the unusual words, and fill in "The Description" (6 minutes); then Muslim 162 and Muslim 173 with an-Nawawi's reconciliation, and fill in "What Came Down from It" (5 minutes); then Muslim 177 with Ibn Kathir's remarks, and fill in "What He Saw There" (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 3, 4 and 6. The teacher stresses three ground rules: the unseen is described only as revelation describes it; the explanation "butterflies of gold" comes in the course of Ibn Mas'ud's own words, so its wording is not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with certainty; and the forgiveness of grave sins is glad tidings, not a reason to take sin lightly.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners read the two ayat slowly, together with their meaning, and each writes a likely example from their own life beside each of the seven phrases (10 minutes); then each sets a fixed time for reciting them before sleep and decides where the activity notebook will be kept (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give two reasons why the Lote Tree is called the Utmost Boundary" and "What are the three gifts?" Then recite the two ayat together, and agree on an optional sharing session a week later, where each learner may name the phrase that felt closest to them.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayat of an-Najm, al-Bukhari 3207 and Muslim 173 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations describing the Lote Tree (al-Bukhari 3207, 3887 and 349, and Muslim 162 and 173) and compare the order in which al-Bayt al-Ma'mur and the Lote Tree appear in each.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show any drawing or video that claims to depict the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, Jibril, or the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Do not mention descriptions of the Lote Tree that are not authentic: the reports found in the tafsir works about branches of pearl and ruby, or an angel on every leaf, are not relied upon in this lesson. Do not open a heated debate on the question of the vision; presenting the reported views is enough. And do not delve into how the Nile and the Euphrates issue from its base.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child names the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary and says that it is in the highest place, with Jannah beside it; describes its fruits and leaves using the two comparisons the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave; says that the prayer is a gift from Allah that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was given there; and repeats the phrase of the night from the end of Surah al-Baqarah with their parents.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large clay jar or pitcher; a real photograph of an elephant's ear (from an animal book); a lote fruit or jujube, if available; drawing paper cut into the shape of a large tree leaf; crayons; a card with the two ayat.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account in short sentences, cuts the drawing paper into leaf shapes ahead of time, and makes sure the elephant picture contains no drawing meant to represent the unseen tree.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The caregiver shows the child the lote fruit and then the big jar, and asks: "Could a fruit ever be as big as this jar?" Then: "Listen to the true story of an amazing tree that our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the explanation and then the account in a calm voice, holding up the jar when the fruit is mentioned and the picture of the elephant's ear when the leaves are mentioned. Pause at the words "no one is able to describe how beautiful it is," and say: "That's why we won't draw the tree itself."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What is the tree's name? What did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, ask Jibril? What gift did Allah give us?" Then link the daily prayers to that night.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 6 minutes:** On the leaf-shaped paper, the child draws something from their day that goes with "We hear and we obey," {سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا}; then agree with the parents on the next two nights.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Notice whether the child can name the tree, give the two comparisons, and name the gift. Then the caregiver reads the two ayat, and the child repeats the phrase of the night.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the single phrase {وَارْحَمْنَا}, "And have mercy on us," is enough for all three nights. Ask older children to tell the account to a brother or sister in four sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, Jibril, or the unseen tree, and do not use dolls or pictures to represent them. Do not ask the child to memorize both ayat in full at this stage; listening and repeating are enough. Do not frighten the child with the mention of the disbelievers at the end of the ayah; explain gently that it is a du'a asking Allah to help the believers.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the account of the Lote Tree in order from al-Bukhari 3207; explains the words *nabiq*, "the jars of Hajar" and "the Garden of Refuge"; gives the reason for the name and the three gifts; drafts a polite reply to a description that is not established; and completes the five-branch tree.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Five sequence cards with no pictures ("By the Ka'bah," "The Seven Heavens," "The Lote Tree and the Rivers," "What Covered It," "The Gifts"); the term cards; the five-branch tree sheet; pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes two columns on the board, "The Prophet Said It" and "Not in the Sources," and prepares three statements about the Lote Tree, some from the hadith and some invented (such as "Its leaves are made of diamonds"), for the students to sort.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the scene with Rami and Lina, and ask: "How do we know whether a piece of information about the unseen is true?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, and have students put the sequence cards in order (6 minutes); then explain the terms and sort the three statements into the two columns (6 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss questions 2, 4 and 6, focusing on how the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked Jibril when he did not know, and on how good manners toward a classmate mean gentleness, not mockery.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student draws their tree with five branches, writes the five phrases on it, and fills in the first night's branch with an expected situation; then read the meaning of the two ayat together.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences: "Why is the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary called by that name?" and "What do I say to someone who describes the unseen with things that aren't in the sources?" Then recite the two ayat, and agree to display the trees a week later.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who struggle a tree with the phrases already written in, so they only draw the situations. Ask advanced students to memorize the first ayah (2:285) and recite it a week later.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show pictures from the internet that claim to depict the Lote Tree, not even to critique them. Do not mock a student who believes a viral video; handle it by asking about the source. Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, Jibril, peace be upon him, or the tree.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the Prophet's method, peace and blessings be upon him, in describing the unseen (comparing it to the familiar, asking about the unknown, and staying silent about what cannot be described); explains "His sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep" using as-Sa'di's words; names the three gifts and the meaning of *al-muqhimat*; drafts a response to viral content about the unseen; and completes the "A Phrase a Night" notebook over seven nights.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a personal notebook that is not collected; three scenario cards (a viral video about the unseen, a religious post with no source, a friend asking for details about Jannah); a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews as-Sa'di's tafsir of an-Najm 53:14-18 and an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 173, and words the scenarios in general terms that point to no particular student or account.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read Huda's situation, and ask: "What makes beautiful religious content spread fast? And what makes it true?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the account, pausing at the welcome in the heavens, the comparisons for the fruit and leaves, the question to Jibril, the words "no one is able to describe it," and the gifts; then read the ayah "His sight did not swerve, nor did it overstep" with as-Sa'di's explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Small groups work on the scenario cards and draft, for each one, a response that is both truthful and kind; then they discuss questions 3 and 5.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students read the two ayat with their meaning; each sets up their notebook with the seven phrases, writes an expected situation under the first, and sets a fixed time for reciting before sleep.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence: "What is the difference between revelation's description of the unseen and what imagination adds to it?" Then recite the two ayat. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question about the experience, without looking at the notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to research briefly an-Nawawi's reconciliation of the sixth-heaven and seventh-heaven reports, and what the commentators say about the meaning of "the Garden of Refuge."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to show their phone or their chats. Do not embarrass anyone who has shared unestablished content in the past; the aim is correction, not public shaming. Do not open the question of seeing Allah, the Exalted, on the night of the Mi'raj as a debate; refer anyone who asks to the adults' section. Do not show images representing the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, the angels, or the Lote Tree.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
