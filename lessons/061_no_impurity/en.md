# The Food and Drink of Jannah Leave Nothing Impure

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the words of Allah, "And their Lord will give them a pure drink" (al-Insan 76:21), and present at-Tabari's statement that part of its purity is that it does not turn into anything foul but into a sweat like the sweat of musk, together with Ibn Kathir's statement that it purifies their inner selves of envy, resentment and rancor.
- Narrate the hadith of Jabir in Sahih Muslim (2835): the people of Jannah eat and drink, and they do not spit, urinate, relieve themselves or blow their noses; and give the Companions' question, "Then what becomes of the food?", along with his answer: "A belch, and a sweat like the sweat of musk."
- Explain the answer the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave to the Jewish man who assumed that eating must lead to the need to relieve oneself (Musnad Ahmad 19269 and Sahih Ibn Hibban 7424), and al-Qurtubi's explanation of why the food of Jannah leaves no waste.
- Distinguish the purity of Jannah, which is built into its very creation, from the purity of this world, which the believer earns through effort, drawing on the hadith "Purification is half of faith" (Muslim 223).
- Carry out the "What Does My Meal Leave Behind?" activity: listing, over a set number of days, every trace a daily meal leaves; cleaning up each one through an action; having a family member check the place; and closing with a reflection.
- End the day with a lesson-authored du'a asking Allah for purity of heart and body, for a good mark left on the earth, and for a pure drink in Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every bite we take in this world leaves something behind: dishes to wash, scraps to throw out, a need of the body that must be answered, and then a purification we have to carry out with water. So one might well ask: if the people of Jannah eat and drink, where does their food go? The Companions asked exactly that question. So did a Jewish man who hoped to silence the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with it.

This lesson brings together texts from which three truths emerge:

1. **Real food, with nothing impure:** The people of Jannah truly eat and drink, yet they do not spit, urinate, relieve themselves or blow their noses. As al-Ashqar points out, this holds for all of its people, not only for the first group to enter.[^11]
2. **A fragrant outlet:** The food passes away as a belch and a sweat like the sweat of musk, so that nothing remains of it but a pleasant scent.
3. **Purity here is a task; purity there is a gift:** In this world we purify ourselves with effort and intention, and "Purification is half of faith"; in Jannah, their Lord gives them "a pure drink."

And why do they eat at all, when there is no hunger in Jannah? Al-Ashqar quotes al-Qurtubi in *at-Tadhkirah*: "Their eating is not out of hunger, nor their drinking out of thirst, nor their perfuming out of any foul smell; rather, these are delights that follow one upon another and blessings that come in succession."[^11] The kinds of food and drink in Jannah, and the first thing its people eat, each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-21" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Their Lord Will Give Them a Pure Drink"

> **{عَالِيَهُمْ ثِيَابُ سُنْدُسٍ خُضْرٌ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٌ ۖ وَحُلُّوا أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَسَقَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًا طَهُورًا}** [الإنسان: ٢١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Upon them will be garments of fine green silk and rich brocade, and they will be adorned with bracelets of silver, and their Lord will give them a pure drink."** (al-Insan 76:21)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "Having mentioned the adornment of their outward selves with silk and jewelry, He then says: 'and their Lord will give them a pure drink,' meaning that it purifies their inner selves of envy, resentment, rancor, harm and every other base trait."[^2] At-Tabari says: "Part of its purity is that it does not turn into impure urine; rather, it turns into a sweat from their bodies like the sweat of musk." He also relates something similar, through his own chains, as the personal statements of Ibrahim at-Taymi and Abu Qilabah.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

Notice the order: first the adornment of the outward self, then a drink that purifies the inner self. And the one who serves the drink is "their Lord." Purity there is a gift from Allah, with no toil and no burden in it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2835" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then What Becomes of the Food?"

> عَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقُولُ: **«إِنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَأْكُلُونَ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ، وَلَا يَتْفُلُونَ، وَلَا يَبُولُونَ، وَلَا يَتَغَوَّطُونَ، وَلَا يَمْتَخِطُونَ»**. قَالُوا: فَمَا بَالُ الطَّعَامِ؟ قَالَ: **«جُشَاءٌ وَرَشْحٌ كَرَشْحِ الْمِسْكِ، يُلْهَمُونَ التَّسْبِيحَ وَالتَّحْمِيدَ كَمَا يُلْهَمُونَ النَّفَسَ»**.[^4]
>
> وفي رواية أبي الزبير عن جابر عند مسلم: **«يَأْكُلُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ، وَلَا يَتَغَوَّطُونَ، وَلَا يَمْتَخِطُونَ، وَلَا يَبُولُونَ، وَلَكِنْ طَعَامُهُمْ ذَاكَ جُشَاءٌ كَرَشْحِ الْمِسْكِ، يُلْهَمُونَ التَّسْبِيحَ وَالْحَمْدَ كَمَا يُلْهَمُونَ النَّفَسَ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"The people of Jannah will eat and drink there, and they will not spit, they will not urinate, they will not relieve themselves, and they will not blow their noses."** They asked, "Then what becomes of the food?" He said: **"A belch, and a sweat like the sweat of musk. Glorifying and praising Allah will come to them as naturally as breathing."**[^4]
>
> And in the narration of Abu az-Zubayr from Jabir, also in Muslim: **"The people of Jannah will eat and drink there, and they will not relieve themselves, they will not blow their noses, and they will not urinate. Rather, that food of theirs becomes a belch like the sweat of musk. Glorifying Allah and praising Him will come to them as naturally as breathing."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi says: "The position of the people of the Sunnah and of Muslims at large is that the people of Jannah eat and drink there, enjoying that and its other pleasures and kinds of bliss with an enjoyment that is everlasting and has no end." Their bliss, he adds, shares nothing with the bliss of this world "except the name and the basic form, and except that they do not urinate, relieve themselves, blow their noses or spit."[^5] Abu al-'Abbas al-Qurtubi says: "Since the nourishment of the people of Jannah is utterly refined and perfectly balanced, it has no residue that would be found repugnant; rather, what it leaves is found pleasant and delightful, and that is what is expressed by musk."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Companions asked a plain, practical question, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not rebuke them for it; he answered clearly. *Rashh* is sweat, and *jusha'* is the air that rises from the stomach after a meal; in Jannah, both carry the scent of musk.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="ahmad-19269" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "But Whoever Eats and Drinks Will Need to Relieve Himself"

> عَنْ زَيْدِ بْنِ أَرْقَمَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: **أَتَى النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم رَجُلٌ مِنَ الْيَهُودِ فَقَالَ: يَا أَبَا الْقَاسِمِ، أَلَسْتَ تَزْعُمُ أَنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَأْكُلُونَ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ؟ وَقَالَ لِأَصْحَابِهِ: إِنْ أَقَرَّ لِي بِهَذِهِ خَصَمْتُهُ. قَالَ: فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «بَلَى وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، إِنَّ أَحَدَهُمْ لَيُعْطَى قُوَّةَ مِائَةِ رَجُلٍ فِي الْمَطْعَمِ وَالْمَشْرَبِ وَالشَّهْوَةِ وَالْجِمَاعِ». قَالَ: فَقَالَ لَهُ الْيَهُودِيُّ: فَإِنَّ الَّذِي يَأْكُلُ وَيَشْرَبُ تَكُونُ لَهُ الْحَاجَةُ. قَالَ: فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «حَاجَةُ أَحَدِهِمْ عَرَقٌ يَفِيضُ مِنْ جُلُودِهِمْ مِثْلُ رِيحِ الْمِسْكِ، فَإِذَا الْبَطْنُ قَدْ ضَمُرَ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Zayd ibn Arqam, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: **A Jewish man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "O Abu al-Qasim, do you not claim that the people of Jannah eat and drink there?" And he had said to his companions, "If he concedes this to me, I will have beaten him in argument." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Yes indeed, by the One in whose hand is my soul! Each one of them will be given the strength of a hundred men in eating, in drinking, in desire and in intimacy." The Jewish man said to him, "But whoever eats and drinks will need to relieve himself." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Their need to relieve themselves is a sweat that flows from their skin, like the scent of musk, and then the stomach has gone slender again."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim cites it in the chapter "The Food of the People of Jannah, Their Drink, and Where It Goes" in *Hadi al-Arwah*, right after the hadith of Jabir, presenting it as an explanation of where the food goes, and he says it comes "with an authentic chain that meets the conditions of the Sahih."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The questioner wanted to turn eating into proof that Jannah must contain something foul. The answer showed that eating in Jannah is pure, complete pleasure, that it ends in a sweat like the scent of musk, and that then the stomach slims down and its owner returns to his bliss.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-223" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Purification Is Half of Faith"

> عَنْ أَبِي مَالِكٍ الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«الطُّهُورُ شَطْرُ الْإِيمَانِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ تَمْلَأُ الْمِيزَانَ، وَسُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ تَمْلَآنِ، أَوْ تَمْلَأُ، مَا بَيْنَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ، وَالصَّلَاةُ نُورٌ، وَالصَّدَقَةُ بُرْهَانٌ، وَالصَّبْرُ ضِيَاءٌ، وَالْقُرْآنُ حُجَّةٌ لَكَ أَوْ عَلَيْكَ، كُلُّ النَّاسِ يَغْدُو، فَبَائِعٌ نَفْسَهُ فَمُعْتِقُهَا أَوْ مُوبِقُهَا»**.[^9]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Malik al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Purification is half of faith. *Al-hamdu lillah* fills the scale. *Subhan Allah* and *al-hamdu lillah* fill (or: fills) what lies between the heavens and the earth. Prayer is light, charity is proof, patience is radiance, and the Qur'an is an argument for you or against you. Every person sets out in the morning and sells his soul: either he sets it free, or he destroys it."**[^9]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn 'Uthaymin says: "*At-tuhur*: by this he means a person's purification. *Shatr al-iman*: that is, half of faith." And: "This is purification: that a person purifies himself, physically and inwardly, of everything that contains harm."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world, nothing unclean goes away without effort: water, wudu', cleaning and repentance. And that very effort is half of faith. Whoever spends a lifetime keeping body, home and heart clean is hoping for an abode where nothing will ever need cleaning again.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of al-Insan presents purity as a gift from the Lord; the hadith of Jabir describes what that purity looks like in the matter of food; the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam uses it to answer an objection; and the hadith of Abu Malik brings us back to today's duty: to clean up whatever traces our lives leave behind.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What are the two aspects of the meaning of "a pure drink" in at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir? How do they fit together?
2. What did the Companions ask in the hadith of Jabir? And what does the answer of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, show about where the food goes?
3. What objection did the Jewish man intend to raise? And how was it answered?
4. How did al-Qurtubi explain why the food of Jannah leaves no waste? And why do the people of Jannah eat when there is no hunger there?
5. In light of the hadith "Purification is half of faith," what is the difference between the purity of Jannah and the purity of this world?

### Activity: What Does My Meal Leave Behind?

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.061.activity.what-my-meal-leaves-behind" -->

1. For seven days, choose one meal each day. When you have finished it and praised Allah, list in a table every trace it left: dishes, crumbs, packaging, leftover food, wasted water, and any word spoken at the table.
2. Next to each trace, write the act of cleaning you carried out: you washed, wiped, sorted for recycling, saved the leftovers, or made amends for a word with an apology. Then wash your hands.
3. On the seventh day, ask someone in your household to check the place after your meal and tell you: did you leave it cleaner than you found it? Then write three lines: Which trace came up most often? What has become a habit? And what does it mean to you that the food of Jannah leaves nothing behind but the scent of musk?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In Jannah there is yummy food and lovely drinks. The people of Jannah eat and drink, and afterward they never need to go to the bathroom. Their noses never run, and they never spit.[^4] So where does the food go? It turns into a beautiful smell, like musk, which is a very lovely perfume.[^4] And Allah gives them "a pure drink," which means a drink that is clean and pure.[^1] But here in this world, we clean up with our own hands: we wash our hands, we clear away our plates, and we put our trash in the bin. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Purification is half of faith." That means being clean and pure is half of believing.[^9]

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Lina and the Picnic in the Park

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.061.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Lina went to the park with her dad and her brother Ammar. They spread a blanket under a big tree, ate sandwiches and oranges, and drank juice.

After they ate, Lina's hands were all sticky from the oranges. Around the blanket lay orange peels, little bags, and an empty juice box. "Let's go play now!" said Lina. "The park worker will come and clean up."

Ammar looked at the peels. Dad looked at Lina and said, "Lina, did you know that the people of Jannah eat, and nothing dirty is ever left over after their food?"

Lina's eyes went wide. "Nothing at all? Ever?"

"The Companions asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, where their food goes," said Dad. "And he said it comes out of them as a lovely smell, like musk. But here in this world, our food leaves peels and bags behind, and it's our job to clean them up."

Lina thought for a moment. Then she said, "Then I won't leave them for the park worker!"

Lina gathered the peels into a bag, and Ammar carried the empty juice box to the trash can. Then Lina went with Dad to the water tap, washed her hands really well with soap, and said, "Al-hamdu lillah."

Lina sniffed her hands. They smelled nice! She looked at where the blanket had been, and the grass was just as clean as before. The park worker walked by, smiled, and said, "May Allah bless you all!"

"Dad," said Lina, "in Jannah we won't need to clean up. But here, I like to leave a place nice and clean."

### Activity: What Does My Meal Leave Behind?

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.061.activity.what-my-meal-leaves-behind" -->

1. For three days, after one meal each day, say "Al-hamdu lillah." Then look around your plate: what did your food leave behind? Crumbs? Peels? A cup?

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every meal here leaves something behind: plates, wrappers, leftovers, a trip to the bathroom. Jannah is completely different. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that its people really do eat and drink, but they don't spit, need the bathroom, or get runny noses.[^4] Their food leaves them as a burp and a musk-scented sweat.[^4] Allah says: "And their Lord will give them a pure drink."[^1] At-Tabari explained that part of its purity is that it never turns into anything foul, only into a sweat like musk.[^3] Why eat if no one there is hungry? Because eating there is a pleasure and a blessing, not a need.[^11] Here, though, staying clean takes work, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Purification is half of faith."[^9]

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Karim and the Trail Bag

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.061.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Karim was eleven when his class took a field trip to a nature reserve. At the entrance, the ranger said, "The reserve has just one rule: whatever you carry in, you carry out. There are no trash cans here."

After the hike, they ate lunch under a big rock. Soon everyone had a pile of wrappers, cans and banana peels.

Karim's friend Tamer stuffed his wrappers under a stone. "Put them here," he said. "Nobody will see, and my backpack has no room for a sticky bag." Two other kids did the same.

Karim looked at his wrappers. His bag was small, it would sit in his backpack for two hours, and it might dirty his books. He lifted a stone to do the same.

Then he remembered reading with his dad how the Companions asked where the food of the people of Jannah goes, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: a belch, and a sweat like the sweat of musk. And he remembered what his dad had said: "Jannah is pure without any effort. But in this world, staying clean takes work, and purification is half of faith."

Karim put the stone back. He gathered his wrappers into the bag, tied it tight, and wrapped it in paper before packing it.

Tamer laughed. "What, are you the reserve's janitor now?"

Karim didn't answer. But before they left, he pulled out another bag and said quietly, "If anyone wants to put their wrappers in here, I'll carry them for you."

The students hesitated; then two dropped their wrappers in. At the last moment, Tamer went back to the stone, pulled out what he had hidden, and put it in the bag without a word.

Passing the spot later, the ranger said, "That's the cleanest rest stop I've seen all day."

At home, Karim threw the bag in the trash, washed his hands, and wrote in his notebook: "Every meal leaves a trace behind. I choose to make it clean."

### A True Report: "Then What Becomes of the Food?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2835" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2835" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true report narrated by Muslim from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, may Allah be pleased with them both. It is not a made-up story.**

Jabir heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, describing the bliss of Jannah. He said: "The people of Jannah will eat and drink there." Then he mentioned that they do not spit, urinate, relieve themselves, or blow their noses.[^4]

The Companions had a natural question: anyone who eats needs the bathroom, so where does the food of the people of Jannah go? They asked it straight out: "Then what becomes of the food?"[^4]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered: "A belch, and a sweat like the sweat of musk." In other words, a little air that comes up after eating, and some sweat, and both smell like musk. Then he said: "Glorifying and praising Allah will come to them as naturally as breathing."[^4]

Notice two things: the Companions weren't embarrassed to ask, because asking is the road to knowledge; and the answer makes cleanliness in Jannah permanent, not a tiring chore.

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2835" -->

- **`Belch (jusha')`** — air that comes up from the stomach after eating; in Jannah, it smells sweet.[^6]
- **`Sweat (rashh)`** — moisture that comes out through the skin; in the hadith it is compared to the sweat of musk.[^4]
- **`A pure drink (sharaban tahura)`** — a drink that is pure in itself and cleanses the one who drinks it of anything harmful.[^3]
- **`Purification (at-tuhur)`** — cleansing yourself with water, wudu' and cleanliness; the hadith calls it half of faith.[^10]

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

2. What did Karim do when Tamer made fun of him? And what did Tamer do in the end?
3. What question did the Companions ask? And how did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answer them?

### Activity: What Does My Meal Leave Behind?

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.061.activity.what-my-meal-leaves-behind" -->

1. For five days, choose one meal a day (school lunch or dinner at home). After saying "Al-hamdu lillah," make a two-column table: "What my meal left behind" and "What I did to clean it up."
2. Clean up every trace: wash your plate or put it away, wipe the table, put wrappers in the right bin, and save leftovers instead of throwing them out. Then wash your hands.
3. On the fifth day, ask a family member to check the place: did you leave it cleaner than you found it? Then write two sentences: Which trace came up most? Which habit will you keep?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.061.dua.purify-us-pure-drink-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

> اللَّهُمَّ طَهِّرْ قُلُوبَنَا وَأَبْدَانَنَا، وَاجْعَلْ أَثَرَنَا فِي الْأَرْضِ طَيِّبًا، وَاسْقِنَا فِي الْجَنَّةِ شَرَابًا طَهُورًا.

What it means: O Allah, purify our hearts of envy and evil, and our bodies of dirt. Make whatever we leave behind on the earth good, and give us in Jannah the pure drink You have promised Your servants.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world, everything we consume leaves a footprint. A meal leaves dishes and leftovers, the body needs constant cleaning, and the table itself can leave behind words we come to regret. The food of Jannah is real and delicious, yet it leaves nothing impure behind: its people do not spit, urinate, relieve themselves or blow their noses, and their food passes away as a belch and a sweat like the sweat of musk.[^4] Al-Qurtubi explained this by saying that their nourishment is "utterly refined and perfectly balanced, and has no residue that would be found repugnant."[^6] And Allah gives them "a pure drink,"[^1] which Ibn Kathir explains as purifying "their inner selves of envy, resentment, rancor and harm."[^2] So purity there is outward and inward at once, and it comes as a gift. Here, it is a choice and a task: "Purification is half of faith," which Ibn 'Uthaymin explained as purification that is "physical and inward, from everything that contains harm."[^10]

<!-- unit:end -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Huda and the Family Dinner

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.061.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Huda was fifteen when her family hosted their relatives for dinner. After the meal, the adults moved to the living room, and the younger ones stayed around the table, which was covered with plates, crumbs and half-empty glasses of juice.

Huda's cousin Dana pulled out her phone. "Look at this picture of Salma at the school party!" Everyone burst out laughing. Then the comments started: about her dress, her weight, the way she talks. And Salma was in Huda's class.

Huda felt something heavy settle in her chest. She knew that a single laugh would make her "one of them," and that staying quiet would make her "the boring one." Through the doorway, she could see her mother carrying the dishes to the kitchen by herself.

Huda remembered what she had read in that week's lesson: the food of Jannah leaves nothing behind but the scent of musk, and its drink purifies hearts of envy and rancor. And she asked herself: "So what is this table leaving behind? Dirty dishes... and talk that's dirtier still."

Huda stood up and said calmly, without looking at the phone: "Salma's in my class, and I don't like us talking about her when she isn't here. And my mom's in the kitchen all alone. Who's going to help me?"

An awkward silence fell. "We were only joking," Dana muttered, annoyed, and turned off her phone.

Huda picked up the plates, and her cousin Yusuf followed her with the glasses. A few minutes later, Dana came into the kitchen, took the dish towel, and silently began drying the plates.

At last Dana said, "You were right. I wouldn't want anyone doing that with a picture of me."

By the time the relatives left, the table was clean and the kitchen was in order. That night, Huda wrote in her journal: "A table leaves two kinds of mess behind: dishes and words. I want to clean up both."

### A True Report: A Question Asked Without Embarrassment

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2835" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2835" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report in Sahih Muslim from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, may Allah be pleased with them both. It is not an imagined scene.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The people of Jannah will eat and drink there, and they will not spit, they will not urinate, they will not relieve themselves, and they will not blow their noses."[^4]

The Companions didn't just listen. They asked the kind of question some people would be too embarrassed to ask: "Then what becomes of the food?" In other words, if they eat, where does what they eat go?[^4]

The answer came clearly, without any awkwardness: "A belch, and a sweat like the sweat of musk. Glorifying and praising Allah will come to them as naturally as breathing."[^4]

An-Nawawi said that the people of Jannah enjoy their food "with an enjoyment that is everlasting and has no end," and that their bliss shares nothing with the bliss of this world except the name and the basic form, while being completely free of anything unpleasant.[^5]

There are two lessons in this scene. First, a sincere question about the religion is nothing to be ashamed of, even when it touches on something people feel shy about. Second, the bliss of Jannah is complete and lacks nothing: even what follows a meal has become something fragrant, and a form of remembrance.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-76-21" -->

- **`A pure drink (sharaban tahura)`** — a drink that cleanses the outward self of anything foul, as at-Tabari said, and the inward self of envy and rancor, as Ibn Kathir said.[^2]
- **`A sweat like the sweat of musk (rashhun ka-rashh al-misk)`** — a sweet-smelling sweat through which food passes away in Jannah.[^4]
- **`Physical and inward purification`** — keeping body and surroundings clean, and keeping heart and tongue clear of harm.[^10]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What made laughing at the picture tempting for Huda? And what made her stand up?
2. How did Huda manage to refuse the backbiting and invite the others to help, without embarrassing anyone?
3. What did the Companions ask? And why does their question count as good manners in seeking knowledge rather than a lack of modesty?
4. How do the explanations of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir of "a pure drink" bring together outward and inward purity?
5. What trace do you usually leave behind after a meal or a get-together? And which is harder to clean up: the dishes or the words?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: What Does My Meal Leave Behind?

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.061.activity.what-my-meal-leaves-behind" -->

1. For seven days, after one meal each day, record in a private notebook every trace you left, in two columns: "Visible traces" (dishes, crumbs, packaging, leftovers, water) and "Hidden traces" (a remark, mockery, complaining, waste).
2. Next to each trace, write what you did to clean it up: washing, wiping, sorting, saving the leftovers, changing the subject, or apologizing. If you didn't clean it up, be honest and leave the box empty.
3. On the seventh day, ask someone in your household to check the place after your meal. Then write a private paragraph: Which column was longer? And which habit do you want to keep once the seven days are over?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.061.dua.purify-us-pure-drink-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ طَهِّرْ قُلُوبَنَا وَأَبْدَانَنَا، وَاجْعَلْ أَثَرَنَا فِي الْأَرْضِ طَيِّبًا، وَاسْقِنَا فِي الْجَنَّةِ شَرَابًا طَهُورًا.
>
> *"O Allah, purify our hearts and our bodies, make the mark we leave on the earth a good one, and give us a pure drink in Jannah."*

This du'a brings both kinds of purity together: purity of the heart from envy and rancor, and purity of the body and of one's surroundings. Then it looks ahead in hope to Jannah, where Allah gives His servants a pure drink that is followed by nothing unpleasant.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites the ayah of al-Insan (76:21) and presents the explanations of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir of "a pure drink"; narrates the hadith of Jabir (Muslim 2835) in both its narrations; explains the answer the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave to the Jewish man (Ahmad 19269); explains, in al-Qurtubi's words, why the food of Jannah leaves no waste; distinguishes the purity of Jannah from the purity of this world in light of Muslim 223; and begins the "What Does My Meal Leave Behind?" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; the activity table printed out (trace, act of cleaning, check); a board with two columns headed "Jannah: purity as a gift" and "This world: purity as a task"; a picture of a table after a meal (dishes and leftovers) with no people in it.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on al-Insan 76:21, the commentaries of an-Nawawi and of al-Qurtubi in *al-Mufhim* on Muslim 2835, the chapter "The Food of the People of Jannah, Their Drink, and Where It Goes" in *Hadi al-Arwah*, and Ibn 'Uthaymin's explanation of the hadith "Purification is half of faith."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the picture of the table and ask: "How many traces has this meal left? And how many minutes will it take to clear them away?" Then: "The Companions asked a question about the food of Jannah that is a lot like this one. What do you think it was?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Insan with the explanations of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir, pointing out that the statements of Ibrahim at-Taymi and Abu Qilabah are their own words, not hadiths attributed to the Prophet. Then read the hadith of Jabir in both its narrations, with the words of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi; then the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam with Ibn al-Qayyim's comment; then the hadith of Abu Malik with Ibn 'Uthaymin's explanation. Learners fill in the two columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion: Why did Allah mention outward adornment first and then the pure drink? How does the Companions' question show good manners in seeking knowledge? Why do the people of Jannah eat when there is no hunger there, as al-Ashqar reports from al-Qurtubi? And what makes purification in this world half of faith?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains the stages of the activity as set out in the activity unit. Learners fill in the first row of the table for the last meal they ate, and each one chooses the daily meal they will follow and the person who will check the place on the seventh day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes down: the two meanings of "a pure drink," the wording of the Companions' question and its answer, and the difference between the purity of Jannah and the purity of this world. Agree that anyone who wishes may share their three lines after seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayah of al-Insan and the hadith of Jabir are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare the narrations of the hadith of Jabir in Muslim (*at-tahmid*, *al-hamd* and *at-takbir*), and to read Ibn al-Qayyim's chapter, distinguishing what is authentic from what the editor graded weak.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Speak about this subject in refined language, without going into detail and without joking. Do not rely in the lesson on the report from 'Ali ibn Abi Talib about the two springs at the gate of Jannah, on the statement of Muqatil, or on the statements of the Successors in at-Tabari, except as reported opinions.[^12] From the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam, take only the part that bears on where the food goes; what it says about the strength of the people of Jannah in desire has its own lesson. And the lesson must not be taken to disparage the needs of the body in this world: they are part of Allah's creation, and keeping clean of them is an act of worship.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah eat and nothing dirty is left over after their food, and that their food turns into a lovely smell like musk; retells the Companions' question; cleans up their place after eating and washes their hands; and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small blanket; clean orange peels, empty bags and a rinsed-out juice box for acting out the story; soap and water or wet wipes; a cotton ball with a little perfume on it; the activity sheet with three boxes for the three days; coloring pencils; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices the story of Lina and the retelling of the true report in simple, polite words; sets up a "picnic spot" with the peels and bags before the session; and makes sure no child is sensitive to the perfume.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What's left on your plate after you eat? And who cleans it up?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell the story of Lina, then say, "And this is a true report," and tell the Companions' question and the answer of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. The child smells the perfumed cotton ball and is told: "The people of Jannah smell much, much nicer than this."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The children sit around the "picnic spot," and the caregiver asks: "Shall we leave it for the park worker?" Then the children gather the peels and bags into the bag, put the juice box in the bin, and say, "Al-hamdu lillah."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The children wash their hands with soap and draw in the first box what they found around the blanket, then color it in after cleaning up, as described in the activity unit. Explain how they will finish the other two days at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "Where does the food of the people of Jannah go?" and "What do we do after we eat?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, gathering the peels and washing their hands is enough. Older children retell Lina's story in their own words and count their stars at the end of the three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the subject with gentle, polite words. Do not let the children laugh at the needs of the body, and never make a child feel ashamed of them; they are natural in this world, and Allah created them. Do not give a child real, dirty trash, and do not use glass. Do not draw the people of Jannah, and do not depict the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the Companions' question and its answer; explains the four terms; gives at-Tabari's meaning of "a pure drink"; connects Karim's decision to the difference between purity in Jannah and purity in this world; and starts the activity table.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the hadith and the ayah written out; cards for the four terms; the two-column activity table; small resealable bags; two scenario cards: "A trip with no trash cans" and "The school lunch table after the bell."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes on the board: "Jannah: purity as a gift / This world: purity as a task," and reviews the commentaries of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi on the hadith in age-appropriate language.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Name everything a school lunch leaves behind." The answers are written on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Tell the hadith of Jabir, pausing at the question, "Then what becomes of the food?", and then at the answer. Read the ayah of al-Insan with at-Tabari's statement, and the hadith "Purification is half of faith," and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Karim's story, pausing at the stone: "What would you have done? Why?" Then the students work in pairs on the two scenario cards, writing a decision and a first practical step for each one.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students fill in the first row of the table for today's meal, as described in the activity unit, practice sealing a bag of wrappers, and each one chooses the meal they will follow for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The food of Jannah passes away as..." and "Purity in this world is a task because..." Then read the du'a. Students share their tables after five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who are struggling a ready-made list of traces to choose from. Ask advanced students to write a short dialogue between Karim and Tamer in which Karim explains the hadith kindly.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Keep the discussion serious and respectful, and do not allow jokes or comments about classmates' bodies. Do not let anyone mockingly call a student who cleans up "the janitor," and thank everyone who helps. Do not present any part of the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam to this age group.

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen explains the texts on the food of Jannah being free of impurity and al-Qurtubi's reasoning; brings together the two explanations of "a pure drink," outward and inward; analyzes Huda's decision to refuse the backbiting and clear the table; and carries out the activity for seven days with its visible and hidden columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; private notebooks; the two-column activity table; blank, unnamed cards; a picture of a table after a group meal, with no people in it.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on al-Insan 76:21, the commentaries of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi on Muslim 2835, and Ibn 'Uthaymin's explanation of the hadith in Muslim 223, and prepares the discussion questions.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Insan with the two explanations; then tell the hadith of Jabir with the words of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi; read the hadith "Purification is half of faith" with Ibn 'Uthaymin's explanation; and explain the terms.

Do not read the private notebooks, and do not ask anyone to name the person they backbit. It is enough to note that the details of the bliss of spouses in Jannah have a lesson of their own.
