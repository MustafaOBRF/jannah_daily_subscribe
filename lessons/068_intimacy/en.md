# The People of Jannah and Their Spouses: A Pure Delight

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the scene of Surah Ya-Sin (36:51-58) in its order: the blowing of the Trumpet and the rising from the graves; then perfect justice, "So today no soul will be wronged in the least"; then the companions of Jannah, "busy, rejoicing," with their spouses in the shade, given whatever they ask for; and finally "Peace: a word from a Merciful Lord."
- Set out the commentators' views on the meaning of "occupation" (*shughul*) as Ibn Kathir and at-Tabari transmit them, at-Tabari's preferred reading that combines them all, and as-Sa'di's description of it as an occupation "that delights the soul and gives it pleasure."
- Draw on the ayah "And for them therein are purified spouses" (al-Baqarah 2:25) and the hadith of Anas in at-Tirmidhi (2536) to show that the delight of married life in Jannah is real and complete, free of all harm and weakness, while keeping what is established separate from what is not relied upon.
- Explain that love for the sake of Allah endures on the Day of Resurrection (az-Zukhruf 43:67), and that the road to this delight is chastity today, as in the hadith of Sahl ibn Sa'd in al-Bukhari (6474).
- Carry out the "Name the Spoiler, Guard the Joy" activity: choosing a lawful family joy, naming what spoils it, telling apart what will only vanish in Jannah from what can be removed now, then guarding the joy from one spoiler for a set period and reflecting on the result.
- Learn by heart a lesson-authored bedtime du'a asking Allah to protect our homes and to gather us and our families in the shade of Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Allah made marriage lawful and placed affection and mercy between husband and wife. Yet in this world even this blessing is never entirely unclouded: there is fatigue and frailty, impurity that calls for ritual washing, jealousy and quarrels, and parting through death. The Qur'an speaks of the delight the people of Jannah share with their spouses in chaste, comprehensive language. The subject of this lesson is that this delight is real and complete; that it is pure, free of everything that clouds its counterpart in this world; and that those who will have the fullest share of it are those who guard themselves most carefully today. The rulings concerning believing wives and the hur al-'ayn each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-36-51-58" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Companions of Jannah Today Are Busy, Rejoicing"

> **{وَنُفِخَ فِي الصُّورِ فَإِذَا هُم مِّنَ الْأَجْدَاثِ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِمْ يَنسِلُونَ ۝ قَالُوا يَا وَيْلَنَا مَن بَعَثَنَا مِن مَّرْقَدِنَا ۜ ۗ هَٰذَا مَا وَعَدَ الرَّحْمَٰنُ وَصَدَقَ الْمُرْسَلُونَ ۝ إِن كَانَتْ إِلَّا صَيْحَةً وَاحِدَةً فَإِذَا هُمْ جَمِيعٌ لَّدَيْنَا مُحْضَرُونَ ۝ فَالْيَوْمَ لَا تُظْلَمُ نَفْسٌ شَيْئًا وَلَا تُجْزَوْنَ إِلَّا مَا كُنتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ ۝ إِنَّ أَصْحَابَ الْجَنَّةِ الْيَوْمَ فِي شُغُلٍ فَاكِهُونَ ۝ هُمْ وَأَزْوَاجُهُمْ فِي ظِلَالٍ عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ مُتَّكِئُونَ ۝ لَهُمْ فِيهَا فَاكِهَةٌ وَلَهُم مَّا يَدَّعُونَ ۝ سَلَامٌ قَوْلًا مِّن رَّبٍّ رَّحِيمٍ}** [يس: ٥١-٥٨][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And the Trumpet will be blown, and at once they will be rushing out of their graves to their Lord. They will say, 'Woe to us! Who has raised us from our place of sleep?' This is what the Most Merciful promised, and the messengers spoke the truth. It will be nothing but a single Blast, and at once they will all be brought before Us. So today no soul will be wronged in the least, and you will be repaid only for what you used to do. Indeed, the companions of Jannah today are busy in their occupation, rejoicing: they and their spouses, in shade, reclining on couches. For them therein is fruit, and for them is whatever they call for. 'Peace': a word from a Merciful Lord."** (Ya-Sin 36:51-58)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that this is "the Blast of resurrection and raising up," and that *nasalan* (the word behind "rushing") is "fast walking." According to more than one of the early generations (the Salaf), the reply "This is what the Most Merciful promised" is spoken by the believers; al-Hasan said it comes from the angels. On ayah 55 he says that they are "busy" "with no thought for anyone else, because of the lasting bliss and the supreme triumph they are in." He transmits from Mujahid: "in bliss, delighted"; from Ibn 'Abbas, that "rejoicing" (*fakihun*) means "joyful"; and from Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, 'Ikrimah, al-Hasan, Qatadah, al-A'mash, Sulayman at-Taymi and al-Awza'i: "Their occupation is the deflowering of virgins." On ayah 56 he transmits from Mujahid that "their spouses" means "their lawful wives," and that the couches (*al-ara'ik*) are "beds beneath canopies"; on ayah 57: "Whatever they ask for, they find, of every kind of delight"; and on ayah 58, from Ibn 'Abbas: "For Allah Himself greets the people of Jannah with peace."[^1]

At-Tabari lists three views: bliss, marriage with virgins, and being occupied away from what the people of the Fire are suffering. Then he says: "The most correct of the views on this is to say as Allah said... That occupation they are in is bliss, the deflowering of virgins, amusement and pleasure, and being occupied away from what the people of the Fire encounter."[^3]

As-Sa'di says they are "busy" in an occupation "that delights the soul and gives it pleasure, made up of everything souls desire, eyes delight in, and wishers wish for." He explains "their spouses" as the hur al-'ayn, and says of "Peace": "When the Merciful Lord greets them with peace, they are granted perfect peace and safety in every respect."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The scene opens with the terror of rising from the grave and closes with the greeting of peace from the Merciful Lord. In between comes a justice with no wrong in it, and then a bliss that occupies its people to the exclusion of all else. And the Qur'an names the most intimate of these delights in a single chaste, all-embracing phrase: "busy in their occupation, rejoicing."

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "And for Them Therein Are Purified Spouses"

> **{وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ أَنَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِن تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ كُلَّمَا رُزِقُوا مِنْهَا مِن ثَمَرَةٍ رِّزْقًا ۙ قَالُوا هَٰذَا الَّذِي رُزِقْنَا مِن قَبْلُ ۖ وَأُتُوا بِهِ مُتَشَابِهًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُّطَهَّرَةٌ ۖ وَهُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [البقرة: ٢٥][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And give glad tidings to those who believe and do righteous deeds that theirs are gardens beneath which rivers flow. Whenever they are given fruit from them as provision, they will say, 'This is what we were provided with before,' for it will be given to them in a likeness. And for them therein are purified spouses, and they will abide therein forever."** (al-Baqarah 2:25)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir transmits from Ibn 'Abbas: "purified of filth and harm"; from Mujahid: "of menstruation, excrement, urine, mucus, spittle, semen and childbearing"; and from Qatadah: "of harm and sin." As-Sa'di notes that Allah did not say "purified of this or that particular flaw," so that the word would embrace every kind of purification: they are "pure in character, pure in form, pure in speech, pure in gaze."[^4]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2536" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Strength of a Hundred

> عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«يُعْطَى الْمُؤْمِنُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ قُوَّةَ كَذَا وَكَذَا مِنَ الْجِمَاعِ»**. قِيلَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَوَيُطِيقُ ذَلِكَ؟ قَالَ: **«يُعْطَى قُوَّةَ مِائَةٍ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"In Jannah the believer will be given such-and-such strength for intimacy."** Someone asked, "O Messenger of Allah, and will he be able to bear that?" He said: **"He will be given the strength of a hundred."**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Mulla 'Ali al-Qari explains the question "and will he be able to bear that?" as meaning: "That is, will he be given that strength, and be able to manage that much?" and explains "the strength of a hundred" as "that is, of a hundred men, so it has been said, or a hundred times."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The question about capacity is the question of someone measuring by the frailty of this world, and the answer is that Allah grants the strength through which the delight is made complete. Ibn al-Qayyim closes his chapter on marriage among the people of Jannah with these words: "Nor does it leave them in a state of major ritual impurity (*janabah*) that would require them to purify themselves, nor bring weakness or loss of strength; rather, their union is a union of pleasure and bliss, with no defect in it in any respect whatsoever." Then he says: "And the most complete of people in it are those who most guarded themselves from the unlawful in this abode."[^8]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-43-67" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Except the God-Fearing"

> **{الْأَخِلَّاءُ يَوْمَئِذٍ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ إِلَّا الْمُتَّقِينَ}** [الزخرف: ٦٧][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Close friends will be enemies to one another on that Day, except the God-fearing."** (az-Zukhruf 43:67)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "Every friendship and companionship that was for other than Allah will turn into enmity on the Day of Resurrection, except what was for Allah, Mighty and Majestic, for that lasts as long as He lasts." As-Sa'di says of "except the God-fearing": "for their love endures and continues, as the One for whose sake they loved endures."[^5]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6474" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whoever Guarantees Me"

> عَنْ سَهْلِ بْنِ سَعْدٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«مَنْ يَضْمَنْ لِي مَا بَيْنَ لَحْيَيْهِ وَمَا بَيْنَ رِجْلَيْهِ أَضْمَنْ لَهُ الْجَنَّةَ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Sahl ibn Sa'd, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever guarantees me what is between his jaws and what is between his legs, I will guarantee him Jannah."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn 'Uthaymin says: "What is between the jaws is the tongue, and what is between the legs is the private parts, whether of a man or of a woman."[^7]

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The Qur'an affirms purified spouses for the people of Jannah and makes their occupation one of joy and bliss, and the hadith affirms that they are given full strength for it. Ibn al-Qayyim establishes that this delight brings no ritual impurity and no weakness: everything that clouds its counterpart in this world is absent from it. Of the bonds of this world, what was for Allah remains, and the whole scene is sealed with the Lord's greeting of peace. The road to it in this world is the chastity of the tongue and of the body. So the practical question is this: what clouds our lawful joys today, and how much of that is ours to remove?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why does the mention of the delight of the companions of Jannah in Surah Ya-Sin come right after "So today no soul will be wronged in the least"?
2. What are the views on the meaning of "occupation" (*shughul*), and how did at-Tabari bring them together?
3. According to as-Sa'di, what does the word "purified," left without any qualification, add?
4. How does the hadith of Anas answer the habit of measuring the delights of the Hereafter by the frailty of this world?
5. What does Ibn al-Qayyim mean by "the most complete of people in it are those who most guarded themselves from the unlawful in this abode"? And how does that connect to the hadith of Sahl ibn Sa'd?

### Activity: Name the Spoiler, Guard the Joy

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.068.activity.name-the-spoiler-guard-the-joy" -->

Choose a lawful joy that comes around regularly and brings you together with your spouse or your household: a shared dinner, an evening sitting together, a weekly outing. In two columns, write down what usually spoils it. In the first column, put what will only disappear in Jannah, such as fatigue, illness or lack of time; in the second, what you can remove now, such as the phone, complaining, comparisons or a hurtful word. Pick one spoiler from the second column and guard the joy from it for seven days, marking each day whether you managed to guard it. On the seventh day, write three lines: What changed in the joy? What clouding remained? And how does that remind you of a delight with no clouding in it at all?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="3.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Have you ever sat with your family under a tree on a lovely day, eating fruit and laughing together? That is a happy time. But sometimes something comes along and spoils it: two brothers fight over a piece, or someone starts crying, or everyone gets tired and it's time to go home.

In Jannah, every happy time is clean and pure, and nothing spoils it. Allah gives every believing man and every believing woman whatever makes them completely happy. A believer and their husband or wife sit there together, and families who love each other for the sake of Allah keep their love forever.

<!-- unit:end -->

### From the Qur'an: "Busy with Joy"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-36-51-58" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In a surah called "Ya-Sin," Allah tells us about the Day of Resurrection.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-36-51-58" audience="4-7" -->

On that Day, all the people stand up and stand before Allah. And Allah is fair: He is not unfair to anyone, not even a tiny bit. Then the believers go into Jannah. There they are happy, busy with beautiful things that make them glad. They sit with their husbands and wives, a believing man with his wife and a believing woman with her husband, in the cool shade, on comfy seats. They have lots of fruit, and whatever they ask for, they find. Then the very best thing of all comes to them: Allah says to them, "Peace." Allah says: "'Peace': a word from a Merciful Lord."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

In another ayah, Allah tells us that people who love each other for His sake will still love each other on the Day of Resurrection.[^5] Look: in Jannah there is a family together, a happiness nothing can spoil, and peace from Allah. And today we can make our time with our family even nicer: no fighting, sharing the fruit, and saying kind words.

### Activity: The Fruit-and-Peace Get-Together

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.068.activity.name-the-spoiler-guard-the-joy" -->

With your mom or dad, pick a time when your family sits together, like fruit time after dinner. Ask each other: What spoils this time sometimes? Fighting, grabbing, or shouting? Choose one of them and agree with your family to keep it away. For three days, at every get-together: say "As-salamu 'alaykum," and offer the fruit to someone else before you take any. If the get-together goes by without that spoiler, draw a fruit on the tree on your paper. On the third day, count your fruits.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Salma's family had planned a picnic for Friday. Everything was perfect: a big park, a basket of fruit, and warm sunshine. But little by little the fun got clouded over. Someone got lost in their phone, someone else kept complaining about the heat, and the two little ones fought over the swing. On the way home, Salma said, "The picnic was nice, but why is it that the fun is never complete?"

Happiness in this world is beautiful, but there is always something mixed in that clouds it. The delights of Jannah, though, are pure and complete, and nothing clouds them. Allah gives every believing man and every believing woman whatever makes them perfectly happy, and He keeps alive the love of those who loved one another for His sake.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-36-51-58" -->

- **`Rejoicing (fakihun)`** — joyful, as Ibn 'Abbas explained, according to Ibn Kathir.[^1]
- **`Couches (al-ara'ik)`** — beautifully adorned seats, as as-Sa'di explained.[^2]
- **`Whatever they call for (ma yadda'un)`** — whatever they ask for: whatever they ask for and wish for, they get, as as-Sa'di explained.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: From the Day of Resurrection to the Peace of Allah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-36-51-58" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah Ya-Sin describes the Day of Resurrection so vividly that it is as if you were watching it.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-36-51-58" audience="8-12" -->

The Trumpet is blown, and people come rushing out of their graves toward their Lord. Those who denied that there would be a resurrection are stunned: "Woe to us! Who has raised us from our place of sleep?" And the answer comes back (many of the early scholars said it is spoken by the believers): "This is what the Most Merciful promised, and the messengers spoke the truth." All the people are gathered together, and Allah says: "So today no soul will be wronged in the least."

Then the scene moves to Jannah: "Indeed, the companions of Jannah today are busy in their occupation, rejoicing." As-Sa'di said they are busy with something that delights and pleases the soul, "made up of everything souls desire and eyes delight in." "They and their spouses, in shade, reclining on couches," meaning in the shade of the trees, as Ibn Kathir explained. They have fruit, and they have whatever they ask for. And then: "'Peace': a word from a Merciful Lord." As-Sa'di said that when the Merciful Lord greets them with peace, "they are granted perfect peace and safety in every respect."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

And in Surah az-Zukhruf: "Close friends will be enemies to one another on that Day, except the God-fearing." As-Sa'di said that the love of the God-fearing "endures and continues, as the One for whose sake they loved endures." So a family whose members love each other for the sake of Allah keeps that love.[^5]

Notice the difference. Salma's family picnic was clouded by the phone, the complaining and the fighting. In Jannah there is no fear, no unfairness and no fighting, only perfect peace. We can't turn this world into Jannah, but we can keep one spoiler, one that is in our power, away from our family's happy time.

<!-- story:end -->

3. What did as-Sa'di say about Allah's greeting of peace to the people of Jannah?
4. According to the ayah of az-Zukhruf, which love lasts on the Day of Resurrection? And why?
5. What spoiled Salma's family picnic? And which of those things could have been kept away?

### Activity: Joy Guard

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.068.activity.name-the-spoiler-guard-the-joy" -->

Pick a family time that happens regularly and that you love, like dinner or sitting together after Maghrib. Write down three things that usually spoil it, then mark each one: "Not in my control," like tiredness or illness, or "I can keep it away," like the phone, grumbling or arguing. Choose one you can keep away, and be the "Joy Guard" for four days: keep it away from that time, and start the get-together with salam. Each day, write down: Did I guard the joy? On the fourth day, write two sentences: What did I notice? And what reminded me of the delights of Jannah?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.068.dua.gather-our-families-in-shade-with-salam" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ احْفَظْ بُيُوتَنَا، وَأَلِّفْ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِنَا فِيكَ، وَاجْمَعْنَا وَأَهْلِينَا فِي ظِلَالِ جَنَّتِكَ، وَاجْعَلْ لَنَا فِيهَا سَلَامًا قَوْلًا مِنْ رَبٍّ رَحِيمٍ.
>
> *"O Allah, protect our homes, join our hearts together in love for Your sake, gather us and our families in the shade of Your Jannah, and grant us there 'Peace,' a word from a Merciful Lord."*
>

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every day, screens may show you things that make the relationship between a man and a woman look cheap, exposed or harmful. Islam sees it through different eyes: marriage is a blessing Allah has made lawful, He has placed affection and mercy between husband and wife, and He has made chastity, before marriage and within it, a road to Jannah.

In Jannah, this delight is completed and purified. Allah says: "And for them therein are purified spouses," and Ibn 'Abbas said, as Ibn Kathir transmits: "purified of filth and harm."[^4] Married life in Jannah is real, then, but it is pure and complete, with no harm and nothing to cloud it. The details belong to the lessons for adults. The question for you today is this: how will you guard yourself so that you reach that delight?

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever guarantees me what is between his jaws and what is between his legs, I will guarantee him Jannah," meaning whoever guards their tongue and guards their chastity, man or woman.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-36-51-58" -->

- **`Rushing (yansilun)`** — walking fast, hurrying, as Ibn Kathir explained.[^1]
- **`Occupation (shughul)`** — a bliss that occupies them to the exclusion of everything else. At-Tabari gathered the commentators' views under it: blessing and pleasure, including the joys of married life, and being occupied away from what the people of the Fire face.[^3]
- **`Purified (mutahharah)`** — purified of all filth and harm; Allah left the word unqualified so that it covers every kind of purity, as as-Sa'di explained.[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: From the Terror of Rising to the Peace of the Most Merciful

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-36-51-58" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah Ya-Sin presents the Day of Resurrection in eight fast-moving ayat, like a series of scenes cutting one into the next.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-36-51-58" audience="13+" -->

Scene one: "And the Trumpet will be blown, and at once they will be rushing out of their graves to their Lord." Scene two: those who denied the resurrection cry out, "Woe to us! Who has raised us from our place of sleep?" and the answer comes: "This is what the Most Merciful promised, and the messengers spoke the truth." Scene three: a single Blast, and suddenly everyone is present, and the verdict is: "So today no soul will be wronged in the least, and you will be repaid only for what you used to do."

Then the final scene: "Indeed, the companions of Jannah today are busy in their occupation, rejoicing: they and their spouses, in shade, reclining on couches. For them therein is fruit, and for them is whatever they call for. 'Peace': a word from a Merciful Lord." Ibn Kathir said they are busy "with no thought for anyone else, because of the lasting bliss they are in." And at-Tabari explained that this occupation brings together blessing and pleasure, including the joys of married life, and being occupied away from what the people of the Fire face.[^1][^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice the language. The Qur'an speaks of the most intimate of delights with a modest word, "occupation," and with no detail that would wound anyone's modesty. That itself is a lesson in good manners: these matters have their proper place and their proper language. And notice that the scene begins with justice: "and you will be repaid only for what you used to do." So whoever guards themselves today while watching others take it lightly does not lose their patience for nothing; they will find with Allah a pure delight beyond compare.[^1]

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why do the ayat begin with the resurrection and with justice before describing the delights of the people of Jannah?
2. What does the word "occupation" (*shughul*) teach us about good manners when talking about private matters?
3. What is the difference between the picture of relationships in much of what appears on screens and the picture in the Qur'an: a pure blessing with no harm in it?
4. The hadith "Whoever guarantees me" mentions the tongue and chastity together. Why do you think they are mentioned side by side?
5. What one step will help you guard your gaze or your tongue this week?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: A Get-Together with Nothing to Spoil It

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.068.activity.name-the-spoiler-guard-the-joy" -->

Pick a regular time you spend with your family, like a meal, a car ride or a weekend evening. In a private note, write down what usually spoils it, and split the list in two: what isn't in your hands, like your parents being tired or there not being enough time; and what is in your hands, like your phone, sarcasm or answering coldly. Choose one thing that is in your hands and guard that time from it for five days, writing down each day: Did I guard it? And what changed in the atmosphere? On the fifth day, write a private paragraph: What have I learned about my own power to create a purer kind of joy? And what does it mean to me that the delights of Jannah have no clouding in them at all?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene of Ya-Sin 36:51-58 in order; sets out the commentators' views on "occupation" (*shughul*) and at-Tabari's preferred reading, which combines them; explains "purified spouses" with the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; draws on the hadith of at-Tirmidhi 2536 while stating its grading; states Ibn al-Qayyim's summary of the purity of this delight; connects it to the hadith of al-Bukhari 6474; and begins the seven-day "Name the Spoiler, Guard the Joy" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with two columns headed "What clouds a blessing in this world" and "What the texts rule out in Jannah"; the two-column activity sheet; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Ya-Sin 36:51-58, al-Baqarah 2:25 and az-Zukhruf 43:67; at-Tabari's tafsir of Ya-Sin 36:55; the fifty-fifth chapter of Ibn al-Qayyim's *Hadi al-Arwah*, together with its editor's notes; and Mulla 'Ali al-Qari's commentary on the hadith of Anas. The teacher decides in advance on the language to be used: chaste language, like the language of the Qur'an, with no detail going beyond the texts. The teacher also reviews the lessons on believing wives and on the hur al-'ayn so as not to repeat their subjects.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Name a lawful joy you love in your home. What sometimes clouds it?" The answers go in the first column, without names or private details.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of Ya-Sin in full, pausing at the turn from justice to bliss, then the views of Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari and as-Sa'di on "occupation" (8 minutes). Then the ayah of al-Baqarah and its tafsir, filling the second column with what the texts rule out (4 minutes). Then the hadith of Anas, its grading and al-Qari's commentary, followed by Ibn al-Qayyim's summary (4 minutes). Then the ayah of az-Zukhruf and the hadith of Sahl ibn Sa'd (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 2 and 5. The teacher makes clear that the early generations' statements on "occupation" are tafsir, not hadith traced back to the Prophet, and that at-Tabari combined them; that the many explicit narrations on this subject are weak and are not relied upon; and that chastity today is the road to the fullness of this delight.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses a lawful family joy, fills in the two columns, chooses one spoiler that is theirs to remove, and writes down how they will guard the joy from it. Anyone who wishes may share their choice in general terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What are the views on the meaning of 'occupation' (*shughul*)?" "What does 'purified' mean according to as-Sa'di?" and "How does chastity today connect to the delights of Jannah?" Then read the bedtime du'a, noting that it is lesson-authored.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only Ya-Sin 36:55-58, al-Baqarah 2:25 and the hadith of Sahl. Ask advanced learners to follow the remarks of the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah* on the chains of the reports about "occupation" and on the weak narrations in the chapter, and to explain why they are not relied upon.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Keep to the Qur'an's own refinement of expression; do not dwell on any description that has no authentic text behind it; and cite weak narrations only to point out their weakness. Be mindful of those present who are unmarried, widowed or divorced, or who are struggling in their marriages: the lesson is not to be presented as a comparison between spouses in this world and in the Hereafter, and no one is to be asked to disclose anything of their private life. Mention the believing woman explicitly: Allah gives every believing man and woman whatever makes their delight complete. If anyone discloses harm or violence in their home, listen to them in confidence and refer them to the appropriate authority.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah are happy in the shade with fruit, and that Allah says "Peace" to them; says that love for the sake of Allah lasts; names one thing that spoils their family's time together and chooses to keep it away; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A plate of cut fruit; a sheet for each child with a tree drawn on it, without fruit; coloring pencils; the du'a card; a piece of green cloth to stand for shade.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the scene in a calm voice, prepares the fruit with attention to food allergies, and sends parents a short note explaining the three-day activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The children sit under the green cloth as if it were the shade of a tree, and the educator asks: "When is your family's time together really nice? And what sometimes spoils it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The educator reads the introduction and then the Qur'anic scene, and the children repeat: "'Peace': a word from a Merciful Lord." Then the educator mentions that people who love each other for the sake of Allah keep their love.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator asks the unit's questions, then the children practice a little get-together: each child says "As-salamu 'alaykum" and offers a piece of fruit to the child next to them before taking one.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** With the educator's help, each child chooses one thing that spoils their family's time together, draws it in a corner of the sheet and crosses it out, then takes the tree sheet home to draw the fruits over three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The educator notes whether the child can say what Allah says to the people of Jannah, and what they will keep away from their family's time together. Then the du'a is read one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, keep to the shade, the fruit and the word "Peace." Ask older children to retell the scene in their own words, from the Day of Resurrection to Allah's greeting of peace.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Say nothing to the children about married life in Jannah or about the hur al-'ayn, and explain "busy in their occupation" only as joy and delight. Be mindful of a child who has lost a parent or whose parents live apart: say that Allah gives every believer whatever makes them completely happy, and never promise a child any particular person. Never blame a child for fighting in their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the scene of Ya-Sin from the blowing of the Trumpet to Allah's greeting of peace; defines the three terms; cites the ayah of az-Zukhruf on the lasting of love for the sake of Allah; tells apart a spoiler that is not in their control from one they can keep away; and begins the four-day "Joy Guard" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; five cards for the scenes of the ayat (the Trumpet, the question, the gathering, justice, Jannah); a "Joy Guard" sheet for each student; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Ya-Sin 36:51-58 and az-Zukhruf 43:67, and prepares a written example of the spoilers of a family time from their own life.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the story of Salma's family picnic and asks: "What clouded the picnic? And which of those things could have been avoided?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes); then the Qur'anic scene, putting the scene cards in order and pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of the commentators (6 minutes); then the ayah of az-Zukhruf and as-Sa'di's comment (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss questions 3 and 5. The teacher explains that nothing clouds the delights of Jannah, that Allah gives every believing man and woman whatever makes them perfectly happy, and that today we can clear some of the clouding from our own joys.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The teacher shares their example; then each student writes three spoilers of a family time they love, sorts them, and chooses one spoiler to guard the joy from.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The people of Jannah are busy with..." and "I will guard my family's joy from...". Then the du'a is read. After four days, anyone who wishes shares what they noticed.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who find it hard, two spoilers and two days are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize Ya-Sin 36:55-58 and to explain "'Peace': a word from a Merciful Lord" in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Explain "and their spouses" as a believing man with his wife and a believing woman with her husband, with no further detail, and say nothing about the hur al-'ayn or married life. Do not ask students to reveal their families' disagreements; spoilers are written in general terms. Be mindful of a student living in a home with serious conflict or harm: listen to them privately and inform the person responsible when needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the scene of Ya-Sin through its four scenes; explains "rushing" (*yansilun*), "occupation" (*shughul*) and "purified" (*mutahharah*); compares the picture of relationships in the Qur'an with the picture in much of the media; explains the hadith "Whoever guarantees me"; and begins the five-day "A Get-Together with Nothing to Spoil It" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; cards for anonymous answers; a two-column sheet headed "The media's picture" and "The Qur'an's picture."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and at-Tabari on Ya-Sin 36:51-58, the tafsir of al-Baqarah 2:25, and the hadith of Sahl ibn Sa'd with its commentary, and decides how to answer sensitive questions briefly and with decorum, and when to refer the student to their parents or to a specialist.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "How do many TV series and clips portray the relationship between a man and a woman? And what is missing from that picture?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the Qur'anic scene with four pauses: the resurrection; the question and the answer; justice; and then the delights of the companions of Jannah and the Lord's greeting of peace, drawing attention to the modesty of the word "occupation."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 2, 3 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher steers toward three points: marriage is a blessing with nothing shameful in it; chastity today is not deprivation but a road to a pure delight; and whoever slips should repent, for Allah accepts repentance.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student starts a private note, sorts the spoilers of a family get-together into what isn't in their hands and what is, and chooses one spoiler to guard the get-together from.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: the meaning of "purified," why the Qur'an chose the word "occupation," and one step for guarding the gaze or the tongue. A week later the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see the notes. Then the du'a is read.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may keep private voice notes. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on as-Sa'di's remark that "purified" was left unqualified "so that it would embrace every kind of purification."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Keep the discussion brief and chaste. Do not mention the hadith of Anas, the early generations' explicit statements on "occupation," or any detail about the hur al-'ayn; these belong to the adult lessons. Never use shame or fear as teaching tools, and accuse no one. Be mindful of anyone who has been exposed to harmful content or exploitation, or who is struggling with a viewing addiction: encourage them to seek help from their parents or a trusted specialist; the teacher follows up confidentially and reports to the responsible authority in line with the child-protection policy.

This lesson does not rely on the explicit narrations attributed to the Prophet that he cites in the chapter and that his editor graded weak, such as the hadith of Abu Umamah in at-Tabarani, "Vigorously, vigorously, but with no semen and no death" (*la maniyy wa la maniyyah*) (weak); the hadith of Abu Sa'id, "When the people of Jannah are intimate with their wives, they return to being virgins" (judged fabricated by the editor); and the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, "with a member that never tires and a desire that never ends" (weak chain). The editor also notes inconsistencies in the chains of some of the reports from Ibn 'Abbas on "occupation."
