# Knowing Our Homes and Chambers in Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the words of Allah, exalted is He, "and admit them into Jannah, which He has made known to them" (Muhammad 47:6) in their context, and explain the phrase "which He has made known to them" (*'arrafaha lahum*) as most commentators understood it: Allah makes Jannah clear to its people so that they find their way to their own homes there without anyone showing them.
- Relate the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih al-Bukhari (6535): "Each of them will know the way to his home in Jannah better than he knew the way to his home in this world," and set out its stages in order: rescue from the Fire, being held back on the bridge, purification from wrongs, permission to enter, and finally finding one's way home.
- Distinguish between knowing a house in this world, which comes through repetition and habit, and knowing one's home in Jannah, which is something Allah makes known and gives.
- Explain the reconciliation offered by as-Sa'di: Allah first makes Jannah known to His servants in this world by describing it and stirring their longing for it, and then makes their homes known to them once they enter; and cite, as an example of that description, the ayah "and fine dwellings in Jannat 'Adn" (at-Tawbah 9:72).
- Carry out the "Know It by Its Description" activity: describing a familiar house by its distinctive landmarks until it can be recognized without an address, then preparing a card of what Allah has said about the dwellings of Jannah and reviewing it at set times.
- Remember the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to admit us into the Jannah He has made known to His servants and to guide us there to our own homes.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We all know the moment. You come home worn out from a long journey, turn into your neighborhood, and walk straight to your own door without reading a single sign or asking anyone the way. Yet that knowledge did not arrive all at once. Anyone who has moved into a new house remembers how many days it took before the way home became second nature. Even so, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore that a believer will know the way to his home in Jannah better than he knew the way to the home he lived in all his life in this world.

This lesson pauses on that one idea alone: how do the people of Jannah come to know their homes? Three texts answer the question: the ayah of Surah Muhammad, "and admit them into Jannah, which He has made known to them"; the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih al-Bukhari; and the ayah of at-Tawbah, which describes the believers' dwellings as "fine." Taken together, they yield three truths:

1. **This knowledge is a gift, not an achievement:** The people of Jannah do not learn its paths by repetition, the way we learn the streets of our cities. It is Allah who makes their homes known to them, so there is no confusion, no asking, and no feeling of being a stranger.
2. **The making-known begins in this world:** Allah has described Jannah to His servants, stirred their longing for it, and shown them the deeds that lead there. Whoever knows its description today knows where they are heading.
3. **Entry comes after purification:** The believers do not reach their homes until every wrong between them has been settled and their hearts have been made clean.

As for the deeds by which homes are built in Jannah, the description of its palaces, chambers, and lofty upper rooms, and the details of the entry itself, each has a lesson of its own. This lesson is confined to "knowing" and to finding the way.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-47-4-6" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Admit Them into Jannah, Which He Has Made Known to Them"

> **{... وَالَّذِينَ قُتِلُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ فَلَنْ يُضِلَّ أَعْمَالَهُمْ * سَيَهْدِيهِمْ وَيُصْلِحُ بَالَهُمْ * وَيُدْخِلُهُمُ الْجَنَّةَ عَرَّفَهَا لَهُمْ}** [محمد: ٤ (آخر الآية) - ٦][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"... And those who are killed in the cause of Allah, He will never let their deeds go to waste. He will guide them and set their condition right, and admit them into Jannah, which He has made known to them."** (Muhammad 47:4 [end of the ayah] to 47:6)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The opening of the fourth ayah, which concerns the rulings of combat, has been left out because the lesson does not rely on it. At-Tabari said: "He made it known to them and made it clear to them, so that a man comes to his home in it, once he has entered, just as he used to come to his home in this world; it is never unclear to him." He also transmitted, with his chain, the words of Mujahid: "Its people find their way to their houses and dwellings, and to wherever Allah has allotted them, without mistake, as if they had lived there since the day they were created; they ask no one to direct them there."[^4] Ibn Kathir reported from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: "They will know their houses when they enter Jannah just as you know your houses when you go home from Jumu'ah."[^5]

As-Sa'di brought two meanings together: Allah first made Jannah known to them by stirring their longing for it, describing it to them, and telling them of the deeds that lead to it; then, once they enter it, He makes known to them their homes and the bliss within them.[^6] Ibn al-Qayyim mentioned another view, that the word comes from *'arf*, a fragrant scent, so that the meaning is "He perfumed it for them." He then favored the view that the meaning is making known and making clear, so that every person knows his own home and does not stray into anyone else's.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The context of these ayat concerns those killed in the cause of Allah, yet at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir both cite here the hadith of Abu Sa'id, which applies to all the believers. Knowing one's home, then, is an honor for every one of the people of Jannah. In the ayah it comes after two forms of guidance: being guided along the way, and having one's condition set right. Then comes the crown of it all: a home where no one is a stranger.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6535" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Each of Them Will Know the Way to His Home in Jannah Better"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«يَخْلُصُ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ مِنَ النَّارِ، فَيُحْبَسُونَ عَلَى قَنْطَرَةٍ بَيْنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، فَيُقَصُّ لِبَعْضِهِمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ مَظَالِمُ كَانَتْ بَيْنَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا، حَتَّى إِذَا هُذِّبُوا وَنُقُّوا أُذِنَ لَهُمْ فِي دُخُولِ الْجَنَّةِ، فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسُ مُحَمَّدٍ بِيَدِهِ، لَأَحَدُهُمْ أَهْدَى بِمَنْزِلِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِنْهُ بِمَنْزِلِهِ كَانَ فِي الدُّنْيَا»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The believers will be delivered from the Fire, and then they will be held back on a bridge between Jannah and the Fire, where retribution will be taken for some of them from others for the wrongs that were between them in this world. Once they have been refined and purified, they will be given permission to enter Jannah. By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, each one of them will know the way to his home in Jannah better than he knew the way to his home in this world."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Yakhlusu* means "is delivered" or "escapes." The *qantarah* is a bridge, and the wording of the hadith itself places it after rescue from the Fire and before entry into Jannah. "Retribution will be taken for some of them from others" means that everyone who was wronged receives his due from the one who wronged him, and "refined and purified" (*hudhdhibu wa nuqqu*) means freed from the consequences of those wrongs. *Ahda* means "more surely guided," and in al-Bukhari's other narration the word is *adall*, "more familiar with the way to it."[^2] Ibn Kathir cites this hadith in explaining the words of Allah, exalted is He, "which He has made known to them."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith arranges the scene in a deliberate order: the believer does not reach his home until he has been cleansed of whatever lay between him and other people. Then comes the Prophet's oath, "By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad," affirming a matter of the unseen that can be known only by being told. And the comparison with the thing we know most intimately, our home in this world, makes the meaning tangible: a knowing with no hesitation and no confusion.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-72" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Fine Dwellings in Jannat 'Adn"

> **{وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا وَمَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً فِي جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ ۚ وَرِضْوَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ}** [التوبة: ٧٢][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Allah has promised the believing men and the believing women gardens beneath which rivers flow, to abide therein forever, and fine dwellings in Jannat 'Adn, the Gardens of permanent residence. But the good pleasure of Allah is greater still. That is the supreme triumph."** (at-Tawbah 9:72)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that "fine dwellings" means "beautifully built, and good to settle in."[^3] The promise in the ayah is made to the believing men and the believing women alike, and the ayah closes by declaring that the good pleasure of Allah is greater than all of it.

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah is an example of the "first making-known" that as-Sa'di described: a description of the dwellings given before they are ever seen. So the believer already knows, here and now, that a fine dwelling awaits him, and knows too that Allah's good pleasure is greater still.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of at-Tawbah describes the dwellings before they are seen; the ayah of Surah Muhammad promises that Allah will make them known to their people; and the hadith of Abu Sa'id swears that this knowing will be more complete than our knowledge of our own homes, and shows that it comes only after hearts have been purified. In this world, then, knowing Jannah means a description to be sought out and reflected upon; in the Hereafter, it is a gift that is simply given.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between the way you know your house in this world and the way a believer knows his home in Jannah, as indicated by the words "which He has made known to them"?
2. How did as-Sa'di reconcile the making-known in this world with the making-known at the moment of entry?
3. Why does the permission to enter come, in the hadith, after the purification from wrongs and not before it?
4. What purpose does the Prophet's oath serve in a report about the unseen such as this one?
5. What do you know today of the description of the dwellings whose people you ask Allah to make you one of?

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Zaynab Knows the Way

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.028.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

It was Friday, and Zaynab prayed at the masjid with her dad. After the prayer, lots and lots of people came out all at once, and the street was full. Zaynab held her dad's hand tight and said, "Dad, there are so many people and so many houses! How does everybody know which house is theirs?"

Her dad smiled. "How about you be the guide today? I'll walk with you, and you choose the way."

Zaynab felt a little scared. What if she got it wrong? Then she said, "I'll try!" and off she went, her hand still in her dad's. She smelled warm, yummy bread and said, "Here's the bakery. We keep going straight." Then she saw the big tree and said, "At the tree, we turn right." Then she saw a green door, with a white cat asleep on the step, and she shouted, "That's our house!"

"Did anybody show you the way?" her dad asked. "No," said Zaynab. "I just know it!"

Her dad sat down with her and said, "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that a believer in Jannah knows his home there even better than he knows his home here." Zaynab was surprised. "But I've never seen my home in Jannah!" Her dad said, "Allah helps its people know it. Allah says: 'and He will bring them into Jannah, which He has made known to them.'[^1] And we ask Allah to make us among its people."

That night, Zaynab raised her hands and said, "O Allah, let me into Jannah, and show me the way to my home there."

**This is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, passed it on to us. It is not a made-up story.**

When a family moves house, it takes days to learn the way home by heart. Jannah is different. Allah says of the believers: "and He will admit them into Jannah, which He has made known to them."[^1] The Qur'an commentator Mujahid explained that its people find their way to their houses and homes without a single mistake, as if they had lived there since the day they were created, never asking anyone for directions.[^4] Some scholars added that Allah is already making Jannah known to us now, by describing it in the Qur'an, making us long for it, and showing us the deeds that lead there.[^6]

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Ammar Leads His Grandpa Home

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.028.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Ammar's family had moved to a new neighborhood two months ago, and his grandpa was visiting for the first time. After Maghrib, Ammar's dad stayed at the masjid for a short meeting. "Ammar, walk Grandpa home," he said. "It's close."

Ammar opened the map on his phone: dead battery. He looked around. Every street looked the same, and so did every house. "Which way, champ?" asked Grandpa.

Ammar's face went hot. He thought about marching off confidently anywhere so Grandpa wouldn't know he was lost. But he stopped and said honestly, "Grandpa, give me a minute to remember."

He closed his eyes and recalled his mom's words on moving day: "After the pharmacy with the green cross, we turn right, and our brown door is next to the lemon tree." He remembered things he had noticed himself, day after day: the wall with a bird painted on it, the red bike outside the neighbors' house. Step by step, reading the landmarks one by one, he led the way to the brown door.

Grandpa laughed. "Well done! Honest when you didn't know, and then you figured it out. You know, I've moved five times in my life, and each time it took me weeks to learn the way home. But the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore that a believer knows his home in Jannah better than he knows his home in this world."[^2]

"But how can anyone know a house he's never seen?" Ammar asked.

"Allah makes it known to him," said Grandpa. "Allah says: 'which He has made known to them.'[^1] And He has already described Jannah to us: 'and fine dwellings in Jannat 'Adn.'[^3] You learned what our house looks like from your mom's words; we learn what Jannah is like from our Lord's words."

So they made a deal: every night of Grandpa's visit, they would read an ayah describing Jannah together, and Ammar would write one new detail in a little notebook he called "What My Home There Is Like."

### A True Hadith: Knowing the Way Home in Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6535" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6535" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true report that Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and it is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that when the believers are rescued from the Fire on the Day of Resurrection, they are stopped on a `qantarah`, a bridge, between Jannah and the Fire. There the wrongs between them in this world are settled, and everyone who was wronged gets back what is owed by the one who wronged them. Once their hearts are pure and clear, they are given permission to enter Jannah.

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore an oath and said: **«فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسُ مُحَمَّدٍ بِيَدِهِ، لَأَحَدُهُمْ أَهْدَى بِمَنْزِلِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِنْهُ بِمَنْزِلِهِ كَانَ فِي الدُّنْيَا»**, which means: **"By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, each one of them will know the way to his home in Jannah better than he knew the way to his home in this world."**[^2]

Notice two things: the believers reach their homes only with clean hearts free of unfairness, and finding your home there needs no map and no asking, because it is a gift from Allah.

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6535" -->

- **`Which He has made known to them ('arrafaha lahum)`** — Allah made Jannah clear to them, so they know their homes there without anyone having to show them the way.[^4]
- **`Better guided to his home (ahda bi-manzilihi)`** — knowing the way to his home better, and finding it more surely.
- **`Fine dwellings (masakin tayyibah)`** — homes that are beautifully built and good to live in, as Ibn Kathir explained.[^3]

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.028.dua.guide-us-to-our-homes" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ كَمَا هَدَيْتَنَا إِلَى بُيُوتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا، فَأَدْخِلْنَا الْجَنَّةَ الَّتِي عَرَّفْتَهَا لِعِبَادِكَ، وَاهْدِنَا فِيهَا إِلَى مَنَازِلِنَا بِرَحْمَتِكَ.
>
> *"O Allah, just as You guided us to our homes in this world, bring us into the Jannah You have made known to Your servants, and guide us there to our own homes, by Your mercy."*

What it means: O Allah, as You guided us home today, bring us into the Jannah You described to us and make known to its people, and guide us there to our homes by Your mercy, not our own strength.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

"Where are you from? And where's home, really?" A simple question, but it throws many young people who have moved between cities or countries, or who live between two cultures. Islam does not erase our love for home, country, and family; that feeling is part of our nature. But it puts that love in its place: every home in this world is a stage on the journey. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore that a believer will know the way to his home in Jannah better than the way to his home in this world,[^2] and Allah says: "and He will admit them into Jannah, which He has made known to them."[^1] There is a home, then, where no one is a stranger and no one has to prove they belong.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Dana's Post

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.028.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Dana, fifteen, has lived in three cities and two countries; this is her third school. A social media challenge was going around her class: post a photo of "my hometown" with the caption "My heart lives here." One by one, the girls posted their old neighborhoods and their grandparents' houses. Then one of them commented under Dana's profile picture: "And you? Where are you even from? You don't have a city!" A string of laughing emojis followed.

That night, Dana put a post together: a gorgeous photo of a city she had lived in for only a few months as a little kid, captioned "My forever home." She knew it was an exaggeration she didn't believe, but it would shut the question down. Just before she tapped "Post," she remembered what her Islamic studies teacher had read to the class that morning: the hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that a believer will know the way to his home in Jannah better than the way to his home in this world.

She froze. "I'm looking for somewhere to belong," she thought, "and I'm buying it with a borrowed photo?" She didn't delete it right away; she hesitated a long time, then deleted it. In its place she wrote: "I've lived in three cities, and each one taught me something. I'm still learning where I belong."

The next morning, not everything had changed. One classmate mocked it; another ignored it. But a new girl in the class, who had also moved from another country, messaged her: "I'm exactly like you." At break, the two of them sat together.

At home, her mom said, "Loving the cities you've lived in isn't wrong, and missing them is natural. But the home we're hoping for is one where we won't need a map, or need to convince anyone." So Dana wrote in her private journal: "I don't want to lie in order to belong. I want to know what the home I'm hoping for is like." Then she began reading one ayah a day describing Jannah, copying each under the heading: "The Home Where No One Is a Stranger."

### A True Hadith: First the Bridge, Then Home

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6535" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6535" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report that Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and it is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, describes a scene on the Day of Resurrection. The believers have been saved from the Fire, but they do not walk straight into Jannah. They are held back on a bridge between Jannah and the Fire, where the wrongs between them in this world are settled in full: a word that wounded, a right that was swallowed up, an injustice its victim kept quiet about. Only once they have been "refined and purified" are they given permission to enter. Then he swears, peace and blessings be upon him: **«فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسُ مُحَمَّدٍ بِيَدِهِ، لَأَحَدُهُمْ أَهْدَى بِمَنْزِلِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِنْهُ بِمَنْزِلِهِ كَانَ فِي الدُّنْيَا»**, that is: **"By Him in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, each one of them will know the way to his home in Jannah better than he knew the way to his home in this world."**[^2]

Think about the order: purification before home. No one enters their home there while holding a wrong done to another, or while another still has a claim against them. Then comes complete knowing: no one needs to ask, and no one needs to prove they belong. This is what Mujahid understood from Allah's words "which He has made known to them": "as if they had lived there since the day they were created."[^4]

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6535" -->

- **`The bridge (al-qantarah)`** — a bridge on which the believers are held back after being saved from the Fire and before entering Jannah, as stated in the wording of the hadith; the details of the entry itself have a lesson of their own.
- **`Refined and purified (hudhdhibu wa nuqqu)`** — freed from the consequences of the wrongs that were between them, so that no one is left owing anyone anything.
- **`Which He has made known to them ('arrafaha lahum)`** — as-Sa'di brought two meanings together here: Allah described Jannah to them in this world and stirred their longing for it, and then makes their homes known to them when they enter.[^6]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why was the "My forever home" post so tempting for Dana? And what made her hesitate?
2. How can you love your country, city, and family without making a place in this world your ultimate belonging?
3. What does it teach you that purification from wrongs comes before reaching one's home?
4. What is the difference between knowledge that comes from habit and knowledge that Allah gives?
5. If someone asked you, "Where's home, really?", what would your honest answer be?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: Know It by Its Description

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.028.activity.know-it-by-its-description" -->

Describe your route home from school or the masjid in just five landmarks, no street names, and give it to someone you trust: can they draw the route from it? Then open a page in your private journal headed "The Home Where No One Is a Stranger." Write the ayah of at-Tawbah (9:72) on it, and every day this week add one ayah or authentic hadith describing Jannah, with its source. Under each one, write a single line: what does this description change in the way I think about belonging?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.028.dua.guide-us-to-our-homes" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ كَمَا هَدَيْتَنَا إِلَى بُيُوتِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا، فَأَدْخِلْنَا الْجَنَّةَ الَّتِي عَرَّفْتَهَا لِعِبَادِكَ، وَاهْدِنَا فِيهَا إِلَى مَنَازِلِنَا بِرَحْمَتِكَ.
>
> *"O Allah, just as You guided us to our homes in this world, bring us into the Jannah You have made known to Your servants, and guide us there to our own homes, by Your mercy."*

This du'a links a blessing we live every night, coming home, with the hope of a far greater blessing that Allah has promised: that He will make our homes in Jannah known to us. Its closing words, "by Your mercy," are a reminder that both the entering and the finding of the way are a favor from Allah.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the meaning of "which He has made known to them" through the words of at-Tabari and Mujahid, presents as-Sa'di's reconciliation of the making-known in this world with the making-known at entry, mentions the other view ("He perfumed it for them") and Ibn al-Qayyim's preference, sets out the stages of al-Bukhari 6535 in order, and prepares a description card for the dwellings of Jannah to review over a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; a board on which to draw a ladder of the hadith's five stages; an activity sheet for each learner; blank cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the commentaries of at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, and as-Sa'di on the ayah (Muhammad 47:6), and Ibn al-Qayyim's chapter in *Hadi al-Arwah* on how the people of Jannah know their homes. On the board, the teacher writes the stages of the hadith as blank rungs: rescue, the bridge, retribution, refinement, permission, finding the way. The teacher also has ready a list of passages for the activity, such as ar-Rahman (55:46-78), al-Waqi'ah (56:10-40), and al-Insan (76:11-22).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "How many days did it take before you could find your way to the last home you lived in without thinking?" The teacher listens to three answers, then reads the Prophet's oath at the end of the hadith without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of Surah Muhammad, pointing out what has been omitted from the fourth ayah and why; then the words of at-Tabari, Mujahid, and Muhammad ibn Ka'b; then as-Sa'di's reconciliation; then the other view and Ibn al-Qayyim's preference. Next, read al-Bukhari 6535 in full and have the learners fill in the ladder of stages on the board, noting the word *adall* in the other narration. Finally, read the ayah of at-Tawbah (9:72) and Ibn Kathir's explanation of "fine dwellings."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher discusses three questions: Why does purification come before entry? What is the difference between knowledge that is acquired and knowledge that is given? How is the Qur'an's description of Jannah a "first making-known"? The teacher also flags the limits of the lesson: the building of homes, the palaces and chambers, and the details of the entry each have lessons of their own.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** The learners carry out the "Know It by Its Description" activity as described in the activity unit. Each learner chooses two passages from the list or from their own reading, and sets the three days for review.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes two sentences: the first explains "which He has made known to them" according to one of the views, attributing it to whoever held it; the second sets out the stages of the hadith in order. The teacher closes with a reminder to review the card, and invites anyone who wishes to share two lines after a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayah, the hadith, and the words of Mujahid are enough. Advanced learners are asked to compare the two wordings *ahda* and *adall* in al-Bukhari's two narrations, and to summarize the three views on the meaning of "which He has made known to them," explaining why one is preferred.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never declare with certainty that a particular person has a home in Jannah; speak of it as a hope and a prayer. Do not elaborate on the bridge or the retribution beyond what is in the text; leave the details to the lesson on entry. The saying attributed to Muqatil ibn Hayyan, that an angel walks the believer to his home, is a report that reached him without a connected chain and cannot be relied upon, so it must not be presented as established.[^7]

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah know their homes there because Allah makes them known to them, picks out the door of their own home from their drawing among other drawings, and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Paper and crayons; three cards of the same size for the doors; a picture or drawing of a short route with a tree, a bakery, and a green door; the du'a card in clear, large writing.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling Zaynab's story in a calm voice, draws two different doors in advance to be mixed in with the child's door, and sets up a simple path in the room with three landmarks (a cushion, a chair, a toy) for the "Guide" game.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The educator asks: "When we come back from the masjid, how do you know which house is ours?" Then the child plays the "Guide," leading the educator along the little path to the "door" by way of the three landmarks.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell Zaynab's story, then say: "And this is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us." Tell the simplified hadith calmly, focusing on how the believers' hearts become clean and how everyone knows their own home.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator and child repeat three sentences together with gestures: "I know my home here" (pointing to the door), "Allah helps us know our homes in Jannah" (raising a finger), "We ask Allah for Jannah" (raising both hands).

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The child does the three-doors activity as described in the activity unit. Then the educator reads the meaning of "and fine dwellings," and the child draws the river and the home on the back of the card.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "Who helps the people of Jannah know their homes?" and "Does anybody get lost in Jannah?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning, and hang the card near the child's bed.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, use stickers instead of drawing, and use only two doors. Older children describe the landmarks of their door out loud before they look for it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the Fire or the retribution in frightening detail; it is enough to say that Allah saves the believers and puts right whatever was wrong between them. Do not present the "Guide" game as encouragement for a child to walk the streets alone; remind the child that Zaynab was with her dad. If the child has recently moved or is living in difficult housing circumstances, treat the "my door" drawing gently and never compare one home with another.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the meaning of "which He has made known to them" through the words of Mujahid, sets out the stages of the hadith in order, distinguishes between knowing a house through habit and knowing one's home in Jannah because Allah makes it known, and starts a "What My Home There Is Like" notebook.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the ayah and the hadith written out; cards for the three terms; mystery cards; a small notebook for each student.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes descriptions of two imaginary houses to be mixed in with the students' cards, and prepares a short list of ayat describing Jannah for the students to choose from over the week.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Who here has moved to a new home? How many days did it take you to learn the way? And what helped you?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the ayah and the words of Mujahid, then tell the hadith, and have the students put its stages in order on cards: rescue, the bridge, settling wrongs, permission, knowing one's home. Then explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Ammar's story, and pause at his moment of embarrassment: "Why was saying 'I don't know right now, give me a minute' better than pretending to know?" Then the students compare Ammar's landmarks with Allah's description of Jannah in the ayah of at-Tawbah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes a mystery card as described in the activity unit. The students swap cards, mixed in with the teacher's two descriptions, and then each student writes the three details from the ayah of at-Tawbah on the back.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student answers aloud: "What did Mujahid say about the meaning of 'which He has made known to them'?" and "What happens before the entry?" Then read the du'a. After a week, each student shows the details they have collected in their notebook.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who need support receive a half-completed mystery card and pre-selected ayat. Advanced students compare the two wordings *ahda* and *adall* and explain how as-Sa'di brought the two kinds of making-known together.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Students must not write their real address or street name on the cards, to protect their families' privacy. Be mindful of any student living in difficult housing circumstances or moving often, and allow them to describe any familiar place instead of their home. Present the settling of wrongs between believers as justice and mercy, not as something to be frightened of.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the idea of belonging in the light of the hadith and the ayah, distinguishes between a natural longing for one's home and country and making a place in this world the place one ultimately belongs, explains the significance of purification coming before arrival home, and starts a private page on the description of Jannah to keep up for a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; paper for the route descriptions; a private journal for each student, or a sheet that can be kept in an envelope.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares an opening question about belonging, is ready for the possibility that the group includes immigrant or refugee students or children of separated families, and reviews the words of Mujahid and as-Sa'di's reconciliation.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher writes on the board: "Where's home, really?" and asks each student to write their answer privately in a single word, without sharing it.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah and the words of Mujahid, then the full text of the hadith, and ask: "Why don't the believers go into their homes the moment they are saved?" Then read as-Sa'di's reconciliation and the ayah of at-Tawbah.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Dana's story, and have students discuss in small groups: What small lies do people tell in order to feel they belong? And how did Dana tell the truth without looking down on her love for the cities she had lived in?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** The students carry out the "Know It by Its Description" activity as described in the activity unit, swapping route descriptions in pairs. Then each student starts their private page without showing it to anyone.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student returns to their one-word answer and writes a line beneath it: Has my answer changed? Why? On an unsigned card, each student writes one sentence explaining the order "purification, then home." After a week, anyone who wishes may share an ayah they have added to their page.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing may make a private voice recording instead. Advanced students are asked to present the three views on the meaning of "which He has made known to them" and explain which is preferred.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No student should be asked to reveal anything about their migration, housing, or family circumstances in front of the group. The lesson must not be taken as belittling love of one's homeland or family, nor as a call to withdraw from friends. Any student who shows signs of bullying or severe isolation should be referred for a one-to-one conversation and to the person responsible for safeguarding in the institution.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
