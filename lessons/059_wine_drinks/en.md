# The Wine and Drinks of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the account of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, in the house of Abu Talhah on the day wine was forbidden (al-Bukhari 2464 and 4617): the caller, the order to pour it out, the wine flowing through the streets of Madinah, and Anas's words: "They neither asked about it nor went back to it."
- Explain the ruling on the wine of this world and the reason behind it from Surah al-Ma'idah (5:90-91), with as-Sa'di's tafsir, and from the hadith "Every intoxicant is khamr, and every intoxicant is forbidden" (Muslim 2003), showing that the name *khamr* covers everything that intoxicates.
- Describe the drink of the people of Jannah from as-Saffat (37:45-47), al-Waqi'ah (56:17-19) and at-Tur (52:23): no harm, no headache, no loss of reason, and no idle talk or sin in its gatherings, so that it shares nothing with the wine of this world but its name.
- Set out the scholars' views on the meaning of "he will not drink it in the Hereafter" as al-Qari reports them from an-Nawawi, while highlighting the condition of repentance stated in the hadith itself.
- Carry out the "Pour It Out Today" activity: identifying one thing that clouds the mind or distracts from prayer, removing it that very day with no bargaining and no going back, then logging clarity of mind every day for a set period and comparing the results at the end.
- Learn by heart a lesson-authored thematic du'a asking Allah to guard our minds and hearts, to make us quick to obey Him, and to give us the drink of Jannah that brings no idle talk and no sin.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On a single day in Madinah, a drink that people had freely enjoyed until that morning ran through the city's streets. No authority announced a penalty, and no guard was posted at any door. A caller proclaimed one sentence, and the people of each house rose, went to their jars, and emptied them.

The previous lesson looked at the river of wine among the four rivers of Jannah. This lesson turns to the drink of the people of Jannah itself: what has been denied of it, what its gatherings are like, and why it can only be understood alongside the prohibition of the wine of this world. It begins with the account that joins the two: people who gave up a drink they loved at the very moment they learned that Allah had forbidden it.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2464" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "It Flowed Through the Streets of Madinah"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: كُنْتُ سَاقِيَ الْقَوْمِ فِي مَنْزِلِ أَبِي طَلْحَةَ، وَكَانَ خَمْرُهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ الْفَضِيخَ، فَأَمَرَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ مُنَادِيًا يُنَادِي: **«أَلَا إِنَّ الْخَمْرَ قَدْ حُرِّمَتْ»**. قَالَ: فَقَالَ لِي أَبُو طَلْحَةَ: اخْرُجْ فَأَهْرِقْهَا، فَخَرَجْتُ فَهَرَقْتُهَا، فَجَرَتْ فِي سِكَكِ الْمَدِينَةِ، فَقَالَ بَعْضُ الْقَوْمِ: قَدْ قُتِلَ قَوْمٌ وَهِيَ فِي بُطُونِهِمْ، فَأَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ: {لَيْسَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ جُنَاحٌ فِيمَا طَعِمُوا} الْآيَةَ.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, said: I was serving the drinks to the people gathered in the house of Abu Talhah, and their wine in those days was *fadikh*. Then the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, ordered a caller to proclaim: **"Listen! Wine has been forbidden."** Abu Talhah said to me, "Go out and pour it away." So I went out and poured it away, and it flowed through the streets of Madinah. Some of the people said, "Some people were killed while it was still in their stomachs." Then Allah revealed: "There is no blame on those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they consumed before..." (to the end of the ayah).[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Fadikh* is a drink made from *busr*, dates that have not yet ripened: they are crushed, water is poured over them, and they are left until the mixture ferments, as al-'Ayni reports from Ibrahim al-Harbi.[^2] *Sikak* means streets. Ibn Hajar reports al-Muhallab as saying: "The wine was poured out in the street only to proclaim its rejection and to make its abandonment widely known." Al-'Ayni said that the hadith establishes the prohibition of wine, "and in it is the acceptance of a report from a single individual (*khabar al-wahid*), and the prohibition of keeping it"; and that the remark "Some people were killed while it was still in their stomachs" came "from overwhelming fear, and compassion," since wine had at first been permitted.[^1] The ayah that was revealed reads in full: "There is no blame on those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they consumed before, so long as they are mindful of Allah, and believe, and do righteous deeds, then are mindful of Him and believe, then are mindful of Him and do good. And Allah loves those who do good." (al-Ma'idah 5:93)[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The command came in the form of a piece of news: "Wine has been forbidden." And the answer came as an action, not as words. The ayah revealed afterward set hearts at rest about those who had died before the prohibition: Allah holds no one to account for a ruling that had not yet been revealed.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-4617" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Neither Asked About It nor Went Back to It"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: مَا كَانَ لَنَا خَمْرٌ غَيْرُ فَضِيخِكُمْ هَذَا الَّذِي تُسَمُّونَهُ الْفَضِيخَ، فَإِنِّي لَقَائِمٌ أَسْقِي أَبَا طَلْحَةَ وَفُلَانًا وَفُلَانًا، إِذْ جَاءَ رَجُلٌ فَقَالَ: وَهَلْ بَلَغَكُمُ الْخَبَرُ؟ فَقَالُوا: وَمَا ذَاكَ؟ قَالَ: حُرِّمَتِ الْخَمْرُ. قَالُوا: أَهْرِقْ هَذِهِ الْقِلَالَ يَا أَنَسُ. قَالَ: فَمَا سَأَلُوا عَنْهَا وَلَا رَاجَعُوهَا بَعْدَ خَبَرِ الرَّجُلِ.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, said: We had no wine other than this *fadikh* of yours, the drink you call *fadikh*. I was standing, serving drinks to Abu Talhah and so-and-so and so-and-so, when a man came and said, "Has the news reached you?" They said, "What news?" He said, "Wine has been forbidden." They said, "Pour out these jars, Anas." And they neither asked about it nor went back to it after the man's report.[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Qilal* is the plural of *qullah*, a large earthenware jar. Al-'Ayni said: "The hadith shows that it is permissible to act on a report from a single individual, and that wine was permitted before the prohibition."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

They asked for no grace period to finish what was left in their cups, and they did not come back to the matter with questions or second thoughts. This is the quality around which the lesson's activity turns: giving something up completely, on the spot, with no going back.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-5-90-91" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "So Will You Not Desist?"

> **{يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّمَا الْخَمْرُ وَالْمَيْسِرُ وَالْأَنْصَابُ وَالْأَزْلَامُ رِجْسٌ مِنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ ۝ إِنَّمَا يُرِيدُ الشَّيْطَانُ أَنْ يُوقِعَ بَيْنَكُمُ الْعَدَاوَةَ وَالْبَغْضَاءَ فِي الْخَمْرِ وَالْمَيْسِرِ وَيَصُدَّكُمْ عَنْ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَعَنِ الصَّلَاةِ ۖ فَهَلْ أَنْتُمْ مُنْتَهُونَ}** [المائدة: ٩٠-٩١][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"O you who believe, wine, gambling, stone altars and divining arrows are nothing but filth, the handiwork of Satan, so shun it, that you may succeed. Satan only wants to stir up enmity and hatred among you through wine and gambling, and to turn you away from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer. So will you not desist?"** (al-Ma'idah 5:90-91)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said that *khamr* is "everything that *khamara* the mind, that is, covered it over with its intoxication": the very name of wine in Arabic means "that which veils." He also said that "success is only complete by leaving what Allah has forbidden." Among its harms he lists that it is filth, that it is the handiwork of Satan, that it stirs up enmity and hatred, and that it "turns the heart away, and the body follows it, from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer." On "So will you not desist?" he said: "Because the person of sound mind, when he looks at even some of these harms, is deterred from them and restrains himself."[^3]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2003a" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Every Intoxicant Is Khamr"

> عَنِ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«كُلُّ مُسْكِرٍ خَمْرٌ، وَكُلُّ مُسْكِرٍ حَرَامٌ، وَمَنْ شَرِبَ الْخَمْرَ فِي الدُّنْيَا فَمَاتَ وَهُوَ يُدْمِنُهَا لَمْ يَتُبْ، لَمْ يَشْرَبْهَا فِي الْآخِرَةِ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Every intoxicant is khamr, and every intoxicant is forbidden. Whoever drinks wine in this world and dies persisting in it, without having repented, will not drink it in the Hereafter."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Qari reports from an-Nawawi two views on the meaning of "he will not drink it in the Hereafter": "It has been said that he will enter Jannah but be denied the drinking of it, for it is among the finest drinks of Jannah"; and it has been said that he will forget any desire for it, since Jannah holds everything that souls desire. Al-Qari also mentions that what is meant may be an emphatic deterrent and a severe warning.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith makes the name all-embracing: whatever intoxicates is *khamr*, however its name or its ingredients may change. And it qualifies the warning with the words "without having repented": the door of repentance stays open to everyone who has fallen into it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-37-45-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "White, a Delight to Those Who Drink"

> **{يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ ۝ بَيْضَاءَ لَذَّةٍ لِلشَّارِبِينَ ۝ لَا فِيهَا غَوْلٌ وَلَا هُمْ عَنْهَا يُنْزَفُونَ}** [الصافات: ٤٥-٤٧][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"A cup from a flowing spring will be passed around among them, white, a delight to those who drink. No harm is in it, nor will they be robbed of their senses by it."** (as-Saffat 37:45-47)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said of *yunzafun*: "Their minds will not depart." As-Sa'di said that it is "free of the *ghawl* of the mind, its stealing away and loss, free of its being drained, and of the draining of its drinker's wealth; and there is no headache in it and no murkiness."[^5]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-17-19" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Suffer No Headache from It"

> **{يَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ وِلْدَانٌ مُخَلَّدُونَ ۝ بِأَكْوَابٍ وَأَبَارِيقَ وَكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ ۝ لَا يُصَدَّعُونَ عَنْهَا وَلَا يُنْزِفُونَ}** [الواقعة: ١٧-١٩][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Circulating among them will be youths who never age, with goblets and pitchers and a cup from a flowing spring. They will suffer no headache from it, nor will they be robbed of their senses."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:17-19)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: "It does not split their heads, nor does it drain away their minds."[^6]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-23" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "No Idle Talk and No Sin"

> **{يَتَنَازَعُونَ فِيهَا كَأْسًا لَا لَغْوٌ فِيهَا وَلَا تَأْثِيمٌ}** [الطور: ٢٣][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"There they will pass to one another a cup that brings no idle talk and no sin."** (at-Tur 52:23)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that *yatanaza'un* means they hand it to one another, and that "no idle talk and no sin" means: "They do not speak over it with idle speech, that is, raving, nor with sinful speech, that is, obscenity, as the drinkers of this world speak." Then he said: "So Allah declared the wine of the Hereafter free of the filth and harm of the wine of this world." As-Sa'di said that once idle talk and sin are ruled out, "the third matter is established: that their talk over it is peace, good and pure, gladdening to souls and delightful to hearts."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayat did not deny only the bodily harm of the drink of Jannah; they denied the harm of the gathering as well: no raving, no insults, no obscenity. It shares nothing with the wine of this world but the name.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### One Thread Through the Evidence

The two ayat of al-Ma'idah explain why the wine of this world was forbidden: it covers the mind, it spoils what lies between people, and it turns them away from remembrance and prayer. The account of Anas shows how the believers received the prohibition: by pouring it out at once, with no questions and no second thoughts. The ayat of as-Saffat, al-Waqi'ah and at-Tur describe a drink from which every one of those harms has been removed: no headache, no loss of reason, no idle talk, no sin. And the hadith of Ibn 'Umar ties the two abodes together: whoever leaves it here will drink there, and whoever has fallen into it has repentance before them.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why was the wine poured out in the street, according to what Ibn Hajar reports from al-Muhallab?

2. What does Anas's remark, "They neither asked about it nor went back to it," tell us?

3. What harms does as-Sa'di identify in the two ayat of al-Ma'idah? Which of them is most visible in our lives today?

4. What does the ayah of at-Tur deny of the drinking gatherings of Jannah, and how did as-Sa'di understand "the third matter"?

5. What is the one thing you know clouds your mind or distracts you from prayer, yet you keep putting off giving up?

### Activity: Pour It Out Today

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.059.activity.pour-it-out-today-clear-mind-log" -->

Write down three things that cloud your mind or distract you from prayer: a habit, a device at a certain hour, a kind of content, or a gathering full of idle talk. Choose one of them and remove it that very day, in a practical way: delete it, take it out of the house, or change the route that leads you to it. Keep to the Companions' rule: no grace period, no bargaining, and no going back for seven days. Each evening, rate two things from 1 to 5: the clarity of your mind, and how present your heart was in prayer. On the seventh day, compare the numbers and write a paragraph: what did you find when you gave something up for Allah?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="3.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Allah gave us a wonderful gift inside our heads: our mind. With our mind we think, we learn, we pray, and we know right from wrong.

In this world there is a drink called "khamr," or wine. It takes away the mind of the person who drinks it, so they don't know what they are saying or doing. That is why Allah has forbidden it, and a Muslim never drinks it.[^3]

But in Jannah, Allah has made lots of lovely drinks ready for the people He loves. One of them is a drink the Qur'an calls by a name like that one, but it is something completely different: a beautiful white cup, delicious, that never hurts your head, never takes away your mind, and never makes anyone say a single unkind word.[^5][^6][^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Day the Drink Ran Through the Streets

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2464" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened in the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. The Companion Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, told us about it. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2464" audience="4-7" -->

In the city of Madinah, Anas ibn Malik was at the house of Abu Talhah, pouring drinks for the people there. Their drink was made from dates. It took away people's minds, but Allah had not forbidden it yet.

All of a sudden, they heard a loud voice out in the street. It was a man the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had sent, and he was calling out: "Listen! Wine has been forbidden!" That meant: Allah has said no to this drink.

What did they do? Did they say, "Let's wait a little"? No! Abu Talhah said to Anas, "Go out and empty it." So Anas went out and emptied the jars, and the drink ran through the streets of Madinah.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

They heard what Allah and His Messenger said, and they obeyed right away. And whoever gives something up for Allah, Allah gives them something even better in Jannah.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On a school trip, a classmate pulls out a brightly colored can and laughs: "This is a grown-up drink. Try some!" The writing on the can is in a language you can't read, and you have no idea what's inside. What do you do?

The Prophet's rule, peace and blessings be upon him, is clear: "Every intoxicant is khamr, and every intoxicant is forbidden."[^4] So any drink that takes away your mind is forbidden, whatever its name or color. And Allah has made clear that wine stirs up hostility between people and turns them away from remembering Allah and from prayer.[^3]

The drink of Jannah is completely different: a white cup, delicious, with no headache in it, that never takes away anyone's mind, and over which no one says anything empty or hurtful.[^5][^6][^7] It shares nothing with the wine of this world but the name.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2464" -->

- **`Fadikh (al-fadikh)`** — a drink made from unripe dates and left until it fermented; it was the wine of the people of Madinah at that time.[^2]
- **`The streets of Madinah (sikak al-Madinah)`** — the city's roads and lanes.[^1]
- **`They will suffer no headache (la yusadda'un)`** — their heads will not ache.[^6]
- **`No idle talk and no sin (la laghwun fiha wa la ta'thim)`** — no empty chatter and no sinful words, as Ibn Kathir explained.[^7]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Jars Emptied on the Spot

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2464" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by the Companion Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, and recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2464" audience="8-12" -->

Anas ibn Malik was in the house of Abu Talhah in Madinah, serving drinks to the people gathered there. Their drink in those days was *fadikh*, made from dates. It was their wine, and it had not yet been forbidden.

Then the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, ordered a caller to announce in the streets: "Listen! Wine has been forbidden." Abu Talhah said to Anas, "Go out and pour it away." So Anas went out and poured it away, until it was flowing through the streets of Madinah.[^1]

In another narration, Anas describes the same scene: a man came and said, "Has the news reached you?" They said, "What news?" He said, "Wine has been forbidden." And they said, "Pour out these jars, Anas." Then Anas said: "They neither asked about it nor went back to it after the man's report."[^2]

Some of the people there said, "Some people were killed while it was still in their stomachs." They meant their brothers who had died before it was forbidden. So Allah revealed: "There is no blame on those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they consumed before," meaning they carried no sin for what they had drunk before the prohibition.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Think about it: the jars were full and the gathering was in full swing, and still no one asked for more time. As Ibn Hajar reports, al-Muhallab said it was poured out in the street "to proclaim its rejection and to make its abandonment widely known."[^1] This is what loving Allah looks like: you leave the forbidden thing, all of it, in that very moment.

<!-- story:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

End-of-year party. Someone passes around a cup: "It's not real alcohol, it's just something light." Someone else offers a vape with something in it that "takes the edge off." The names keep changing, but the question stays the same: does it cloud the mind? And the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him, leaves no loophole: "Every intoxicant is khamr, and every intoxicant is forbidden."[^4]

As-Sa'di said of the two ayat of al-Ma'idah that *khamr* is "everything that *khamara* the mind, that is, covered it over with its intoxication," and that it "turns the heart away, and the body follows it, from the remembrance of Allah and from prayer."[^3]

Against that, the Qur'an describes the drink of Jannah: "No harm is in it, nor will they be robbed of their senses by it," and "a cup that brings no idle talk and no sin."[^5][^7] No headache, no lost mind, and no gathering where things get said that people regret the next day. A shared name, and an entirely different reality.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-37-45-47" -->

- **`Harm (ghawl)`** — damage that befalls the drinker; as-Sa'di includes in it the stealing away and loss of the mind, headache, and murkiness.[^5]
- **`Robbed of their senses (yunzafun)`** — losing their minds to intoxication, which is denied of the drink of Jannah.[^5]
- **`Sin (ta'thim)`** — sinful and obscene talk, the kind that happens at drinking gatherings in this world.[^7]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Day No One Asked for More Time

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2464" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, and recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. Any explanation added to it comes from the scholars.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2464" audience="13+" -->

A gathering in the house of Abu Talhah in Madinah. Anas ibn Malik is on his feet, serving the guests. The drink is *fadikh*, their wine in those days, and it has not yet been forbidden. Then the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, sends out a caller: "Listen! Wine has been forbidden." Abu Talhah says, "Go out and pour it away." Anas goes out and pours it away, and it flows through the streets of Madinah.[^1]

In another narration, Anas adds: "They neither asked about it nor went back to it after the man's report."[^2]

Then some of those present said, "Some people were killed while it was still in their stomachs," meaning those who had died before the prohibition. And Allah the Exalted revealed: "There is no blame on those who believe and do righteous deeds for what they consumed before."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice three things. First, the break was total and immediate: no grace period, no going back. Second, it was public. As Ibn Hajar reports, al-Muhallab said it was poured out in the street "to proclaim its rejection and to make its abandonment widely known." Third, their worry for their brothers who had died before the prohibition, and the ayah's merciful answer: there is no sin on anyone whom a ruling had not yet reached. Al-'Ayni said that their remark came "from overwhelming fear, and compassion."[^1]

Today you stand in the same place, only in a smaller room: a group of friends, a cup going around, and one second to decide.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why doesn't a new name or a new flavor change the ruling on something that intoxicates?

2. What is the difference between quitting something "gradually, when I'm ready" and quitting the way the household of Abu Talhah did?

3. Why was the wine poured out publicly in the street? When does taking a stand openly in front of your friends become a strength, for you and for them?

4. The ayah of at-Tur denies idle talk and sin in the drinking gatherings of Jannah. What do the gatherings of this world look like when intoxicants are served?

5. The hadith of Ibn 'Umar contains the condition "without having repented." What does that mean for someone who has slipped and wants to come back?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the account of Anas in the house of Abu Talhah from the two narrations of al-Bukhari, 2464 and 4617; explains the reasons for the prohibition in al-Ma'idah 5:90-91 with as-Sa'di's tafsir; shows how far "Every intoxicant is khamr" reaches; describes the drink of Jannah and what is denied of it from as-Saffat, al-Waqi'ah and at-Tur; sets out the views on "he will not drink it in the Hereafter" together with the condition of repentance; and begins a seven-day "Pour It Out Today" plan.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with two columns headed "The wine of this world" and "The drink of Jannah"; an activity sheet with a seven-day table and two columns for ratings from 1 to 5; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the commentaries of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni on al-Bukhari 2464 and 4617, as-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Ma'idah 5:90-91, and the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on at-Tur 52:23, and reviews the previous lesson on the four rivers so as not to repeat what was explained there about the river of wine.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What is the hardest habit you have ever tried to give up? Did you give it up all at once, or gradually?" Then read the first sentence of the introduction.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Bukhari 2464 and then 4617, and discuss the remarks of al-Muhallab and al-'Ayni and the ayah of al-Ma'idah 5:93 (7 minutes). Then read the two ayat of al-Ma'idah 5:90-91 with as-Sa'di's tafsir, and have learners fill in the column "The wine of this world" (5 minutes). Then the hadith of Ibn 'Umar and the scholars' views (3 minutes). Then the ayat of as-Saffat, al-Waqi'ah and at-Tur, with learners filling in the column "The drink of Jannah" opposite each harm (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 2 and 4, and compare gradualness in legislation, which is Allah's doing, with gradualness in obedience after the ruling is known, which the Companions did not practice. Stress that mentioning the wine of Jannah is no encouragement toward anything of this world.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes their list of three on their own, chooses one item, writes down the practical step they will take today before going to sleep, and fills in the first night's rating at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two reasons for the prohibition from al-Ma'idah," "Name two things denied of the drink of Jannah," and "What is your step tonight?" A week later, follow up with a general question, "What did you notice in your numbers?", without asking for the sheets.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, al-Bukhari 2464, al-Ma'idah 5:90 and at-Tur 52:23 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the other narrations of the account in al-Bukhari (5582 and 5583) and Muslim (1980) and compare their wording.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the drink of Jannah in terms borrowed from the drinking culture of this world, and do not show images or brand names of alcoholic drinks. Be mindful of anyone with a family member who drinks or struggles with addiction: no one is to be shamed, and the hadith is an invitation to repentance. Anyone who needs help with addiction should be referred, confidentially, to a trusted specialist. Do not rely in the lesson on circulating reports and stories whose authenticity has not been verified.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the mind is a gift a Muslim keeps safe, that Allah has forbidden wine in this world, and that the drink of Jannah is good and does no harm; retells the story of the caller and Anas in three sentences; and puts one thing in the "Rest Box" for three nights.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small decorated box for each child; a sheet with three squares for a sun or a cloud; crayons; cups of water and milk to hand out at the end; the du'a card; a short note for parents.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account calmly, without describing the drink or its taste, and writes a note to parents explaining the three-night activity and suggesting that they choose the item that suits their child.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver puts a hand on their head and asks: "What's in here that helps us pray and learn?" Then says: "Our mind is a gift, and Allah loves for us to keep it safe."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Account — 7 minutes:** Read the introduction and then the account. Ask the children to repeat the caller's words, and to act out emptying the jar with their hands when Anas is mentioned.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions, and stress that the Companions obeyed "right away," and that the drink of Jannah is something good and completely different.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child decorates their box and draws a sun on it, then takes it home with the three-square sheet. Hand out water or milk, and the children say "Bismillah" before drinking.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Notice whether the child remembers what the caller said and what Anas did. Then read the du'a one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the sun and the cloud are enough, without counting. Ask older children to tell the story to a brother or sister at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not explain to children how wine is made or describe its effects in detail; it is enough that it takes away the mind and that Allah has forbidden it. Do not mention the warning in the hadith to them. If a child mentions that a relative drinks, say gently, "We love them, and we ask Allah to guide them," and do not ask for details; inform the parents or the designated safeguarding lead if anything raises concern for the child's safety.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the rule "Every intoxicant is khamr" and applies it to a situation; narrates the account of Anas from both narrations; names three things denied of the drink of Jannah; and starts a five-day log after removing something that clouds their mind.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a log sheet for each student; a mushaf; two large cards: "The wine of this world" and "The drink of Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Ibn Kathir's tafsir of at-Tur 52:23 and al-Waqi'ah 56:19, and prepares three short scenarios for practice, including the school-trip scenario.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the school-trip scenario and ask students to write their response in a single line before the lesson, then keep it.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Account — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the account in both narrations, pausing at how quickly they responded and at Anas's remark that they did not go back to it (6 minutes). Then write under the two cards what is denied of the drink of Jannah (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Groups discuss the three scenarios using the rule "Every intoxicant is khamr." Then each student returns to their first response and revises it if they wish.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes their list, chooses one item, and writes down the step they will agree on with their parents today. The teacher sets a review day five days later.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The drink of Jannah has no..., no..., and no...." Then read the du'a. Five days later, anyone who wishes shares what changed.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, the first narration and two terms are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize the ayah of at-Tur 52:23 and explain it in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show cans or images of alcoholic drinks. Never mock a student whose family includes someone who does not keep this ruling. Teach students to tell a trusted adult if they are ever offered something unknown.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes how "Every intoxicant is khamr" covers new names; explains the three points in the account of Anas: immediacy, public declaration, and mercy toward those who died before the prohibition; compares the gatherings of this world with the gatherings of Jannah through the ayah of at-Tur; and starts a private seven-day log.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a private notebook that is not collected; a mushaf; cards for anonymous answers; a list of trusted local support services for anyone who needs them.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the commentaries of al-'Ayni and Ibn Hajar on al-Bukhari 2464, as-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Ma'idah 5:90-91, and the scholars' views on Muslim 2003 as al-Qari reports them, and prepares a calm referral to people of knowledge for detailed fiqh questions.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the party scenario, and have students write on anonymous cards: "What line usually gets said to persuade someone to try it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the account, with three pauses: the immediacy and the refusal to go back; then the public declaration and al-Muhallab's remark; then the revealed ayah and al-'Ayni's remark about compassion.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 12 minutes:** Read out the anonymous cards, and have students answer them in groups using the rule "Every intoxicant is khamr" and as-Sa'di's tafsir. Then discuss questions 4 and 5, stressing that the door of repentance is open.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes their list in their private notebook, chooses one item, and decides on the step they will take today.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: one reason for the prohibition, one quality of the drink of Jannah, and one line they could say to someone who offers them an intoxicant. Then read the du'a. A week later, follow up with a general question, without asking for the notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find written logging difficult may keep private voice notes instead. Ask advanced students to research briefly the meaning of "*khamara* the mind" and how the name connects to the ruling.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to confess a personal experience in front of the class. Do not get into contested fiqh details the students do not need; refer detailed questions to people of knowledge. Present the hadith of Ibn 'Umar together with its condition of repentance, and pass no judgment on any specific person. Anyone who appears to need help should be referred confidentially to their parents or a trusted specialist.
