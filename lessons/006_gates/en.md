# The Eight Gates of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives And Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite `حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا` ("until, when they reach it, and its gates are opened," az-Zumar 39:73), and explain that the opening of the gates is a scene of welcome and honor tied to the moment of arrival, not a barrier to fear.
- Narrate the hadith of the eight gates of Jannah and the gate of ar-Rayyan reserved for those who fast (Sahih al-Bukhari), and connect it to the hadith of the call from the gates of prayer, jihad, ar-Rayyan, and charity, and Abu Bakr as-Siddiq's question about being called from all the gates and the Prophet's hope for him.
- Explain the hadith of ablution and the opening of the eight gates for whoever perfects their ablution and testifies the two testimonies (Sahih Muslim), and show that a simple daily act may open more than one gate.
- Distinguish between the variety of gates of obedience (prayer, fasting, charity, jihad, filial devotion, knowledge, kinship ties) and reducing religiosity to a single gate or to an appearance others can see.
- Carry out the "Map of the Gates of Good" activity to identify a gate that is truly open in the learner's life and another gate that needs opening or strengthening.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.8" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The mention of Jannah's gates in the Qur'an and Sunnah adds to the believer's picture of it a meaning of honored entry. Jannah is not bliss reached by an unknown path; it is an abode with gates, keepers, calls, and greetings of peace. The gates in the texts are not merely architectural detail; they carry an educational meaning: every act of obedience has its own gate, and Allah may open several gates for His servant who combines good qualities with sincerity. This lesson joins the scene of the gates opening upon arrival, the hadith of the eight gates and the gate of ar-Rayyan, Abu Bakr as-Siddiq's question, and the hadith of ablution that opens all eight gates through one simple daily act.

<!-- unit:end -->

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-73" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Those Who Feared Their Lord Will Be Driven To Jannah In Groups"

> **وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ.** [الزمر: ٧٣][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who feared their Lord will be driven to Jannah in groups until, when they reach it, and its gates are opened, and its keepers say to them, 'Peace be upon you; you have been good, so enter it to abide therein eternally.'"** (az-Zumar 39:73)[^1]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

The commentators mention that the opening of the gates here is tied to the moment of arrival, not before it; the gates are not opened in advance to wait, but rather in honor at the moment of arrival, and the keepers of Jannah receive its people with the greeting and glad tiding `سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ` ("Peace be upon you; you have been good").[^7][^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

This scene teaches us that the gates of Jannah are not a barrier to fear, but a welcome to hope for; whoever feared his Lord in this world finds a gate opened for him with peace and welcome in the Hereafter, with no humiliating standing or waiting.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3257" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "In Jannah There Are Eight Gates, Among Them A Gate Called Ar-Rayyan"

> عَنْ سَهْلِ بْنِ سَعْدٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«فِي الْجَنَّةِ ثَمَانِيَةُ أَبْوَابٍ، فِيهَا بَابٌ يُسَمَّى الرَّيَّانَ، لَا يَدْخُلُهُ إِلَّا الصَّائِمُونَ».**[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Sahl ibn Sa'd, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"In Jannah there are eight gates, among them a gate called ar-Rayyan; none enters it except those who fast."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators mention that the eight gates of Jannah point to the variety of causes of entry, and that naming a gate ar-Rayyan is a special honor for those who fast, because of the patience with hunger and thirst in fasting that no one else shares.[^8][^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The gate of ar-Rayyan reminds us that for every act of worship, Allah may open a special gate for its people, and that fasting — despite its hardship — has with Allah this special merit that is not shared by whoever did not fast.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-1897" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whoever Spends A Pair In The Path Of Allah Is Called From The Gates Of Jannah"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«مَنْ أَنْفَقَ زَوْجَيْنِ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، نُودِيَ مِنْ أَبْوَابِ الْجَنَّةِ: يَا عَبْدَ اللَّهِ، هَذَا خَيْرٌ. فَمَنْ كَانَ مِنْ أَهْلِ الصَّلَاةِ دُعِيَ مِنْ بَابِ الصَّلَاةِ، وَمَنْ كَانَ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْجِهَادِ دُعِيَ مِنْ بَابِ الْجِهَادِ، وَمَنْ كَانَ مِنْ أَهْلِ الصِّيَامِ دُعِيَ مِنْ بَابِ الرَّيَّانِ، وَمَنْ كَانَ مِنْ أَهْلِ الصَّدَقَةِ دُعِيَ مِنْ بَابِ الصَّدَقَةِ».** قَالَ أَبُو بَكْرٍ الصِّدِّيقُ رضي الله عنه: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، مَا عَلَى مَنْ دُعِيَ مِنْ تِلْكَ الْأَبْوَابِ مِنْ ضَرُورَةٍ، فَهَلْ يُدْعَى أَحَدٌ مِنْ تِلْكَ الْأَبْوَابِ كُلِّهَا؟ قَالَ: **«نَعَمْ، وَأَرْجُو أَنْ تَكُونَ مِنْهُمْ».**[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Whoever spends a pair of anything in the path of Allah will be called from the gates of Jannah: 'O servant of Allah, this is good.' So whoever is of the people of prayer will be called from the gate of prayer, whoever is of the people of jihad will be called from the gate of jihad, whoever is of the people of fasting will be called from the gate of ar-Rayyan, and whoever is of the people of charity will be called from the gate of charity."** Abu Bakr as-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "O Messenger of Allah, there is no necessity upon whoever is called from any one of those gates — but will anyone be called from all of those gates together?" He said: **"Yes, and I hope that you will be among them."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators on the hadith explain that the call from each gate is an audible glad tiding specific to the people of that very act of obedience, and that Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him, was not a request to praise himself, but an inquiry about whether the gates could all be gathered for one who combined the acts of obedience — to which came the Prophet's hope, peace and blessings be upon him, not a definitive declaration of merit.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith teaches us that varied acts of obedience — prayer, jihad, fasting, and charity — each one is an independent path to Jannah, and that whoever Allah enables to combine several acts of obedience with sincerity, it is hoped that he will be called from all of their gates, just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped that for Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-13-23-24" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And The Angels Enter Upon Them From Every Gate"

> **جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا وَمَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّاتِهِمْ ۖ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ كُلِّ بَابٍ * سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ بِمَا صَبَرْتُمْ ۚ فَنِعْمَ عُقْبَى الدَّارِ.** [الرعد: ٢٣-٢٤][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Gardens of eternity, which they will enter, along with whoever were righteous among their fathers, their spouses, and their descendants — and the angels enter upon them from every gate. 'Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured; and excellent is the final home.'"** (ar-Ra'd 13:23-24)[^4]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

The commentators mention that the angels' entering upon the people of Jannah from every gate is a renewed honor not confined to a single moment, and that their greeting `سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ بِمَا صَبَرْتُمْ` ("Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured") links the bliss to the fruit of patience in this world, not to mere wishful thinking.[^7][^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

This scene shows that the gates of Jannah are not silent passageways, but places of recurring honor through which the angels enter upon its people bearing peace and praise for their patience; so every gate reminds its owner that his patience in this world was the cause of this honor.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-15-46" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Enter It In Peace, Secure"

> **ادْخُلُوهَا بِسَلَامٍ آمِنِينَ.** [الحجر: ٤٦][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Enter it in peace, secure."** (al-Hijr 15:46)[^5]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

This short ayah joins two meanings: peace from every harm, and complete security from any future fear; the commentators counted it among the greatest descriptions of how the people of Jannah enter its gates.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Entering through the gates of Jannah, then, is not an ordinary entry, but an entry of complete peace and full security untouched by anxiety, and this increases the believer's hope when he remembers that every gate of obedience today may be a path to that security.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-234a" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Eight Gates Of Jannah Are Opened For Him, Entering Through Whichever He Wishes"

> عَنْ عُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«مَنْ تَوَضَّأَ فَأَحْسَنَ الْوُضُوءَ، ثُمَّ قَالَ: أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ، فُتِحَتْ لَهُ أَبْوَابُ الْجَنَّةِ الثَّمَانِيَةُ، يَدْخُلُ مِنْ أَيِّهَا شَاءَ».**[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Whoever performs ablution and perfects the ablution, then says: 'I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger,' the eight gates of Jannah are opened for him; he enters through whichever of them he wishes."**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators on Sahih Muslim explain that this merit is tied to perfecting the ablution itself, not merely pouring the water, and that saying the two testimonies afterward is a direct cause for opening all eight gates, not a single gate.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith links a simple daily act of worship — ablution — to the opening of all eight gates of Jannah, not a single one, and in this is glad tidings that a small, well-performed deed may be a cause for opening more than one gate; it need not be a rare, magnificent deed.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between the scene of az-Zumar (39:73) in the opening of the gates upon arrival, and the scene of ar-Ra'd in the angels' entering from every gate?
2. Why were those who fast singled out with a gate named ar-Rayyan, distinct from the people of other acts of obedience?
3. What do we learn from the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him, to Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him, about all the gates together?
4. How does the hadith of ablution link a simple daily act to opening all eight gates?
5. Which gate of obedience do you feel Allah has opened for you more than others, and why?

### A Teaching Story: Yusuf and the Gate of Ar-Rayyan

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.006.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story, not a true story from history.**

Yusuf loved looking at gates everywhere he visited: the big school gate, the garden gate, and his grandfather's old house gate. On the first day of Ramadan, he saw his father opening the mosque gate to go in for Maghrib prayer, and he asked, "Father, why are all these gates important?" The father sat beside him after the prayer and said, "Gates open a path for us, my son. And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Jannah has eight gates, and among them is a gate called `ar-Rayyan` that only those who fast enter."[^2]

Yusuf's eyes widened with joy: "I can fast!" The father said, "You are young, so fast as much as you're able, even half a day; this is a beautiful practice Allah loves from you."

The next day, Yusuf tried to fast until noon. But at noon he felt very tired and thirsty, and he watched the clock wishing time would pass quickly. He thought for a moment: should he ask his mother for a secret sip of water? Then he remembered the gate of ar-Rayyan, and said to himself, "I'll be patient a little longer; this patience is the gate." So he completed his fast until noon as he had agreed with his parents, and he did not drink secretly even though no one would have known.

At iftar, Yusuf sat with his family, proud, and made a quiet du'a: `O Allah, make me among the people of Jannah`. His father told him, "No one can tell you exactly which gate Allah opened for you, but every sincere fast is a step toward the gate of ar-Rayyan."

A few days later, Yusuf went into his sister's room and slammed the door shut, upsetting her, and she cried a little. Yusuf remembered that the gates of Jannah receive their people with peace, so he went back and opened the door gently and said, "Peace be upon you, I'm sorry." His sister smiled, and the father said, "Whoever loves the gates of Jannah learns to come to people with peace, not with disturbance."

### A True Story: Abu Bakr's Question About All the Gates

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-1897" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-1897" audience="4-7" -->

This is a true story narrated by al-Bukhari, not an imagined one. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told his companions that every righteous deed has its own gate in Jannah: whoever prayed was called from the gate of prayer, whoever fasted was called from the gate of ar-Rayyan, and whoever gave charity was called from the gate of charity.[^3] Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, heard this and, since he loved doing good very much, asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: could anyone be called from all the gates together? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered him: "Yes, and I hope that you will be among them."[^3] This teaches us that whoever prays, fasts, gives charity, and does much good, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped for him many gates, not just one.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Jannah has eight gates, among them the gate of ar-Rayyan for those who fast, and other gates for those who pray, give charity, and strive in Allah's cause. ### A Teaching Story: Samir's Gates at School and the Mosque

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.006.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story, not a true story from history.**

At Samir's school, every activity had a different door: the library door, the lab door, and the sports club door. Samir loved the lab, and his sister Reem loved the library. At the mosque circle on Friday, Samir heard the teacher explaining the hadith of the eight gates of Jannah, that some people are called from the gate of prayer, some from the gate of ar-Rayyan, and some from the gate of charity.[^3] Samir raised his hand and asked, "Does this mean each person has a gate of good they love most?" The teacher said, "Yes, while still keeping all the obligatory duties."

Samir went home thinking: which gate of good suited him? He knew he didn't have much money to give in charity like some of his classmates did, and he felt a little sad, thinking the gate of charity was closed to him. The next day, he saw a new student standing confused in front of the classrooms, not knowing his class. Samir hesitated for a moment, because he was in a hurry for the lab class he loved, but he decided to be a little late and walk the student to his correct class, then helped him understand a difficult math problem afterward.

That evening, Samir told his father what happened, and the father said, "This too is charity, my son; every good deed is a charity, and you gave your time, not your money." Samir realized the gate of charity was not closed to him after all, but simply needed a different form besides money. During Ramadan, he practiced fasting too, and remembered the gate of ar-Rayyan whenever he felt thirsty, saying, "O Lord, make me among the sincere ones who fast."

Samir wrote in his notebook: "I learned that the gates of good are many, and that having little money doesn't close all the gates, for Allah may open a gate for me through my time or my kind word."

### A True Story: Abu Bakr and His Question About All the Gates

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-1897" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-1897" audience="8-12" -->

This is an authentic hadith narrated by al-Bukhari from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him.[^3] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told them that whoever spends two things of his wealth in the path of Allah is called from the gates of Jannah, and that whoever is of the people of prayer is called from the gate of prayer, whoever is of the people of jihad is called from the gate of jihad, whoever is of the people of fasting is called from the gate of ar-Rayyan, and whoever is of the people of charity is called from the gate of charity. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with him, asked: O Messenger of Allah, could anyone be called from all of these gates together? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Yes, and I hope that you will be among them." Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, was not content with a single gate, but aspired to gather all the acts of obedience, so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped that for him. This teaches the student to try to diversify good deeds: prayer, fasting, charity, and service — not to be content with just one.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-3257" -->

- **`ar-Rayyan`** — the name of one of the eight gates of Jannah, reserved for whoever is consistent in fasting.[^2]
- **`نُودِيَ مِنْ بَابِ كَذَا`** ("called from such-and-such gate") — that the angels call the doer of an act of obedience by the name of its gate on the Day of Judgment, in his honor.
- **`أَهْلُ الطَّاعَة`** ("the people of an act of obedience") — those who consistently practice a particular righteous deed until they become known for it.

### Activity: My Personal Map of the Gates of Good

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.8" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

True religiosity is not a single mold by which people compare themselves to one another; the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that every act of obedience has an independent gate in Jannah, and that whoever gathers varied acts of obedience with sincerity, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped that he would be called from all the gates, as happened with Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him. This opens a personal question: which gate has Allah truly opened for me in sincerity, not the gate I want people to see?

<!-- unit:end -->

### A Teaching Story: Nora and the Gate of Sincerity

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.006.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story, not a true story from history.**

Nora felt that religiosity among some of her friends had become a race of appearances: one talked about how much voluntary fasting she did, another about how fast she memorized Qur'an, and a third about attending every class publicly. Nora loved good, but she feared entering a race of image rather than a race of sincerity. In a lesson about the gates of Jannah, she heard the hadith of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, and heard the teacher say: "The gates are not badges to boast about, but doorways of mercy; ask yourself which gate brings you closer to Allah in sincerity, not which gate impresses people."[^3]

A week after the lesson, a sharp dispute broke out in her friends' message group, words grew harsh, and each side waited for someone to publicly take their side. Nora hesitated: should she post her opinion to show she was fair to everyone, or intervene quietly without announcing it? She remembered that the gates of Jannah are opened by sincere deeds, not by publicly declared positions, so she wrote a calm message to each side separately, mentioning something good about the other side, without posting anything publicly. The dispute did not end completely, but it eased, and no one in the group knew that Nora was the one who had mediated between them.

In her prostration that night she said: `O Allah, open for me a gate to You`. Over the days, she became more careful about turning every good deed into a public post; whenever she did something good, she asked herself: do I intend benefit or admiration? She did not abandon visible beneficial deeds, but she made sure to keep in her life one deed known only to Allah, hoping it might be her gate that combines sincerity with variety in obedience.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A True Story: Abu Bakr's Aspiration to All the Gates

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-1897" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-1897" audience="13+" -->

This is an authentic hadith narrated by al-Bukhari from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him.[^3] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told them that whoever spends in the path of Allah, whoever is of the people of prayer, whoever is of the people of jihad, whoever is of the people of fasting, and whoever is of the people of charity — each is called from the gate of his own act of obedience. Abu Bakr as-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with him, asked: could anyone be called from all of these gates together? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answered him: "Yes, and I hope that you will be among them." Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him, was not a request for self-praise, for he was known for the variety of his acts of obedience, the abundance of his spending, and the sincerity of his faith without excessive announcement, and so came the Prophet's hope for him, peace and blessings be upon him, not merely a passing wish. This places before the teenager a model of sincere spiritual aspiration: to strive to diversify one's acts of obedience not to be spoken of, but to be worthy of such a hope.

<!-- retelling:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. How do we distinguish between a gate of good Allah has truly opened for us and an image we want people to see?
2. Why was Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him, about all the gates not a request for self-praise?
3. Which gate do you feel you neglect despite being capable of it?
4. How did Nora's quiet decision change the course of the dispute between her friends?
5. What one good deed can you hide from people this week so it remains only between you and Allah?

### Activity: Map of My Gates to Allah

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the scene of the gates opening in the ayah of az-Zumar, narrates the hadith of the eight gates and the gate of ar-Rayyan and the hadith of Abu Bakr, explains the hadith of ablution, and carries out the Map of the Gates of Good activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A complete copy of az-Zumar 39:73, ar-Ra'd 13:23-24, and al-Hijr 15:46; the texts of Sahih al-Bukhari 3257 and 1897 and Sahih Muslim 234a; a model of the gates map; paper and pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews these texts in their sources, reviews what Ibn al-Qayyim, Abu Nu'aym, and al-Ashqar mention regarding the number and description of Jannah's gates to be ready for discussion, and prepares a neutral example of varied voluntary deeds in the local community.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If every righteous deed you did had its own gate in Jannah, which gate would you wish to be called from?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Groups read the three ayat and the three hadith in full, and each group draws out: the meaning of the gates opening upon arrival, the number of gates and the names of some of them, and the significance of Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains the difference between diversifying acts of obedience and being content with a single one, and discusses how a simple deed like ablution opens several gates.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners individually carry out the Map of the Gates of Good activity, then whoever wishes shares one example with the group without being required to disclose personal details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two gates of Jannah mentioned in the lesson, and explain what Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him, teaches you." The teacher closes by reading the du'a, noting it is his own educational composition.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a beginner a sheet with the texts and key words highlighted, and assign an advanced learner to compare the hadith of ablution with the hadith of the call from the gates in terms of the mechanism of opening.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not turn the discussion of the gates into a classification of people or a judgment on anyone's religiosity, do not invent a visual description of the gates' form without evidence, and present the Prophet's hope for Abu Bakr, peace and blessings be upon him, as encouragement, not a definitive declaration of merit.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="30" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child states the number of gates of Jannah, repeats the name of the gate of ar-Rayyan, and participates in the gate-card activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large paper and colors to draw a gate; a du'a card in clear handwriting; a symbolic picture of a mosque gate if possible.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher practices narrating Yusuf's story simply, keeping to one gentle general sentence about patience with thirst without frightening exaggeration.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Have you seen a beautiful gate somewhere you love? What was behind it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The teacher narrates Yusuf's story and the true story of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, simply, mentioning the gate of ar-Rayyan clearly.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 6 minutes:** The teacher explains that Jannah has eight gates, and that fasting opens a special gate called ar-Rayyan.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 9 minutes:** The children draw and color the gate of ar-Rayyan card, and each child chooses one small good deed to do today.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What is the name of the gate for those who fast?" Then read the du'a slowly so the children can repeat what they can.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Allow pointing to the drawing instead of a full answer, and give an older child a chance to explain a full sentence about Abu Bakr's question, may Allah be pleased with him.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not encourage the child to complete a full fast that is hard on them; remind them that partial fasting is an acceptable practice, and do not compare children against each other in how quickly they fast or complete it.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="45" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student summarizes the hadith of the eight gates of Jannah and the hadith of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, explains the three terms, and applies the personal Map of the Gates of Good activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the hadiths; the three term cards; a notebook for the map activity; pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares a completed example of the gates map to display as a model before students begin their own.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "If your school had a special gate for every activity, which gate would you love to be called through by name?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Students read the hadith of the eight gates and the hadith of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, in full, and underline the names of the gates mentioned.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher explains the three terms and connects them to Samir's story and the gate of charity without money.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 14 minutes:** Students carry out the Map of the Gates of Good activity, and each shares one example with a classmate without sensitive details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences summarizing a gate of good they want to open this week, then the teacher reads the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a struggling student a ready-made list of gates of good to choose from, and assign an advanced student to explain the difference between being called from a single gate and being called from all the gates.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not allow mocking a student who has no money for charity, affirm that charity of time and help is legally recognized, and do not use the activity to compare students' commitment.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="55" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 55 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the meaning of the variety of Jannah's gates, distinguishes between sincerity and showing off in righteous deeds, and carries out the Map of My Gates to Allah activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A file of the six pieces of evidence; a personal map template; exit slips.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral examples of displaying worship on social media without singling out any specific person negatively.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Have you ever felt that your good deed became more of a comparison with others than an act of worship?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Three groups read the ayah of az-Zumar, the hadith of the eight gates, and the hadith of Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, and each group draws out the practical significance of each text.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** The teacher explains the difference between Abu Bakr's sincere aspiration, may Allah be pleased with him, and showy religiosity, and discusses Nora's story.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Students begin the Map of My Gates to Allah activity, and write an opening paragraph about a gate they want to open sincerely.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** The student writes a three-line response connecting the opening question to the meaning of sincerity in the gates of good, then the teacher reads the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a struggling student three ready-made gates to choose from, and assign an advanced student to discuss how to balance showing a good deed to encourage others with hiding another deed for sincerity.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not use the lesson to accuse anyone specifically of showing off, and do not require anyone to disclose a private deed they wish to keep hidden in front of the class.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
