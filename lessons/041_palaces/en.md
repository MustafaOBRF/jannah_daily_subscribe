# The Palaces and Dwellings of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate, in sequence, the hadith of the palace of Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, in Jannah (al-Bukhari 3242 and Muslim 2395): the Prophet's dream, peace and blessings be upon him; the question "Whose palace is this?"; how he remembered Umar's protective jealousy and turned away; and then Umar's tears and his words, "Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned, O Messenger of Allah?"
- Affirm only what authentic texts establish about the palaces of Jannah: a palace of gold (at-Tirmidhi 3688); a house for Khadijah, may Allah be pleased with her, made of hollow pearl, with no clamor in it and no toil (al-Bukhari 3820); and `pleasant dwellings in the Gardens of Eternity` (at-Tawbah 9:72).
- Explain that these palaces are real, already prepared, and assigned to their owners by name, and that Allah's good pleasure is greater than all of them.
- Draw two courtesies from the hadith: being mindful of the owner's feelings before entering his place, and humility when one is honored.
- Carry out the activity "Remember the Owner Before You Enter": asking a member of one's household what they would like respected about their space or privacy, keeping to that for a set period, and then hearing how it felt to them.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for pleasant dwellings in Jannah and for homes in this world filled with tranquility.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Many people dream of a spacious home, and anyone who has ever lived in one knows that even a beautiful house can fill up with noise, exhaustion and quarreling. Revelation tells us that the people of Jannah have palaces and dwellings that are real, created already, assigned to their owners by name, and free of everything that clouds the homes of this world.

Earlier lessons dealt with the deeds by which homes in Jannah are built, and with how its people will recognize their own places there. This lesson turns to the palaces themselves: what has actually been established about them? It is built on an authentic hadith in which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw a palace belonging to Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him. In one brief scene it teaches us that the palace is real, that it has an owner, and that courtesy toward its owner does not lapse, even in Jannah.

In describing them we confine ourselves to what is authentic: a palace of gold, a house of hollow pearl with no clamor and no toil, and pleasant dwellings in the Gardens of Eternity. The books on the description of Jannah contain many reports about its palaces and their private chambers, and some of these were never established as the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. We build nothing on them. What is established is proof enough, and longing enough.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3242" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whose Palace Is This?"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: بَيْنَا نَحْنُ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ إِذْ قَالَ: **«بَيْنَا أَنَا نَائِمٌ رَأَيْتُنِي فِي الْجَنَّةِ، فَإِذَا امْرَأَةٌ تَتَوَضَّأُ إِلَى جَانِبِ قَصْرٍ، فَقُلْتُ: لِمَنْ هَذَا الْقَصْرُ؟ فَقَالُوا: لِعُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ، فَذَكَرْتُ غَيْرَتَهُ، فَوَلَّيْتُ مُدْبِرًا»**. فَبَكَى عُمَرُ وَقَالَ: **أَعَلَيْكَ أَغَارُ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟**[^1]
>
> وَفِي رِوَايَةِ مُسْلِمٍ: قَالَ أَبُو هُرَيْرَةَ: **فَبَكَى عُمَرُ وَنَحْنُ جَمِيعًا فِي ذَلِكَ الْمَجْلِسِ مَعَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ، ثُمَّ قَالَ عُمَرُ: بِأَبِي أَنْتَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَعَلَيْكَ أَغَارُ؟**[^1]
>
> وَعَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: **«رَأَيْتُنِي دَخَلْتُ الْجَنَّةَ، فَإِذَا أَنَا بِالرُّمَيْصَاءِ امْرَأَةِ أَبِي طَلْحَةَ، وَسَمِعْتُ خَشَفَةً، فَقُلْتُ: مَنْ هَذَا؟ فَقَالَ: هَذَا بِلَالٌ. وَرَأَيْتُ قَصْرًا بِفِنَائِهِ جَارِيَةٌ، فَقُلْتُ: لِمَنْ هَذَا؟ فَقَالَ: لِعُمَرَ. فَأَرَدْتُ أَنْ أَدْخُلَهُ فَأَنْظُرَ إِلَيْهِ، فَذَكَرْتُ غَيْرَتَكَ»**. فَقَالَ عُمَرُ: **بِأُمِّي وَأَبِي يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَعَلَيْكَ أَغَارُ؟**[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, said: While we were sitting with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, he said: **"While I was asleep, I saw myself in Jannah, and there was a woman making wudu' beside a palace. I asked, 'Whose palace is this?' They said, 'It belongs to Umar ibn al-Khattab.' Then I remembered his protective jealousy, and I turned and walked away."** Umar wept and said: **"Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned, O Messenger of Allah?"**[^1]
>
> In the narration of Muslim, Abu Hurayrah said: **"Umar wept, while all of us were there in that gathering with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him. Then Umar said, 'May my father be ransomed for you, O Messenger of Allah! Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned?'"**[^1]
>
> Jabir ibn Abd Allah, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"I saw myself enter Jannah, and there before me was al-Rumaysa', the wife of Abu Talhah. I heard the soft sound of footsteps and asked, 'Who is this?' He said, 'This is Bilal.' And I saw a palace with a young woman in its courtyard, and I asked, 'Whose is this?' He said, 'It belongs to Umar.' I wanted to go in and look around it, but then I remembered your protective jealousy."** Umar said: **"May my mother and my father be ransomed for you, O Messenger of Allah! Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned?"**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-ghayrah*, protective jealousy, is a man's sense of honor and his care to shield his family and those under his protection from the gaze of others and from anyone entering upon them.[^9] This was a dream, and the dreams of prophets are true. At-Tirmidhi relates that Ibn Abbas said, "The dreams of prophets are revelation"; this is why the Companions passed it on as information about Jannah and as a merit of Umar.[^3] Jabir's narration explains why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, turned away: he wanted to go into the palace and look around it, but he remembered Umar's protective jealousy, and so he did not enter.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith teaches three truths about the palaces of Jannah: they exist now; they belong to their owners by name; and they have courtyards and people dwelling in them. It also teaches two courtesies. A noble Prophet takes his Companion's feelings into account before entering his palace. And the Companion, given glad tidings in front of everyone, weeps rather than swelling with pride, and declares that jealousy has no place at all where the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, is concerned.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### A Palace of Gold

> عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«دَخَلْتُ الْجَنَّةَ فَإِذَا أَنَا بِقَصْرٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ، فَقُلْتُ: لِمَنْ هَذَا الْقَصْرُ؟ قَالُوا: لِشَابٍّ مِنْ قُرَيْشٍ، فَظَنَنْتُ أَنِّي أَنَا هُوَ، فَقُلْتُ: وَمَنْ هُوَ؟ فَقَالُوا: عُمَرُ بْنُ الْخَطَّابِ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"I entered Jannah, and there before me was a palace of gold. I asked, 'Whose palace is this?' They said, 'It belongs to a young man of Quraysh.' I thought it was I, so I asked, 'And who is he?' They said, 'Umar ibn al-Khattab.'"**[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

This narration names the palace's material, gold, and shows that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, at first thought the palace was his own before he was told it belonged to Umar. We add nothing to what has been reported, neither shape nor size: what is with Allah is far too great to be pictured in the details of our own imagining.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3820" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### A House of Hollow Pearl, with No Clamor and No Toil

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: أَتَى جِبْرِيلُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ فَقَالَ: **يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، هَذِهِ خَدِيجَةُ قَدْ أَتَتْ مَعَهَا إِنَاءٌ فِيهِ إِدَامٌ أَوْ طَعَامٌ أَوْ شَرَابٌ، فَإِذَا هِيَ أَتَتْكَ فَاقْرَأْ عَلَيْهَا السَّلَامَ مِنْ رَبِّهَا وَمِنِّي، وَبَشِّرْهَا بِبَيْتٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِنْ قَصَبٍ، لَا صَخَبَ فِيهِ وَلَا نَصَبَ**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, said: Jibril came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: **"O Messenger of Allah, here is Khadijah, on her way to you with a vessel holding food to go with bread, or food, or drink. When she reaches you, convey to her greetings of peace from her Lord and from me, and give her the glad tidings of a house in Jannah made of *qasab*, in which there is no clamor and no toil."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "*Qasab* here means hollow tubes of pearl."[^5] *Sakhab* is uproar and a clamor of voices, and *nasab* is weariness and toil.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith rules out, for Khadijah's house, the two things that most often spoil a home: noise and exhaustion. The glad tidings reached her while she was on her way to bring food to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and perhaps there is a fitting match here with what she was doing: a house with no toil for a woman who wore herself out for the comfort of another. And Allah knows best.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-72" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### Pleasant Dwellings in the Gardens of Eternity

> **{وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا وَمَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً فِي جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ ۚ وَرِضْوَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ}** [التوبة: ٧٢][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Allah has promised the believing men and the believing women gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will abide forever, and pleasant dwellings in the Gardens of Eternity. And the good pleasure of Allah is greater still. That is the supreme triumph."** (at-Tawbah 9:72)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, explains `pleasant dwellings` as "beautifully built, and a good place to settle."[^7] As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, says that they "have been adorned and made beautiful and prepared for Allah's God-fearing servants," that the rivers flow "beneath their palaces, their homes and their trees," and that Allah's good pleasure is "greater than the bliss of the gardens."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah ends its description of the dwellings by declaring that Allah's good pleasure is greater. The palace is not the final goal of our seeking; it is one of the signs of Allah's being pleased with His servant.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### Activity: Remember the Owner Before You Enter

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.041.activity.remember-the-owner-before-entering" -->

Ask each person in your home, or a colleague you share a workspace with, one question: "What would you like me to ask permission for first: your room, your things, your phone, or your time to rest?" Write down one specific rule of respect for each person, and keep to it for seven days. On the seventh day, ask each of them, "Did you notice a difference?" Then write two lines: Which rule was hardest for you to keep? ### An Authentic Hadith: Umar's Palace in Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3242" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3242" audience="4-7" -->

The Companions were sitting with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He told them: "I fell asleep, and I saw myself in Jannah." And when a prophet sees something in a dream, it is true.[^3]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw a beautiful palace, and next to it a woman was making wudu'. He asked, "Whose palace is this?" They said, "It belongs to Umar ibn al-Khattab."[^1]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to go into the palace and look around.[^2] But then he remembered something: Umar loves to protect his family and his home. So what did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, do? He didn't go in. He turned back.[^1]

Umar was sitting there, listening. He cried, and he said, "Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned, O Messenger of Allah?"[^1] That means: I could never mind you coming in, O Messenger of Allah!

Do you see? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not go into his friend's home without permission, not even in Jannah. And Umar did not show off when he heard he had a palace. He cried, and he stayed humble.

### An Authentic Hadith: "Whose Palace Is This?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3242" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3242" audience="8-12" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, says: We were sitting with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, when he said, "While I was asleep, I saw myself in Jannah." The dreams of prophets are true, so what he saw was real news about Jannah.[^1][^3]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw a palace, and beside it a woman was making wudu'. He asked, "Whose palace is this?" They said, "It belongs to Umar ibn al-Khattab."[^1] In the narration of Anas, the palace was made of gold, and at first the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was told, "It belongs to a young man of Quraysh," so he thought it was his own. Then he was told: "Umar ibn al-Khattab."[^3]

Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to go into the palace and look around it.[^2] This was the moment of decision. The palace was beautiful, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was the most honored of all people in Allah's sight. But he remembered Umar's protective jealousy and how much he cared about protecting his family. So he turned away and did not go in.[^1]

Umar was right there in the gathering with the other Companions. He wept, and then he said, "May my father be ransomed for you, O Messenger of Allah! Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned?"[^1] In other words: I could never feel that way about you, O Messenger of Allah.

This hadith teaches us three things. The palaces of Jannah are real, and they belong to their owners by name. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, respected his Companion's feelings, even in a place where there is nothing to fear. And Umar did not boast about his palace in front of everyone; he wept and stayed humble.

### Bedtime Du'a

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ لَنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً وَقُصُورًا لَا صَخَبَ فِيهَا وَلَا نَصَبَ، وَاجْعَلْ بُيُوتَنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا بُيُوتَ سَكِينَةٍ وَأَدَبٍ.
>
> *"O Allah, grant us in Your Jannah pleasant dwellings and palaces with no clamor and no toil, and make our homes in this world homes of tranquility and good manners."*

What it means: O Allah, grant us in Your Jannah the pleasant dwellings You have promised the believers, and palaces with no uproar and no weariness, and make our homes today calm places where we respect one another.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live at a time when it has become easy to walk right into other people's private lives: a glance at a friend's screen, a screenshot of a private chat dropped into a group, opening a brother's door without knocking. And so often the excuse is, "Relax, we're all friends here." At the same time, we love being honored, and we may show off a little when someone praises us in public. An authentic hadith about the palaces of Jannah brings both of these together: a Prophet stopping at the door of a palace that is not his, and a Companion who weeps when he is given the news of his palace in front of everyone. Before the story, keep this in mind: the palaces of Jannah are real. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described them in terms of gold and pearl,[^3][^4][^5] and Allah called them `pleasant dwellings`, then added: `And the good pleasure of Allah is greater still`.[^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-3242" -->

- **`Protective jealousy (al-ghayrah)`** — a man's sense of honor and his care to shield his family from the gaze of others and from anyone entering upon them; in its proper place, it is a praiseworthy trait.[^9]
- **`I turned and walked away (fa-wallaytu mudbiran)`** — I turned back and left without going in.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-3820" -->

- **`Qasab`** — in this hadith, hollow tubes of pearl, as Ibn al-Qayyim explained.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: A Prophet at a Palace Door, and a Companion in Tears

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3242" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3242" audience="13+" -->

The setting is a gathering in Madinah. Abu Hurayrah and a number of other Companions are sitting with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and Umar ibn al-Khattab is among them. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, says, "While I was asleep, I saw myself in Jannah."[^1] And the dreams of prophets are true.[^3]

He sees a palace, with a woman making wudu' beside it, and asks, "Whose palace is this?" He is told, "It belongs to Umar ibn al-Khattab."[^1] In the narration of Anas, the palace is made of gold, and at first he is told, "It belongs to a young man of Quraysh," so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, thinks it is his own, until Umar is named.[^3]

Then comes the moment the whole hadith turns on. Jabir, in his narration, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to go into the palace and look around it.[^2] No one is stopping him. He is the master of the children of Adam, and this is Jannah. But he "remembered Umar's protective jealousy," and he turned and walked away.[^1] He did not treat his closeness to Umar as a pass to walk in. Instead he took the owner's feelings into account, and then he told him about it in front of everyone.

Now look at Umar, the strong man people stood in awe of. He hears, in front of everyone, that he has a palace in Jannah, and the hadith does not record a single word of pride from him. Abu Hurayrah says, "Umar wept, while all of us were there in that gathering," and then Umar said, "May my father be ransomed for you, O Messenger of Allah! Would I ever feel jealous where you are concerned?"[^1]

There is a lesson here for you in both of these moments. First: privacy is not something to shrug off with "relax, it's fine." If the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, remembered his Companion's feelings at the door of a palace in Jannah, then you have even more reason to remember the owner of the phone before you look, the owner of the conversation before you forward it, and the owner of the room before you walk in. Second: when you are praised in front of people, let the praise draw you toward humility, not toward showing off.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why is it so striking that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, turned away from the palace, when no one was stopping him?
2. How is praiseworthy protective jealousy different from control and domination?
3. What does Umar's weeping in front of everyone reveal about the way he received honor?
4. What does "walking in without permission" look like in digital life? Which forms of it do you run into most often?
5. Why, after describing the dwellings, did Allah say: `And the good pleasure of Allah is greater still`?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Remember the Owner Before You Enter

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.041.activity.remember-the-owner-before-entering" -->

Privately ask a family member or a close friend: "What boundary would you like me to respect: your room, your things, your phone, or our private conversations?" Agree together on one clear rule, for example: "I won't send a screenshot of our chat to anyone without your permission." Keep to it for seven days, and in a private note, record every moment you came close to crossing it when no one was watching. At the end of the week, ask them how it felt, and write a paragraph: What was hard? And why do good manners matter when no one is watching you?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.041.dua.pleasant-dwellings-without-noise-or-toil" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ لَنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً وَقُصُورًا لَا صَخَبَ فِيهَا وَلَا نَصَبَ، وَاجْعَلْ بُيُوتَنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا بُيُوتَ سَكِينَةٍ وَأَدَبٍ.

> *"O Allah, grant us in Your Jannah pleasant dwellings and palaces with no clamor and no toil, and make our homes in this world homes of tranquility and good manners."*

What it means: O Allah, grant us the pleasant dwellings You have promised Your servants, and palaces where rest is complete, with no noise and no weariness; and make our homes today places of tranquility, where each of us respects the rights and the privacy of everyone else.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of Umar's palace in its three narrations (Abu Hurayrah, Jabir and Anas) and draws from it three truths about the palaces of Jannah and two courtesies; explains the meaning of *al-ghayrah*, of "no clamor in it and no toil," and of "tubes of pearl"; connects the description of pleasant dwellings in at-Tawbah 9:72 with the statement that Allah's good pleasure is greater; and sets written rules of respect with members of their household or colleagues, then reviews them after a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "What is established about the palaces," "Courtesy toward the owner of a place," and "Courtesy of the one who is honored"; the activity sheet with a table of people and rules; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 3242 and 3679, Muslim 2394 and 2395, at-Tirmidhi 3688 together with its grading, al-Bukhari 3820, the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on at-Tawbah 9:72, and Chapter 36 of *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher should note that the detailed reports in the books on the description of Jannah about other palaces (such as the palace of pearl for Ibrahim, peace be upon him) are disputed as to whether they go back to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or stop at a Companion, so they are not used in this lesson. The teacher should also bear in mind that the lessons "The Deeds That Build Homes in Jannah" and "Knowing One's Place in Jannah" have already been covered, and should not repeat their material.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Describe a home you would love to live in. Then name one thing that spoils even the most beautiful homes in this world." Write the answers up, then ask: What do we know for certain about the palaces of Jannah?

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Abu Hurayrah, then the addition in Muslim, then the narration of Jabir, and ask: What does each narration add? (8 minutes). Then read the hadith of Anas, pointing out at-Tirmidhi's grading (3 minutes). Then the hadith of Khadijah, with Ibn al-Qayyim's comment and the meaning of *sakhab* and *nasab* (4 minutes). Then the ayah of at-Tawbah with the two tafsirs, filling in the first column (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 3 and 4, steering the discussion toward two points: taking people's feelings into account is a Prophetic courtesy that does not fall away because of love and closeness, and humility on receiving glad tidings is a sign of sincerity. Fill in the second and third columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes the names of the people they will ask (3 minutes) and words the question so that it does not sound like an accusation (4 minutes). Then pairs share the rule they expect to find hardest and how they will remember it at the right moment (8 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two proofs describing the palaces of Jannah, and one courtesy from the hadith of Umar's palace that you will practice this week." Then read the ayah of at-Tawbah one last time, and agree to review the activity in a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the hadith of Abu Hurayrah and the ayah of at-Tawbah. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations of the hadith of Umar's palace and compare their wording, and to review Chapter 36 of *Hadi al-Arwah* along with the editor's notes on the grading of its reports.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not go into details about the woman the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw, or into descriptions that have not been reported. Do not declare that any specific person has a particular palace except those named by revelation. Present protective jealousy as a praiseworthy trait in its proper place, never as an excuse for domination, suspicion or violence within the family. Do not ask anyone to reveal private matters about their home during the discussion. Speak of Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, and of the Mothers of the Believers with due respect.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that there are real palaces in Jannah, including a palace of gold and a house of pearl; tells how the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw Umar's palace and did not go in because he remembered Umar's feelings; and knocks on the door and waits for permission before going into someone's room.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A picture of a beautiful palace with no writing and no people in it; a pearl or a shiny bead; large sheets of paper and coloring pencils; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver closes the door of a nearby room so the child can practice knocking and waiting. The caregiver also practices telling the report without adding any words or feelings that were not reported, and without describing the woman beyond what is in the hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Show the picture of the palace and the pearl, and ask: "If this were your friend's palace, would you go in without asking?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the explanation and then the report, pausing at the question "What did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, do?", and repeats with the child: "He remembered Umar, so he didn't go in."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 1, 3 and 4, and explain protective jealousy in one simple sentence: "He loves to protect his family and his home."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child asks a family member the activity question, then draws the door and the three stars, and practices knocking and waiting at the closed door. The coloring is finished over the next three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Notice whether the child knocks on the door and waits. Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one sentence is enough, "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, didn't go into his friend's home without permission," along with a single practice. Ask older children to tell the story to a brother or sister in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions, and do not act them out in role-play; act out the situation with the door alone. Do not present details about the palace that have not been reported as if they were certain. Teach the child that asking permission never stops them from calling an adult for help when they are frightened or in danger.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith in order, along with the additions from Jabir and Anas; explains the terms *al-ghayrah* and "no clamor in it and no toil"; names three established descriptions of the dwellings of Jannah; and sets a rule about asking permission with a family member and keeps it for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a five-day chart; paper for the rule; a mushaf; a picture of an ordinary house and a picture of a palace, with no writing.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes at-Tawbah 9:72 on the board and prepares examples of permission situations from the students' own lives (a brother's room, a friend's backpack, a parent's phone or computer).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask the students to describe their dream house in three words, then ask: "What can make a house annoying to live in, even if it's big?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah and the explanation (3 minutes), then the story, pausing at "the moment of decision" (6 minutes), then explain the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 2, 3 and 4. Make clear that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was the most honored of all people and could have gone in, but he chose to be considerate of his Companion, and that Umar met the honor with humility.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes their two questions and chooses the person, drafts a trial rule to adjust after hearing the answer at home, and prepares the five-day chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes: "Why didn't the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, go into the palace? And what rule will I keep?" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, offer ready-made rules to choose from. Ask advanced students to memorize at-Tawbah 9:72 and explain why it ends with Allah's good pleasure.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not read families' responses aloud. Make clear that asking permission does not mean hiding anything that is hurting the student or someone else, and that they should tell a trusted adult if they feel in danger. Do not draw the Companions or act out their roles.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the stances of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and of Umar in the hadith; distinguishes praiseworthy protective jealousy from control; identifies forms of digital privacy violation; explains why Allah's good pleasure is greater than the dwellings; and keeps an agreed rule for a week, privately recording the hard moments.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; cards for anonymous questions; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares three neutral scenarios: a screenshot of a private chat, looking through a friend's phone, and walking into a brother's room. The teacher also prepares for the question: "Isn't jealousy the cause of a lot of problems?"

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When has someone told you, 'Relax, we're all friends here,' and you felt that something was not okay?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at three moments: the question "Whose palace is this?", the turning away, and Umar's tears. Then read the terms and the ayah of at-Tawbah.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** In groups, discuss the three scenarios in light of questions 1 and 4, then question 2. The teacher guides students to see that praiseworthy protective jealousy means protection and respect, and that control, spying and coercion are no part of it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student identifies the person and the proposed rule, writes how they will raise the question gently, and anticipates one situation that might tempt them to break the rule.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "What did Umar's tears teach me about receiving praise?" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about how the experience went, without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short study of the narrations of the hadith of Umar's palace and compare their wording.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask anyone to reveal their conversations or their family's private matters. Make clear that respecting privacy never means keeping quiet about harm, blackmail or danger, and that in such cases telling a trusted adult is a duty. Do not show pictures of the Companions or act out their roles.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
