# Security, Everlasting Life, and the Slaughter of Death

## Lesson Objectives

- Explain that the security enjoyed by the people of Jannah is complete: no death, sickness, old age, or end to its delight.
- Read the central texts in full and distinguish revelation from scholarly interpretation and educational explanation.
- Explain the slaughter of death as it appears in the authentic hadith, without forced representation or denial of the unseen.
- Understand key expressions such as `مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ` (a secure dwelling), `نَصَبٌ` (weariness), `يَشْرَئِبُّونَ` (they crane their necks), and `كَبْشٌ أَمْلَحُ` (a black-and-white ram).
- Allow belief in everlasting life to produce righteous action and hope in Allah's mercy—not self-righteousness or a guarantee of Jannah for any particular person.

## Academic Section for Adults

### Introduction

Every pleasure in this world is accompanied by some fear of sickness, loss, or an ending. The delight of Jannah, however, is not a beautiful moment threatened with disappearance; it is a home of lasting security. This lesson brings together three inseparable meanings: **security from every affliction, everlasting life without expulsion, and the end of death itself**.

The image of death being slaughtered should not be taken apart from its context. The Qur'an first establishes that the people of Jannah are in a secure dwelling, that they will not taste death there, and that they will never be removed from it. The authentic hadith then describes the scene of the final announcement: `خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ`—“Everlasting life, with no more death.”

### Central Meanings

1. **Complete security:** The people of Jannah are secure from death, sickness, old age, sorrow, exhaustion, and expulsion from Jannah.
2. **True everlasting life:** Their continuance is not an extremely long period that eventually ends; it is life after which there is no death and delight that never stops.
3. **The slaughter of death is established unseen knowledge:** The hadith reports that death will be brought in the form of a black-and-white ram and slaughtered while both groups see it. We affirm the report as it came and entrust the manner of this unseen event to Allah.
4. **An increase in joy:** The last possibility of loss disappears for the people of Jannah, so their joy increases still further.
5. **An effect on faith in this world:** A person certain that the everlasting home is coming will not place a passing pleasure ahead of obedience to Allah, nor allow fear of death to drive them to despair.

### First Qur'anic Text: A Secure Dwelling and Deliverance From Death

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-44-51-57" kind="quran" -->

#### Complete Arabic Text

> **إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ ۝ فِي جَنَّاتٍ وَعُيُونٍ ۝ يَلْبَسُونَ مِنْ سُنْدُسٍ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ ۝ كَذَٰلِكَ وَزَوَّجْنَاهُمْ بِحُورٍ عِينٍ ۝ يَدْعُونَ فِيهَا بِكُلِّ فَاكِهَةٍ آمِنِينَ ۝ لَا يَذُوقُونَ فِيهَا الْمَوْتَ إِلَّا الْمَوْتَةَ الْأُولَىٰ وَوَقَاهُمْ عَذَابَ الْجَحِيمِ ۝ فَضْلًا مِنْ رَبِّكَ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ.** [الدخان: ٥١-٥٧][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

#### English Translation

> **Indeed, the people of taqwa will be in a secure dwelling, amid gardens and springs, wearing fine silk and rich brocade, facing one another. So it will be; and We will join them with companions of beautiful, wide eyes. There they will call for every kind of fruit, in complete security. They will not taste death there beyond the first death, and He will protect them from the punishment of the Blaze—as a bounty from your Lord. That is the magnificent triumph.** [Al-Dukhan 44:51–57][^1]

#### Source and Status

This is the Qur'an and is established with certainty. The connected context from verses 51 through 57 is included to show that security is not limited to the absence of death; it forms part of complete delight, protection, and Allah's bounty. The English wording is a meaning-based project translation for review.

#### Tafsir

Al-Sa'di explained `مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ`—“a secure dwelling”—as a station combining every quality of security, safe from death, expulsion, worry, and sorrow. Al-Qurtubi explained that `إِلَّا الْمَوْتَةَ الْأُولَى`—“beyond the first death”—does not establish a new death in Jannah. Rather, they will never taste death there at all; they already tasted the first death in this world.[^2][^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The verses connect taqwa with reward, then conclude that admission into Jannah and its delight are `فَضْلًا مِنْ رَبِّكَ`—“a bounty from your Lord.” Righteous action is a commanded means, but Jannah is a bounty and mercy. A person must therefore neither admire their own deeds nor declare with certainty that those deeds have been accepted.

### Second Qur'anic Text: No Weariness and No Expulsion

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-15-45-48" kind="quran" -->

#### Complete Arabic Text

> **إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي جَنَّاتٍ وَعُيُونٍ ۝ ادْخُلُوهَا بِسَلَامٍ آمِنِينَ ۝ وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَانًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ ۝ لَا يَمَسُّهُمْ فِيهَا نَصَبٌ وَمَا هُمْ مِنْهَا بِمُخْرَجِينَ.** [الحجر: ٤٥-٤٨][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

#### English Translation

> **Indeed, the people of taqwa will be amid gardens and springs. “Enter them in peace and security.” We will remove whatever resentment remains in their hearts, so that they are like brothers, facing one another on couches. No weariness will touch them there, nor will they ever be removed from it.** [Al-Hijr 15:45–48][^4]

#### Source and Status

This is the Qur'an and is established with certainty. All four verses are included because the final verse completes the picture of security: peace upon entry, soundness within the heart, the absence of weariness, and the absence of expulsion. The English wording is a meaning-based project translation for review.

#### Tafsir

Regarding `لَا يَمَسُّهُمْ فِيهَا نَصَبٌ`—“No weariness will touch them there”—al-Sa'di said there will be no outward or inward fatigue, because Allah will bring the people of Jannah into a perfect creation not susceptible to afflictions. He explained `وَمَا هُمْ مِنْهَا بِمُخْرَجِينَ`—“nor will they ever be removed from it”—to mean that they will remain there at all times.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

Security in Jannah is not merely staying alive. It is an existence without bodily exhaustion, turmoil in the heart, or fear of being expelled. This complete picture prevents us from reducing Jannah to physical pleasures detached from purity of heart and sound relationships.

### First Hadith: The Call of Health, Life, Youth, and Delight

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2837" kind="hadith" -->

#### Complete Arabic Text

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ وَأَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنهما، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«يُنَادِي مُنَادٍ: إِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَصِحُّوا فَلَا تَسْقَمُوا أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَحْيَوْا فَلَا تَمُوتُوا أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَشِبُّوا فَلَا تَهْرَمُوا أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَنْعَمُوا فَلَا تَبْأَسُوا أَبَدًا». فَذَلِكَ قَوْلُهُ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: ﴿وَنُودُوا أَنْ تِلْكُمُ الْجَنَّةُ أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ﴾ [الأعراف: ٤٣].**[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

#### English Translation

> Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with them both) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: **“A caller will call out: You will remain healthy and never become ill; you will live and never die; you will remain young and never grow old; and you will enjoy delight and never suffer misery.” This is the meaning of Allah's words: “They will be called: This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit because of what you used to do.”** [Al-A'raf 7:43][^6]

#### Source and Status

Narrated by Muslim in his *Sahih*, The Book of Paradise and the Description of Its Bliss and Its People, no. 2837. It is therefore an authentic hadith. The English wording is a meaning-based project translation for review.

#### Lesson Explanation

The call gathers four afflictions people fear: illness, death, old age, and misery. It denies each of them forever. They are not merely postponed; they are permanently removed from the people of Jannah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### Second Hadith: The Slaughter of Death and the Final Announcement

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2849" kind="hadith" -->

#### Complete Arabic Text

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«يُجَاءُ بِالْمَوْتِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كَأَنَّهُ كَبْشٌ أَمْلَحُ، فَيُوقَفُ بَيْنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، فَيُقَالُ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، هَلْ تَعْرِفُونَ هَذَا؟ فَيَشْرَئِبُّونَ وَيَنْظُرُونَ وَيَقُولُونَ: نَعَمْ، هَذَا الْمَوْتُ. وَيُقَالُ: يَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ، هَلْ تَعْرِفُونَ هَذَا؟ فَيَشْرَئِبُّونَ وَيَنْظُرُونَ وَيَقُولُونَ: نَعَمْ، هَذَا الْمَوْتُ. فَيُؤْمَرُ بِهِ فَيُذْبَحُ، ثُمَّ يُقَالُ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ، وَيَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ، خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ». ثُمَّ قَرَأَ رَسُولُ اللهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: ﴿وَأَنْذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ وَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ﴾ [مريم: ٣٩]، وَأَشَارَ بِيَدِهِ إِلَى الدُّنْيَا.**[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

#### English Translation

> Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **“On the Day of Resurrection, death will be brought as though it were a black-and-white ram. It will be made to stand between Jannah and the Fire. It will be said, ‘People of Jannah, do you recognize this?’ They will crane their necks, look, and say, ‘Yes. This is death.’ It will be said, ‘People of the Fire, do you recognize this?’ They will crane their necks, look, and say, ‘Yes. This is death.’ Then the command will be given and it will be slaughtered. It will then be said, ‘People of Jannah, everlasting life with no more death! People of the Fire, everlasting life with no more death!’” The Messenger of Allah ﷺ then recited, “Warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will have been decided, while they remain heedless and do not believe” [Maryam 19:39], and he pointed with his hand toward the world.**[^7]

#### Source and Status

Narrated by Muslim in his *Sahih*, The Book of Paradise and the Description of Its Bliss and Its People, no. 2849. Al-Bukhari also narrated it with similar wording; its authenticity is agreed upon. The English wording is a meaning-based project translation for review.

#### Tafsir and Explanation

The Prophet ﷺ connected this scene to the expression `يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ`—“the Day of Regret.” In this context, al-Tabari explained `إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ`—“when the matter will have been decided”—as the completion of the judgment of everlasting residence for the people of both homes and the slaughter of death.[^8] The scene is a true report about the unseen, which we affirm as it was transmitted. We do not attempt to describe how death is given this form or how it is slaughtered; Allah has power over all things.

The joy of the people of Jannah reaches its completion when they know through sight and proclamation that there will be no death after that. By contrast, the verse warns the people of this world against heedlessness before the time for action comes to an end.

### Theological and Educational Safeguards

- We believe that Jannah and the Fire are real and that the everlasting life of the people of Jannah is established by the Qur'an and Sunnah.
- We affirm the slaughter of death as the Prophet ﷺ reported it. We do not reduce it to a mere symbol or speculate about a manner not given in the text.[^9]
- `أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ`—“which you have been made to inherit because of what you used to do”—does not mean that deeds independently purchase Jannah. The verses themselves say `فَضْلًا مِنْ رَبِّكَ`—“a bounty from your Lord.” Entry into Jannah is through Allah's mercy, while deeds are a means.
- We do not guarantee Jannah for a specific person unless revelation testifies to it. We hope for the person who does good and fear for the person who does wrong.
- When teaching a child, center the lesson on the believer's security and hope in Allah's mercy. Do not use descriptions of the Fire or the slaughter of death to frighten children or threaten them over passing misbehavior.
- A grieving or bereaved person needs compassion and careful listening. Do not use this lesson to deny their grief; use it to open a door to hope while respecting their pain.

## Children Ages 4–7

### Simple Explanation

In this world, we become tired and sick, and we may worry that beautiful things will end. Allah tells the people of Jannah, however, that they will always be safe: they will not become sick, grow old and weak, die, or leave Jannah. Their hearts will be at peace and their joy will never end.

### Fictional Teaching Story: The Gift That Never Ends

**This is a fictional teaching story, not a hadith or historical report.**

Maryam asked her teacher, “Why do I sometimes feel sad while I am playing with my favorite toy?” The teacher replied, “Because you know that playtime will end, and perhaps you are afraid the toy will be lost.”

The teacher then drew a peaceful heart and said, “In Jannah, its people will not fear that their happiness will end. Allah promises them life with no death after it and joy that never disappears.”

Maryam said, “So the most beautiful thing is not only the gift—it is being safe with Allah!” The teacher replied, “Yes. We ask Allah for Jannah and do what He loves.”

### True Story From Islamic History

This is not an imaginary story. It is an authentic report that the Companion Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) carefully preserved from the Prophet ﷺ and conveyed to the Muslims. It tells us about something that will occur in the Hereafter.

**Reflection question:** What helps you feel peaceful when you remember that the joy of Jannah never ends?

### Short Questions

1. Will the people of Jannah become sick?
2. Will the joy of Jannah ever end?
3. What can we say when we ask Allah for Jannah?
4. What announcement will the people of Jannah hear after death is slaughtered?

### Home or Circle-Time Activity

The child makes a card shaped like a door and, with an adult's help, writes inside it: `خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ`—“Everlasting life with no more death.” The child then names one deed Allah loves that they want to continue, such as truthfulness, prayer, kindness, or mercy.

### Words Before Sleep

“Jannah is a home of security that never ends. Its people do not become sick or die, and their joy remains. O Allah, guide us to what You love and grant us Jannah through Your mercy.”

## Children Ages 8–12

### Age-Appropriate Explanation

Many beautiful things in this world come with anxiety: Will this end? Will I lose it? The texts describe Jannah as `مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ`—a secure dwelling. Its people are not waiting for sickness, death, or expulsion. Then they witness death being slaughtered and hear the final announcement, so their knowledge of everlasting life becomes complete joy untouched by fear.

### Fictional Teaching Story: The Competition Timer

**This is a fictional teaching story, not a hadith or historical report.**

Salim entered a competition he enjoyed, but he kept looking at the timer in the corner of the screen. As it moved closer to zero, his enjoyment decreased, even though he was ahead.

After the competition, his father asked, “Why were you anxious?” Salim answered, “I was afraid time would run out before I finished.” His father said, “That teaches us an important difference between this world and Jannah. Almost everything in this world has a timer, but in Jannah there is no death, no expulsion, and no end to its delight.”

Salim said, “So everlasting life is not just a very long time; it is the disappearance of the fear that things will end.” His father replied, “Exactly. That is why we prepare for the Hereafter with faith and righteous deeds while hoping for Allah's bounty.”

### True Story From Islamic History

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) conveyed to the Muslims a complete report he heard from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. The hadith tells of an established future scene and explains why the announcement of everlasting life will be so momentous.

#### Complete Arabic Text

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«يُجَاءُ بِالْمَوْتِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كَأَنَّهُ كَبْشٌ أَمْلَحُ، فَيُوقَفُ بَيْنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، فَيُقَالُ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، هَلْ تَعْرِفُونَ هَذَا؟ فَيَشْرَئِبُّونَ وَيَنْظُرُونَ وَيَقُولُونَ: نَعَمْ، هَذَا الْمَوْتُ. وَيُقَالُ: يَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ، هَلْ تَعْرِفُونَ هَذَا؟ فَيَشْرَئِبُّونَ وَيَنْظُرُونَ وَيَقُولُونَ: نَعَمْ، هَذَا الْمَوْتُ. فَيُؤْمَرُ بِهِ فَيُذْبَحُ، ثُمَّ يُقَالُ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ، وَيَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ، خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ». ثُمَّ قَرَأَ رَسُولُ اللهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: ﴿وَأَنْذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ وَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ﴾ [مريم: ٣٩]، وَأَشَارَ بِيَدِهِ إِلَى الدُّنْيَا.**[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

#### English Translation

> Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **“On the Day of Resurrection, death will be brought as though it were a black-and-white ram. It will be made to stand between Jannah and the Fire. It will be said, ‘People of Jannah, do you recognize this?’ They will crane their necks, look, and say, ‘Yes. This is death.’ It will be said, ‘People of the Fire, do you recognize this?’ They will crane their necks, look, and say, ‘Yes. This is death.’ Then the command will be given and it will be slaughtered. It will then be said, ‘People of Jannah, everlasting life with no more death! People of the Fire, everlasting life with no more death!’” The Messenger of Allah ﷺ then recited, “Warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will have been decided, while they remain heedless and do not believe” [Maryam 19:39], and he pointed with his hand toward the world.**[^7]

#### Source and Status

Narrated by Muslim, no. 2849, and by al-Bukhari, no. 4730, with similar wording. It is an authentic hadith whose authenticity is agreed upon. The English wording is a meaning-based project translation for review.

#### Age-Appropriate Explanation

The caller asks both groups so that they recognize death. Then it is slaughtered before them, and then the announcement is made. The sequence matters: recognition, witnessing, and finally a proclaimed certainty that there will be no death. We do not need to invent details the hadith does not mention.

<!-- evidence:end -->

The Companion preserved the report as he heard it, including the verse that the Prophet ﷺ recited with it. This teaches us that the Sunnah explains the Qur'an: the words `إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ`—“when the matter will have been decided”—are clarified in this hadith by the scene of death being slaughtered and everlasting life becoming settled.

**Reflection question:** How might believing that this world is the time for action and the Hereafter is the everlasting home change one decision you make this week?

### Important Vocabulary

- **Everlasting life (`الخلود`):** remaining alive with no end afterward.
- **Weariness (`النَّصَب`):** fatigue and hardship.
- **They crane their necks (`يشرئبون`):** they raise their heads and stretch their necks to look attentively.
- **Regret (`الحسرة`):** deep remorse over good that was missed and can no longer be recovered.
- **A secure dwelling (`مقام أمين`):** a place of residence safe from fear and disappearance.

### Comprehension and Thinking Questions

1. What kinds of fear do the texts remove from the people of Jannah?
2. Why do the verses say more than simply, “They will not die”?
3. What is the relationship between the hadith about the slaughter of death and Maryam 19:39?
4. Why does describing Jannah not mean that we may guarantee it for ourselves?
5. How do righteous deeds come together with seeking Allah's bounty and mercy?

### Practical Activity

The teacher gives students cards labeled `sickness`, `old age`, `death`, `weariness`, `expulsion`, and `sorrow`. Students search the complete texts for the wording that removes each affliction, then write down its reference. Afterward, each student chooses one righteous deed to maintain for a week while seeking Allah's pleasure through it.

## Teens Ages 13+

### Deeper Explanation

Revelation does not present everlasting life as an abstract idea. It builds the meaning within us in stages: texts establish security and the absence of expulsion; a call removes sickness, old age, misery, and death; then death is slaughtered and everlasting life is announced. In this way, any idea that the delight of Jannah could become threatened or diminished by the passage of time disappears.

The lesson also corrects two opposing misconceptions. The first is that death is an absolute end that leaves life without a purpose. The second is that mere affiliation or wishful thinking is enough for success. The verse calls Jannah a bounty from Allah while connecting it to taqwa and action, and the hadith shows that this world is the place of heedlessness from which one must awaken before the `يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ`—the Day of Regret.

### Fictional Teaching Story: The File That Was Not Saved

**This is a fictional teaching story, not a hadith or historical report.**

Layan spent hours preparing a digital project, but she ignored the backup reminder. The device failed, and a large part of her work was lost. What hurt her most was not the effort she had spent, but knowing that the warning had been in front of her and she had postponed acting on it.

She told her teacher, “Regret is not just sadness. It is knowing that the time to correct something was available and then passed.” Her teacher replied, “That brings an important meaning closer to us, while the enormous difference between this example and the Hereafter remains. The Qur'an warns us of the Day of Regret so that we pay attention now. As long as we remain in this world, the door of repentance and action is open.”

Layan rebuilt her project and wrote at the top of her plan, “I will not postpone what truly benefits me.” She did not allow fear to paralyze her; she turned it into repentance, organization, and action.

Narrated by Muslim, no. 2849, and by al-Bukhari, no. 4730, with similar wording. It is an authentic hadith. The English wording is a meaning-based project translation for review.

#### Age-Appropriate Explanation

A believer accepts the report according to its apparent meaning in a way befitting the unseen: Allah will bring death in the form His Messenger described and command that it be slaughtered. The fact that death is a meaning or attribute in our worldly experience does not allow us to negate the text; nor may we claim to know how it will happen. The intended theological conclusion is clear: death has ended and everlasting life is established.[^9]

<!-- evidence:end -->

The Companion preserved the hadith's connection with the verse. This teaches an important method: the scene of death being slaughtered is not read for visual excitement, but to understand the warning and the moral decision before us. As long as a person is in this world, they can believe, repent, and make amends. Once the matter has been decided, the time for choice is over.

**Reflection question:** What is the difference between fear that leads to repentance and action and fear that causes despair? How does hope in Allah's bounty preserve the balance?

### Discussion Questions

1. How does the possibility that a blessing may disappear contribute to human anxiety in this world?
2. What evidence in the complete texts shows that security in Jannah is physical, emotional, social, and future-facing?
3. Why does the Prophet's connection of the hadith with the verse from Maryam demonstrate an important principle for understanding the Sunnah's explanation of the Qur'an?
4. How do we affirm the report of death being slaughtered without resemblance-making, negation, or speculation about its manner?
5. How do we bring together `بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ`—“because of what you used to do”—and `فَضْلًا مِنْ رَبِّكَ`—“a bounty from your Lord”?
6. How should this subject be presented to someone experiencing bereavement or severe anxiety about death?

### Practical Applications

- Write down one beneficial habit you keep postponing and identify its first step within the next twenty-four hours.
- Memorize the hadith of the call in Sahih Muslim 2837, or memorize its four statements with understanding.
- When you worry about losing a blessing, thank Allah for it, take appropriate practical measures, and ask Allah for delight that never runs out.
- Avoid content that turns details of the Hereafter into definitive illustrations or entertainment scenes not governed by the revealed texts.
- Comfort someone who has lost a loved one with compassionate words suited to their circumstances; do not reduce their grief to a quick sermon.

### Reflective or Writing Activity

Create a map with four circles titled: **What ends? What remains? What can I do now? What do I hope for from Allah?** Fill them with examples from the texts and from your own life. Then write a paragraph explaining how serious preparation can come together with hope and freedom from despair.

## Detailed Lesson Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning outcomes:** Learners will identify four dimensions of security from the texts, explain the hadith of the slaughter of death and its connection to Maryam 19:39, distinguish source text from tafsir and educational explanation, and write one practical application.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Printed copies of Al-Dukhan 44:51–57, Al-Hijr 15:45–48, and Sahih Muslim 2837 and 2849; a copy of the terminology table; whiteboard and markers; cards showing `أمين`, `نصب`, `يشرئبون`, and `الحسرة`; small exit-ticket slips.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** Verify the texts and references, divide attendees into groups, and draw three columns on the board labeled “Text,” “Scholarly interpretation,” and “Educational effect.” Learn beforehand whether any attendee is recently bereaved.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask, “Why is fear mixed into some joys in this world?” Gather two or three responses, then say, “Today we will study how Allah removes from the people of Jannah the very fear that joy will end.”

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Evidence study — 15 minutes:** One participant reads the verses in full; another reads the complete text of hadith 2849. Ask attendees to underline every expression that removes an affliction and circle every expression that establishes permanence. Identify the source and status of each text.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided instruction — 15 minutes:** Explain `مَقَامٍ أَمِينٍ`, `إِلَّا الْمَوْتَةَ الْأُولَى`, and `إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ` from the terminology table. Ask, “Where does the text end? Where does tafsir begin? What application may we derive without attributing it to the commentator?” Then briefly present the theological safeguards.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Assign each group one dimension of security: physical, emotional, social, or future security. The group collects its evidence, explanation, and a warning against one mistaken understanding, then presents for two minutes. Correct any claim guaranteeing Jannah to a particular person or speculating about the manner of the unseen.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and closing — 10 minutes:** Exit ticket: (1) cite a text proving that no one will be expelled; (2) explain the exception in Al-Dukhan 44:56; (3) write one practical effect. Close by asking Allah for Jannah and seeking refuge from the Fire without requiring any wording that has not been established.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give beginners a partially completed table. Ask advanced learners to compare al-Tabari's and al-Qurtubi's explanations of the exception. Allow anyone affected by the subject of death simply to listen or briefly step out.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching cautions:** Do not turn the lesson into a debate about the fate of particular individuals, assert how death will be slaughtered, or use the texts to minimize bereavement. Always balance warning, hope, and Allah's bounty.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

### Children Ages 4–7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning outcomes:** Each child will say that Jannah is a home of security with no death, repeat `خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ`, and name one deed Allah loves.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large card reading `خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ`; door-shaped paper; child-safe coloring supplies; two simple faces showing worry and peace without depicting the unseen; star stickers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** Prepare the cards, practice reading the passage calmly, and remove from the visual presentation any image of death or Jannah. Ensure that another responsible adult is available if needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Show the worried and peaceful faces and ask, “When do you feel safe?” Welcome answers without asking any child to recount a painful experience.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Evidence reading — 5 minutes:** Explain that Abu Sa'id transmitted an authentic report from the Prophet ﷺ, then read the complete text printed on the page. Ask children to raise a star card when they hear `خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ`, without an extended explanation of the Fire.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided instruction — 5 minutes:** Explain, “In Jannah there is no sickness, death, or end to joy. Allah is able to do all things. We ask Him for Jannah and obey Him.” Have the children repeat the central sentence once or twice.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** Each child colors a door card and writes or pastes the central sentence inside it. The child then tells a partner one good deed: truthfulness, prayer, sharing, or mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and closing — 4 minutes:** Ask three yes-or-no questions: “Will the people of Jannah become sick? Will they die? Do we do good and hope for Allah's mercy?” Then read the words before sleep from the lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Provide a ready-made card for a child who does not write and allow responses by pointing. Ask an advanced child to name one difference between this world and Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching cautions:** Do not use slaughter or the Fire to frighten a child, display pictures of the scene, or tell an individual child that they are among the people of Jannah. If a child mentions a recent death, listen gently and refer the matter to their guardian when needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

### Children Ages 8–12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning outcomes:** Students will connect three texts, define five vocabulary terms, arrange the hadith scene in the correct order, and choose one weekly application.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards containing the complete texts; vocabulary and definition cards; six affliction cards; a paper strip for ordering the events of the hadith; pens and a weekly tracking sheet.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** Print the texts in a clear typeface. Separate the hadith-order cards: call, recognition, looking, slaughter, and the announcement of everlasting life. Arrange groups of three or four.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show a stopped timer and ask, “How does your feeling in a game you enjoy change when time is about to run out?” Connect the responses to the lesson title.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Evidence study — 10 minutes:** Students take turns reading Al-Hijr 15:45–48 and hadith 2849 in full. They underline expressions of security, then match the vocabulary with its meanings.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided instruction — 8 minutes:** Build a chain on the board: “security — no weariness — no expulsion — no death.” Explain that the hadith is an authentic report about the unseen and that we do not add details. Then show its connection to Maryam 19:39.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Groups arrange the hadith cards, then match each affliction to the text that removes it. Each group presents one choice and justifies it with words from the text rather than relying only on memory.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences: “I learned that…” and “This week I will…” Check whether the evidence is used correctly, then close by asking Allah for Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Print keywords in a different color for learners needing support. Ask advanced learners to explain the difference between a very long time and everlasting life with no end.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching cautions:** Do not permit the activity to become a dramatization of slaughter or allow participants to assign people's final fates. If the subject causes anxiety, return the focus to the security of the people of Jannah, Allah's mercy, and the action that is possible now.

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning outcomes:** Students will construct a text-based argument for everlasting life in Jannah, explain three interpretive terms, distinguish affirming an unseen report from speculating about its manner, and apply the meaning of the Day of Regret to a real decision.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** An evidence pack containing the complete texts and terminology table; whiteboard; worksheets with columns labeled “Text / Tafsir / Inference”; discussion case cards; exit tickets.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** Review the references and safeguards. Choose a practical scenario suitable for students, such as delaying prayer, returning a right, or repenting. Avoid any scenario that exposes a particular student's private life.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask, “Does knowing that something will end reduce a person's enjoyment of it?” Request reasons, then explain that the texts address fear of an ending, not only the length of time.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Evidence study — 12 minutes:** Two groups read Al-Dukhan 44:51–57 and hadith 2849 in full. A third group reads Al-Hijr 15:45–48 and hadith 2837. Each group records the claim, evidence, and decisive wording.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided instruction — 13 minutes:** Explain the exception in `إِلَّا الْمَوْتَةَ الْأُولَى`, the relationship between `إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ` and the hadith, and the principle of affirming unseen realities without describing how. Discuss the difference between deeds as a means and Allah's bounty in the reward.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Groups complete the “Text / Tafsir / Inference” worksheet, then analyze the case of someone postponing repentance while knowing it is necessary. They propose one practical step that combines fear and hope without despair or a false sense of security from Allah's plan.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and closing — 5 minutes:** Exit ticket: “Write one proof of everlasting life, the meaning of one term, and one step to take within twenty-four hours.” Read an accurate example without identifying its author.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give learners needing support a sentence frame and one specified proof. Ask advanced learners to compare al-Tabari's and al-Qurtubi's tafsir of Al-Dukhan 44:56 or formulate a brief response to someone who reduces the hadith to a mere symbol.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching cautions:** Do not tolerate ridicule of questions about the unseen, and acknowledge the limits of our knowledge regarding manner. Do not turn discussion of regret into a space for exposing sins. Be considerate of students affected by death or anxiety and refer concerns needing further support to family or an appropriate professional.
