# Security, Eternity, and the Slaughter of Death

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of the slaughter of death (al-Bukhari 4730 and Muslim 2849) in its proper sequence: death is brought in the form of a black-and-white ram; the people of Jannah are called, then the people of the Fire, and both recognize it; it is slaughtered; the call rings out, "Eternity, and no death"; and then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recites the ayah of the Day of Regret.
- Explain that the security of Jannah is complete: no death, no weariness, no expulsion, and no rancor in the heart, drawing on the ayat of al-Hijr 15:45-48 and the tafsir of as-Sa'di.
- Explain the added wording "joy upon their joy" (al-Bukhari 6548): the announcement of eternity brings the people of Jannah a new joy on top of their bliss, the settled assurance that it will never end.
- Distinguish between affirming this matter of the unseen exactly as it has been reported and refraining from speculating about how it happens, with reference to Ibn al-Qayyim's point that the scene is a reality, not an allegory.
- Connect the hadith to the warning it carries: heedlessness happens in this world, and the time to act is now, without turning the lesson into a lesson in fear.
- Carry out the "Before the Day of Regret" activity: a nightly review for one week, noting one deed that may add to one's joy and one moment of heedlessness, turned into a specific fix for the next day.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to admit us to Jannah in security, to increase our joy there, and to keep us from being heedless.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every blessing in this world carries the shadow of its own ending. Health gives way to frailty, every meeting ends in a parting, and every life ends in death. That is why no human joy here is ever complete: the fear of losing it is always in the room. The people of Jannah, however, have been promised a security that pulls this fear out by the roots. Death itself will be brought forward and slaughtered in full view of everyone, and a voice will call out: "Eternity, and no death." This lesson reads that established scene from the unseen and asks two questions: What does it mean to have a security that nothing can threaten? And what does that ask of us while we are still in the world of deeds?

The lesson completes the earlier lesson in this series on the eternity of Jannah. There the focus was the permanence of the bliss itself; here it is the announcement that removes every possibility of that bliss ending, and the all-embracing security that surrounds the people of Jannah from the moment they enter. We will also see that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not close his account with a description of joy. He closed it with an ayah of warning addressed to the people of this world. The scene of the Hereafter is told in order to awaken us in the present.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-4730" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of the Slaughter of Death: "Eternity, and No Death"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«يُؤْتَى بِالْمَوْتِ كَهَيْئَةِ كَبْشٍ أَمْلَحَ، فَيُنَادِي مُنَادٍ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، فَيَشْرَئِبُّونَ وَيَنْظُرُونَ، فَيَقُولُ: هَلْ تَعْرِفُونَ هَذَا؟ فَيَقُولُونَ: نَعَمْ، هَذَا الْمَوْتُ، وَكُلُّهُمْ قَدْ رَآهُ. ثُمَّ يُنَادِي: يَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ، فَيَشْرَئِبُّونَ وَيَنْظُرُونَ، فَيَقُولُ: هَلْ تَعْرِفُونَ هَذَا؟ فَيَقُولُونَ: نَعَمْ، هَذَا الْمَوْتُ، وَكُلُّهُمْ قَدْ رَآهُ. فَيُذْبَحُ، ثُمَّ يَقُولُ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ، وَيَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ، خُلُودٌ فَلَا مَوْتَ». ثُمَّ قَرَأَ: {وَأَنْذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ} وَهَؤُلَاءِ فِي غَفْلَةٍ أَهْلُ الدُّنْيَا {وَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ}**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Death will be brought in the form of a black-and-white ram, and a caller will call out, 'O people of Jannah!' They will crane their necks and look, and he will say, 'Do you know what this is?' They will say, 'Yes, this is death,' for every one of them will have seen it. Then he will call out, 'O people of the Fire!' They will crane their necks and look, and he will say, 'Do you know what this is?' They will say, 'Yes, this is death,' for every one of them will have seen it. Then it will be slaughtered, and he will say, 'O people of Jannah: eternity, and no death. O people of the Fire: eternity, and no death.'" Then he recited: "And warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will have been decided, while they are in heedlessness" (and those in heedlessness are the people of this world) "and they do not believe."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The narration in Muslim adds: "and it will be made to stand between Jannah and the Fire," and "the command will be given concerning it, and it will be slaughtered," and at its end: "and he pointed with his hand toward this world."[^1] *Amlah* describes an animal whose white is mixed with black, and *yashra'ibbun* means that they stretch out their necks and lift their heads, straining to see.[^6] Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, affirmed that this scene is a reality and not an allegory: Allah is able to make death take on the form of a ram that can be seen and slaughtered, just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Surah al-Baqarah and Surah Al Imran will come on the Day of Resurrection as though they were two clouds.[^6] Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, explained `when the matter will have been decided` to mean that the people of Jannah and the people of the Fire have been separated, and each has entered the abode that has become theirs, to remain there forever.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The scene brings together three things: certain recognition ("This is death," and every one of them has seen it), then the removal of that familiar fear before everyone's eyes, and then a decisive call that will never be revoked. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, pointed with his hand toward this world, showing that heedlessness lives here and that the time to act is here. Notice, too, that the call uses the very same words for both groups: "Eternity, and no death." The word that is the height of security for the people of Jannah is the height of anguish for the people of the Fire, and the difference between them was made in this world. We affirm what has been reported exactly as it has been reported; we do not speculate about how it takes place, and we do not reduce it to a mere symbol.

### "And the People of Jannah Will Gain Joy upon Their Joy"

> عَنِ ابْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«إِذَا صَارَ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ، وَأَهْلُ النَّارِ إِلَى النَّارِ، جِيءَ بِالْمَوْتِ حَتَّى يُجْعَلَ بَيْنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، ثُمَّ يُذْبَحُ، ثُمَّ يُنَادِي مُنَادٍ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ لَا مَوْتَ، يَا أَهْلَ النَّارِ لَا مَوْتَ، فَيَزْدَادُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ فَرَحًا إِلَى فَرَحِهِمْ، وَيَزْدَادُ أَهْلُ النَّارِ حُزْنًا إِلَى حُزْنِهِمْ»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"When the people of Jannah have gone to Jannah and the people of the Fire to the Fire, death will be brought and placed between Jannah and the Fire. Then it will be slaughtered, and a caller will call out: 'O people of Jannah, no death! O people of the Fire, no death!' So the people of Jannah will gain joy upon their joy, and the people of the Fire will gain grief upon their grief."**[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith pins down the timing: it happens after both groups have settled into their abodes. The people of Jannah are already in bliss before the call, and then a new joy is added to it: the certainty that it will never end. The perfection of a blessing is to be safe from losing it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-19-39-40" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Warn Them of the Day of Regret"

> **{وَأَنْذِرْهُمْ يَوْمَ الْحَسْرَةِ إِذْ قُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ وَهُمْ فِي غَفْلَةٍ وَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ ۝ إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَرِثُ الْأَرْضَ وَمَنْ عَلَيْهَا وَإِلَيْنَا يُرْجَعُونَ}** [مريم: ٣٩-٤٠][^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will have been decided, while they are in heedlessness and they do not believe. Indeed, it is We who will inherit the earth and all who are on it, and to Us they will be returned."** (Maryam 19:39-40)[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, reports from Ibn Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them both, that the Day of Regret "is one of the names of the Day of Resurrection; Allah has made it momentous and warned His servants of it."[^4] As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, said: what regret could be greater than losing Allah's good pleasure and His Jannah "in a way that leaves no possibility of going back to begin the work anew," while in this world they are "heedless of this tremendous matter; it does not cross their hearts, and if it does, it crosses them heedlessly."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The second ayah completes the meaning: everything on earth will be inherited from those who now hold it, and the return is to Allah alone. Heedlessness, then, is not ignorance of death; it is being too busy with what will be left behind to think about it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-15-45-48" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Enter It in Peace, Secure"

> **{إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي جَنَّاتٍ وَعُيُونٍ ۝ ادْخُلُوهَا بِسَلَامٍ آمِنِينَ ۝ وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَانًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ ۝ لَا يَمَسُّهُمْ فِيهَا نَصَبٌ وَمَا هُمْ مِنْهَا بِمُخْرَجِينَ}** [الحجر: ٤٥-٤٨][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, the God-conscious will be amid gardens and springs. 'Enter them in peace, secure.' And We will remove whatever rancor is in their hearts: brothers, on couches, facing one another. No weariness will touch them there, nor will they ever be made to leave."** (al-Hijr 15:45-48)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On `secure`, as-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, said: secure "from death, sleep, and toil; from fatigue, and from any part of the bliss they enjoy being cut off or diminished; and from illness, sorrow, worry, and every other thing that clouds happiness." On `No weariness will touch them there`, he said: "neither outward nor inward." And on `nor will they ever be made to leave`: "at any time whatsoever."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

These ayat name four faces of security: security on entering, security in the heart through the removal of rancor, security of the body from weariness, and security of one's dwelling from ever being expelled. The hadith of the slaughter of death seals all four by removing the last thing anyone could still fear. Notice that security from other people sits between security on entering and security from weariness: no one is truly secure while carrying rancor toward a brother, or fear of him, in their chest. That is why purifying the heart of envy and spite in this world is preparation for that security, and not merely good social manners.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### A True Hadith: The Call "No More Death, Ever!"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-4730" authenticated="true" -->

**This is true news that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us. It is not a made-up story.**

Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about a great day. It comes after the people of Jannah have gone into Jannah and are happy in all its blessings.

Then a voice calls out: "O people of Jannah!" They lift their heads and stretch their necks to look. What's going on?

Death is brought in, in the shape of a ram, a white sheep with some black in its wool. Someone asks them, "Do you know what this is?" And they say, "Yes, this is death." Every one of them knows it. The people of the Fire are called too, and they know it as well.

Then the command is given, and death is slaughtered. That means death itself comes to an end, and it never, ever comes back.

Then a voice calls out: "O people of Jannah, forever, and no death!" That means: "You will stay here always and always, and you will never die." And the people of Jannah become even happier than they already were.[^2]

After the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us this, he pointed with his hand toward this world, and reminded us that the people of this world forget about that day.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-4730" audience="4-7" -->

Here is what it means, simply: one day, death itself will come to an end, and the people of Jannah will be told, "Stay here always. No more death." There will be nothing to be afraid of there, and no goodbyes. And right now, here in this world, we remember that day, so we do good things and we don't forget about it.[^1]

### A True Hadith: "O People of Jannah, Eternity, and No Death"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-4730" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

After the people of Jannah have gone to Jannah and the people of the Fire to the Fire, something happens that has never happened before.

Death is brought in the form of a black-and-white ram and made to stand between Jannah and the Fire. Then a caller calls out, "O people of Jannah!" They crane their necks and look. He asks, "Do you know what this is?" They answer, "Yes, this is death." Every one of them has seen it. Then he calls out, "O people of the Fire!" They crane their necks and look, they are asked the same question, and they answer, "Yes, this is death."

Pay attention: everyone recognizes death. Nobody can say, "I didn't know what it was." So when it is removed, it happens right before everyone's eyes.

Then the command is given, and it is slaughtered. And then the call goes out: "O people of Jannah, eternity, and no death. O people of the Fire, eternity, and no death."[^1]

In the narration of Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, the people of Jannah at that moment gain "joy upon their joy."[^2] They were already in bliss, and now they know for certain that it will never end.

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recited the words of Allah, exalted is He: `And warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will have been decided, while they are in heedlessness and they do not believe`, and he pointed with his hand toward this world. It was as if he were saying: this is where people forget, and the chance to act is here, and it is now.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-4730" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: death is brought in the form of a ram that everyone recognizes, it is slaughtered, eternity is announced to the people of both abodes, and the people of Jannah become even happier. And the ayah the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, recited reminds us to get ready today instead of forgetting.[^1][^2]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A lot of people deal with the thought of death in one of two ways: they run from it by staying busy all the time, or they worry about it so much that the worry ruins the present. And you hear "Live in the moment, you only live once" as if that settles it. Islam offers a third answer: death is a reality we face with open eyes, and beyond it, for those who believe, lies a security that nothing can threaten. In an authentic hadith, death itself is brought forward and slaughtered, and a voice calls out: "Eternity, and no death." The question this hadith puts to you is not "Are you going to die?" It is: "Are you heedless right now?"

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-4730" -->

- **`Eternity (al-khulud)`** — remaining forever, with no interruption and no end; it is what is announced to the people of both abodes after death is slaughtered.[^1]
- **`Heedlessness (al-ghaflah)`** — when something tremendous is right in front of you and never crosses your heart, or crosses it without leaving a mark; in the ayah, it describes the people of this world.[^7]
- **`Secure (aminin)`** — in the ayah of al-Hijr: safe from death, weariness, illness, sorrow, and any interruption of their bliss.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### A True Hadith: The Moment the Last Fear Ends

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-4730" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

Look at it from the point of view of the people of Jannah. Death and the reckoning are behind them; they have entered, and they have received what they were promised. The hadith does not describe what they feel before the call, but it does tell us that their joy grows after it.

Then the call comes: "O people of Jannah!" They crane their necks and look. Death is brought in the form of a black-and-white ram and made to stand between Jannah and the Fire. "Do you know what this is?" "Yes, this is death." Every one of them has seen it. The people of the Fire are called, and they recognize it too.

Notice the detail: eternity is not announced with a word alone. First death is put on display, everyone admits they know it, and then the command is given and it is slaughtered in front of them. The fear that has shadowed every human being for their whole life is removed in a way everyone can see and no one can doubt.

Then: "O people of Jannah, eternity, and no death. O people of the Fire, eternity, and no death."[^1] And in the narration of Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both: "So the people of Jannah will gain joy upon their joy, and the people of the Fire will gain grief upon their grief."[^2]

Then comes the part you might miss: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not end the hadith on the joy. He recited `And warn them of the Day of Regret, when the matter will have been decided, while they are in heedlessness and they do not believe`, and pointed with his hand toward this world.[^1] The whole scene takes place there, but the message is aimed here: at the person who burns through hours with no purpose, at the one who keeps putting off prayer because he is "busy," at anyone who thinks time is open-ended and will never run out.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-4730" audience="13+" -->

To put it another way: death is put on display and everyone recognizes it; then it is slaughtered and eternity is announced, and the security of the people of Jannah is crowned with a new joy. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, turns everyone's eyes back to this world, where heedlessness happens, and where the decision is still in your hands.[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why does the hadith put death on display and have it recognized before it is slaughtered? What does that add to the people's peace of mind?
2. How is "joy upon their joy" different from the ordinary joy you know?
3. What is the difference between "live in the moment" as a way of escaping, and "seize the moment" because it is the time to act?
4. What makes you heedless more than anything else in your day? Do you choose it, or does it choose you?
5. How might this hadith ease the anxiety of someone who is afraid of death, or afraid of losing someone they love?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: Before the Day of Regret

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.032.activity.before-the-day-of-regret" -->

For seven nights, write three lines before you sleep, in a private notebook or a note on your phone: one deed you hope will add to your joy there; one moment of heedlessness today (When was it? What stole it?); and one specific fix for it tomorrow, at a set time. Start each night by checking whether you carried out yesterday's fix. On the seventh night, write a private paragraph: What pattern did you discover in your heedlessness, and what will you change in your surroundings (not just in your intentions)?

> اللَّهُمَّ أَدْخِلْنَا الْجَنَّةَ آمِنِينَ، وَزِدْنَا فِيهَا فَرَحًا إِلَى فَرَحٍ، وَلَا تَجْعَلْنَا مِنَ الْغَافِلِينَ.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of the slaughter of death in sequence, together with the additional wording in Muslim's narration; explains the added phrase "joy upon their joy" and its timing; lists the four faces of security in al-Hijr 15:45-48 with the help of as-Sa'di's tafsir; distinguishes between affirming the unseen as it has been reported and speculating about how it happens; and begins a week-long nightly review with two columns and a specific fix.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf open to Surah al-Hijr and Surah Maryam; a board divided into three columns headed "Recognition," "Removal," and "The Call"; an activity sheet with seven rows and two columns; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 4730 and 6548 and Muslim 2849 and 2850 and compares their wording; reviews Ibn Kathir's tafsir of Maryam 19:39, as-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Hijr 15:46 and 15:48, and the section on the slaughter of death in Ibn al-Qayyim's *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher keeps in mind that the earlier lesson in this series on the eternity of Jannah covered the ayat of al-Waqi'ah and the hadith of Muslim 2837, so these are only referred to here, not repeated. The teacher should also be ready for the question: "How can death be slaughtered when it is not a physical body?"

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What is the most beautiful blessing in your life right now? And what takes something away from your joy in it?" Gently steer the discussion toward the point that the fear of losing it accompanies every worldly blessing, then pose the lesson's question.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Abu Sa'id in full, and have learners fill in the three columns on the board (8 minutes). Then read the additional wording in Muslim and the hadith of Ibn Umar, and ask: "When does this happen, and what does it add?" (5 minutes). Then read the ayat of al-Hijr together with as-Sa'di's tafsir, and have learners draw out the four faces of security (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss Ibn al-Qayyim's point that the scene is a reality, and that Allah gives forms to meanings, as in the hadith of the Two Radiant Ones (*az-Zahrawan*, al-Baqarah and Al Imran), while stressing that we affirm it without speculating about how it happens. Then ask: "Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, close with the ayah of warning?" Steer toward the point that heedlessness is a feature of this world, and that the hadith brings the listener back to the present moment.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner fills in the first row of the review sheet about their own day (5 minutes); then pairs swap their "fix" and help each other rephrase it until it is specific and measurable (7 minutes); then each learner sets a fixed time for the nightly review (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name three things the people of Jannah are secure from, with the evidence for each," and "What fix will you carry out tomorrow?" Close by reading al-Hijr 15:46 and making du'a for everyone present to enter Jannah in security.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith of Abu Sa'id and al-Hijr 15:46 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations of the hadith (Abu Sa'id, Ibn Umar, and Abu Hurayrah in at-Tirmidhi 2557), compare their wording, and consult the section on the slaughter of death in *Hadi al-Arwah*.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Affirm the unseen as it has been reported; do not explain the slaughter away as a mere symbol, and do not speculate about how it happens. Do not depict or act out the scene. Be mindful of anyone present who has recently lost a loved one, and present the hadith as a source of hope and reassurance, not as a way of making light of grief. Do not declare Jannah or the Fire certain for any particular person, and do not use the mention of the people of the Fire simply to frighten.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that in Jannah nothing lovely ever ends and no one ever has to leave; says that death is brought in the shape of a ram and then comes to an end, and that the people of Jannah are told, "Forever, and no death"; and starts the night-sky star picture with a parent.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet of dark blue paper for the night sky; gold star stickers; a pen for drawing the empty stars; coloring pencils or crayons; a picture of a beautiful garden with no people in it; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account in a calm, reassuring voice, leaves out any detail about a knife or blood, and simply says, "Death comes to an end and never comes back." The caregiver also prepares a gentle answer in case the child asks about someone who has died: "We ask Allah to bring us together with them in Jannah, where there are no goodbyes."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask: "What game do you love most? How do you feel when someone says, 'Time's up'?" Then say: "Today we're going to learn about a place where the happy time never runs out."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the explanation and then the account, asks the child to lift their head and stretch their neck at the words "they lift their heads and stretch their necks," and then repeats together with the child: "Forever, and no death!"

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Why were the people of Jannah happy?" Explain that the most wonderful thing about Jannah is that no one there is ever afraid of things ending, and that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, reminded us not to forget that day even while we are playing.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child gets the sky picture ready, sticks on the first gold star for something good they did today, and draws the first empty star for what they will do tomorrow; agree with the parent to keep it going every night.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Notice whether the child can say what the people of Jannah were told, and whether they can name one good thing they did. Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, the gold star and the sentence "In Jannah, the happiness never ends" are enough. Ask older children to tell the account in their own words to a brother or sister.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show any slaughter or a slaughtered ram, and do not act out the scene as a game. Do not go into detail about the people of the Fire; it is enough that they recognize it too. Keep an eye on any child who has lost a loved one or who is afraid of death; reassure them, and do not pressure them to talk. The empty stars must never be used as a reproach, only as a gentle chance to try again.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith in sequence; explains the three terms (*kabsh amlah*, *yashra'ibbun*, the Day of Regret); names two faces of security in the ayat of al-Hijr; and starts the nightly review chart with a specific fix.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; four sequence cards with no pictures ("The Call," "Recognition," "The Slaughter," "Eternity"); copies of the week chart; pens; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes al-Hijr 15:46 and 15:48 on the board and prepares an example of a vague fix ("I'll be better") and a specific one ("I'll pray 'Asr on time") so the class can tell them apart.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Describe the last day of vacation and ask: "Why can't we enjoy it all the way?" Listen, then connect the answers to the idea that everything in this world has an ending.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story while students arrange the four sequence cards as they listen (8 minutes), then explain the three terms and read the two ayat of al-Hijr (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher asks questions 1, 2, and 4, and steers toward the point that because everyone recognizes death, its removal becomes a certainty beyond any doubt, and that the gesture of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, toward this world means that the time to act is now.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students fill in the first row of the chart about their own day; then pairs swap papers to check that each "fix" is specific, without reading the "moment I forgot" column unless its owner gives permission.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences: "What were the people of Jannah told?" and "What does it mean that they `will never be made to leave`?" Then read the du'a, and agree to share the number of check marks after one week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide a chart with only two columns and ready-made examples of fixes. Ask advanced students to memorize al-Hijr 15:45-48 and to compare the narrations of Abu Sa'id and Ibn Umar.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the slaughter only in the words of the hadith, without description or acting out. Do not ask any student to reveal their shortcomings in front of classmates. Be attentive to anyone who has lost a relative, and present the hadith to them as a door of hope. Do not describe the people of the Fire in detail.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the structure of the scene (display, acknowledgment, removal, announcement) and how it produces certainty; distinguishes between running from the thought of death, being anxious about it, and being aware of it; explains the meaning of heedlessness in the ayah; and begins a private nightly review that uncovers their pattern of heedlessness and adjusts their surroundings.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a personal sheet that is not collected; cards for anonymous questions; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral examples of sources of heedlessness (long scrolling sessions, procrastination, being constantly busy), reviews as-Sa'di's tafsir of Maryam 19:39 on the meaning of heedlessness, and gets ready for questions about fear of death or the loss of loved ones.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Put up the slogan "Live in the moment, you only live once" and ask: "What does this slogan get right? And what does it get wrong?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the scene, pausing at three moments: "Do you know what this is?", "Eternity, and no death," and the gesture of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, toward this world. Then read the definition of heedlessness from the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Small groups discuss questions 3 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher steers toward the point that Islam does not ask for constant anxious brooding over death, but for an awareness that moves a person to act and brings the heart peace. Open the anonymous question cards for question 5.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes the first night's lines on their private sheet and identifies one source of their heedlessness and one change to their surroundings (where the phone stays, a reminder, a companion); then pairs practice phrasing a fix with a set time.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "How does this hadith change the way I think about death?" Read the du'a. After one week, the teacher follows up with a general question about the experience, without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short research piece on Ibn al-Qayyim's discussion of Allah giving forms to meanings, together with the hadith of the Two Radiant Ones.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Handle with sensitivity any student who suffers from severe anxiety about death or grief over a loss, and refer them to a counselor or parent when needed. Do not ask anyone to disclose their sins. Affirm the unseen without speculating about how it happens, and do not let the mention of the people of the Fire become material for mockery or for passing judgment on specific people.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
