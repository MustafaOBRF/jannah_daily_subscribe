# The Hur al-'Ayn and Their Qualities

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Cite the ayat that describe the hur al-'ayn (ar-Rahman 55:56-58 and 55:70-74, and al-Waqi'ah 56:22-24), and explain their qualities from the tafsirs of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir: "restrained in glance," "rubies and coral," "good and beautiful," and "pearls kept hidden."
- Narrate the hadith of Anas in Sahih al-Bukhari (2796) in its three parts: a journey in the path of Allah in the morning or the evening; a space in Jannah the length of a bow; and the woman of the people of Jannah who, if she looked out upon the earth, would light up all that lies between and fill it with fragrance, and whose veil is better than this world and everything in it.
- Explain the meanings of *nasif*, *hur* and *'ayn* as the commentaries on al-Bukhari give them, and note that al-Bukhari placed the hadith in the chapter on the hur al-'ayn.
- Present the question of whether believing women or the hur al-'ayn are superior with integrity: a view attributed to the scholar who held it, and a hadith that the lesson does not rely on because it is weak.
- Show that in Jannah Allah gives every believing man and woman whatever their souls desire and their eyes delight in, reunites the righteous with their families, and removes all rancor from their hearts (an-Nisa' 4:124, az-Zukhruf 43:71, ar-Ra'd 13:23 and al-Hijr 15:47).
- Carry out the "What Enters the Eye" activity: reviewing a space where one's gaze lives, clearing out what clouds it, placing a source of light there, and keeping it that way for a set period.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Qur'an speaks of the delights of Jannah in plain language, language that sound human nature neither blushes at nor cheapens. Among those delights are the hur al-'ayn: women whom Allah has brought into being in Jannah for its people, and whom He describes by purity, modesty and clarity before He describes their beauty. *Hur* is the plural of *hawra'*, a woman the white of whose eyes is intensely white and the dark intensely dark; *'ayn* is the plural of *'ayna'*, a woman with wide eyes.[^5]

This lesson stays with what the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah say about the hur al-'ayn, and with the question of how they compare with believing women. The believing wives in Jannah have a lesson of their own, as does marriage in Jannah, so we do not go into either here. The words "hur, sheltered in pavilions" came up earlier, in the lesson on the pavilions of Jannah, but only for what they tell us about the pavilions.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-55-56-58" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Restrained in Glance"

> **{فِيهِنَّ قَاصِرَاتُ الطَّرْفِ لَمْ يَطْمِثْهُنَّ إِنْسٌ قَبْلَهُمْ وَلَا جَانٌّ ۝ فَبِأَيِّ آلَاءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ ۝ كَأَنَّهُنَّ الْيَاقُوتُ وَالْمَرْجَانُ}** [الرحمن: ٥٦-٥٨][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"In them are maidens restrained in glance, whom no human and no jinn has touched before them. So which of the favors of your Lord will you both deny? They are like rubies and coral."** (ar-Rahman 55:56-58)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says of "restrained in glance": "That is, they lower their eyes from all but their husbands, and see nothing in Jannah more beautiful than their husbands," and he attributes this to Ibn 'Abbas, Qatadah, 'Ata' al-Khurasani and Ibn Zayd. As-Sa'di says of "whom no one has touched": "That is, no one of mankind or the jinn has reached them before them," and of "They are like rubies and coral": "That is because of their purity, the beauty of their appearance and their radiance."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The first quality the Qur'an mentions is a gaze, not a face: eyes that do not stray toward anyone but their own. Faithfulness and modesty are part of the beauty of Jannah; a lovely face is not the whole of it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-55-70-74" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Good and Beautiful"

> **{فِيهِنَّ خَيْرَاتٌ حِسَانٌ ۝ فَبِأَيِّ آلَاءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ ۝ حُورٌ مَقْصُورَاتٌ فِي الْخِيَامِ ۝ فَبِأَيِّ آلَاءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ ۝ لَمْ يَطْمِثْهُنَّ إِنْسٌ قَبْلَهُمْ وَلَا جَانٌّ}** [الرحمن: ٧٠-٧٤][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"In them are women good and beautiful. So which of the favors of your Lord will you both deny? Hur, sheltered in pavilions. So which of the favors of your Lord will you both deny? No human and no jinn has touched them before them."** (ar-Rahman 55:70-74)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di explains "good and beautiful" as: "That is, good in character and beautiful in face; they unite outward and inward beauty," and "sheltered in pavilions" as: "That is, kept within pavilions of pearl, having made themselves ready and prepared themselves for their husbands." Ibn Kathir compares the two descriptions: "There is no doubt that she who has restrained her own glance is better than she who has been restrained, even though all of them are kept in seclusion."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Qur'an puts "good" before "beautiful": character is named before the face. This is the lesson's own observation on the order of the words.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-22-24" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Like Pearls Kept Hidden"

> **{وَحُورٌ عِينٌ ۝ كَأَمْثَالِ اللُّؤْلُؤِ الْمَكْنُونِ ۝ جَزَاءً بِمَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ}** [الواقعة: ٢٢-٢٤][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And hur with wide, lovely eyes, like pearls kept hidden in their shells: a reward for what they used to do."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:22-24)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "That is, as though they were fresh pearls in their whiteness and purity." As-Sa'di describes the pearl as "hidden from eyes, wind and sun... and so too the hur al-'ayn: there is no flaw in them," and says of "a reward for what they used to do": "Just as their deeds were good, Allah made their reward good."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

*Maknun* means guarded and kept out of sight, so being guarded is part of the beauty. And the passage ends with deeds: this is a reward, not a passing stroke of luck.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2796" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "And the Veil on Her Head Is Better Than This World and Everything in It"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«لَرَوْحَةٌ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ أَوْ غَدْوَةٌ خَيْرٌ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا وَمَا فِيهَا، وَلَقَابُ قَوْسِ أَحَدِكُمْ مِنَ الْجَنَّةِ أَوْ مَوْضِعُ قِيدٍ -يَعْنِي سَوْطَهُ- خَيْرٌ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا وَمَا فِيهَا، وَلَوْ أَنَّ امْرَأَةً مِنْ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ اطَّلَعَتْ إِلَى أَهْلِ الْأَرْضِ لَأَضَاءَتْ مَا بَيْنَهُمَا وَلَمَلَأَتْهُ رِيحًا، وَلَنَصِيفُهَا عَلَى رَأْسِهَا خَيْرٌ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا وَمَا فِيهَا»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Setting out in the path of Allah in the evening, or in the morning, is better than this world and everything in it. A space in Jannah the length of one of your bows, or the space of a *qid* (meaning his whip), is better than this world and everything in it. And if a woman of the people of Jannah were to look out upon the people of the earth, she would light up all that lies between them and fill it with fragrance; and the veil on her head is better than this world and everything in it."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Bukhari placed this hadith in the "Chapter on the Hur al-'Ayn and Their Description" in the Book of Jihad. Ibn Hajar says: "What is intended by it for this chapter heading is the last part," that is, the mention of the woman, and he explains *nasif* as "the *khimar*," the head veil. Al-'Ayni says that al-Bukhari mentioned "that in it is a woman who, if she were to look out... and she is one of the hur al-'ayn," and that the hadith contains "two magnificent qualities among the qualities of the hur al-'ayn." In the language, "the path of Allah" is "general, applying to every sincere deed," and when it is used without qualification it most often means jihad, as *an-Nihayah* states.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith lays down three measures, one after another: an hour spent in obeying Allah, a tiny patch of Jannah, and the veil of a woman of its people. Each of them is better than the entire world. If her head covering alone is worth that much, what of the light and the fragrance? And every quality named here belongs to modesty and protection: light, fragrance and a veil, never display.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-4-124" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whether Male or Female"

> **{وَمَنْ يَعْمَلْ مِنَ الصَّالِحَاتِ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنْثَىٰ وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ فَأُولَٰئِكَ يَدْخُلُونَ الْجَنَّةَ وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ نَقِيرًا}** [النساء: ١٢٤][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And whoever does righteous deeds, whether male or female, while being a believer, those will enter Jannah, and they will not be wronged even by the speck on a date stone."** (an-Nisa' 4:124)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di explains "will enter Jannah" as Jannah "which contains whatever souls desire and eyes delight in," and "they will not be wronged even by the speck on a date stone" as: "That is, neither little nor much of the good they did."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The promise is the same for men and women, so the mention of the hur al-'ayn must never be read as an honor given to one sex and withheld from the other.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-43-71" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whatever Souls Desire and Eyes Delight In"

> **{يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِصِحَافٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ ۖ وَفِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِيهِ الْأَنْفُسُ وَتَلَذُّ الْأَعْيُنُ ۖ وَأَنْتُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [الزخرف: ٧١][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Platters of gold and cups will be passed around among them, and in it is whatever souls desire and eyes delight in, and in it you will abide forever."** (az-Zukhruf 43:71)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says: "This is a comprehensive expression that takes in every delight and joy, every coolness of the eye and gladness of the heart."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Every believing soul, man or woman, will find in Jannah whatever brings joy to its eyes, in keeping with what Allah knows of its needs and its happiness.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-13-23" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Those Who Were Righteous Among Their Fathers and Their Spouses"

> **{جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا وَمَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّاتِهِمْ ۖ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ كُلِّ بَابٍ}** [الرعد: ٢٣][^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Gardens of 'Adn, which they will enter together with those who were righteous among their fathers, their spouses and their offspring; and the angels will come in to them from every gate."** (ar-Ra'd 13:23)[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says: "Part of the completeness of their bliss and the coolness of their eyes is that they 'will enter it together with those who were righteous among their fathers,' male and female, 'and their spouses,' meaning the husband or the wife."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The bliss of Jannah does not forget the family: the righteous are reunited there with their spouses, their parents and their children.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-15-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And We Will Have Removed Whatever Rancor Is in Their Breasts"

> **{وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَانًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ}** [الحجر: ٤٧][^11]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We will have removed whatever rancor is in their breasts: brothers, on couches, facing one another."** (al-Hijr 15:47)[^11]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says: "So their hearts remain sound, free of all malice and envy, clear toward one another and full of love."[^11]

#### Lesson Explanation

There is no hurtful jealousy in Jannah and no rivalry over anyone's heart. Allah purifies every breast, so that each person rejoices in what they have been given.

<!-- evidence:end -->

**Believing women and the hur al-'ayn: which are superior?** Ibn 'Uthaymin was asked whether the qualities mentioned for the hur al-'ayn also include the women of this world. He said: "What appears to me is that the women of this world will be better than the hur al-'ayn, even in their outward qualities," and: "The righteous woman in this world (meaning the wife) will be better than the hur al-'ayn in the Hereafter."[^9] This is the reasoning of a scholar, not a text. A hadith on the subject is narrated from Umm Salamah, "Rather, the women of this world are superior to the hur al-'ayn," but it is a *munkar* (rejected) hadith, and the lesson does not rely on it.[^10] The faithful summary is this: we know of no authentic hadith that either establishes or denies such superiority; the believing woman enters Jannah through her deeds and her faith, and the hur al-'ayn are a creation that Allah brought into being there.[^9]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why does the Qur'an begin its description of the hur al-'ayn with "restrained in glance," before any mention of beauty?

2. What is the difference between "restrained in glance" and "sheltered in pavilions," as Ibn Kathir explains it?

3. What are the three measures in the hadith of Anas? And what does the repetition of "better than this world and everything in it" convey?

4. How would you present the question of whether believing women or the hur al-'ayn are superior without attributing to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, anything that is not established?

5. How would you answer someone who thinks that Jannah honors men alone?

### Activity: What Enters the Eye

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.067.activity.curate-what-enters-the-eye" -->

Choose one space where your gaze lives every day: your phone's home screen and the accounts you follow, the wall of your room, or your desk. On the first day, list what is there in two columns: what brings light (a reminder of Allah, or permissible beauty that lifts the heart) and what clouds the eye (what pulls your gaze toward what is not lawful, or plants envy and discontent). Remove or mute three things from the second column, and put one source of light in their place. For seven days, write one line each evening: did the space stay clean? On the seventh day, write a paragraph: what has changed in my heart and in my gaze?

### An Authentic Hadith: The Light of the People of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-2796" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. Al-Bukhari reported it in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2796" audience="4-7" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, tells us that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about Jannah, and about how very precious it is.[^4]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that a little place in Jannah, as small as one bow, is better than the whole world and everything in it![^4] Just imagine: all the houses, all the toys, all the seas and all the mountains, and a little place in Jannah is better than all of them.

Then he told us about the light of the people of Jannah and their lovely smell: if a woman of the people of Jannah looked down at the earth, she would light up everything in between, and fill it all with a sweet smell. And just the covering on her head is better than the whole world and everything in it![^4][^5]

How wonderful are the things Allah has made ready for the people of Jannah! Light, a sweet smell, and everything pure and clean. Whoever wants to be one of the people of Jannah keeps their heart and their eyes clean, and does good.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Salma came to school wearing a shiny new watch. The other kids crowded around her, and one of them said, "That's the most expensive watch I've ever seen!" On the way home, Salma asked her dad, "What's the most precious thing in the whole world?" He smiled and said, "Let me tell you about something small that's worth more than the whole world."

Jannah holds delights unlike anything we know. Allah has promised it to everyone who believes and does good, "whether male or female,"[^6] and in it is "whatever souls desire and eyes delight in,"[^7] meaning everything that makes the heart glad and the eyes happy. The righteous enter it "together with those who were righteous among their fathers, their spouses and their offspring,"[^8] so families are together again there. And Allah will have removed "whatever rancor is in their breasts,"[^11] so there is no envy there, and no jealousy that hurts.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2796" -->

- **`Fi sabil Allah (in the path of Allah)`** — on the road of obeying Allah; it covers every deed done sincerely for Allah, and it is most often used for jihad.[^5]
- **`Qab qaws (the length of a bow)`** — the length of a bow, meaning a very small space.[^5]
- **`An-nasif`** — the *khimar*, the veil that covers the head.[^5]

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: Worth More Than the World and Everything in It

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-2796" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that al-Bukhari reported in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2796" audience="8-12" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, who served the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tells us that he heard him mention three things, and after each one he said: "better than this world and everything in it."[^4]

The first: a person setting out in the path of Allah at the start of the day or at its end. One hour spent obeying Allah is worth more than the entire world.[^4][^5]

The second: a space in Jannah the length of one of our bows, or the size of a whip. Something tiny, and yet it is better than this world and everything in it.[^4]

The third: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that if a woman of the people of Jannah were to look out upon the people of the earth, she would light up everything between them and fill it with a sweet fragrance; and her *nasif* on her head, that is, her veil, is better than this world and everything in it.[^4][^5]

Think about it: if just the covering on her head is worth more than the whole world, what about the light and the fragrance that fill everything in between?

And look closely at the descriptions: light, a sweet fragrance, and a veil that covers the head. Every one of them is about purity and modesty. Beauty in Jannah is pure, protected beauty.

When Dad finished telling Salma the hadith, she looked down at her watch and said, "So my watch is worth nothing next to a tiny place in Jannah!" Her dad said, "Your watch is a blessing, and we thank Allah for it. We just don't make it the most precious thing we have."

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. What are the three things that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said are each better than this world and everything in it?

2. What does *qab qaws* mean? And why do you think the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, mentioned such a small space?

3. Which descriptions in the hadith point to purity and modesty?

4. Whom did Allah promise Jannah in the ayah of Surah an-Nisa'?

5. What do you think of as "the most precious thing"? And how has the hadith changed your mind?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.067.dua.pure-eyes-delight-for-every-believer" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

You scroll, and the images keep coming: edited faces, bodies on display, beauty engineered to grab your eye and then leave it hungry. Someone might tell you, "It's just a look. What's the big deal?" But the eye is the doorway to the heart, and whatever comes in through it leaves a mark.

The Qur'an presents the beauty of Jannah in a completely different way. Among its delights are the hur al-'ayn, a creation Allah brought into being there. He describes them as "restrained in glance,"[^1] meaning their eyes do not stray toward anyone but their husbands; as "like pearls kept hidden,"[^3] pearls guarded and out of sight; and as "good and beautiful,"[^2] good in character before beautiful in face. Beauty there is protected, not put on display. And then: "a reward for what they used to do."[^3]

The promise is for everyone, "whether male or female,"[^6] and in Jannah is "whatever souls desire and eyes delight in."[^7] Some scholars, such as Ibn 'Uthaymin, held that a believing woman from this world will be better in Jannah than the hur al-'ayn; that is his own scholarly reasoning, not an authentic text.[^9]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-55-56-58" -->

- **`Al-hur al-'ayn`** — *hur* is the plural of *hawra'*, one the white of whose eyes is intensely white and the dark intensely dark; *'ayn* is the plural of *'ayna'*, one with wide eyes.[^5]
- **`Qasirat at-tarf (restrained in glance)`** — lowering their gaze from all but their husbands.[^1]
- **`Al-maknun (kept hidden)`** — guarded and kept out of sight, shielded from eyes, sun and wind.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: A Veil Worth More Than the World

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-2796" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that al-Bukhari reported in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2796" audience="13+" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that he heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, set three measures before his Companions, each of them "better than this world and everything in it."[^4]

The first: an evening or a morning spent in the path of Allah, that is, setting out at the end of the day or the start of it. A short time, but given to Allah.[^4][^5]

The second: a space in Jannah the length of one of your bows, or of your whip. Barely room for a single step.[^4]

The third: "And if a woman of the people of Jannah were to look out upon the people of the earth, she would light up all that lies between them and fill it with fragrance; and the veil on her head is better than this world and everything in it."[^4] Al-Bukhari placed this hadith in the chapter on the hur al-'ayn, and the commentators explain the *nasif* as the *khimar*, the head veil.[^5]

Look at the picture the hadith paints. It does not describe a body; it describes light, fragrance and a veil. And the most precious thing it mentions is a piece of covering on the head. That is the exact opposite of what screens sell you: there, beauty is exposed so it can be consumed; here, it is protected, and its worth grows.

The three measures reorder what we count as valuable. An hour of obedience, a single step in Jannah, a veil in that bliss: each is worth more than everything paraded in front of us. Whoever guards their eyes today, hoping for what Allah has promised, loses nothing. They are choosing what lasts.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why does the Qur'an begin its description of the hur al-'ayn with lowered eyes and protection, not with the body?

2. What do the three measures in the hadith of Anas have in common?

3. What is the difference between "protected" beauty and beauty "on display"? Which kind do screens push at you more?

4. How would you answer someone who says, "It's just a look. What's the big deal?"

5. Why do we call Ibn 'Uthaymin's view "scholarly reasoning" and not "a text"?

### Activity: What Enters the Eye

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.067.activity.curate-what-enters-the-eye" -->

This is a private activity; you don't show it to anyone. Open the screen you spend the most time on and go through the first twenty accounts or clips that come up. Sort them on a private sheet of paper: light, neutral, or clouds the eye. Unfollow or mute three, and add one source that does you good. For seven days, mark each evening: did what I removed come back? Did I let my eyes roam, or did I lower my gaze? On the seventh day, write to yourself: what has changed in my heart?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites ar-Rahman 55:56-58 and 55:70-74 and al-Waqi'ah 56:22-24 and explains the qualities of the hur al-'ayn from as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir; narrates al-Bukhari 2796 with its three measures and explains *nasif* and "the path of Allah" from the commentaries; presents the question of superiority with integrity, distinguishing scholarly reasoning from text and from a *munkar* hadith; shows that the promise is general to men and women alike; and begins the seven-day "What Enters the Eye" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "The quality," "Its meaning in tafsir" and "What it teaches us"; the two-column activity sheet; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on the ayat; the commentary of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni on the hadith; Ibn 'Uthaymin's answer as quoted on IslamQA; and the section in which Ibn al-Qayyim cites the hadith of Umm Salamah, together with the editor's grading of it. The teacher keeps in mind that the believing wives and marriage in Jannah each have a lesson of their own.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "How does the media present beauty today? And how does the Qur'an present it?" Write the key words in two columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Recite the ayat of ar-Rahman and al-Waqi'ah and fill in the table of qualities (8 minutes). Then read the hadith of Anas in full, and explain al-Bukhari's chapter heading and the commentators' explanations of *nasif* and "the path of Allah" (7 minutes). Then read the ayat of an-Nisa', az-Zukhruf and ar-Ra'd to show that the promise is general and that families are reunited (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read the paragraph on superiority and discuss questions 4 and 5. The teacher points the group to the levels of evidence: a Qur'anic text, an authentic hadith, a scholar's reasoning, and a *munkar* hadith that cannot serve as proof.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses a space and fills in the first two-column list (8 minutes), decides on the three things to remove and the source of light to add (4 minutes), and agrees on a time to review in a week (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two qualities of the hur al-'ayn that point to protection and modesty, and state what is established and what is not on the question of superiority." Close with the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayat of ar-Rahman and the hadith are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare Ibn Kathir's comments on "restrained in glance" and "sheltered," and to read al-'Ayni's explanation of why this chapter appears in the Book of Jihad.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Use dignified language, and do not dwell on physical descriptions or on marriage; each has its proper context. Do not attribute to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, any statement that believing women are superior to the hur al-'ayn, since the hadith of Umm Salamah is *munkar*.[^10] Do not present the topic as an honor for men alone. Be mindful of the women in the room, and of anyone who has lost a spouse or never married: remind them that Allah gives every believing man and woman whatever will satisfy them.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that a little place in Jannah is better than the whole world; describes the light and the sweet smell of the people of Jannah; says that Allah lets everyone who believes and does good into Jannah, boy or girl; makes a Corner of Light with their family; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small lamp; a bottle of light perfume (after checking for allergies); a clean cloth; a picture of a plant with no writing on it; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the hadith in the lesson's own words without adding anything, and sets up a model corner in the classroom.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Turn off the lights, switch on the little lamp, and ask: "What does the room look like now?" Then spray a little perfume and ask: "What can you smell?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation and then the hadith. Pause at "a little place is better than the whole world," ask the children to count off beautiful things in the world, and then say: "And Jannah is even more beautiful."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 1, 4 and 5, and stress that Jannah is for everyone who believes and does good, and that good families are together again there.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The children clean the model corner together and place the three things in it; then each child takes home a "Corner of Light" card to make one with their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask: "What is in Jannah?" The child names the light, the sweet smell, and families being together. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the lamp and the du'a are enough. Ask older children to tell the hadith to their family in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not mention the hur al-'ayn or marriage in Jannah to children of this age, and do not elaborate on the woman mentioned in the hadith; the light, the sweet smell and the head covering are enough. If a child asks, "Who is this woman?", answer: "A woman of the people of Jannah, and Allah has made all the people of Jannah full of light and goodness." Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or Anas, may Allah be pleased with him. Be mindful of any child who has lost a parent, and reassure them that Allah brings the righteous together with their families.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student lists the three measures in the hadith; explains *qab qaws*, *nasif* and "in the path of Allah"; cites an-Nisa' 4:124, az-Zukhruf 43:71 and ar-Ra'd 13:23; shows that the beauty of Jannah is pure and protected; and begins the five-day "Desk of Light" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a sheet with two boxes labeled "Before" and "After"; a mushaf; a bow cut from cardboard to make *qab qaws* concrete.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the three ayat on the board, makes a cardboard bow, and prepares a sample set of "Before" and "After" pictures.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What is the most precious thing you own?" Then hold up the cardboard bow and say: "A space in Jannah this size is better than the whole world."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the ayat (4 minutes), then the story, pausing after each measure to ask "What does this mean?" (5 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 3, 4 and 5. The teacher explains that the hadith teaches us the true value of things, and that Jannah is promised to boys and girls alike.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student draws a "Before" picture of their desk or a corner of their room, and decides on the three things to remove and the one thing to add.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "Better than this world and everything in it is..." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide a ready-made drawing of a room for them to mark up. Ask advanced students to memorize the hadith, or part of it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not mention the hur al-'ayn or marriage in Jannah at this age, and do not open up questions about the woman in the hadith beyond saying that she is one of the people of Jannah. Explain "the path of Allah" as the road of obeying Allah, and do not elaborate on fighting. Do not mock a student for owning something valuable or for not owning it, and do not show pictures of students' rooms to the class without their permission.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen explains the Qur'anic qualities of the hur al-'ayn as qualities of protection and modesty; analyzes the three measures in al-Bukhari 2796; contrasts protected beauty with beauty on display; distinguishes text from scholarly reasoning on the question of superiority; and begins the private seven-day "What Enters the Eye" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet of paper that is not collected; a pearl, or a picture of a closed shell with no writing on it, to make "kept hidden" concrete.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on the ayat and the commentary on the hadith, and prepares a written description of the techniques platforms use to capture the eye (editing, repetition, infinite scroll) without showing any images.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the closed shell and ask: "Why does the pearl stay flawless inside it?" Connect the answer to "pearls kept hidden."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the terms (4 minutes), then the story, pausing at each measure (8 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Discuss questions 3 and 4 in small groups, then question 5 with the whole class. The teacher makes the point that lowering the gaze is not repression; it is choosing what lasts.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student begins sorting the first twenty items on their private sheet, and decides what to mute and what to add.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes two private sentences: "What enters my eyes most? And what do I want to enter them?" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students without a phone, use another space: the wall of their room, or the books and magazines they read. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on why the hadith of Umm Salamah was graded *munkar*.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Keep any talk about the hur al-'ayn brief and dignified, with no physical descriptions and no discussion of marriage. Do not show any images from social platforms in class. Private sheets are never revealed, and no student is asked what they watch. Remind boys and girls alike that the promise is for everyone, and that a student who struggles to lower their gaze is helped with gentleness and du'a, never with public shaming.
