# The Four Rivers of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Read the two ayat of Surah Muhammad (47:14-15) and explain the contrast they draw: the one who stands "on clear proof from his Lord" is not like one whose "evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him, and who follow their own desires"; and the reward of the mindful is a Jannah with four rivers in it: water, milk, wine and honey.
- Show that each river is described by denying a flaw that people know in its earthly counterpart: water "that never turns stale," milk "whose taste never changes," wine "a delight to those who drink," and honey "pure and clarified," together with as-Sa'di's principle that whatever in Jannah has a counterpart in this world carries no defect at all.
- Describe the wine of Jannah precisely from as-Saffat (37:45-47) and al-Waqi'ah (56:18-19): no harm, no headache and no loss of reason, so that it resembles the forbidden wine of this world in nothing but its name.
- Draw evidence from the hadith of at-Tirmidhi (2571) on the four seas of Jannah; from the hadith of al-Bukhari (5575) that whoever drinks wine in this world and does not repent will be deprived of it in the Hereafter; and from the hadith of al-Bukhari (5576) in which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose the milk on the night of the Isra', and Jibril said, "He guided you to the fitrah."
- Carry out the activity "See Past the Shine, Choose the Fitrah": break down an attractive presentation of something harmful into "What it shows" and "What it hides," test it with the questions of the four rivers, and then choose the more beneficial option in a situation that comes up every day, for a set period, keeping a record.
- Learn by heart the bedtime du'a that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught al-Bara' ibn 'Azib (al-Bukhari 247), and connect his words "you will die upon the fitrah" to the choice of milk on the night of the Isra'.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

All of us know things that are sweet at the start and bitter at the end: water left standing in a jug for days until it smells off; milk that sours; honey thick with wax and grit; an intoxicating drink dressed up in glossy advertising that ends up taking a person's mind, health and money. Much of the temptation in our lives works exactly this way: a gleaming picture that hides where things lead.

In Surah Muhammad, Allah sets two pictures side by side: the two paths a person can take in this world, and the reward that awaits in the next. He describes Jannah through four rivers, each one free of the flaw its earthly counterpart carries. This lesson reads the two ayat in full, looks closely and carefully at the wine of Jannah, and then asks: how do we tell what has merely been made to look good to us from what is truly good?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-47-14-15" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Likeness of the Jannah Promised to the Mindful"

> **{أَفَمَنْ كَانَ عَلَىٰ بَيِّنَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّهِ كَمَنْ زُيِّنَ لَهُ سُوءُ عَمَلِهِ وَاتَّبَعُوا أَهْوَاءَهُمْ ۝ مَثَلُ الْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي وُعِدَ الْمُتَّقُونَ ۖ فِيهَا أَنْهَارٌ مِنْ مَاءٍ غَيْرِ آسِنٍ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ لَبَنٍ لَمْ يَتَغَيَّرْ طَعْمُهُ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ خَمْرٍ لَذَّةٍ لِلشَّارِبِينَ وَأَنْهَارٌ مِنْ عَسَلٍ مُصَفًّى ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا مِنْ كُلِّ الثَّمَرَاتِ وَمَغْفِرَةٌ مِنْ رَبِّهِمْ ۖ كَمَنْ هُوَ خَالِدٌ فِي النَّارِ وَسُقُوا مَاءً حَمِيمًا فَقَطَّعَ أَمْعَاءَهُمْ}** [محمد: ١٤-١٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Is the one who stands on clear proof from his Lord like one whose evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him, and who follow their own desires? The likeness of the Jannah promised to those mindful of Allah: in it are rivers of water that never turns stale, rivers of milk whose taste never changes, rivers of wine, a delight to those who drink, and rivers of honey, pure and clarified. In it they will have every kind of fruit, and forgiveness from their Lord. Are they like one who will abide forever in the Fire, and who are given boiling water to drink, so that it tears their bowels apart?"** (Muhammad 47:14-15)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explains "on clear proof from his Lord" as being on insight and certainty in the command and religion of Allah, through the guidance and knowledge Allah sent down in His Book, "and through the upright fitrah upon which Allah created him"; the two are not alike. On the rivers, he reports from Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan and Qatadah that *ghayri asin* means "unchanged," adding: "The Arabs say *asina al-ma'*, 'the water has gone stale,' when its smell has changed." The milk "whose taste never changes" is "of the utmost whiteness, sweetness and richness." The wine, "a delight to those who drink," is "not foul in taste and smell like the wine of this world; rather, it is beautiful to look at, beautiful in taste and smell, and beautiful in its effect." The honey, *musaffa*, is "of the utmost purity, beautiful in color, taste and scent." And on "like one who will abide forever in the Fire," he says: "The one who rises through the degrees is not like the one who sinks through the depths."[^1]

As-Sa'di says of the water: "unchanged, whether by staleness or a foul smell, by bitterness or by murkiness"; of the milk: unchanged "by sourness or anything else"; of the wine: its drinker takes delight in it, "not like the wine of this world, whose taste is loathsome, which splits the head and steals away the mind"; and of the honey: clarified "of its wax and all its impurities." He closes: "So glory be to the One who made such a difference between the two abodes and the two rewards, between the two kinds of people and the two kinds of deeds."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The first ayah is a question about two paths: clear insight from Allah, or an evil deed made to look good to the one who does it, so that he follows his desires. The second ayah answers it with a picture of reward. Notice that the rivers are described by what they lack before what they have: no change, no sourness, nothing loathsome, no impurities. Whatever is made to look good in this world hides its flaw; the bliss of Jannah has no flaw to hide.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-37-45-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "No Harm Is in It"

> **{يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ ۝ بَيْضَاءَ لَذَّةٍ لِلشَّارِبِينَ ۝ لَا فِيهَا غَوْلٌ وَلَا هُمْ عَنْهَا يُنْزَفُونَ}** [الصافات: ٤٥-٤٧][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"A cup from a flowing spring will be passed around among them, white, a delight to those who drink. No harm is in it, and they will not be robbed of their senses by it."** (as-Saffat 37:45-47)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that "no harm (*ghawl*) is in it" means it does not give them stomach pain as the wine of this world does, which is the view of Mujahid, Qatadah and Ibn Zayd; it has also been said to mean headache, and this is reported from Ibn Abbas. Then he says: "The correct view is that of Mujahid." And "they will not be robbed of their senses by it" means: "their minds will not depart." He also reports from Ibn Abbas, by way of ad-Dahhak: "Wine has four traits: intoxication, headache, vomiting and urine. Allah mentioned the wine of Jannah and declared it free of these traits."[^3] As-Sa'di says: "free of *ghawl*, the stealing away and loss of the mind... and there is no headache in it and no murkiness."[^2]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-18-19" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Suffer No Headache from It"

> **{بِأَكْوَابٍ وَأَبَارِيقَ وَكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ ۝ لَا يُصَدَّعُونَ عَنْهَا وَلَا يُنْزِفُونَ}** [الواقعة: ١٨-١٩][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"With goblets and ewers and a cup from a flowing spring, from which they will suffer no headache, and by which they will not be intoxicated."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:18-19)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "It does not split their heads, nor does it drain away their minds."[^4] As-Sa'di says that it gives them no headache, "as the wine of this world gives its drinker a headache," and then adds: "In sum: every kind of bliss in Jannah whose like exists in this world is found in Jannah without any defect."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The wine of Jannah shares only its name with the wine of this world. The very effect for which the wine of this world was forbidden, the loss of reason and everything that follows from it, is explicitly denied of it in the text. So its mention carries no encouragement toward any wine of this world.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2571" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Sea of Water and the Sea of Honey"

> عَنْ حَكِيمِ بْنِ مُعَاوِيَةَ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ بَحْرَ الْمَاءِ، وَبَحْرَ الْعَسَلِ، وَبَحْرَ اللَّبَنِ، وَبَحْرَ الْخَمْرِ، ثُمَّ تُشَقَّقُ الْأَنْهَارُ بَعْدُ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Hakim ibn Mu'awiyah, from his father, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"In Jannah there is the sea of water, the sea of honey, the sea of milk and the sea of wine, and then the rivers branch out from them."**[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith agrees with the ayah on the four kinds, and adds that each kind has a sea from which its rivers branch out. The details of the rivers' courses and springs belong to another lesson.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5575" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "He Will Be Deprived of It in the Hereafter"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«مَنْ شَرِبَ الْخَمْرَ فِي الدُّنْيَا، ثُمَّ لَمْ يَتُبْ مِنْهَا، حُرِمَهَا فِي الْآخِرَةِ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever drinks wine in this world and then does not repent of it will be deprived of it in the Hereafter."**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Hajar summarizes the views of the scholars. He reports from Ibn 'Abd al-Barr that, according to the people of the Sunnah, the hadith is understood to mean that such a person is under threat of this unless Allah pardons him, as with the other major sins; and that he may enter Jannah through Allah's pardon, "and then not drink wine in it, nor will his soul desire it." Ibn Hajar then says: "And the hadith shows that repentance expiates even the major sins."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The river of wine in Jannah is for those who left wine alone in this world. The hadith shuts the door on having it both ways, in this world and the next, and in the same breath it holds open the door of repentance.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5576" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "He Guided You to the Fitrah"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ أُتِيَ لَيْلَةَ أُسْرِيَ بِهِ بِإِيلِيَاءَ بِقَدَحَيْنِ مِنْ خَمْرٍ وَلَبَنٍ، فَنَظَرَ إِلَيْهِمَا، ثُمَّ أَخَذَ اللَّبَنَ، فَقَالَ جِبْرِيلُ: **«الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي هَدَاكَ لِلْفِطْرَةِ، وَلَوْ أَخَذْتَ الْخَمْرَ غَوَتْ أُمَّتُكَ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him: on the night he was taken on the Night Journey, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was brought two cups at Iliya', one of wine and one of milk. He looked at them both, then took the milk. Jibril said: **"All praise belongs to Allah, who guided you to the fitrah. Had you taken the wine, your ummah would have gone astray."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Iliya' is Jerusalem (Bayt al-Maqdis). Ibn Hajar says he chose the milk "because it was familiar to him, easy, wholesome and pure, pleasant for those who drink it, and safe in its outcome, unlike wine in all of that. And what is meant by the fitrah here is steadfastness upon the true religion." He also mentions the possibility that he shied away from wine, "so that his nature agreed in advance with its prohibition, which was to come later."[^7]

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The two ayat of Surah Muhammad open with a choice: clear proof, or a pleasing polish? Then they describe the reward of the mindful as rivers without flaw. As-Saffat and al-Waqi'ah strip every harm away from the wine of Jannah. The hadith of Ibn 'Umar makes clear that this bliss belongs to those who gave up what was forbidden in this world, and the hadith of the Isra' paints a picture of that very choice: two cups, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, looked at them, then chose the one that was "safe in its outcome." So the practical question is this: what is being made to look good to you today, and where does it lead?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the point of the contrast between "on clear proof from his Lord" and "whose evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him"? And how did Ibn Kathir connect it to the fitrah?
2. What flaw does the ayah deny of each of the four rivers?
3. What harms do as-Saffat and al-Waqi'ah deny of the wine of Jannah, and why can its mention not rightly be taken as encouragement toward the wine of this world?
4. What views did Ibn Hajar summarize on the meaning of "he will be deprived of it in the Hereafter," and where does repentance fit in the hadith?
5. What is being made to look good to you today while hiding where it leads?

### Activity: See Past the Shine, Choose the Fitrah

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.050.activity.unmask-the-adorned-choose-the-fitrah" -->

Choose an attractive presentation of something you know harms you or wastes your time: an advertisement, a post, or a habit that feels comfortable. Write two columns: "What it shows" and "What it hides." Then test it with the questions of the four rivers: Does it change and spoil over time? Does it leave bitterness behind? Does it take something from your mind or your awareness? Does it carry impurities you cannot see? Next, pick a situation that comes up every day in which you face this temptation, choose the more beneficial option in it for seven days, and record each time. Finally, write a paragraph: What changed when you looked at the outcome before the shine?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Have you ever left a glass of milk on the table for a whole day? It turns sour! And water that sits for a long time starts to smell funny. Honey can have bits of wax and little specks in it, too.

In Jannah there are beautiful rivers that never, ever change: a river of clear water, a river of milk whose taste never changes, a river of pure honey, and a river of a delicious drink that the Qur'an calls *khamr*, "wine." But it is not like the wine of this world, which is forbidden and hurts people. The drink of Jannah never gives anyone a headache, and it never takes away anyone's mind.[^1][^4]

On the night of the Isra', the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was offered two cups: a cup of milk and a cup of wine. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, looked at them both and chose the milk. Then Jibril said: "All praise belongs to Allah, who guided you to the fitrah," which means the good path that Allah loves.[^7]

### From the Qur'an: Two Paths and Four Rivers

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-47-14-15" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In a surah called "Muhammad," Allah asks us an important question about two kinds of people.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-47-14-15" audience="4-7" -->

The first kind is a person who knows the right path from his Lord, so he walks along it as if he were carrying a light. The second kind is a person who sees a bad thing as beautiful, so he does whatever he wants, even when it is wrong. Are they the same? No!

Then Allah tells us about the Jannah He has promised to the people who fear Him and obey Him. In it are rivers of water that never changes, rivers of milk whose taste never changes, rivers of a drink that is a delight to those who drink it, and rivers of pure honey. They will have every kind of fruit there, and Allah will forgive them their sins.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Whoever chooses the good path in this world, Allah gives the best rivers in Jannah. And you can choose what is good, too, just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose the milk.

### Activity: The Picture and the Truth

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.050.activity.unmask-the-adorned-choose-the-fitrah" -->

Sit with your mom or dad and look at a box of candy or a brightly colored drink. In one square, draw what the picture on the package shows. In another square, draw what is really inside, like lots and lots of sugar. Then pick three days, and at snack time choose water or milk instead of the colored drink. Every time you choose, drop a white bead into your "milk cup." On the last day, count the beads together and say: "All praise belongs to Allah, who guided me."

### The Prophet's Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.050.dua.i-submit-my-face-to-you" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-247" attribution="prophetic" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught one of his Companions to make wudu' before going to sleep, then to lie down on his right side and say:

> **اللَّهُمَّ أَسْلَمْتُ وَجْهِي إِلَيْكَ، وَفَوَّضْتُ أَمْرِي إِلَيْكَ، وَأَلْجَأْتُ ظَهْرِي إِلَيْكَ، رَغْبَةً وَرَهْبَةً إِلَيْكَ، لَا مَلْجَأَ وَلَا مَنْجَا مِنْكَ إِلَّا إِلَيْكَ، اللَّهُمَّ آمَنْتُ بِكِتَابِكَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلْتَ، وَبِنَبِيِّكَ الَّذِي أَرْسَلْتَ.**[^8]
>
> *"O Allah, I submit my face to You, I entrust my affairs to You, and I lean my back on You, in hope of You and in awe of You. There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You. O Allah, I believe in Your Book which You sent down, and in Your Prophet whom You sent."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, I am Yours. I leave everything in my life to You. I lean on You the way a back leans against a wall. I hope for Your mercy and I am in awe of You, and there is no safe place except with You. I believe in the Qur'an and in the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^8]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In the school store there is a shiny can of energy drink with a famous athlete on it and loud, bold colors. Your classmate says, "Try it, it'll make you stronger!" But on the back of the can, in tiny letters, there is a huge amount of sugar and caffeine, and a warning that it is not for children. The big picture says one thing, and the fine print says something else.

In this lesson we read two ayat from Surah Muhammad about a person who sees the truth clearly and a person who has been fooled into seeing evil as good. Then we get to know the four rivers of Jannah, which have no flaw at all.[^1]

On the night of the Isra', in Jerusalem, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was offered two cups: a cup of wine and a cup of milk. He looked at them both, then took the milk, and Jibril said: "All praise belongs to Allah, who guided you to the fitrah. Had you taken the wine, your ummah would have gone astray." Ibn Hajar explains that the fitrah here means staying firm on the true religion, and that milk is "safe in its outcome."[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-47-14-15" -->

- **`On clear proof ('ala bayyinah)`** — seeing clearly and with certainty, by the light Allah gives, as Ibn Kathir explains.[^1]
- **`Never stale (ghayri asin)`** — its smell and taste never change, however long it is left.[^1]
- **`Pure and clarified (musaffa)`** — strained clean of wax and every impurity, as as-Sa'di explains.[^2]
- **`No headache, no intoxication (la yusadda'una 'anha wa la yunzifun)`** — the drink gives them no headache, and it never takes away their minds.[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### From the Qur'an: Clear Proof or Pretty Polish?

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-47-14-15" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an, and any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah Muhammad was revealed speaking about the believers and the disbelievers, and right in the middle of it, Allah asks a question that makes the reader stop and think.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-47-14-15" audience="8-12" -->

Allah says: "Is the one who stands on clear proof from his Lord like one whose evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him, and who follow their own desires?" One person sees the path clearly, by the light of his Lord. Another sees his own bad deeds as beautiful, so he chases whatever he craves. Can they ever be equal?

Then Allah describes the Jannah promised to those who are mindful of Him: rivers of water that never turns stale, and rivers of milk whose taste never changes; Ibn Kathir says it is perfectly white and perfectly sweet. There are rivers of wine, a delight to those who drink; as-Sa'di says it is nothing like the wine of this world, which tastes awful, splits the head with pain and steals away the mind. And there are rivers of honey, strained clean of its wax and every speck of dirt. They will have every kind of fruit there, and forgiveness from their Lord.[^1][^2]

The ayah ends by saying that these people are not like someone in the Fire who is given boiling water to drink. Ibn Kathir says: "The one who rises through the degrees is not like the one who sinks through the depths."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Did you notice? Each river is described as free of a flaw we know from this world. As-Sa'di says that every blessing in Jannah that has something like it in this world is found in Jannah "without any defect."[^2] Whoever chooses clear proof over pretty polish in this world will win the bliss that has no flaw at all.

<!-- story:end -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. What is the difference between the one who is "on clear proof from his Lord" and the one whose "evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him"?
2. What flaw is missing from each of the four rivers?
3. How is the wine of Jannah different from the wine of this world?
4. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, choose the milk? And what did Jibril say?
5. What looks shiny in your life, while its fine print says something else?

### The Prophet's Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.050.dua.i-submit-my-face-to-you" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-247" attribution="prophetic" -->

Al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him: When you go to bed, make wudu' as you do for prayer, then lie down on your right side, and then say:

> **اللَّهُمَّ أَسْلَمْتُ وَجْهِي إِلَيْكَ، وَفَوَّضْتُ أَمْرِي إِلَيْكَ، وَأَلْجَأْتُ ظَهْرِي إِلَيْكَ، رَغْبَةً وَرَهْبَةً إِلَيْكَ، لَا مَلْجَأَ وَلَا مَنْجَا مِنْكَ إِلَّا إِلَيْكَ، اللَّهُمَّ آمَنْتُ بِكِتَابِكَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلْتَ، وَبِنَبِيِّكَ الَّذِي أَرْسَلْتَ.**[^8]
>
> *"O Allah, I submit my face to You, I entrust my affairs to You, and I lean my back on You, in hope of You and in awe of You. There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You. O Allah, I believe in Your Book which You sent down, and in Your Prophet whom You sent."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, I hand myself over to You, I leave all my affairs to You, and I rely on You, longing for Your mercy and in awe of You. There is nowhere to run from You except to You. I believe in the Qur'an You sent down and in the Prophet You sent. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that whoever says this and then sleeps is "upon the fitrah," the very same word Jibril used when the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose the milk.[^8][^7]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A short clip: young people laughing at a party, music, gorgeous lighting, and a can of something, or a vape, passed from hand to hand. What the clip never shows: the headache the next morning, the addiction a year later, the money draining away, the regret. Glamorizing is a whole industry, and it runs on one rule: show the pleasure, hide the outcome.

The Qur'an calls this by its name: "whose evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him." Then, in the very next ayah, it describes the four rivers of Jannah, and wine itself is among them, but a wine stripped of every harm.[^1] And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever drinks wine in this world and then does not repent of it will be deprived of it in the Hereafter."[^6]

On the night of the Isra', the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was offered two cups, one of wine and one of milk, at a time when wine had not yet been forbidden. He looked at them both, then took the milk, and Jibril said: "All praise belongs to Allah, who guided you to the fitrah. Had you taken the wine, your ummah would have gone astray." Ibn Hajar says he chose it because it was "easy, wholesome and pure... safe in its outcome, unlike wine in all of that."[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-47-14-15" -->

- **`Made to seem fair (zuyyina lahu su'u 'amalihi)`** — the evil deed has become beautiful in his eyes, so he believes he is in the right, as as-Sa'di explains.[^2]
- **`Ghawl`** — a harm that creeps up on the drinker and ambushes him; Ibn Kathir judged it most likely to mean stomach pain, while others said headache or the loss of reason.[^3]
- **`Yunzafun`** — their minds are drained away by intoxication, something denied of the wine of Jannah.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: The One Who Sees and the One Who Is Dazzled

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-47-14-15" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an, and any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah Muhammad speaks about two groups: those who believed and did righteous deeds, and those who disbelieved and took their fill of this world. In ayat 14 and 15, Allah places both groups inside a single question.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-47-14-15" audience="13+" -->

The question: "Is the one who stands on clear proof from his Lord like one whose evil deeds have been made to seem fair to him, and who follow their own desires?" Ibn Kathir explains: on insight and certainty through the guidance Allah sent down, "and through the upright fitrah upon which Allah created him." As-Sa'di says of the other: he "followed his desires without guidance from Allah, and despite that, he sees what he is upon as the truth."[^1][^2]

Then the answer comes as a picture: "The likeness of the Jannah promised to those mindful of Allah." In it are rivers of water "that never turns stale," of milk "whose taste never changes," of wine, "a delight to those who drink," which Ibn Kathir says is "not foul in taste and smell like the wine of this world," and of honey, "pure and clarified." They will have every kind of fruit there, "and forgiveness from their Lord."[^1]

And at the close: "Are they like one who will abide forever in the Fire, and who are given boiling water to drink, so that it tears their bowels apart?" A drink set against a drink, and an outcome set against an outcome.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

As-Saffat and al-Waqi'ah complete the picture of the wine of Jannah: "No harm is in it, and they will not be robbed of their senses by it," and "from which they will suffer no headache, and by which they will not be intoxicated."[^3][^4] In other words, everything that makes the wine of this world dangerous is absent from it.

Notice: whatever is glamorized shows the pleasure and hides the outcome, while the bliss of Jannah is pleasure with no bad outcome at all. Whoever turns away from the glamorized thing in this world because they saw where it leads is among the people of clear proof.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why does the ayah of the rivers come right after the question "Is the one who stands on clear proof from his Lord...?"
2. As-Sa'di said that the one whose evil deeds are made to seem fair to him "sees what he is upon as the truth." How does that happen today through clips and ads?
3. Why is the mention of the wine of Jannah not an encouragement toward the wine of this world?
4. What does it teach you that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, "looked" at the two cups before he chose?
5. What situation keeps coming up for you in which you need to see the outcome before the shine?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: Strip Off the Glamour

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.050.activity.unmask-the-adorned-choose-the-fitrah" -->

Choose a clip, an ad, or a trend that glamorizes something harmful: an energy drink, vaping, staying up all night, or a dangerous challenge. In a private notebook, write three columns: "What it shows," "What it hides," and "Who profits." Then test it with the questions of the four rivers: Does it change and spoil? Does it leave bitterness behind? Does it take something from your mind? Does it carry impurities you cannot see? Next, pick a situation that keeps coming up for you, choose the more beneficial option in it for seven days, and record each time without telling anyone. Finally, write a paragraph: When did you see the outcome before the shine, and what changed?

### The Prophet's Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.050.dua.i-submit-my-face-to-you" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-247" attribution="prophetic" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught al-Bara' ibn 'Azib, may Allah be pleased with him, to make wudu' before sleeping, to lie down on his right side and say the following, and to make these the last words he spoke:

> **اللَّهُمَّ أَسْلَمْتُ وَجْهِي إِلَيْكَ، وَفَوَّضْتُ أَمْرِي إِلَيْكَ، وَأَلْجَأْتُ ظَهْرِي إِلَيْكَ، رَغْبَةً وَرَهْبَةً إِلَيْكَ، لَا مَلْجَأَ وَلَا مَنْجَا مِنْكَ إِلَّا إِلَيْكَ، اللَّهُمَّ آمَنْتُ بِكِتَابِكَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلْتَ، وَبِنَبِيِّكَ الَّذِي أَرْسَلْتَ.**[^8]
>
> *"O Allah, I submit my face to You, I entrust my affairs to You, and I lean my back on You, in hope of You and in awe of You. There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You. O Allah, I believe in Your Book which You sent down, and in Your Prophet whom You sent."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, I have surrendered my face to You, handed my affairs over to You, and leaned my back on You, longing for what is with You and in awe of You. There is no refuge from You and no rescue except in You. I believe in Your Book and in Your Prophet. And he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "If you die that night, you will die upon the fitrah." So you close your day upon the very fitrah the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose when he took the milk.[^8][^7]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the contrast in Muhammad 47:14-15 between clear proof and things made to seem fair; states the flaw denied of each river, citing Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; describes the wine of Jannah precisely from as-Saffat 37:45-47 and al-Waqi'ah 56:18-19; presents the scholars' views on al-Bukhari 5575 as Ibn Hajar summarized them; connects the hadith of the Isra' (al-Bukhari 5576) to the fitrah; and plans a week of choosing the more beneficial option in a recurring situation.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with four columns headed "Water," "Milk," "Wine" and "Honey," each with two rows beneath it, "In this world" and "In Jannah"; the activity sheet with the columns "What it shows" and "What it hides"; two neutral printed advertisements for consumer products; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Muhammad 47:14-15, as-Saffat 37:47 and al-Waqi'ah 56:19, and Ibn Hajar's commentary on al-Bukhari 5575 and 5576 in *Fath al-Bari*, and chooses the two advertisements so that neither promotes anything plainly forbidden and neither embarrasses anyone.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Name something that is sweet at the start and bitter at the end." Then: "What makes an advertisement work: what it shows, or what it hides?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read Muhammad 47:14-15 in full, and have learners discuss the question in the first ayah (4 minutes). Then they fill in the chart of the four rivers from the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (6 minutes). Then as-Saffat and al-Waqi'ah, with Ibn Abbas's statement on the four traits (4 minutes). Then the three hadiths, pausing at Ibn Hajar's summary of the meaning of "he will be deprived of it" (6 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher discusses the third and fourth questions, stressing that the wine of Jannah shares nothing with the wine of this world but its name, and that the hadith is a warning that leaves the door of repentance open. Then the teacher asks: How do we tell clear proof from mere polish in everyday life?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** In pairs, learners analyze one of the printed advertisements using the columns "What it shows" and "What it hides" and the questions of the four rivers. Then each learner, alone, chooses a personal temptation and a recurring situation, and writes down how they will record their seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What flaw is denied of the river of honey?", "What did as-Saffat deny of the wine of Jannah?" and "What is your recurring situation?" Then read the bedtime du'a from al-Bukhari 247, connecting "upon the fitrah" to the hadith of the Isra'.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only Muhammad 47:15, as-Saffat 37:47 and the hadith of the Isra'. Ask advanced learners to summarize the views Ibn Hajar reported on the meaning of "he will be deprived of it in the Hereafter," attributing each view to the scholar who held it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the drink of Jannah in terms borrowed from the culture of alcohol in this world, and do not use the lesson to show pictures of alcohol or its brand names. Do not rely on the hadiths Ibn Kathir attributes to the Prophet without stating their grading, such as the one saying the milk "did not come out of the udders of livestock," nor on the report of Laqit ibn 'Amir in at-Tabarani; the lesson has not relied on them.[^9] Be mindful of anyone whose family member struggles with addiction: present the hadith as a call to repentance, and never single anyone out. Do not go into more detail about the punishment of the people of the Fire than the ayah itself gives.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child names the four rivers, says that they never change and never hurt anyone, and says that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose the milk; draws "The Picture and the Truth" for one package; and chooses water or milk for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Four clear cups: water, milk, honey thinned with water, and an empty cup, explained as standing for the drink of Jannah whose taste we do not know; a brightly colored box of candy; paper and crayons; white beads and a small cup for each child; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the scene calmly, sends parents a short note explaining the three-day activity, and checks that no child is allergic to milk or honey.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver shows the cups and asks: "What happens to milk if we leave it in the sun for a whole day?" Then says: "In Jannah there are rivers that never, ever change."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the Qur'anic scene, and asks the children to count the rivers on their fingers and to repeat: "All praise belongs to Allah, who guided you to the fitrah."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver asks the unit's questions, stressing that Allah loves the one who chooses what is good, and that the drink of Jannah never hurts anyone.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** With the caregiver's help, the children draw the "Picture" and "Truth" squares for the candy box; then each child takes their cup and beads home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver watches whether each child can count the four rivers and remembers what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose. Then the children are shown how to lie down on their right side, and the bedtime du'a is read together, one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, drawing the picture and memorizing «اللَّهُمَّ أَسْلَمْتُ وَجْهِي إِلَيْكَ» ("O Allah, I submit my face to You") is enough. Ask older children to name what makes each river special.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not explain to the children in detail what wine is in this world; it is enough to say that it is forbidden and harms the mind. Do not mention the warning in the hadith or any details of the Fire. Do not embarrass a child who loves candy: the point is to learn to look at what is inside, not to forbid what is permitted.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the question in Muhammad 47:14; defines the four terms; states the flaw denied of each river; analyzes an advertisement using the columns "What it shows" and "What it hides"; and records their choice for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; an empty drink can or fast-food package whose back can be read; a chart sheet for each student; a mushaf; four river cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares a package in which the difference between the front and the ingredients list is obvious, and reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Muhammad 47:15.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the front of the package, then the back, and asks: "Which one tells the whole truth?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes); then the Qur'anic scene, placing each river's quality on its card and pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (6 minutes); then the hadith of the Isra' (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** In groups, each group analyzes the package with the questions of the four rivers. Then the teacher discusses the third question, stressing that the wine of Jannah resembles the wine of this world in nothing but its name.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student chooses an advertisement and a recurring situation, as in the activity unit, and sets a review day with the teacher five days later.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "In Jannah there is a river of... with no..." Then read the bedtime du'a. Five days later, anyone who wishes shares how many times they made their choice.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, two rivers and one simple advertisement are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize Muhammad 47:15 and to explain "never stale" (*ghayri asin*) and "pure and clarified" (*musaffa*) in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show advertisements for anything plainly forbidden. Do not mock a student who drinks a particular drink or whose family buys it. For the Fire, keep to what is in the ayah, without frightening detail.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the idea of "evil deeds made to seem fair" and how it plays out in media; explains why the mention of the wine of Jannah is no encouragement toward the wine of this world; connects the hadith of the Isra' and the bedtime du'a to the fitrah; and completes "Strip Off the Glamour" along with a seven-day record.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private notebook that is not collected; a mushaf; a short, neutral promotional clip for an energy drink or a similar product; cards for anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Muhammad 47:14-15 and as-Saffat 37:47, and Ibn Hajar's commentary on al-Bukhari 5575, and makes sure the chosen clip does not show anything plainly forbidden.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher plays the clip with the sound off and asks: "What does it want you to feel? And what did it not show you?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the Qur'anic scene, with three pauses: the question and as-Sa'di's comment; then the qualities of the rivers; then the closing contrast and the denial of every harm in the wine of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 2, 3 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher draws out that looking at the outcome is a skill that can be learned, just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, looked at the two cups.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student starts the three-column chart on the clip that was shown, then chooses a personal temptation and a recurring situation in their private notebook.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: what is being glamorized for their generation, the difference between the wine of Jannah and the wine of this world, and what their recurring situation is. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking for the notebooks. Then read the bedtime du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find written records difficult may keep private voice notes instead. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on as-Sa'di's words, "found in Jannah without any defect," applying them to another of the blessings of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to admit personal experiences with intoxicants or smoking in front of the class. Present the hadith of Ibn 'Umar together with the door of repentance that Ibn Hajar mentions, and pass no judgment on any particular person. Confidentially refer anyone who needs help with addiction to a trusted professional.
