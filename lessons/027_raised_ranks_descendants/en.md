# Raised Ranks and Descendants Reunited

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Explain the ayah of at-Tur, `We shall join their descendants with them` (at-Tur 52:21), and show that Allah, out of His grace, gathers a believer together with their believing descendants at the believer's own rank, without taking anything away from the parents' reward, and that the condition for this is faith.
- Connect the ayah of at-Tur with the ayat of ar-Ra'd (13:23) and Ghafir (40:8) through the phrase `whoever was righteous among their fathers, their spouses, and their descendants`, and show that the angels' prayer for the believers reaches their righteous families too.
- Narrate the hadith `through your child's asking forgiveness for you` (Ibn Majah 3660, a hasan hadith) and the hadith `or a righteous child who prays for him` (Muslim 1631), and show that a child, son or daughter alike, can be the means by which their parents' rank is raised.
- Tell the account of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, may Allah have mercy on him, and his sons at his deathbed as the historians report it, distinguish it from revealed, infallible text, and draw out its point: the most precious thing a parent leaves behind is the righteousness of their children, not money.
- Narrate the parting counsel of Ya'qub, peace be upon him, to his sons, `What will you worship after me?` (al-Baqarah 2:132-134), and show that a righteous lineage is no substitute for a person's own deeds.
- Carry out the "Two-Way Du'a Chain" activity: links of du'a and prayers for forgiveness for those who came before, and a final link for what the learner hopes to pass on to those who come after.
- Recall the lesson's du'a asking forgiveness for one's parents, guidance for one's family, and a reunion in Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

One of the widest doors of hope concerning Jannah is this: Allah does not make a believer's bliss a solitary, sealed-off happiness. He gathers to that believer whoever among their family was righteous, raising the lower-ranked to the rank of the higher so that the believer's eyes may be gladdened by them, while the higher loses nothing of their reward. This grace does not cancel justice. The Qur'an makes faith the condition for any descendant who is joined in this way, and it closes the very same ayah with `Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned`. Lineage alone admits no one to Jannah.

This grace runs in both directions. Allah may raise a righteous parent's descendants up to that parent, and Allah may raise a parent's rank through the prayers for forgiveness of a righteous child. That is why Ya'qub, peace be upon him, on his deathbed asked his sons about their worship, not their wealth; and why Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, may Allah have mercy on him, refused to bequeath his sons what was not theirs, and entrusted them to Allah instead.

And there is hope here for everyone. Whoever has lost their parents, or grew up without one of them, can still be the child who prays. And whoever is the first Muslim in their family can be the root to which their own descendants are one day joined.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-21" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "We Shall Join Their Descendants with Them"

> **{وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَاتَّبَعَتْهُمْ ذُرِّيَّتُهُمْ بِإِيمَانٍ أَلْحَقْنَا بِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَمَا أَلَتْنَاهُمْ مِنْ عَمَلِهِمْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ ۚ كُلُّ امْرِئٍ بِمَا كَسَبَ رَهِينٌ}** [الطُّور: ٢١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who believed, and whose descendants followed them in faith, We shall join their descendants with them, and We shall not deprive them of anything of their deeds. Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned."** (at-Tur 52:21)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir relates that Ibn Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, said: "Allah raises the descendants of the believer to his rank, even if they fell short of him in deeds, so that his eyes may be gladdened by them." He explains that `We shall not deprive them` means that We do not reduce the parents' reward in the slightest by joining their children to them.[^1] The commentators differ over who the "descendants" are here: the young children who follow their parents in the ruling of faith, the grown children who believed by their own choice, or both groups? Al-Wahidi held that both are included, while Ibn al-Qayyim inclined to the view that restricting it to young children is the more apparent reading.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah holds together grace, in the joining of descendants, and justice, in every soul being held in pledge for what it has earned. So no child may lean on the righteousness of a parent.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-13-22-24" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Gardens of 'Adn, Which They Will Enter, Along with Whoever Was Righteous"

> **{وَالَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا ابْتِغَاءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِمْ وَأَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَأَنْفَقُوا مِمَّا رَزَقْنَاهُمْ سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَةً وَيَدْرَءُونَ بِالْحَسَنَةِ السَّيِّئَةَ أُولَٰئِكَ لَهُمْ عُقْبَى الدَّارِ ۝ جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا وَمَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّاتِهِمْ ۖ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ كُلِّ بَابٍ ۝ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ بِمَا صَبَرْتُمْ ۚ فَنِعْمَ عُقْبَى الدَّارِ}** [الرَّعْد: ٢٢-٢٤][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who are patient, seeking the Face of their Lord, who establish the prayer, spend secretly and openly from what We have provided for them, and repel evil with good: theirs is the blessed outcome of the Home: Gardens of 'Adn, which they will enter, along with whoever was righteous among their fathers, their spouses, and their descendants. And the angels will come in to them from every gate, [saying]: 'Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured. How excellent is the outcome of the Home!'"** (ar-Ra'd 13:22-24)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: "That is, He brings them together there with their loved ones, their fathers, families, and children, those who are fit to enter Jannah." He added that Allah raises the lower to the rank of the higher without lessening the higher, as a gift and an act of kindness from Him.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The joining extends to fathers and spouses as well as descendants, and it is bounded by the condition `whoever was righteous`.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-40-7-8" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### The Prayer of the Bearers of the Throne for the Believers and Their Families

> **{الَّذِينَ يَحْمِلُونَ الْعَرْشَ وَمَنْ حَوْلَهُ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّهِمْ وَيُؤْمِنُونَ بِهِ وَيَسْتَغْفِرُونَ لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا رَبَّنَا وَسِعْتَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ رَحْمَةً وَعِلْمًا فَاغْفِرْ لِلَّذِينَ تَابُوا وَاتَّبَعُوا سَبِيلَكَ وَقِهِمْ عَذَابَ الْجَحِيمِ ۝ رَبَّنَا وَأَدْخِلْهُمْ جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ الَّتِي وَعَدْتَهُمْ وَمَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّاتِهِمْ ۚ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ}** [غَافِر: ٧-٨][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those who bear the Throne, and those around it, glorify their Lord with praise, believe in Him, and ask forgiveness for those who believe: 'Our Lord, You encompass all things in mercy and knowledge, so forgive those who repent and follow Your path, and shield them from the punishment of the Blaze. Our Lord, and admit them into the Gardens of 'Adn that You have promised them, together with whoever was righteous among their fathers, their spouses, and their descendants. You alone are the Almighty, the All-Wise.'"** (Ghafir 40:7-8)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explained the meaning of this prayer as: "That is, bring them all together." He also related the words of Mutarrif ibn Abdullah: "Of all Allah's servants, the most sincere well-wishers of the believers are the angels."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

So let the believer pray for their family with the very prayer the angels make for them.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="ibnmajah-3660" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Through Your Child's Asking Forgiveness for You"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«إِنَّ الرَّجُلَ لَتُرْفَعُ دَرَجَتُهُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ فَيَقُولُ: أَنَّى هَذَا؟ فَيُقَالُ: بِاسْتِغْفَارِ وَلَدِكَ لَكَ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"A man's rank in Jannah is raised, and he asks, 'How did this come to me?' And he is told, 'Through your child's asking forgiveness for you.'"**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

In Sunan Ibn Majah, these words come after a separate statement about the *qintar* (a vast measure of reward), which is not quoted here. "How did this come to me?" is a question of astonishment: where did this rank come from, when I never worked for it? The Arabic word *walad*, rendered here as "child," covers both sons and daughters. Ibn Kathir and al-Busiri judged its chain of transmission to be sound, al-Albani graded it hasan, and some transmitted it as the words of Abu Hurayrah himself rather than of the Prophet (*mawquf*).[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

Asking forgiveness is a small deed within reach of every child, and yet through it Allah may raise a parent in Jannah to a rank that parent never reached by their own deeds.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-1631" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Or a Righteous Child Who Prays for Him"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«إِذَا مَاتَ الْإِنْسَانُ انْقَطَعَ عَنْهُ عَمَلُهُ إِلَّا مِنْ ثَلَاثَةٍ: إِلَّا مِنْ صَدَقَةٍ جَارِيَةٍ، أَوْ عِلْمٍ يُنْتَفَعُ بِهِ، أَوْ وَلَدٍ صَالِحٍ يَدْعُو لَهُ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"When a person dies, their deeds are cut off except for three: an ongoing charity, knowledge from which benefit continues to be drawn, or a righteous child who prays for them."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cited this hadith in his commentary on the ayah of at-Tur, because it shows the other face of the joining: a parent benefiting, after death, from the good deeds and prayers of a righteous child.[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith describes the child as righteous before it mentions the prayer. Raising a child to righteousness, then, is the "ongoing charity" that stays on in the home.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" kind="historical" mode="canonical" -->

### The Account of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and His Sons at His Deathbed

> ذكر ابن كثير في أحداث سنة إحدى ومئة أن عمر بن عبد العزيز لما اشتدّ مرضه قيل له: "هؤلاء بنوك، وكانوا اثني عشر، ألا توصي لهم بشيء؛ فإنهم فقراء؟" فقال: {إِنَّ وَلِيِّيَ اللَّهُ الَّذِي نَزَّلَ الْكِتَابَ ۖ وَهُوَ يَتَوَلَّى الصَّالِحِينَ} [الأعراف: ١٩٦]، "والله لا أعطيهم حقّ أحد، وهم بين رجلين: إما صالح فالله يتولّى الصالحين، وإما غير صالح فما كنت لأعينه على فسقه... ثم استدعى بأولاده فودّعهم وعزّاهم بهذا، وأوصاهم بهذا الكلام، ثم قال: انصرفوا عصمكم الله، وأحسن الخلافة عليكم".
>
> وفي رواية أخرى ذكرها أهل السير أن مسلمة بن عبد الملك دخل عليه فقال: "يا أمير المؤمنين، إنك قد أفغرت أفواه ولدك من هذا المال، فلو أوصيت بهم إليّ وإلى نظرائي من قومك فكفوك مؤونتهم"، وأن عمر لما رأى بنيه "ترقرقت عيناه"، وقال لهم: "يا بنيّ، إني قد تركت لكم خيرًا كثيرًا، لا تمرّون بأحد من المسلمين وأهل ذمتهم إلا رأوا لكم حقًّا... قوموا عصمكم الله، قوموا رزقكم الله".[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Ibn Kathir mentions, among the events of the year 101 AH, that when the illness of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz grew severe, he was told: "These are your sons (there were twelve of them). Will you not make some provision for them in your will? They are poor." He replied: `"Indeed, my Protector is Allah, who sent down the Book, and He takes the righteous into His care"` (al-A'raf 7:196). "By Allah, I will not give them what is owed to anyone else. They will be one of two kinds of men: either righteous, and Allah takes the righteous into His care; or not righteous, and then I would never help him in his wrongdoing... Then he called for his children, bade them farewell, consoled them with this, and left them these words as his counsel. Then he said: 'Go now, may Allah keep you safe from sin, and may He watch over you well after I am gone.'"
>
> In another narration that the biographers mention, Maslamah ibn Abd al-Malik came in to him and said: "O Commander of the Believers, you have left your children with their mouths open and empty of this wealth. If only you would entrust them to me and to my peers among your people, we would spare you the burden of their keep." And when Umar saw his sons, "his eyes welled with tears," and he said to them: "My sons, I have left you a great deal of good: you will not pass by anyone among the Muslims or the non-Muslims under their protection without their seeing that you have a rightful claim on them... Rise, may Allah keep you safe from sin; rise, may Allah provide for you."[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

This is a historical report transmitted by the historians and biographers. It is not a Prophetic hadith, it is not graded by the standards of the hadith critics, and it is cited here as an educational illustration, not as legal proof. At the first ellipsis, the omitted text consists of other narrations of the same sentence's meaning; at the second, it is Umar's weighing of his sons' wealth against the safety of his own Hereafter.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Umar was not indifferent to his sons' future; he simply defined that future differently: a righteousness that Allah takes into His care, not a fortune that helps them disobey Him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-132-134" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "What Will You Worship After Me?"

> **{وَوَصَّىٰ بِهَا إِبْرَاهِيمُ بَنِيهِ وَيَعْقُوبُ يَا بَنِيَّ إِنَّ اللَّهَ اصْطَفَىٰ لَكُمُ الدِّينَ فَلَا تَمُوتُنَّ إِلَّا وَأَنْتُمْ مُسْلِمُونَ ۝ أَمْ كُنْتُمْ شُهَدَاءَ إِذْ حَضَرَ يَعْقُوبَ الْمَوْتُ إِذْ قَالَ لِبَنِيهِ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ بَعْدِي قَالُوا نَعْبُدُ إِلَٰهَكَ وَإِلَٰهَ آبَائِكَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ إِلَٰهًا وَاحِدًا وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ ۝ تِلْكَ أُمَّةٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ ۖ لَهَا مَا كَسَبَتْ وَلَكُمْ مَا كَسَبْتُمْ ۖ وَلَا تُسْأَلُونَ عَمَّا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ}** [الْبَقَرَة: ١٣٢-١٣٤][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And Ibrahim enjoined this upon his sons, and so did Ya'qub: 'My sons, Allah has chosen this religion for you, so do not die except as Muslims.' Or were you witnesses when death came to Ya'qub, when he said to his sons, 'What will you worship after me?' They said, 'We will worship your God and the God of your fathers, Ibrahim, Isma'il, and Ishaq, one God, and to Him we submit as Muslims.' That was a community that has passed away. It will have what it earned, and you will have what you have earned, and you will not be asked about what they used to do."** (al-Baqarah 2:132-134)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that Ibrahim enjoined upon his sons "this way of life, which is submission (*islam*) to Allah," and that `so do not die except as Muslims` means: "Do good while you are alive and hold fast to this, so that Allah grants you death upon it." Of the last ayah he said that the prophets and righteous people who went before "will not benefit you by your being descended from them if you do not do good whose benefit returns to you."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Ya'qub, peace be upon him, did not rely on lineage. In his final moments he asked his sons about their worship, and their answer, `and to Him we submit as Muslims`, is exactly what `whose descendants followed them in faith` means.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of at-Tur lays down the principle; the ayat of ar-Ra'd and Ghafir widen it to include fathers and spouses; and the two hadiths open the opposite direction, in which a child raises a parent. Ya'qub and Umar then show how a parent lives this meaning out: their last concern is the righteousness of their descendants.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. How does the ayah of at-Tur hold together the grace of the joining and the justice of `Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned`?
2. What does the condition `whoever was righteous` add in the ayat of ar-Ra'd and Ghafir?
3. Why was the man in the hadith of Ibn Majah astonished, asking "How did this come to me?", and what does that teach you about the effect of asking forgiveness for someone?
4. How do the account of Umar and the counsel of Ya'qub differ in their degree of authentication, and how does that affect the way we cite them?
5. If you were asked today, "What do you want to leave behind for those who come after you?", what would you answer?

### Activity: The Two-Way Du'a Chain

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.027.activity.two-way-dua-chain" -->

Draw a chain of links. In the upper links, write symbols for people who came before you and for whom you want to pray: parents, grandparents, a teacher, or whoever raised you. Choose a fixed time each day, after a particular prayer, and make du'a for them, asking forgiveness for those for whom it is permissible to ask forgiveness; add a link each time you do so, for seven days. In the final, lowest link, write one sentence in the spirit of Ya'qub: "What do I want to remain after me?", together with one practical step toward it.

### A Story from Our History: Umar's Gift to His Sons

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a story the historians tell about a good man from our history. It is not a hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" audience="4-7" -->

Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, may Allah have mercy on him, was the leader of the Muslims, and he ruled over many, many lands. He had twelve sons. Even though he was the leader, he never took any of the Muslims' money that did not belong to him, so his sons were poor.

When Umar became very sick near the end of his life, someone said to him, "Your sons are poor. Won't you leave them some money?"

Umar loved his sons. But he said: I will not give them money that is not theirs. Then he recited an ayah from the Qur'an that means: Allah is the One who looks after good people and keeps them safe.

Then he called his sons to him, said goodbye to them, and made du'a for them. He said, "May Allah keep you safe."[^6]

Umar left his sons something worth more than money: being good. And Allah looks after the good.

### A Story from the Qur'an: Ya'qub's Question for His Sons

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-132-134" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-132-134" audience="4-7" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an.**

Ya'qub, peace be upon him, was one of Allah's prophets, and his grandfather was the Prophet Ibrahim, peace be upon him. Ya'qub had lots of sons.

When Ya'qub came near the end of his life, he talked to his sons. He didn't ask them about houses. He didn't ask them about money. He asked them just one important question: "What will you worship after I am gone?"

And his sons answered: "We will worship your God, the God of your fathers Ibrahim, Isma'il, and Ishaq, the One God, and to Him we give ourselves as Muslims."[^7]

The most important thing to Ya'qub was that his sons would keep loving Allah and worshipping Him alone. And that is the kind of family Allah brings together in Jannah: a family that loves Allah.

### Short Questions

1. What gift did Umar leave his sons instead of money?

### A Story from Our History: The Leader Who Left His Sons No Money

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a historical report told by the historians. It is not a Prophetic hadith, so we learn from it without treating it as a religious text.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" audience="8-12" -->

About thirteen hundred years ago, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, may Allah have mercy on him, was the caliph, the leader of all the Muslims. He was famous for his justice, and he never took anything from the Muslims' public treasury beyond what was rightly his. So his twelve sons lived as poor boys, even though their father was the most powerful man in the land.

Then Umar fell into his last illness, and someone said to him: "These sons of yours are poor. Won't you set something aside for them in your will?" It was an easy chance: one word from him, and his sons would be given a fortune.

But Umar saw that this money was not theirs. So he recited Allah's words: "Indeed, my Protector is Allah, who sent down the Book, and He takes the righteous into His care." And he said something like this: By Allah, I will not give them what belongs to someone else. My sons will turn out one of two ways. Either a son is righteous, and then Allah takes care of him and looks after him; or he is not, and then I will not use money to help him disobey Allah.

Then he called his sons, said goodbye to them, and told them: "Go now, may Allah keep you safe from sin, and may He watch over you well after I am gone." In other words: may Allah protect you from doing wrong and take good care of you once I am no longer here.[^6]

Umar did not leave his sons palaces. He left them advice that would lead them to Allah.

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Parting Advice of Ya'qub, Peace Be upon Him

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-132-134" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-132-134" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true story that Allah tells us in Surah al-Baqarah.**

The Qur'an tells us that Ibrahim, peace be upon him, urged his sons to hold firm to Islam, and his grandson Ya'qub, peace be upon him, did the same. Each of them said: "My sons, Allah has chosen this religion for you, so do not die except as Muslims."

Then the Qur'an describes a very special moment. When death came to Ya'qub, he asked his sons: "What will you worship after me?" He didn't ask, "How will you make a living?" or "What will you own?" And they answered: "We will worship your God and the God of your fathers, Ibrahim, Isma'il, and Ishaq, one God, and to Him we submit as Muslims."

Right after that, Allah says: "That was a community that has passed away. It will have what it earned, and you will have what you have earned." In other words, every generation has its own deeds. Even the children of prophets need to choose faith for themselves.[^7]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

You might hear, "My family is religious, so I'm fine." Or: "My family is nowhere near religious, so why even try?" The Qur'an answers both. Allah says: "And those who believed, and whose descendants followed them in faith, We shall join their descendants with them." Out of His grace, Allah gathers the believer together with their believing descendants at one and the same rank. Then He closes the ayah: "Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned."[^1] Your family tree cannot carry your choices for you, and the lack of a righteous family tree cannot stop you from starting one yourself.

In fact, you might be the reason someone else is raised. In a hasan hadith, a man's rank in Jannah is raised, and he asks, "How did this come to me?" He is told: `Through your child's asking forgiveness for you`.[^4] And in Sahih Muslim, when a person dies their deeds are cut off except for three, one of which is `a righteous child who prays for them`.[^5] Even if your relationship with one of your parents is difficult, praying for them remains an open door, and no one else ever has to see it.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-52-21" -->

- **`Held in pledge (rahin)`** — bound to one's own deeds and answerable for them; no one carries anyone else's deeds.
- **`We shall not deprive them (wa ma alatnahum)`** — We did not reduce the parents' reward in the slightest when We raised their children up to them.[^1]
- **`He takes the righteous into His care (yatawalla as-salihin)`** — He looks after them, protects them, and is enough for them. This is the ayah Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz relied on.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A Story from Our History: Pressure at the Sickbed

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a historical report mentioned by Ibn Kathir and the biographers. It is not a Prophetic hadith, so we take its lesson without treating it as legal proof.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="history-umar-ibn-abd-al-aziz-sons" audience="13+" -->

In the year 101 AH, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, may Allah have mercy on him, was caliph of the Muslims, and his illness had taken a serious turn. He had twelve sons, and they were poor, because he had barred himself and his family from the Muslims' public treasury except for what was rightfully theirs.

According to one narration, the person who spoke to him was Maslamah ibn Abd al-Malik, one of the leading princes of the ruling family: You have left your sons with nothing. If only you would make provision for them in your will! It was a reasonable request, from a relative who cared, at a moment of weakness.

But Umar saw that this "security" would come out of what belonged to other people. So he recited: "Indeed, my Protector is Allah, who sent down the Book, and He takes the righteous into His care." And he said: My sons will be one of two kinds of men. Either a son is righteous, and Allah will take care of him; or he is not, and then I would never help him in his disobedience. Then he called his sons in. That narration says his eyes welled with tears when he saw them, and he told them he had left them a great deal of good: a reputation that would make people see they had a rightful claim. Then he said: "Rise, may Allah keep you safe from sin; rise, may Allah provide for you."[^6]

Umar was not hard-hearted; his tears show how much he loved them. But he refused to buy his sons' comfort with what was not his, and he staked everything on their righteousness and on Allah's care for them.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: Ya'qub's Final Question

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-132-134" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-132-134" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an, in Surah al-Baqarah.**

Ya'qub, peace be upon him, was a prophet, the son of a prophet, the grandson of a prophet. If lineage were enough, he could have rested easy about his sons. Yet the Qur'an paints the scene as death came to him: he asked his sons, "What will you worship after me?" And they answered: "We will worship your God and the God of your fathers, Ibrahim, Isma'il, and Ishaq, one God, and to Him we submit as Muslims."

Notice that they said "your God and the God of your fathers," and then added "and to Him we submit as Muslims." They didn't stop at naming their fathers' faith; they declared their own. Then Allah comments: "That was a community that has passed away. It will have what it earned, and you will have what you have earned." Ibn Kathir explains that being descended from prophets and righteous people does nothing for someone who does not do good whose benefit returns to him.[^7]

This is exactly the condition in the ayah of at-Tur: descendants who followed their parents "in faith," not in name only.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the ayah of at-Tur using the commentators' views on the meaning of "descendants," connects it with the ayat of ar-Ra'd and Ghafir, narrates the hadiths of Ibn Majah 3660 and Muslim 1631 together with the grading of each, distinguishes infallible revealed text from the historical account of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, and starts a week-long du'a chain with the sentence "What do I want to remain after me?"

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Printed copies of the seven pieces of evidence; a mushaf; a board divided into two columns, "Grace" and "Justice"; an activity sheet showing an empty chain of eight links; pens in two colors.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews Ibn Kathir's commentary on the ayah of at-Tur and Ibn al-Qayyim's section on the joining of descendants in *Hadi al-Arwah*; the grading of the Ibn Majah hadith and what the scholars said about it (its chain judged sound, its grading as hasan, and its transmission as Abu Hurayrah's own words); and the text of the Umar account in *al-Bidayah wa an-Nihayah*. The teacher also prepares a calm answer to questions about non-Muslim relatives (see the cautions).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "If you were told that someone might raise your rank in Jannah after your death through some small deed, who would you expect it to be?" Gather the answers without comment, then say: "Let's see what revelation says."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of at-Tur in full and ask the learners to place each of its phrases under either the "Grace" column or the "Justice" column. Then read the ayat of ar-Ra'd and Ghafir and ask: What do they add to the ayah of at-Tur? Next, the hadiths of Ibn Majah and Muslim, with a brief explanation of the grading of each. Then read the ayat of Ya'qub's parting counsel, and finally the account of Umar, stating explicitly that it is a historical report.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher briefly discusses the three views on the meaning of "descendants," then asks: "Why does the ayah close with `Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned` immediately after mentioning the joining?" Guide the learners to see that grace does not cancel individual responsibility, as the ayah `That was a community that has passed away` also makes clear.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner begins the Two-Way Du'a Chain as described in the activity unit: writing symbols in the upper links, choosing the fixed prayer, then writing their sentence and its practical step in the lowest link. Anyone who wishes may share their lowest-link sentence only.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes two lines: the condition for the joining in the ayah of at-Tur, and the grading of the hadith "through your child's asking forgiveness for you." Close by reading Allah's words in the angels' prayer (Ghafir 40:8), and remind the learners that there will be a check-in after seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayah of at-Tur, the Ibn Majah hadith, and the story of Ya'qub are enough. Advanced learners are asked to read Ibn al-Qayyim's section on the joining of descendants and to summarize the three views, with the argument for each, in a paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not pronounce on the fate of any specific relative, and do not open a public discussion about learners' relatives. If someone asks about a non-Muslim parent, explain that praying for their guidance during their lifetime is legitimate and that treating them with kindness and respect is obligatory, and refer questions about particular cases after death to a trusted scholar in a one-to-one conversation. Be sensitive to anyone who has lost their parents or lived through divorce or a parent's absence, and mention that the chain includes any Muslim who came before them and raised them or showed them kindness. Do not present the account of Umar as a hadith, and do not use it to disparage anyone from the Umayyad family. As for the widely circulated report about the wealth of Umar's sons and the poverty of other men's sons, its narrator is unnamed in the source, so do not present it as established.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the relationship between grace and individual responsibility in the ayah of at-Tur, distinguishes Qur'anic text from historical report, applies private du'a to a real family relationship, and writes a personal answer to Ya'qub's question.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The evidence texts; private notebooks that the teacher sees only with the student's permission; two large cards bearing the sentences "My family is religious, so I'm fine" and "My family is far from the deen, so what's the point."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the two narrations of the Umar account and how they differ, and the grading of the Ibn Majah hadith, and gets ready to discuss sensitive questions about non-Muslim relatives and strained relationships with parents.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the two cards and ask the students: "Which of these two sentences is more dangerous, and why?" Leave the answer open until the end of the lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah of at-Tur and the hadiths of Ibn Majah and Muslim, then the story of Ya'qub, then the account of Umar, with discussion: How do the two accounts differ in their degree of authentication? And how can we benefit from a historical report without turning it into proof?

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher returns to the two cards and shows that `Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned` and `That was a community that has passed away` reject the first, while the joining and the hadith of asking forgiveness reject the second, because a young person may be the first righteous link in their family. Then ask: What pressure did Umar face? And when do you face similar pressure from people you love?

**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student starts their private chain as described in the activity unit, writing the symbols and choosing the prayer, and is given quiet time with no required sharing.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->

**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, the ayah of at-Tur and the story of Ya'qub are enough. Advanced students compare the three commentators' views on the meaning of "descendants" and explain how each view shapes the understanding of the ayah.

**Teaching Cautions:** The notebooks are private and are not collected. If a student discloses abuse or neglect at home, do not tell them to "just make du'a"; follow the institution's approved safeguarding procedure. Do not pronounce on the fate of any specific relative, and refer legal questions about relatives who died outside Islam to a trusted scholar in a one-to-one conversation. Make clear that praying for one's parents does not mean accepting mistreatment or giving up healthy boundaries in the relationship.
