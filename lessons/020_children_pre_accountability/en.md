# The Little Ones Who Went Ahead of Us to Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner comes away certain that a child whom Allah takes back to Himself before reaching the age of accountability is not held to account for anything, because the Pen has been lifted from that child, and that, by Allah's grace and mercy, such a child is among the people of Jannah. The learner meets the hadith of "the little ones of Jannah" (*da'amis al-jannah*), which shows that a young child does not forget his parents but waits for them with love, ready to take hold of their garment and lead them into Jannah; and the hadith of the dream related by Samurah ibn Jundab, may Allah be pleased with him, which pictures Allah's Prophet Ibrahim, peace be upon him, as a tender guardian of these children in a beautiful green garden in Jannah. The learner understands that these two glad tidings are a source of hope and mercy, not an occasion for sorrow, and that whoever loses a child has with Allah an immense reward and a means of entering Jannah. As part of the lesson's outcomes, each learner, according to age, also carries out the "Garden Lantern" activity, expressing through a drawing or in writing the quiet light and loving wait that this scene represents, without being drawn into painful details; and learns by heart the du'a "O Allah, have mercy on the weak of our ummah and on our children," keeping its meaning in mind.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Few things bring a believer's heart greater peace than knowing that Allah, glory be to Him, has not made a child answerable for anything until that child reaches the age of accountability. The Pen has been lifted from the child: no bad deed is written against them, and no shortcoming is brought to account. So when Allah takes a little one back to Himself before that age, the child is with their Lord in a mercy untouched by reckoning or punishment, a mercy made only of glad tidings and grace. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, made these glad tidings plain to his ummah in more than one authentic hadith, and he promised parents who bear their loss with patience, seeking its reward from Allah, that the children who have gone ahead of them into the Hereafter hold a great station with Him. They are in His Jannah, in honored and gentle care, waiting for their parents with love rather than sorrow. More than that, they become a means by which their parents themselves enter Jannah.

As for the ruling on the children of non-believers who die before reaching accountability, an explicit Prophetic statement on it appears within the long dream hadith cited in this lesson (see the last passage in the evidence unit). The scholars have discussed the question with courtesy and respect for one another, and in all of it the final word belongs to Allah's justice and to His mercy, which encompasses all things.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-21" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Those Who Believed, and Whose Offspring Followed Them in Faith, We Shall Join with Them Their Offspring"

> **وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَاتَّبَعَتْهُمْ ذُرِّيَّتُهُمْ بِإِيمَانٍ أَلْحَقْنَا بِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَمَا أَلَتْنَاهُمْ مِنْ عَمَلِهِمْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ ۚ كُلُّ امْرِئٍ بِمَا كَسَبَ رَهِينٌ.** [الطُّورِ: ٢١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who believed, and whose offspring followed them in faith, We shall join with them their offspring, and We shall not deprive them of anything of their deeds. Every person is held in pledge for what he has earned."** (at-Tur 52:21)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators explain that this ayah gives the believers glad tidings: Allah will join their offspring with them in Jannah as an honor to the parents' faith, without reducing the reward of the parents' own deeds in the slightest. More than one scholar has drawn on this ayah, together with others such as the words of Allah, exalted is He, `And never would We punish until We had sent a messenger` (al-Isra' 17:15), as proof that young offspring are not held in pledge for deeds they have not yet earned.

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah is the Qur'anic root of every hadith that follows in this lesson. Allah does not let the bond between a believer and his offspring be lost; rather, by His mercy He reunites them in Jannah, and not by reckoning with a child who was never yet made accountable.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="hadith.rafa_al-qalam" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Pen Has Been Lifted from Three"

> عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«رُفِعَ الْقَلَمُ عَنْ ثَلَاثَةٍ: عَنِ الصَّبِيِّ حَتَّى يَبْلُغَ، وَعَنِ النَّائِمِ حَتَّى يَسْتَيْقِظَ، وَعَنِ الْمَجْنُونِ حَتَّى يَعْقِلَ»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The Pen has been lifted from three: from the child until he reaches maturity, from the sleeper until he wakes, and from the insane until he regains his reason."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators on this hadith explain that "the Pen has been lifted" means that the angels charged with recording deeds write no sin or act of disobedience against these three, because their capacity for accountability is not complete: the child has not yet reached full understanding and responsibility, the sleeper's awareness is absent, and the insane person has lost the power of discernment.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is the legal foundation for the very title of this lesson. Not a single bad deed is written against a child who has not reached maturity. So if Allah takes that child before maturity, the child meets their Lord carrying no sin to be reckoned with, but as pure as on the day they were born.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari.muslim.three_children" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "There Is No Muslim Who Loses Three Children Who Have Not Reached the Age of Accountability"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«مَا مِنَ الْمُسْلِمِينَ مَنْ يُتَوَفَّى لَهُ ثَلَاثَةٌ مِنَ الْوَلَدِ لَمْ يَبْلُغُوا الْحِنْثَ، إِلَّا أَدْخَلَهُ اللَّهُ الْجَنَّةَ بِفَضْلِ رَحْمَتِهِ إِيَّاهُمْ»**. مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"There is no Muslim who loses three of his children before they reach the age of accountability, but that Allah will admit him into Jannah by the grace of His mercy toward them."** Agreed upon (al-Bukhari and Muslim).[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The scholars say that "before they reach the age of accountability" (literally, "have not reached *al-hinth*") means they had not yet reached the age at which a person's sins begin to be recorded against them. They add that the parent's entry into Jannah here is a favor from Allah, not something earned by the child's deeds, since the child has no deeds yet.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith turns a gaze of grief into a gaze of hope. The child whom parents lose is not a loss and nothing more; that child becomes a means of raising the parents to Jannah, if they are patient and seek their reward from Allah. In this way they gather two things at once: the reward of patience and the intercession of their child.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim.ahmad.daaamees_al_jannah" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Their Little Ones Are the *Da'amis* of Jannah"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«صِغَارُهُمْ دَعَامِيصُ الْجَنَّةِ، يَتَلَقَّى أَحَدُهُمْ أَبَاهُ - أَوْ قَالَ: أَبَوَيْهِ - فَيَأْخُذُ بِثَوْبِهِ - أَوْ قَالَ: بِيَدِهِ - كَمَا آخُذُ أَنَا بِصَنِفَةِ ثَوْبِكَ هَذَا، فَلَا يَتَنَاهَى - أَوْ قَالَ: فَلَا يَنْتَهِي - حَتَّى يُدْخِلَهُ اللَّهُ وَإِيَّاهُ الْجَنَّةَ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Their little ones are the *da'amis* of Jannah. One of them meets his father (or he said: his parents) and takes hold of his garment (or he said: his hand), just as I am now taking hold of the hem of this garment of yours, and he does not let go (or he said: he does not stop) until Allah admits him, and his father with him, into Jannah."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The lexicographers explain that *da'amis* is the plural of *du'mus*, a tiny creature that darts and tumbles through water freely and at ease. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, likened the little ones of Jannah to these creatures because they roam about it so freely, delighting in its bliss without effort or fear. *As-sanifah* means the edge or hem of a garment.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith paints the most beautiful picture of a child who has passed into Allah's mercy. The child has not forgotten their father or mother. Instead, they stand waiting with joy, and the moment they see their parents, they take hold of their garment and will not let go until the two enter Jannah together. Losing a child, then, is neither forgetting nor severance; it is love waiting for a reunion yet to come, by Allah's leave.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of the Dream Related by Samurah ibn Jundab, May Allah Be Pleased with Him: Ibrahim, Peace Be upon Him, and the Children of the People

> كَانَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ يَقُصُّ عَلَى أَصْحَابِهِ فِي كَثِيرٍ مِنَ الصَّبَاحَاتِ مَا رَآهُ فِي مَنَامِهِ، وَقَدْ رَوَى عَنْهُ سَمُرَةُ بْنُ جُنْدَبٍ رضي الله عنه رُؤْيَا طَوِيلَةً ذَاتَ مَشَاهِدَ كَثِيرَةٍ، فِيهَا آتِيَانِ أَخَذَا بِيَدِهِ ﷺ يُطْلِعَانِهِ عَلَى أَحْوَالِ النَّاسِ فِي الْبَرْزَخِ [وَقَدِ اقْتُصِرَ هُنَا عَلَى مَوْضِعِ الشَّاهِدِ مِنْهَا، وَحُذِفَتْ بَقِيَّةُ مَشَاهِدِهَا الطَّوِيلَةِ لِخُرُوجِهَا عَنْ مَوْضُوعِ هَذَا الدَّرْسِ]: قَالَ ﷺ: **«فَانْطَلَقْنَا حَتَّى أَتَيْنَا عَلَى رَوْضَةٍ مُعْتَمَّةٍ، فِيهَا مِنْ كُلِّ لَوْنِ الرَّبِيعِ، وَإِذَا فِي وَسَطِ الرَّوْضَةِ رَجُلٌ طَوِيلٌ لَا أَكَادُ أَرَى رَأْسَهُ طُولًا فِي السَّمَاءِ، وَإِذَا حَوْلَهُ مِنْ أَكْثَرِ وِلْدَانٍ رَأَيْتُهُمْ قَطُّ. قُلْتُ لَهُمَا: مَا هَذَا؟ وَمَنْ هَؤُلَاءِ؟»** حَتَّى انْتَهَيَا بِهِ إِلَى مَوْضِعِ الْبَيَانِ، فَقَالَا لَهُ: **«أَمَّا الرَّجُلُ الطَّوِيلُ الَّذِي فِي الرَّوْضَةِ فَإِبْرَاهِيمُ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ، وَأَمَّا الْوِلْدَانُ الَّذِينَ حَوْلَهُ فَكُلُّ مَوْلُودٍ مَاتَ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ»**. فَقَالَ بَعْضُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، وَأَوْلَادُ الْمُشْرِكِينَ؟ فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«وَأَوْلَادُ الْمُشْرِكِينَ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On many mornings the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, would tell his Companions what he had seen in his sleep. Samurah ibn Jundab, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from him a long dream with many scenes, in which two visitors took him by the hand, peace and blessings be upon him, and showed him the states of people in the *barzakh* [only the passage relevant to this lesson is given here; the remaining long scenes of the dream have been left out because they lie outside the subject of this lesson]. He said, peace and blessings be upon him: **"We went on until we came to a garden so lush it was dark with green, holding every color of spring. And there, in the middle of the garden, was a tall man; so tall was he, reaching up into the sky, that I could scarcely see his head. And around him were more children than I had ever seen. I said to the two of them: 'What is this? And who are these?'"** At length the two brought him to the point where all was explained, and they said to him: **"As for the tall man in the garden, he is Ibrahim, peace be upon him. And as for the children around him, they are every child who died upon the *fitrah*."** Some of the Muslims asked, "O Messenger of Allah, and the children of the polytheists?" The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"And the children of the polytheists."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Dr. Umar Sulayman al-Ashqar cites this passage of the long dream hadith in his book *al-Jannah wa an-Nar* (Paradise and Hell) as evidence of the destiny of the young children of those who affirm Allah's oneness. He notes that the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him, "And the children of the polytheists," are an explicit text bearing on a well-known question over which the scholars differed long ago. Some took this addition at face value and held that the children of the polytheists, too, are among the people of Jannah, in keeping with the general wording "died upon the *fitrah*" and with the hadith "Every child is born upon the *fitrah*." Others withheld judgment on them and left the matter to Allah's knowledge and will. This is a legitimate difference among eminent scholars, and all of it comes back to one truth: Allah wrongs no one by so much as the weight of a speck of dust, and He is far too knowing and far too merciful to call to account anyone to whom the proof never came.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is the heart of the lesson. A child who dies young does not go into the unknown, nor into loneliness, but into a beautiful green garden in Jannah, where the father of the prophets, Ibrahim, peace be upon him, watches over them with tender care, as a loving grandfather watches over his grandchildren. This scene alone is enough to turn the grief of loss into peace and hope.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What does it mean that "the Pen has been lifted" from the child, and how does this relate to the destiny of a child who dies before maturity?
2. How does the hadith "There is no Muslim who loses three children..." shift parents' outlook from grief alone to hope?
3. What does the hadith of "the *da'amis* of Jannah" reveal about the bond between a child and their parents after the child has passed into Allah's mercy?
4. Why did Allah choose Ibrahim, peace be upon him, in particular, to be the guardian of the children of the people in that garden, judging from what you know of his life and his tenderness?
5. How would you answer, with courtesy and compassion, a family who have lost a young child and ask you about the child's destiny with Allah?

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Islam teaches us that a child who has not yet reached "the age of accountability" is not held to account for anything, because the Pen has been lifted from them. That means their deeds are not written down against them the way grown-ups' deeds are. So if Allah takes a young child back to Himself before they grow up, that child is with Allah in a tremendous mercy, with no reckoning and no punishment. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "There is no Muslim who loses three of his children before they reach the age of accountability, but that Allah will admit him into Jannah by the grace of His mercy toward them."[^3] So a young child is a reason for their parents to enter Jannah, and not simply a loss.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: The Prophet's Dream and the Garden of Ibrahim, Peace Be upon Him

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Samurah ibn Jundab, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would often tell his Companions about what he had seen in his sleep, and Samurah ibn Jundab, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from him a long dream in which he said: "We went on until we came to a garden so lush it was dark with green, holding every color of spring. And there, in the middle of the garden, was a tall man; so tall was he, reaching up into the sky, that I could scarcely see his head. And around him were more children than I had ever seen." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked his two companions in the dream about this scene, and they told him: "As for the tall man in the garden, he is Ibrahim, peace be upon him. And as for the children around him, they are every child who died upon the *fitrah*."[^5]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: in his sleep the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw a beautiful green garden where Ibrahim, peace be upon him, was looking after a huge number of children gathered around him, children who had died young, before reaching the age of accountability. The father of the prophets had become their gentle, caring guardian in that beautiful garden inside Jannah.[^5]

### A True Hadith: The *Da'amis* of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim.ahmad.daaamees_al_jannah" authenticated="true" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Their little ones are the *da'amis* of Jannah. One of them meets his father and takes hold of his garment (just as I am now taking hold of the hem of this garment of yours), and he does not let go until Allah admits him, and his father with him, into Jannah."[^4]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim.ahmad.daaamees_al_jannah" audience="8-12" -->

Picture this beautiful scene with me: a little child is waiting at the gate of Jannah. They see their father coming, take hold of the edge of his clothes lovingly, and never let go until the two of them enter Jannah together. A young child who has passed into Allah's mercy still loves their parents and longs to be with them again.[^4]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="hadith.rafa_al-qalam" -->

- **`The age of accountability (sinn at-taklif; al-hinth)`** — the age at which a person becomes answerable to Allah for what they do; before it, the Pen is lifted from the child, so they are not held to account.[^2]
- **`The da'amis of Jannah (da'amis al-jannah)`** — a description of the little ones of Jannah, who roam about it freely and happily, the way a tiny water creature darts and tumbles through water.[^4]
- **`The natural pure state (al-fitrah)`** — the pure, natural state in which every human being is born, before growing up and becoming accountable.[^5]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In Islam, the idea of "the age of accountability" rests on a simple principle: religious obligation depends on reason and maturity. The Pen has been lifted from the child until they reach maturity, so no bad deed is written against them before then. That is why the children of believers who die before that age are among the people of Jannah, by Allah's grace, and not because of deeds that were never yet recorded. As for the children of non-believers who die before reaching accountability, an explicit Prophetic statement about them appears within the dream hadith you will meet in this lesson. Even so, some details of the question have remained a matter of scholarly reasoning (*ijtihad*) for centuries. This is a legitimate difference of opinion and nothing that should unsettle anyone, because the whole matter comes back to Allah's justice and His vast mercy.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: The Dream Related by Samurah ibn Jundab, May Allah Be Pleased with Him

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Samurah ibn Jundab, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

On most mornings it was the habit of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to tell his Companions what he had seen in his sleep the night before. Samurah ibn Jundab, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from him a long dream of many scenes, in which two visitors took him and showed him the states of people. Among those scenes, he said, peace and blessings be upon him: "We went on until we came to a garden so lush it was dark with green, holding every color of spring. And there, in the middle of the garden, was a tall man; so tall was he, reaching up into the sky, that I could scarcely see his head. And around him were more children than I had ever seen. I said to the two of them: 'What is this? And who are these?'"[^5]

At the end of the dream, when the two visitors explained to him everything he had seen, they said of this scene in particular: "As for the tall man in the garden, he is Ibrahim, peace be upon him. And as for the children around him, they are every child who died upon the *fitrah*." Some of those present asked, "O Messenger of Allah, and the children of the polytheists?" And he said, peace and blessings be upon him: "And the children of the polytheists."[^5]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" audience="13+" -->

Put another way: in his sleep Allah showed His Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, a true scene of a garden in Jannah, where Ibrahim, peace be upon him (the father of the prophets and the intimate friend of the Most Merciful), cares for every child who died upon the *fitrah* before becoming accountable, with the tender care of a loving grandfather for his grandchildren. And when the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was asked outright about the children of the polytheists, he included them in this same ruling. That answer led the scholars to discuss the question in greater depth over the centuries, though some of its details remain a matter of *ijtihad*.[^5]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.authenticated-story" kind="authenticated_story" -->

### A True Hadith: The *Da'amis* of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim.ahmad.daaamees_al_jannah" authenticated="true" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Their little ones are the *da'amis* of Jannah. One of them meets his father (or he said: his parents) and takes hold of his garment (or he said: his hand), just as I am now taking hold of the hem of this garment of yours, and he does not let go until Allah admits him, and his father with him, into Jannah."[^4]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim.ahmad.daaamees_al_jannah" audience="13+" -->

This hadith redefines what loss itself means. The bond between a child and their parents is not cut when the child dies young. It turns into intercession and a loving wait on the Day of Resurrection, until the child holds fast to the garment of their father or mother and will not let go until they enter Jannah together. This means that patience and *ihtisab* when a young child is lost are not merely enduring pain; they are an investment in a relationship that will bear fruit in a reunion, and a togetherness that lasts forever, by Allah's leave.[^4]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="hadith.rafa_al-qalam" -->

- **`Religious accountability (at-taklif)`** — the Shari'ah's binding of a sane, mature person to its commands and prohibitions; before maturity, a person is not described as accountable and is not called to account.[^2]
- **`The natural pure state (al-fitrah)`** — the sound, natural state in which every human being was created, before their surroundings shape them or they reach the age of accountability.[^5]
- **`Seeking the reward (al-ihtisab)`** — a believer's patience under hardship while hoping for its reward from Allah, rather than merely putting up with it without a thought for that reward.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. How does the hadith of Samurah ibn Jundab's dream show that the destiny of a child who dies young is neither unknown nor uncertain?
2. Why was the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him, to the question "And the children of the polytheists?" so important for this question in particular?
3. How does the hadith of "the *da'amis* of Jannah" change your understanding of what it means to "lose" a young child?
4. How could you help a friend or relative who has lost a young child find real hope in these two hadiths, rather than just words of comfort?
5. Why do some details about the destiny of the children of non-believers remain a matter of scholarly *ijtihad*, even though there is an explicit text, and how can that bring you peace rather than worry?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="50" -->

### Adults — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the legal basis for a child not being held to account before reaching the age of accountability, narrates the hadith of "the *da'amis* of Jannah" and the hadith of Samurah ibn Jundab's dream faithfully, and responds with courtesy and compassion to someone who has lost a young child.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf for referring to the ayah of at-Tur; small blank cards for the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** Before the session, the teacher reviews the five pieces of evidence with their sources and gradings, gently confirms whether anyone in the group has suffered a recent loss, and prepares to set a calm, compassionate tone rather than a dry legal debate.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What comes to mind when you hear that a young child has passed into Allah's mercy?" and then listens attentively without correcting anyone straight away.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of at-Tur, then the hadith of the lifting of the Pen, then the hadith of the three children, then the hadith of "the *da'amis* of Jannah," then the hadith of Samurah ibn Jundab's dream in full, pausing especially at the question "And the children of the polytheists?" and its answer.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** The teacher draws the five pieces of evidence together into one complete picture: the lifting of accountability, then the reward for the parents, then the child's enduring love, then the scene of the garden and the care of Ibrahim, peace be upon him. Alongside this, the teacher gives a brief, balanced account of the scholarly difference over the details of the ruling on the children of non-believers, without decisively favoring one view.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Learners carry out the "Garden of Hope" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each learner is asked to say, in one sentence, how this lesson has changed the way they understand the loss of a young child.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For learners with a background in Islamic scholarship, the discussion can be widened to the views of an-Nawawi and Ibn Taymiyyah on the children of non-believers; for others, it is enough to cover the first four pieces of evidence and a summary of the fifth hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** This lesson must never be used to pronounce a verdict on a particular child whom a family present has lost. Anyone who appears deeply affected should be offered a supportive one-to-one conversation after the session. The focus of the gathering stays on mercy and hope, not on legal debate.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="18" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 18 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child can describe again, in a simple sentence, the beautiful garden where Ibrahim, peace be upon him, looks after the children, and feels reassured, not frightened, while hearing the lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Paper and colors; a du'a card in large, clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver prepares a calm, reassuring voice, avoids any word that might frighten the child, and reviews the two simplified retellings before the session.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver asks the child: "Do you like beautiful green gardens full of flowers?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** Tell the story of the garden of Ibrahim, peace be upon him, in a warm, calm voice, then mention briefly that the little child there waits lovingly for their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 3 minutes:** The caregiver reminds the child that Allah loves children very much, and that Jannah is a place of light and happiness where there is no sadness.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 5 minutes:** The child carries out the "Garden Lantern" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 1 minute:** Observe whether the child can describe the garden in their own words; then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, one sentence is enough: "Allah loves children, and He takes them to a beautiful garden."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not mention any detail at all about death, illness, or pain. Always use the expression "passed into Allah's mercy" or something similar. If the child asks a hard, direct question, answer with a short, reassuring sentence and then turn the conversation back to the garden and its happiness.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="30" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the meaning of "the age of accountability" and "the *da'amis* of Jannah," and connects the two hadiths with Ibrahim's care, peace be upon him, for the children in Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards for the three terms; a glass jar and colored paper for making the lantern; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares the lantern materials in advance and reviews the three terms so as to explain them clearly and simply.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask the students: "What do you think about someone being completely excused from being held to account for what they do? Who is that person in our religion?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Briefly explain the hadith of the three children, then tell the full story of the Prophet's dream, peace and blessings be upon him, then the hadith of "the *da'amis* of Jannah," explaining the terms as you read.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher discusses with the students the meaning of "before they reach the age of accountability" (*lam yablughu al-hinth*), why Allah chose Ibrahim, peace be upon him, in particular, to care for the children, and how the hadith of the *da'amis* describes a child's enduring love for their parents.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 6 minutes:** Students carry out the "A Lantern for the Garden of Jannah" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 2 minutes:** Each student is asked to explain aloud, in their own words, the meaning of one of the three terms; then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, cover only the hadith of the three children and the hadith of "the *da'amis* of Jannah"; for advanced students, add the full detail of the dream story along with the question about the children of the polytheists.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention a child's passing into Allah's mercy briefly, in a way that is not frightening, and without going into its cause. Do not ask any student directly whether they have lost a brother, sister, or young relative; leave them free to share if they wish, without any pressure.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the relationship between the lifting of accountability from the young and Allah's mercy toward the children of believers, discusses the question of the children of non-believers with courtesy, and writes a personal reflection on the meaning of loss and hope.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a sheet for the writing activity; the term cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares for a balanced discussion of the question of the children of non-believers, and is ready to support any student who may have lost a brother, sister, or young relative.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Pose the question: "Have you ever tried to comfort someone who lost a young child? What did you say to them?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the four hadiths, focusing especially on the dream hadith and the question "And the children of the polytheists?", with a brief discussion of what it means that some of its details remain a matter of *ijtihad*.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher explains that *ihtisab* when a child is lost is not merely enduring pain, but an investment in a relationship that bears fruit in intercession and reunion; and that respecting scholarly difference over the details takes nothing away from certainty in Allah's justice and mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** Students carry out the "My Own Lantern" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences summarizing how this lesson will change the way they comfort someone who loses a young child.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For those who want a deeper discussion, add a brief presentation of the views of an-Nawawi and Ibn Taymiyyah; for those who prefer quiet reflection, personal writing without group discussion is enough.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The teacher must take care not to pressure any teen into sharing a personal experience of loss in front of the group. Anyone who appears deeply affected should be guided to a supportive one-to-one conversation after the session. The lesson must never be used to pronounce on the destiny of a particular child.
