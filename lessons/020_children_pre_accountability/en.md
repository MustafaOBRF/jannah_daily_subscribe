# Children and Those Who Died Before the Age of Accountability

It is part of Allah's mercy toward His servants that children who pass away before reaching the age of religious accountability (taklif) are not held to account the way adults are, because the Pen has been lifted from them. The Prophet ﷺ gave believers wonderful glad tidings: their children who die young are in Allah's Paradise, in tender loving care, waiting for their mothers and fathers with longing and love. This lesson speaks gently of this great glad tiding, and reminds young and old alike that Allah's mercy encompasses all things, and that whoever has lost a child has with Allah an immense reward and a hope that never ends. This lesson stops at the boundary of glad tidings and mercy, and does not touch on any detail that might worry a child or stir sorrow in the heart of anyone listening.

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="14" -->

### For Adults

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.020.adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Among the things that most settle the hearts of believers is that their children who die before reaching the age of accountability are among the people of Paradise by Allah's favor and mercy — not by their own deeds, since they were never yet charged with any obligation. Many authentic texts from the Prophet ﷺ point to this, so much so that more than one scholar has reported a scholarly consensus on this specifically regarding the children of believers.

<!-- evidence:start id="jannah.020.ev.dawamees" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «صِغَارُهُمْ دَعَامِيصُ الْجَنَّةِ، يَتَلَقَّى أَحَدُهُمْ أَبَاهُ - أَوْ قَالَ: أَبَوَيْهِ - فَيَأْخُذُ بِثَوْبِهِ - أَوْ قَالَ: بِيَدِهِ - كَمَا آخُذُ أَنَا بِصَنِفَةِ ثَوْبِكَ هَذَا، فَلَا يَتَنَاهَى - أَوْ قَالَ: فَلَا يَنْتَهِي - حَتَّى يُدْخِلَهُ اللهُ وَإِيَّاهُ الْجَنَّةَ» رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ وَأَحْمَدُ، وَصَحَّحَهُ الْأَلْبَانِيُّ فِي سِلْسِلَةِ الْأَحَادِيثِ الصَّحِيحَةِ (١/١٧٤، رَقْمُ ٤٣٢).[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->
Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Their little ones are the *da'amees* (little creatures moving freely) of Paradise. One of them meets his father — or he said his parents — and takes hold of his garment, or he said his hand, just as I am taking hold of the edge of your garment now, and will not let go until Allah admits him and his parent into Paradise together." Narrated by Muslim and Ahmad; graded authentic (sahih) by al-Albani in *Silsilat al-Ahadith as-Sahihah* (1/174, no. 432).[^1]

<!-- evidence:end -->

**Explanation and connection to the lesson:** The word "da'amees al-jannah" means they move about freely and joyfully in Paradise, the way a small water-creature moves through water without fear. This hadith shows that a child who dies young does not forget his parents — rather, he waits for them at the gate of Paradise and clings to them until Allah admits them together, a scene of love and loyalty that turns a believer's grief into hope.

<!-- story:start audience="adults" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" authenticated="true" -->

This mercy is further confirmed by the long, well-known hadith of the dream (ru'ya) of Samurah ibn Jundab (may Allah be pleased with him), which the Prophet ﷺ used to relate to his companions. In it, he ﷺ saw in his dream that he was taken to a green meadow with a great tree, and at its base a very tall man whose head could barely be seen for his height, and around him more children than he had ever seen. When asked about them, he was told:

عَنْ سَمُرَةَ بْنِ جُنْدَبٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ فِي حَدِيثِ الرُّؤْيَا الطَّوِيلِ: «الرَّجُلُ الطَّوِيلُ فِي الرَّوْضَةِ إِبْرَاهِيمُ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ، وَالْوِلْدَانُ حَوْلَهُ أَوْلَادُ النَّاسِ». رَوَاهُ الْإِمَامُ الْبُخَارِيُّ فِي كِتَابِ التَّعْبِيرِ ضِمْنَ حَدِيثِ الرُّؤْيَا الطَّوِيلِ.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->
"The tall man in the meadow is Ibrahim (Abraham), peace be upon him, and the children around him are the children of the people." Narrated by Imam al-Bukhari, Book of the Interpretation of Dreams, within the long dream hadith.[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" audience="adults" -->

So Ibrahim (peace be upon him) — the father of the prophets and the close friend (Khalil) of the Most Merciful — was made a tender guardian for these little ones in that green meadow, caring for them as a loving grandfather cares for his grandchildren. This meaning is made explicit in another hadith from Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him), attributed to the Prophet ﷺ:

عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ مَرْفُوعًا: «ذَرَارِيُّ الْمُسْلِمِينَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ يَكْفُلُهُمْ إِبْرَاهِيمُ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ» رَوَاهُ أَحْمَدُ وَابْنُ حِبَّانَ وَالْحَاكِمُ، وَقَالَ الْحَاكِمُ: صَحِيحٌ، وَوَافَقَهُ الذَّهَبِيُّ، وَحَسَّنَهُ الْأَلْبَانِيُّ فِي سِلْسِلَةِ الْأَحَادِيثِ الصَّحِيحَةِ (٢/١٥٦، رَقْمُ ٦٠٣).[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->
"The offspring of the Muslims are in Paradise, cared for by Ibrahim, peace be upon him." Narrated by Ahmad, Ibn Hibban, and al-Hakim (who said it was sahih, and adh-Dhahabi agreed), from Abu Hurayrah; graded hasan (good) by al-Albani in *Silsilat al-Ahadith as-Sahihah* (2/156, no. 603).[^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

**A note on sourcing:** The dream hadith of Samurah is very long and well-known in the Book of the Interpretation of Dreams in Sahih al-Bukhari. We have taken care to convey the relevant portion faithfully, based on Dr. Umar Sulayman al-Ashqar's citation in his book *al-Jannah wa an-Nar* (Paradise and the Fire); however, in this authoring session we were unable to independently confirm the exact hadith number on sunnah.com due to a network access limitation, so reviewers should confirm the precise reference before final publication.

**Regarding the children of non-believers:** Scholars have differed on their ruling in a well-known way, and it is fitting that this be presented to adults honestly and with scholarly courtesy, without asserting a single definitive position, since the matter belongs to the unseen (ghayb) that Allah alone fully knows. Imam an-Nawawi (may Allah have mercy on him) said the correct, chosen view is that they are among the people of Paradise, citing Allah's words: "And We would not punish until We sent a messenger" (al-Isra 17:15). Others — such as Hammad ibn Zayd, Hammad ibn Salamah, Ibn al-Mubarak, and Ishaq ibn Rahwayh, and this was attributed by Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari to Ahl as-Sunnah — held that their matter is left to Allah's will, with no definitive judgment either way, and Allah knows best what they would have done had they lived. Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him) chose a view that combines these meanings: that they will be tested on the Day of Judgment in its open plains with a command they will obey or disobey, citing a report about a fire that is kindled and they are commanded to enter it — whoever obeys finds it cool and safe and enters Paradise, whoever disobeys is punished. This report was narrated by al-Bazzar from Anas and Abu Sa'id, and by at-Tabarani from Mu'adh ibn Jabal. Dr. Umar Sulayman al-Ashqar, in *al-Jannah wa an-Nar*, discussed its grading and the objection raised against it (that the Hereafter is a home of recompense, not a home of trial), and concluded that there are accepted parallels for this in the presentation of deeds, the questioning of the grave, and other states of the barzakh and the Hereafter that are not of the same nature as worldly accountability.[^4] In sum, this is a purely scholarly, legitimate matter of difference that no one should be blamed for either side of, and the final reference point is Allah's justice and mercy, which encompass all things — Allah wrongs no one by so much as an atom's weight.

**A note for educators:** This section of the lesson is for adults only, and should never be simplified or relayed to children at any age. If someone has lost a child among their relatives or loved ones, they should know that this scholarly discussion does not concern them if the child was the child of believers — that matter enjoys scholarly consensus regarding Allah's mercy. And even in the cases where scholars differed, this scholarly discussion should never be raised with a grieving family or used to pass judgment on a specific case; Ibn Taymiyyah himself stated that "no specific named child among the children of believers may be testified to individually as being in Paradise, though the general ruling for the category stands."[^4] How much more so, then, for a matter of genuine scholarly difference! Let our speech with the bereaved always be one of mercy and good thoughts of Allah, never of scholarly argument.

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" authenticated="true" -->

### For Children (Ages 8-12)

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.020.812.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

It is part of Allah's mercy that a child whom Allah takes young, before growing up and becoming accountable for commands and prohibitions, is not held to account the way grown-ups are; rather, Allah admits him to Paradise by His favor and mercy. The Prophet ﷺ told us that he saw in his dream a garden in Paradise where Ibrahim, peace be upon him — the father of the prophets — cares for little children, being for them like a loving grandfather who never falls short in caring for them for even a moment.

<!-- evidence:start id="jannah.020.ev.anas_three" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «مَا مِنَ النَّاسِ مُسْلِمٌ يُتَوَفَّى لَهُ ثَلَاثَةٌ لَمْ يَبْلُغُوا الْحِنْثَ، إِلَّا أَدْخَلَهُ اللهُ الْجَنَّةَ بِفَضْلِ رَحْمَتِهِ إِيَّاهُمْ». رَوَاهُ الْبُخَارِيُّ.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->
Anas ibn Malik (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There is no Muslim among the people whose three children die before reaching the age of accountability, except that Allah admits him to Paradise by the grace of His mercy upon them." Narrated by al-Bukhari.[^5]

<!-- evidence:end -->

**Explanation and connection to the lesson:** "Before reaching the age of accountability" means before reaching the age at which sins begin to be recorded against a person. These children become a means for their parents to enter Paradise if they are patient and seek Allah's reward, and they themselves are in Paradise by Allah's mercy, waiting for their loved ones.

It is also reported from Abu Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) that the Prophet ﷺ said: "Their little ones are the da'amees of Paradise. One of them meets his father and takes hold of his garment — just as I am taking hold of the edge of your garment now — and will not let go until Allah admits him and his parent into Paradise together." Narrated by Muslim and Ahmad.[^1] Picture this beautiful scene with me: a little child standing at the gate of Paradise, who sees his father approaching, and grabs hold of his garment joyfully, not letting go until they enter Paradise together!

<!-- terminology:start source_id="jannah.020" -->

**al-hinth** — the age of accountability, i.e., the age of puberty at which deeds begin to be recorded against a person.
**da'amees al-jannah** — a description of little children in Paradise, moving about freely and joyfully, like a little water-creature moving through water.
**the Pen is lifted** — a prophetic expression meaning that deeds are not recorded against a child, a sleeping person, or someone of unsound mind, until they wake, recover, or reach puberty.

### For Ages 13 and Up

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.020.13.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Muslim scholars agree that a child is not held accountable until reaching puberty, because accountability is tied to sound reason and puberty, and the Pen is lifted from a young person until they reach maturity. From this, the children of believers who die before that point are among the people of Paradise by the consensus of the majority of scholars, by Allah's grace — not by deeds not yet recorded against them.

<!-- evidence:start id="jannah.020.ev.tur" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

قَالَ اللهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَاتَّبَعَتْهُمْ ذُرِّيَّتُهُمْ بِإِيمَانٍ أَلْحَقْنَا بِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَمَا أَلَتْنَاهُمْ مِنْ عَمَلِهِمْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ﴾ (الطُّورِ: ٢١).

<!-- evidence:translation -->
Allah, the Exalted, says: "And those who believed and whose descendants followed them in faith — We will join with them their descendants, and We will not deprive them of anything of their deeds." (at-Tur 52:21)

<!-- evidence:end -->

**Scholarly explanation:** Ali ibn Abi Talib (may Allah be pleased with him) used this verse, together with Allah's words "And We have fastened every man's fate to his own neck" (al-Isra 17:13) and "Every soul, for what it has earned, is held in pledge" (al-Muddathir 74:38), as evidence that young offspring are not held in pledge for deeds they never earned, and so are joined with their believing parents in Paradise as a favor from Allah.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari.tabir.samurah_ruya" authenticated="true" -->

In the long, well-known dream hadith of Samurah ibn Jundab (may Allah be pleased with him), which the Prophet ﷺ used to relate to his companions every morning, he ﷺ mentioned that he saw in his dream a green meadow with a great tree, and at its base a very tall man, whose head could barely be seen against the sky for his height, and around him more children than the Prophet ﷺ had ever seen. He was told that the tall man was Ibrahim, peace be upon him, and that the children around him were "the children of the people." Narrated by al-Bukhari, Book of the Interpretation of Dreams.[^2]

<!-- story:end -->

**A note on sourcing:** We have conveyed the relevant portion of this long hadith as cited by Dr. Umar Sulayman al-Ashqar in *al-Jannah wa an-Nar*. We were not able to directly verify the hadith number on sunnah.com in this authoring session due to a technical fault in our search tools, so whoever reviews this lesson for publication should confirm the precise reference.

**The matter of the children of non-believers:** As for children who die before accountability who are not the children of believers, this is a well-known matter of scholarly difference, which we mention here briefly and respectfully, since it belongs to the unseen and no single opinion should be asserted with certainty:

**The first view:** That they are among the people of Paradise. This was the view favored by Imam an-Nawawi (may Allah have mercy on him), citing Allah's words: "And We would not punish until We sent a messenger" (al-Isra 17:15), and a report from the Companion Khansa bint Mu'awiyah, from her aunt, attributed to the Prophet ﷺ: "...and the newborn child is in Paradise." Narrated by Imam Ahmad with a hasan (good) chain.[^6]

**The second view:** That their matter is left to Allah's will, with no definitive judgment for Paradise or the Fire either way. This was the view of Hammad ibn Zayd, Hammad ibn Salamah, Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, and Ishaq ibn Rahwayh, and Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari attributed it to Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, relying on verses such as: "And Allah knows best what they would have done" (al-An'am 6:128).

**The third view:** That they will be tested on the Day of Judgment in its open plains with a command they will either obey or disobey. This was the choice of Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah (may Allah have mercy on him), based on a report about a test involving a kindled fire they are commanded to enter — whoever obeys it finds it cool and safe and enters Paradise, and whoever disobeys is punished. This report was narrated by al-Bazzar from Anas and Abu Sa'id (may Allah be pleased with them), and by at-Tabarani from Mu'adh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with him), and scholars have differed on the strength of its chain. Dr. Umar Sulayman al-Ashqar discussed the objection raised against this test (that the Hereafter is a home of recompense, not a home of trial), and responded that there are accepted parallels for this in the states of the barzakh and the plains of the Resurrection, which differ in nature from worldly accountability.[^4]

**Summary and etiquette in approaching this matter:** This is a legitimate difference among eminent scholars, and no one should be faulted for holding either view; ultimately, the whole matter returns to Allah's justice and mercy, which encompass all things. This discussion must never be used to pass judgment on a specific child, nor raised with a family who has lost a child, for that stirs sorrow and anxiety without any legitimate benefit. Allah, glorified is He, wrongs no one by so much as an atom's weight, and He is far too knowing, wise, and merciful to hold accountable someone against whom the proof was never established.

## Detailed Lesson Plans For Teachers

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="14" -->

**Outcomes:** The learner will recognize the authentic evidence regarding the fate of believers' children, and will become familiar, with courtesy, with the legitimate scholarly difference regarding children of non-believers.
**Materials:** Lesson copy, small slips of paper, a symbolic bowl.
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the hadiths and scholarly statements in advance, and checks that no one in the group has recently experienced a loss before beginning.
**Opening:** The teacher reminds participants of the vastness of Allah's mercy before going into detail.
**Presenting the evidence:** Read the da'amees hadith and the dream hadith slowly, noting the sourcing caveat.
**Instruction:** Explain the consensus regarding believers' children first and very clearly, then present the difference regarding non-believers' children briefly and with scholarly neutrality, without a definitive ruling.
**Activity:** Carry out the "Garden of Hope" activity.
**Assessment:** Ask participants how this mercy affects their outlook on the loss of children.
**Differentiation:** For those with an academic background, one may expand on the statements of Ibn Taymiyyah and an-Nawawi.
**Safeguards:** Never raise the matter of non-believers' children in front of someone who has recently lost a child, avoid any generalization that would make anyone anxious about the fate of a specific child, and constantly remind participants that Allah is more merciful to His servants than a mother is to her child.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="6" -->

**Outcomes:** The child will feel safe and reassured about the topic of death without fear, and will become familiar with Ibrahim, peace be upon him, as a gentle guardian in Paradise.
**Materials:** Drawing paper and colors.
**Preparation:** The educator ensures a calm, smiling tone throughout the lesson.
**Opening:** Ask the child: Do you like beautiful green gardens?
**Presenting the evidence:** Tell the dream story with complete simplicity, without directly using the word "death."
**Instruction:** Focus on Ibrahim's love for the children and their happiness in the garden.
**Activity:** Draw the garden.
**Assessment:** Observe whether the child feels comforted rather than worried after the lesson.
**Differentiation:** For those closer to age 7, the term "da'amees al-jannah" may be mentioned as a gentle word.
**Safeguards:** Never mention any detail about how a child died, and never ask the child about a sibling or relative who has passed away. If the child spontaneously asks about someone they lost, answer very gently and briefly with reassurance only, and quickly return the conversation to the joyful image of the garden.

**Outcomes:** The child will understand the meaning of the Pen being lifted from one who is not yet accountable, and will feel Allah's mercy toward little ones.
**Materials:** A glass cup, green paper leaves, small cards.

**Instruction:** Explain the meanings of "al-hinth" and "da'amees" from the terminology unit.
**Activity:** Make the Garden Lantern.
**Assessment:** Ask the child to retell the meaning of the hadith in their own words.

**Safeguards:** Avoid the matter of non-believers' children entirely for this age group. If a child asks about it, say: "That's a deep question we'll talk about when you're a bit older," and redirect to Allah's all-encompassing mercy, being fully attentive to anyone in the group who may have lost a young sibling or relative.

**Outcomes:** The teen will grasp the difference between a matter of consensus and a matter of legitimate scholarly difference, and will learn the etiquette of presenting scholarly disagreement.
**Materials:** Lesson copy, discussion papers.

**Safeguards:** The teacher should explicitly note at the start of this section that anyone who feels this topic touches a personal wound has every right not to participate in this part of the discussion, and should stress that this is a scholarly difference that must never become a verdict on the personal situation of anyone present or their family.
