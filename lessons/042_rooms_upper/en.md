# The Lofty Chambers of Jannah, Rising One Above Another

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Explain what the Qur'an means by "the lofty chamber" (*al-ghurfah*) and "the lofty chambers" (*ghuraf* and *al-ghurufat*): high dwellings in Jannah, some above others, drawing on the ayat of al-Furqan (25:75-76), az-Zumar (39:20) and Saba' (34:37) and on the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di.
- Narrate the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri in Sahih al-Bukhari (3256): the people of Jannah look up at the people of the lofty chambers above them as one sees a brilliant star lingering on the horizon; and explain the answer of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "Yes indeed! By the One in whose hand is my soul, men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."
- List the deeds by which the texts describe the people of the lofty chambers: patience, mindfulness of Allah (*taqwa*), faith joined with righteous deeds, good speech, feeding others, regular fasting, and praying at night while people sleep (at-Tirmidhi 1984, with its grading made clear).
- Tell apart how this world measures height (wealth, children and status) and how Allah measures it, as in His words: "It is not your wealth or your children that bring you near to Us in rank."
- Carry out the "A Star Among Us" activity: interviewing an ordinary person nearby who has quietly kept up a good deed for a long time, then taking on that deed for a set period with a daily check-in, and going back to thank them.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to make us among the people of the lofty chambers and to grant us patience and good speech.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On a clear night, look at a bright star hanging low near the edge of the sky, and you see it at once as distant, high and shining. That is the image the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used for what the people of Jannah see when they raise their eyes to the people of the lofty chambers above them. A *ghurfah* is a raised dwelling, named for its height, as Ibn Kathir reports from a number of the early generations.[^2] The Qur'an uses the word in the singular, "the lofty chamber" (*al-ghurfah*), and in two plurals, *ghuraf* and *al-ghurufat*, and every time it is part of the believers' reward.

This lesson brings together five texts on the lofty chambers and their people, and taken together they yield three truths:

1. **The lofty chambers are a real height, rising in degrees:** exalted dwellings, some above others, whose people differ in rank as stars differ in distance.
2. **Their people are described by their deeds, not their wealth:** patience, mindfulness of Allah, and faith joined with righteous deeds.
3. **Such heights are not reserved for the prophets:** the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore that men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers will reach those dwellings.

The building of Jannah and its palaces have been covered in their own lessons, and how the people of Jannah recognize their homes has a lesson of its own. This lesson keeps to the height of the lofty chambers and to those who are given them as reward.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-25-75-76" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Those Will Be Rewarded with the Lofty Chamber for Their Patience"

> **{أُولَٰئِكَ يُجْزَوْنَ الْغُرْفَةَ بِمَا صَبَرُوا وَيُلَقَّوْنَ فِيهَا تَحِيَّةً وَسَلَامًا * خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا ۚ حَسُنَتْ مُسْتَقَرًّا وَمُقَامًا}** [الفرقان: ٧٥-٧٦][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those will be rewarded with the lofty chamber for their patience, and they will be met there with greetings and peace, abiding there forever. How excellent it is as a settled home and a place to stay!"** (al-Furqan 25:75-76)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explains "those" as "the ones characterized by these" qualities; "will be rewarded" as "meaning, on the Day of Resurrection"; and "the lofty chamber" as "which is Jannah." He relates from Abu Ja'far al-Baqir, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, ad-Dahhak and as-Suddi: "It was called that because of its height"; and he explains "for their patience" as "meaning, in carrying all of that out."[^2] As-Sa'di says: "Since their aspirations and their aims were lofty, the reward was of the same kind as the deed, so He rewarded them with lofty dwellings." He explains the lofty chamber as "the elevated dwellings and elegant abodes," and the greetings and peace as coming "from their Lord, from His noble angels, and from one another."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah closes the portrait of "the servants of the Most Merciful" at the end of Surah al-Furqan: humility, answering the ignorant with peace, standing in prayer at night, moderation in spending, avoiding the major sins, repentance, and praying for righteous spouses and children. Then all of it is gathered into a single phrase: "for their patience." None of those qualities lasts without patience.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-20" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Lofty Chambers, Above Them Chambers Built High"

> **{لَٰكِنِ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ لَهُمْ غُرَفٌ مِنْ فَوْقِهَا غُرَفٌ مَبْنِيَّةٌ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ وَعْدَ اللَّهِ ۖ لَا يُخْلِفُ اللَّهُ الْمِيعَادَ}** [الزمر: ٢٠][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"But those who are mindful of their Lord will have lofty chambers, above them chambers built high, beneath which rivers flow. That is Allah's promise; Allah never fails in His promise."** (az-Zumar 39:20)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that they will have chambers in Jannah, "which are the towering palaces," and that "above them chambers built high" means "tier upon tier, built, solid, adorned and lofty."[^5] As-Sa'di, explaining the ayah, brings it together with the two hadiths that follow: they are high dwellings "whose outside is seen from their inside and their inside from their outside," and "they are seen as the lingering star is seen on the eastern or the western horizon." He closes with the words: "So let them fulfill the qualities of mindfulness of Allah, and He will pay them their reward in full."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah is where the idea of "upper stories" (*al-'alali*) in the lesson's title comes from: chambers with more chambers above them. And Allah calls them "Allah's promise," so they are a promised reality, not a poet's fancy and not a mere symbol.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-34-37" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And They Will Dwell Secure in the Lofty Chambers"

> **{وَمَا أَمْوَالُكُمْ وَلَا أَوْلَادُكُمْ بِالَّتِي تُقَرِّبُكُمْ عِنْدَنَا زُلْفَىٰ إِلَّا مَنْ آمَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَالِحًا فَأُولَٰئِكَ لَهُمْ جَزَاءُ الضِّعْفِ بِمَا عَمِلُوا وَهُمْ فِي الْغُرُفَاتِ آمِنُونَ}** [سبأ: ٣٧][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"It is not your wealth or your children that bring you near to Us in rank, but only those who believe and do righteous deeds: for them is a manifold reward for what they did, and they will dwell secure in the lofty chambers."** (Saba' 34:37)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On the opening of the ayah, Ibn Kathir says: "These are not a sign of Our love for you, nor of Our care for you," and then: "What brings you near to Us in rank is only faith and righteous deeds." On "and they will dwell secure in the lofty chambers," he says: "meaning, in the high dwellings of Jannah, secure from every harm, fear and injury, and from every evil that one guards against."[^8] As-Sa'di adds that they are "secure from ever leaving them and from grief within them."[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah corrects the way we measure height. Wealth and children are blessings, but neither is a rank with Allah. Whoever owns the highest house in this world may fear losing it; the people of the lofty chambers have been given height and security together.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3256" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Like a Brilliant Star Lingering on the Horizon"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«إِنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَتَرَاءَيُونَ أَهْلَ الْغُرَفِ مِنْ فَوْقِهِمْ، كَمَا يَتَرَاءَيُونَ الْكَوْكَبَ الدُّرِّيَّ الْغَابِرَ فِي الْأُفُقِ مِنَ الْمَشْرِقِ أَوِ الْمَغْرِبِ، لِتَفَاضُلِ مَا بَيْنَهُمْ»**. قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، تِلْكَ مَنَازِلُ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ لَا يَبْلُغُهَا غَيْرُهُمْ؟ قَالَ: **«بَلَى وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، رِجَالٌ آمَنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَصَدَّقُوا الْمُرْسَلِينَ»**.[^10]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The people of Jannah will look up at the people of the lofty chambers above them just as they look at a brilliant, pearl-like star lingering on the horizon in the east or the west, because of the difference in rank between them."** They said, "O Messenger of Allah, those are the dwellings of the prophets, which no one else will reach?" He said: **"Yes indeed! By the One in whose hand is my soul, men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."**[^10]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-ghabir* here means the star that remains on the horizon, since *ghabir* "is used both for what has passed and for what remains." On the word *bala*, "yes indeed," Ibn Hajar says: "The implied meaning is: yes, they are the dwellings of the prophets, by Allah's having made them theirs by right; but Allah may, out of His bounty, grant others to reach those dwellings."[^11] Al-'Ayni says: "'Men who believed in Allah,' that is, as He truly deserves to be believed in, 'and affirmed the truth of the messengers,' that is, as they truly deserve to be affirmed; otherwise, everyone who enters Jannah has believed in Allah and affirmed His messengers." He also explains that in mentioning the east and the west, "the primary aim is distance, and height follows from it."[^12]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Companions assumed these dwellings belonged to the prophets alone, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore that others would reach them too. The door is open, then, but the condition is not the mere foundation of faith; it is faith made complete and a sincere following of the messengers.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-1984" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Chambers Whose Outsides Can Be Seen from Within"

> عَنْ عَلِيٍّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ غُرَفًا تُرَى ظُهُورُهَا مِنْ بُطُونِهَا، وَبُطُونُهَا مِنْ ظُهُورِهَا»**. فَقَامَ أَعْرَابِيٌّ فَقَالَ: لِمَنْ هِيَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟ قَالَ: **«لِمَنْ أَطَابَ الْكَلَامَ، وَأَطْعَمَ الطَّعَامَ، وَأَدَامَ الصِّيَامَ، وَصَلَّى لِلَّهِ بِاللَّيْلِ وَالنَّاسُ نِيَامٌ»**.[^13]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"In Jannah there are lofty chambers whose outsides can be seen from their insides, and their insides from their outsides."** A Bedouin stood up and said, "For whom are they, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: **"For whoever speaks good words, feeds others, fasts regularly, and prays to Allah at night while people are asleep."**[^13]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Mubarakfuri explains "lofty chambers" as "meaning upper rooms of the utmost delicacy and the ultimate purity and cleanness," whose outsides are seen from within "because they are translucent and do not screen what lies behind them." He explains "whoever speaks good words" as one who "has good character with people," and links it to the servants of the Most Merciful described in the words of Allah: "Those will be rewarded with the lofty chamber for their patience."[^14]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is weaker than the hadith found in both Sahihs, so it is cited to spell out the deeds and as supporting testimony for them, while the subject rests mainly on the ayat and the hadith of Abu Sa'id. Its four deeds bring together the rights of people (a good word and feeding others) and the right of Allah in private (fasting, and praying while people sleep).

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of al-Furqan, az-Zumar and Saba' establish the lofty chambers, tie them to patience, mindfulness of Allah, faith and righteous deeds, and deny that wealth and children are the ladder that reaches them. The hadith of Abu Sa'id pictures the distance between the ranks and opens the door to those who are not prophets but whose faith is true. The hadith of Ali spells out everyday deeds that everyone is able to do. Height in Jannah cannot be bought; it is hoped for, through Allah's mercy, by whoever is patient and true.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What are the three words the ayat use for the lofty chambers, and what meaning do they share in the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di?
2. Why are all the qualities of the servants of the Most Merciful gathered into the words "for their patience"?
3. How did Ibn Hajar explain the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him, with *bala*, "yes indeed"? And what does al-'Ayni's phrase "as He truly deserves to be believed in... as they truly deserve to be affirmed" add?
4. What does the ayah of Saba' correct in the way we measure height and status?
5. Which of the four deeds in the hadith of Ali is hardest for you, and why?

### Activity: A Star Among Us

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.042.activity.ordinary-star-interview" -->

Choose someone in your circle who is known for neither wealth nor position, and whom you know has quietly kept up a good deed for years: patience through an illness, gentleness in speech, feeding people, fasting, or prayer at night. Ask them three questions: How did you begin? What helps you keep going? And what do you do when you fall off? Write their answer in five lines without using their name. Then pick one habit from what they told you and keep it for fourteen days, with a daily tracking chart. At the end, go back to them, tell them what you took from them, and thank them. Finally, write a paragraph: How has the ayah of Saba' changed your view of who is truly "high"?

Have you ever climbed up somewhere high and looked at the stars? In Jannah there are rooms that are very, very high, one on top of another. ### A Made-Up Teaching Story: Yaman and the Stairs to the Roof

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.042.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a made-up teaching story. It is not a hadith or a true event from history.**

One warm summer night, Yaman's family was staying at his grandma's house. His dad said, "Let's go up on the roof and look at the stars!"

Yaman was six, and he was so excited that he ran for the stairs. He wanted to get there first, so he could see the bright star before it went down behind the houses.

But then he heard his grandma behind him: "Slowly, Yaman." He turned around and saw her climbing very slowly, holding the wall with one hand and carrying her pillow in the other.

Yaman stopped. He wanted to run... and he wanted to help his grandma. Then he went back down two steps, took the pillow, and said, "I'm right here with you, Grandma. One step at a time."

They climbed up slowly together, while his little sister counted the steps. Every time Grandma got tired, Yaman stopped and waited, and he didn't complain.

At last they reached the top. Yaman put the pillow down, and Grandma sat on it, smiling and making du'a for him. Then he looked up, and there was the star, still shining at the edge of the sky!

His dad sat down next to him and said, "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah see the people of the high rooms above them, just like we see this faraway star. And Allah said they got the high room because they were patient."

Yaman said, "I was patient with Grandma!" His dad laughed and said, "Being patient is something Allah loves. Come on, let's ask Allah to make us people of the high rooms."

### An Authentic Hadith: Rooms Like Stars

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3256" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3256" audience="4-7" -->

**This is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, told it to us. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told his Companions that the people of Jannah look up at the people of the high rooms above them, and they see them far away and shining, like a bright star we see at the edge of the sky.

The Companions asked, "O Messenger of Allah, are these rooms only for the prophets?"

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore by Allah, and told them that believers who believed in Allah and believed His messengers will reach them too.[^10]

So the high rooms are not only for the prophets. Every believer who loves Allah and obeys Him can hope for them.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Sometimes we're impressed by someone on the top floor, or with the biggest house. But the Qur'an tells us about a different kind of height. In Jannah there are "lofty chambers, above them chambers built high,"[^4] which means high homes, one above another. And money is not the way there: "It is not your wealth or your children that bring you near to Us in rank, but only those who believe and do righteous deeds."[^7]

### A Made-Up Teaching Story: Anas and the Twentieth Floor

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.042.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a made-up teaching story. It is not a hadith or a true event from history.**

Anas, a sixth grader, had just moved into an apartment on the twentieth floor of a brand-new tower. His classmates kept asking about the super-fast elevator and the balcony that looked out over the whole city, and Anas felt he had become "important."

In art class, the teacher had everyone draw their home. Anas drew a tall tower poking into the clouds. Bilal, the new kid, drew a small house on the ground floor, with a lemon tree out front.

Faris laughed out loud. "The ground floor! You live underneath all of us, Bilal!" A few kids laughed too, then looked at Anas, waiting for him to join in. He was "the tower guy," after all.

Anas hesitated. The joke was on the tip of his tongue. Then he remembered an ayah he had recently read with his Qur'an teacher: "It is not your wealth or your children that bring you near to Us in rank." And he remembered his teacher saying that real height with Allah comes from faith and good deeds.

Anas swallowed the joke and said, "That lemon tree is really nice, Bilal. We don't have a tree." At recess he sat beside him, and when he saw Bilal had forgotten his lunch, he split his sandwich with him.

While they ate, Bilal said, "My grandpa lives with us. He gets up at night to pray, and every Friday he picks lemons and gives them out to the neighbors." Anas went quiet. He felt Bilal's grandpa was "higher" than every tower he had ever seen.

That evening Anas stood on his balcony and saw a bright star close to the horizon. He told his dad everything. His dad said, "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah see the people of the lofty chambers above them the way we see a faraway star like that one. And he said those dwellings will be reached by men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers. He didn't mention money, or towers."

Anas smiled and decided to ask his dad to visit Bilal's grandpa with him on Friday.

### An Authentic Hadith: Are They Only for the Prophets?

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3256" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3256" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true report, narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in the Sahih collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

The Companions were sitting with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, when he told them that the people of Jannah look up at the people of the lofty chambers above them, and see them as we see a bright, faraway star at the edge of the sky in this world, in the east or the west, because some of them are higher in rank than others.

Amazed at such height, the Companions said, "O Messenger of Allah, those are the dwellings of the prophets, which no one else will reach?"

He answered them with an oath: "Yes indeed! By the One in whose hand is my soul, men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."[^10]

Notice two things. The gap between the ranks is huge, as far as a star is from us. And the way up is not a famous family or money; it is true faith and following the messengers.

- **`Lofty chambers (al-ghuraf)`** — the plural of *ghurfah*, a high dwelling; the lofty chambers of Jannah are exalted homes, one above another.[^5]
- **`Brilliant star (al-kawkab ad-durri)`** — a star that shines very brightly, as clear as a pearl (*durr*).
- **`Lingering on the horizon (al-ghabir fi al-ufuq)`** — still there at the edge of the sky, looking far away even though it shines.[^11]
- **`Nearness in rank (zulfa)`** — closeness to Allah and a high standing with Him.

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْغُرَفِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِكَ وَصَدَّقُوا الْمُرْسَلِينَ، وَارْزُقْنَا الصَّبْرَ وَطِيبَ الْكَلَامِ، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ يُلَقَّوْنَ فِيهَا تَحِيَّةً وَسَلَامًا.

What it means: O Allah, make us among the people of the high dwellings in Jannah, who believed in You and in Your messengers. Give us patience and kind words, and make us among those welcomed there with greetings and peace.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in an age that measures "height" in numbers: follower counts, which phone you carry, what floor you live on. The Qur'an offers a different measure: "Those will be rewarded with the lofty chamber for their patience, and they will be met there with greetings and peace."[^1] The high dwellings of Jannah are the reward for patience and sincerity, not for fame. And in a hadith narrated by at-Tirmidhi, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described lofty chambers in Jannah as belonging to whoever speaks good words, feeds others, fasts regularly, and prays at night while people are asleep.[^13]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Made-Up Teaching Story: Huda and the Photo That Never Got Posted

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.042.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a made-up teaching story. It is not a hadith or a true event from history.**

Huda is fifteen. Since last Ramadan she has kept a resolution she never told anyone about: to pray two rak'ahs before she goes to sleep, however packed her day has been.

On the school camping trip, Huda shared a cabin with five classmates. After midnight the only light in the room came from phone screens. Huda got up quietly, made wudu', and spread out her prayer rug in the corner.

Dana noticed and held up her phone. "Don't move! This is perfect: 'the girl who prays while the rest of us stay up.' This is going to get thousands of views!" And from under her blanket Reem said, in a mocking voice, "Putting on a show of being religious for us?"

Huda felt her face go hot. Two easy ways out were right in front of her: sit down and skip the prayer so no one would make fun of her, or let Dana film and enjoy the likes. And in her head a cutting comeback for Reem was ready to go.

Huda finished her prayer. Then she said to Dana, gently, "Please don't film this. It's between me and my Lord." She turned to Reem and said calmly, "I'm not putting on a show, Reem. I just need these two rak'ahs, the way you need your phone before bed." And she swallowed her cutting comeback.

A few of the girls laughed, and then the room went back to its screens. Huda fell asleep unsure of herself: had she gotten it wrong? Had she become "the weird one" in their eyes?

The next morning at the sinks, Reem came over and said quietly, "Will you wake me up tonight? I want to try." Huda didn't tell anyone what had happened, and she didn't post a thing.

That night she wrote in her journal: "I read that in Jannah there are chambers whose outside can be seen from the inside, and the inside from the outside. I don't know what they are really like, but I hope my inside can be like my outside: not hiding my prayer out of embarrassment, and not showing it off for likes."

<!-- story:end -->

### An Authentic Hadith: Stars Not Measured by Follower Counts

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3256" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3256" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report, narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in the Sahih collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tells us that the people of Jannah raise their eyes to the people of the lofty chambers above them and see them as one sees a bright star lingering on the far horizon, in the east or the west, because of the difference in rank between them. The Companions assumed such heights belonged to the prophets alone, so they said: «تِلْكَ مَنَازِلُ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ لَا يَبْلُغُهَا غَيْرُهُمْ؟», "Those are the dwellings of the prophets, which no one else will reach?" And he swore: «بَلَى وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، رِجَالٌ آمَنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَصَدَّقُوا الْمُرْسَلِينَ», "Yes indeed! By the One in whose hand is my soul, men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."[^10]

Al-'Ayni explains that they believed in Allah "as He truly deserves to be believed in" and affirmed the messengers "as they truly deserve to be affirmed."[^12] Think about it: the hadith mentions no good looks, no wealth and no audience. It mentions sincere faith that shows itself in following the messengers. The stars of this world are measured by how many people see them; those dwellings are reached by what Allah sees.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="tirmidhi-1984" -->

- **`Speaks good words (atab al-kalam)`** — makes their speech good and gentle; al-Mubarakfuri explains it as good character with people.[^14]
- **`Whose outsides can be seen from their insides (tura zuhuruha min butuniha)`** — their outside is seen from within and their inside from without, because of their clearness, as the commentators say; their true nature belongs to the unseen, and we do not know how they are.[^14]
- **`The lofty chambers (al-ghurufat)`** — the high dwellings of Jannah, where their people are "secure" from fear, from grief and from ever having to leave.[^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What were the two easy ways out in front of Huda, and why would either one have been a loss?
2. How did Huda answer Reem, and how does that connect to "speaks good words"?
3. Why didn't Huda post anything about what happened, even after Reem asked her to wake her up?
4. What did the Companions think about the lofty chambers, and what is the condition for reaching them in the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him?
5. What is the difference between "height" measured by numbers and "height" measured by Allah?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.042.dua.people-of-the-rooms-patience-good-word" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْغُرَفِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِكَ وَصَدَّقُوا الْمُرْسَلِينَ، وَارْزُقْنَا الصَّبْرَ وَطِيبَ الْكَلَامِ، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ يُلَقَّوْنَ فِيهَا تَحِيَّةً وَسَلَامًا.
>
> *"O Allah, make us among the people of the lofty chambers, who believed in You and affirmed the truth of the messengers. Grant us patience and good speech, and make us among those who are met there with greetings and peace."*

This du'a takes its words from the texts: "believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers" comes from the hadith of Abu Sa'id, and "met there with greetings and peace" from the ayah of al-Furqan. It asks for the patience that was made the reason for the lofty chamber, and for good speech, which is within everyone's reach.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner cites the three ayat on the lofty chambers and explains their wording through the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; narrates al-Bukhari 3256 in its own wording and explains Ibn Hajar's reading of *bala* and al-'Ayni's phrase "as He truly deserves to be believed in"; lists the deeds in at-Tirmidhi 1984 and states its grading; applies the measure of the ayah of Saba' to how they themselves regard people; and carries out the "A Star Among Us" interview and keeps a habit taken from it.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board divided into two columns headed "What are the lofty chambers?" and "Who are their people?"; an interview card with the three questions; a fourteen-day tracking chart for each learner.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the full passage describing the servants of the Most Merciful (al-Furqan 25:63-76); the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Furqan 25:75, az-Zumar 39:20 and Saba' 34:37; the commentary of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni on al-Bukhari 3256; and at-Tirmidhi's remarks on the hadith of Ali together with al-Albani's grading of it as *hasan*.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Who is the person of highest standing you know? And by what measure did you choose them?" Write three answers on the board without comment, and come back to them at the close.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Furqan with a summary of the qualities of the servants of the Most Merciful; then the ayah of az-Zumar with Ibn Kathir's "tier upon tier"; then the ayah of Saba' with Ibn Kathir's words on wealth and children. Read the hadith of Abu Sa'id in its own wording, and explain *al-ghabir*, Ibn Hajar's reading of *bala*, and al-'Ayni's comment. Then read the hadith of Ali, and make clear that it ranks below the hadith of the two Sahihs, that at-Tirmidhi called it *gharib*, and that al-Albani graded it *hasan* on the strength of its supporting reports, so it is used to spell out the deeds and not as the foundation. The learners fill in the two columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion: Why did the Companions assume these dwellings were for the prophets alone? And what keeps us from seeking the high ranks: despair, or laziness? Each learner writes a single sentence: "The highest thing I can offer this week is..."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner decides whom they will interview, writes the three questions, and prepares the tracking chart, as in the activity unit. In pairs, learners practice wording the questions courteously and without causing embarrassment.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** From memory, the learner writes three qualities of the people of the lofty chambers drawn from the texts, and explains in one line the meaning of *bala* in the hadith. Then the teacher returns to the opening answers and asks, "Has your measure changed?", and closes by inviting anyone who wishes to share their paragraph after fourteen days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the ayah of al-Furqan and the hadith of Abu Sa'id. Ask advanced learners to compare al-Bukhari's wording with Muslim's («لَيَتَرَاءَوْنَ... كَمَا تَتَرَاءَوْنَ», "they will surely look up... as you look"), and to gather the narrations of the hadith of the chambers whose outsides are seen from within and explain why it was graded *hasan*.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not declare with certainty that any specific person is among the people of the lofty chambers. Do not take the ayah of Saba' as a condemnation of wealth or children; it denies only that they are the measure of nearness. Describe the lofty chambers with the words that have been reported, without adding imaginative detail. Leave aside the weak reports about the description of the lofty chambers collected in books on the description of Jannah, and rely only on what is established. In the interview, take care not to embarrass the person being asked, and keep what they say in confidence.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Jannah has high rooms that look like a faraway star, and that they are for whoever is patient and does good; names one way Yaman was patient; draws a good deed done by an older person in their family; and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Black or dark blue paper; shiny star stickers; crayons or colored pencils; a small pillow for role-play; a du'a card in clear, large writing.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling Yaman's story in a calm voice. Before the session, stick one shiny star high on the wall, and another near the edge of the dark paper, to stand for "the star at the edge of the sky."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The educator turns off some of the lights and points to the star high on the wall: "Look! It's far away, but it shines. Do you know what in Jannah looks like the stars?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell Yaman's story, then say, "And this is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said," and read the children's retelling of the hadith slowly. The children raise their hands toward the star whenever the high rooms are mentioned.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator repeats three sentences together with the children: "In Jannah there are high rooms." "The people of Jannah see them like stars." "We are patient, we do good, and we ask Allah for Jannah." A child acts out Yaman's part, carrying the pillow and walking slowly beside "Grandma."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** Each child draws a big star on the dark paper and draws a good deed inside it, as in the activity unit. Agree with the families that the child will ask a grandparent at home and color in one little star each day for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child, "What do the rooms of Jannah look like?" and "What did Yaman do for his grandma?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, it is enough to stick on a ready-made star and choose a picture of a good deed. Older children retell the story in their own words in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not tell a child that they have a particular room in Jannah; we hope, and we ask Allah. Do not draw any picture of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, of the Companions, or of the angels. Be gentle with a child who has no grandparent or who has recently lost a relative, and suggest another older person from the family or a well-known neighbor instead.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the meaning of "lofty chambers, above them chambers"; narrates the hadith of Abu Sa'id, including the Companions' question and the answer of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; applies the ayah of Saba' to a situation in which a poorer child is mocked; and interviews someone from their own circle and keeps a habit learned from them for seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the two ayat and the hadith, printed out; cards for the four terms; an interview card with the three questions; a seven-day tracking chart; a blank thank-you card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares a drawing of a tall tower with a small house next to it, and writes a question beneath it: "Which is higher in Allah's sight?" Send families a short note explaining the activity and asking that one parent be present at the interview.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the drawing and asks, "Which one is higher?" After the students answer, say: "Today we're learning about a kind of height that isn't measured in floors."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the ayah of az-Zumar and explain the meaning of "lofty chambers, above them chambers"; then the ayah of Saba' and the meaning of *zulfa*; then tell the hadith of Abu Sa'id, and have the students read the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him, in its own words. Explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Anas's story, and pause at the moment he hesitates: "If Anas had laughed, what would he have lost? And who would have been hurt?" Then, in pairs, students act out a similar situation and come up with a kind response.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student chooses the person they will interview, writes the three questions on the card, and prepares the seven-day chart, as in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Students answer aloud: "What did the Companions think? And how did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answer them?" They give the meaning of *zulfa*, and then the du'a is read. A week later, the students share what they learned from the people they interviewed.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who need support get an interview card with the questions already written and a picture-based chart. Advanced students write a paragraph comparing the people of the lofty chambers in the ayah of al-Furqan with their people in the hadith of Ali.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** A student never interviews a stranger; the interview takes place with a parent present or with their permission. Do not mention in class the names of anyone who lives in a modest home, or anyone's financial situation. The story should not be taken to mean that living in a tower is something bad; the point is that money is not the measure of nearness to Allah.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes digital measures of "height" in light of the ayah of al-Furqan and the hadith of Abu Sa'id; explains al-'Ayni's phrase "as He truly deserves to be believed in... as they truly deserve to be affirmed"; lists the deeds in the hadith of Ali and knows its grading; distinguishes hiding worship out of embarrassment from displaying it for admiration; and carries out an interview and keeps a habit for ten days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; three cards showing common measures of status (followers, brand, floor), without brand names; an interview card; a private ten-day tracking chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Furqan 25:75, the commentary of al-'Ayni and Ibn Hajar on al-Bukhari 3256, and al-Mubarakfuri's remarks on the hadith of Ali, and prepares two open-ended questions about Huda's story.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the three cards and ask: "Which of these makes people look up to you? And does it last?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Furqan with a summary of the qualities of the servants of the Most Merciful; then read the hadith of Abu Sa'id in its own wording, with al-'Ayni's comment; then the hadith of Ali, explaining that at-Tirmidhi called it *gharib* and that al-Albani graded it *hasan* on the strength of its supporting reports. Explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Huda's story, and have groups discuss: "What were the two easy ways out? And why didn't she take either of them?" Then ask: "Was Reem's request in the morning the result of the prayer, or of the kind word?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student silently decides whom they will interview, drafts the questions, and prepares the ten-day chart, as in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** On an unsigned card, the student writes: "Before this lesson, my measure of height was... and now it is..." Then the du'a is read. After ten days, anyone who wishes may share their paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A student who has trouble finding a suitable person may interview a parent or a trusted teacher. Advanced students write a comparison of the "lofty chambers" in al-Furqan, az-Zumar and Saba' in terms of the qualities of their people.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No student is asked to reveal their private acts of worship. Do not mock the students who are most active on social media; the aim is to correct the measure, not to put people down. Present Huda's journal reflection on the translucent chambers as a personal reflection, not as an interpretation of the hadith. The interview is never with a stranger or online; it is with someone the family knows.
