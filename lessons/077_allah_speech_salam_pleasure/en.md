# Allah's Greeting of Peace, His Speech, and His Good Pleasure upon the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (Sahih al-Bukhari 6549 and Sahih Muslim 2829) in its proper sequence: Allah calls out to the people of Jannah; they answer, "Here we are at Your call"; He asks, "Are you pleased?"; they acknowledge His blessing; and then He causes His good pleasure to descend upon them, so that He will never be displeased with them after it.
- Explain, from Ibn Hajar's words, the meaning of *uhillu* ("I will send down"), and why Allah's good pleasure is greater than every other delight, and relate what he cites from Ibn Battal and Ibn Abi Jamrah.
- Cite the ayah "'Peace': a word spoken by a Merciful Lord" as evidence that Allah greets the people of Jannah with peace; cite Al 'Imran 3:77, as Ibn al-Qayyim used it, as evidence that Allah speaks to His believing servants; and cite the ayah "And good pleasure from Allah is greater still" as evidence that His good pleasure is the greatest bliss.
- Affirm the attributes of speech, greeting with peace, and good pleasure for Allah exactly as He has described Himself, without likening Him to creation, without asking how, and without denial, drawing on the ayah "There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing" and as-Sa'di's tafsir of it.
- Tell apart what is authentically established on this subject from what the lesson does not rely on because it is weak or anomalous.
- Carry out the "Are You Pleased?" activity: answering that question each night with specific blessings from the day, choosing a hidden deed done to seek Allah's pleasure alone, and then reflecting on what has changed within.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world the believer lives in hope that Allah will be pleased with him. He prays, he endures, he seeks forgiveness, and he does not know how his life will end. Then, once the people of Jannah have entered Jannah and settled into it, their Lord honors them with something above every palace and river: a greeting of peace from Him, glorified is He, words with which He honors them, and a good pleasure that is never followed by displeasure.

This lesson takes up these three honors, and alongside them sets out the method of Ahl as-Sunnah in affirming the attributes of Allah. Seeing Allah in Jannah has the next lesson to itself; the angels' greeting of peace to the people of Jannah, and the greeting the people of Jannah exchange among themselves, have each been covered in an earlier lesson.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6549" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Will Cause My Good Pleasure to Descend upon You"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَقُولُ لِأَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ: يَا أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ، يَقُولُونَ: لَبَّيْكَ رَبَّنَا وَسَعْدَيْكَ، فَيَقُولُ: هَلْ رَضِيتُمْ؟ فَيَقُولُونَ: وَمَا لَنَا لَا نَرْضَى وَقَدْ أَعْطَيْتَنَا مَا لَمْ تُعْطِ أَحَدًا مِنْ خَلْقِكَ، فَيَقُولُ: أَنَا أُعْطِيكُمْ أَفْضَلَ مِنْ ذَلِكَ، قَالُوا: يَا رَبِّ، وَأَيُّ شَيْءٍ أَفْضَلُ مِنْ ذَلِكَ؟ فَيَقُولُ: أُحِلُّ عَلَيْكُمْ رِضْوَانِي فَلَا أَسْخَطُ عَلَيْكُمْ بَعْدَهُ أَبَدًا»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Allah will say to the people of Jannah, 'O people of Jannah!' They will say, 'Here we are at Your call, our Lord, ready and glad to serve You.' He will say, 'Are you pleased?' They will say, 'And how could we not be pleased, when You have given us what You have not given to any of Your creation?' He will say, 'I will give you something better than that.' They will say, 'O Lord, and what could be better than that?' He will say, 'I will cause My good pleasure to descend upon you, and I will never be displeased with you after it, ever.'"**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

In Muslim's narration, and in al-Bukhari's narration in the Book of Tawhid, the people of Jannah add, "and all good is in Your hands," and Allah asks, "Shall I not give you something better than that?" There al-Bukhari gave the hadith the chapter title "The Lord's Speech with the People of Jannah."[^1] Ibn Hajar explains that *uhillu* means "I will send down." Calling to mind the ayah "And good pleasure from Allah is greater still," he gives the reason why good pleasure is greater than every other delight: "His pleasure is the cause of every triumph and every happiness, and anyone who knows that his master is pleased with him finds that more delightful to his eye and sweeter to his heart than any bliss, because of the honor and esteem it carries."[^2]

Ibn Hajar also relates Ibn Battal's answer to those who found the words "I will never be displeased with you after it" puzzling: entering Jannah and enjoying its bliss are by Allah's grace, "and as for its lasting forever, that is a further gift of His grace beyond the reward." And he relates from Ibn Abi Jamrah that the hadith teaches "courtesy in asking," and that "it contains evidence that each of the people of Jannah is content with his own state, despite the differences in their stations and the variety of their ranks, because all of them answered with one and the same words."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The conversation opens with a call from Allah, and they give the most beautiful of answers: "Here we are at Your call, ready and glad." Then He asks them, though He knows them better than they know themselves: "Are you pleased?" They acknowledge His blessing and ask for nothing more. So He gives them what it never occurred to them to ask for: a good pleasure that lasts forever. They were pleased with what He gave them, and so He gave them His good pleasure.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-36-58" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Peace," a Word Spoken

> **{سَلَامٌ قَوْلًا مِنْ رَبٍّ رَحِيمٍ}** [يس: ٥٨][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"'Peace': a word spoken by a Merciful Lord."** (Ya Sin 36:58)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says: "Here is the speech of the Lord, exalted is He, to the people of Jannah, and His greeting of peace to them, and He emphasized it by saying 'a word spoken.' When the Merciful Lord greets them with peace, they attain complete safety in every respect, and they receive the greeting above which there is no greeting, and a bliss like no other." And Ibn Kathir relates from Ibn 'Abbas: "For Allah Himself is the one who greets the people of Jannah with peace."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah closes a passage in Surah Ya Sin about the bliss of the people of Jannah. Its context was presented in an earlier lesson; this lesson pauses on its final words. The peace here is a "word spoken" by the Merciful Lord, not merely safety from harm.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-3-77" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Nor Will Allah Speak to Them"

> **{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يَشْتَرُونَ بِعَهْدِ اللَّهِ وَأَيْمَانِهِمْ ثَمَنًا قَلِيلًا أُولَٰئِكَ لَا خَلَاقَ لَهُمْ فِي الْآخِرَةِ وَلَا يُكَلِّمُهُمُ اللَّهُ وَلَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَيْهِمْ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ وَلَا يُزَكِّيهِمْ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ}** [آل عمران: ٧٧][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those who sell Allah's covenant and their own oaths for a paltry price will have no share in the Hereafter. Allah will not speak to them, nor look at them on the Day of Resurrection, nor purify them, and theirs will be a painful punishment."** (Al 'Imran 3:77)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim devoted a chapter "to His speaking, glorified is He, to the people of Jannah, His addressing them, His conversing with them, and His greeting them with peace," and opened it with this ayah. He then said: "If He did not speak to His believing servants, they and the enemies of Allah would be alike in this, and there would be no point at all in singling out His enemies as those to whom He will not speak."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

Being denied Allah's speech is a punishment for these people, which shows that His speaking to His friends is an honor. The hadith of Abu Sa'id is an explicit example of that speech.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-72" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Good Pleasure from Allah Is Greater Still"

> **{وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا وَمَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً فِي جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ ۚ وَرِضْوَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ}** [التوبة: ٧٢][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Allah has promised the believing men and the believing women gardens beneath which rivers flow, to abide in them forever, and goodly dwellings in the Gardens of Eternity. And good pleasure from Allah is greater still. That is the supreme triumph."** (at-Tawbah 9:72)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "That is, Allah's pleasure with them is greater, more majestic and more tremendous than the bliss they are in," and he then cites the hadith of Abu Sa'id. As-Sa'di says that "good pleasure from Allah," which "He causes to descend upon the people of Jannah," is "greater" than the bliss they are in, "because it is the goal toward which the worshippers set out, and the end toward which the lovers strive; and so the pleasure of the Lord of the earth and the heavens is greater than the bliss of the gardens."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah names the gardens and the dwellings, and then places good pleasure above them all. Whoever worships Allah so that He will be pleased with him has aimed at the highest thing in Jannah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-42-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "There Is Nothing Like Him"

> **{فَاطِرُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۚ جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا وَمِنَ الْأَنْعَامِ أَزْوَاجًا ۖ يَذْرَؤُكُمْ فِيهِ ۚ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ}** [الشورى: ١١][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"The Originator of the heavens and the earth. He has made for you mates from among yourselves, and mates among the livestock, multiplying you thereby. There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."** (ash-Shura 42:11)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says of "There is nothing like Him": "That is, nothing among His creatures resembles Him, exalted is He, or is His equal, not in His essence, nor in His names, nor in His attributes, nor in His actions," and "this ayah and others like it are evidence for the position of Ahl as-Sunnah wa al-Jama'ah: affirming the attributes, and denying any likeness to created things."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

We affirm that Allah greets the people of Jannah with peace, speaks to them, and is pleased with them, just as He and His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, have told us, and we affirm it in the way that befits His majesty: without likening it to the speech and pleasure of created beings (*tashbih*), without asking how (*takyif*), and without denial (*ta'til*) or any reinterpretation that empties the report of its meaning.

<!-- evidence:end -->

The lesson does not rely on the hadith of Jabir in Ibn Majah about a light shining upon the people of Jannah and the Lord greeting them with peace, nor on the report from 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz about His greeting them and asking them. The first is weak, and of the second Ibn Kathir said: "an anomalous report."[^8]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What are the stages of the conversation in the hadith of Abu Sa'id? And why did the people of Jannah ask for nothing more when they were asked?
2. Why is Allah's good pleasure greater than all the bliss of Jannah, according to Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and Ibn Hajar?
3. How did Ibn al-Qayyim use Al 'Imran 3:77 as evidence that Allah speaks to His believing servants?
4. What does it mean to affirm the attributes of speech, greeting with peace, and good pleasure without likening, without asking how, and without denial? Where do you find this in the ayah of ash-Shura?
5. If you were asked tonight, "Are you pleased?", what would you answer? And what deed will you do to seek Allah's pleasure alone?

### Activity: Are You Pleased?

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.077.activity.are-you-pleased-evening-answer" -->

For seven nights, before you sleep, write your answer to the question "Are you pleased?" with three specific blessings from your day. Make one of them something you disliked at first and later saw good in, and close with "Alhamdulillah." Then choose, for the following day, one hidden deed through which you seek nothing but Allah's pleasure, in a situation where the approval of people or the pull of your own desires draws you elsewhere. Keep a three-column table: the three blessings, the situation, and what you chose in order to seek Allah's pleasure. After the seventh night, write a paragraph: Has my answer become more truthful? And has anything changed in what I do to win people's approval?

When you help your grandma, what does she say to you? Lots of grandmas say, "May Allah be pleased with you!" That is a very beautiful thing to say, because the most beautiful thing of all is for Allah to be pleased with us.

Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Allah talks to the people of Jannah, greets them with peace, and tells them that He is pleased with them. Allah speaks in the way that is right for Him, and there is nothing like Him. We don't know how, but we believe what He has told us, and it makes us happy.[^7]

### A True Hadith: "Are You Pleased?"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6549" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6549" audience="4-7" -->

Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that after the people of Jannah go into Jannah, Allah will call to them: "O people of Jannah!"[^1]

And they will answer happily: "Here we are, our Lord, ready and glad!"[^1]

Allah will ask them: "Are you pleased?"[^1]

They will say: "How could we not be pleased, when You have given us what You have not given to anyone else You made?"[^1]

Allah will say: "I will give you something better than that."[^1]

They will be amazed and say: "O Lord, what could be better than that?"[^1]

Allah will say: "I will give you My good pleasure, and I will never be angry with you after it, ever." That means: "I am pleased with you, and I will never be angry with you, ever."[^1][^2]

How wonderful! The people of Jannah thanked Allah for what He gave them, and Allah gave them the most beautiful gift: His pleasure with them forever. And here in this world, we do what Allah loves and say to Him, "Alhamdulillah," hoping He will be pleased with us.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Yusuf finished the model mosque he had spent a whole week building and set it down in front of his dad. He wasn't hoping for a prize; he was waiting for one thing only. His dad smiled and said, "I'm pleased with you, Yusuf. Well done." To Yusuf, those words felt better than any present.

If a father's approval can make us that happy, what about Allah's? Speaking of the people of Jannah, after mentioning their gardens and their homes, Allah says: "And good pleasure from Allah is greater still,"[^6] meaning that Allah's pleasure with them is greater than all the delights of Jannah. The Qur'an also tells us that Allah greets the people of Jannah with peace: "'Peace': a word spoken by a Merciful Lord."[^4] Allah speaks, greets, and is pleased in the way that befits His majesty, and "there is nothing like Him." So we don't compare Him to anyone, we don't ask how, and we believe what He has told us.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6549" -->

- **`Here we are at Your call, ready and glad (labbayka wa sa'dayk)`** — answering You again and again, and hurrying to obey You time after time.[^9]
- **`I will cause to descend upon you (uhillu 'alaykum)`** — I will send down upon you.[^2]
- **`My good pleasure (ridwani)`** — My being pleased with you.[^9]
- **`I will not be displeased (fa la askhatu)`** — I will not be angry with you.[^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### A True Hadith: Better Than Every Gift

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6549" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6549" audience="8-12" -->

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, told what will happen once the people of Jannah have entered Jannah and settled in.[^1]

Allah will call out to them: "O people of Jannah!" And they will answer: "Here we are at Your call, our Lord, ready and glad!" In another narration they add: "And all good is in Your hands."[^1]

He will ask them: "Are you pleased?" And without a moment's hesitation they will answer: "How could we not be pleased, when You have given us what You have not given to any of Your creation?"[^1] Notice that they didn't ask for anything new. They gave thanks and acknowledged His blessing.

Allah will say: "Shall I not give you something better than that?" And they will ask politely: "O Lord, what could be better than that?" They can't imagine anything better than what they already have.[^1][^3]

Then He will say: "I will cause My good pleasure to descend upon you, and I will never be displeased with you after it, ever."[^1]

The scholars explain that "cause to descend" means "send down," and that Allah's pleasure is greater than every delight, because when someone knows his master is pleased with him, nothing could delight his eyes more or warm his heart more.[^2] And notice this too: not one of them said, "You gave someone else more than me." They all gave one and the same answer, even though their places in Jannah are different.[^3]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The like count. A friend's comment. An approving look from a teacher or a coach. The silence of the group chat when it doesn't like what you did. A lot of us go through the day measuring ourselves by other people's approval: we change our opinions to please them, and hide what we really believe so we won't lose them. And people's approval is fickle: they give it today and take it back tomorrow.

This lesson sets out a different kind of approval: Allah's pleasure with the people of Jannah, of which He said, "And good pleasure from Allah is greater still,"[^6] and which comes with an explicit promise: "I will never be displeased with you after it, ever."[^1] Alongside it, the lesson sets out His greeting of peace to them, "'Peace': a word spoken by a Merciful Lord,"[^4] and His speaking to them. We affirm all of this just as Allah has told us, and "there is nothing like Him": without likening Him to anything, without asking how, and without denial.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6549" -->

- **`I will cause My good pleasure to descend upon you (uhillu 'alaykum ridwani)`** — I will send down My pleasure upon you and make it yours for good, so that it never leaves you.[^2][^9]
- **`I will not be displeased (fa la askhatu)`** — *sukht* (displeasure) is the opposite of *rida* (pleasure); that is, He will never be angry with them after that.[^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### A True Hadith: The Approval That Is Never Withdrawn

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6549" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6549" audience="13+" -->

Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah will say to the people of Jannah, 'O people of Jannah!'" and they will answer: "Here we are at Your call, our Lord, ready and glad."[^1] In the Book of Tawhid, al-Bukhari placed this hadith under the chapter title "The Lord's Speech with the People of Jannah," meaning, as Ibn Hajar explains, after they have entered Jannah.[^1][^3]

Then He will ask them: "Are you pleased?" And they will say: "How could we not be pleased, when You have given us what You have not given to any of Your creation?"[^1] These people are not all at one level; Jannah has ranks. Even so, they all gave one and the same answer, and from this Ibn Abi Jamrah drew the conclusion that each of them is content with his own state, "despite the differences in their stations and the variety of their ranks."[^3] No comparing, and no feeling that someone else got more.

Then He will say: "I will give you something better than that." And they will ask politely, because they know of nothing better than what they already have: "O Lord, what could be better than that?"[^1][^3]

Then He will say: "I will cause My good pleasure to descend upon you, and I will never be displeased with you after it, ever."[^1] Ibn Hajar explains why: His pleasure is "the cause of every triumph and every happiness," and anyone who knows that his master is pleased with him finds that "more delightful to his eye and sweeter to his heart than any bliss."[^2]

Compare the two kinds of approval. People's approval has to be won again every day, and you can lose it with a single word. Allah's pleasure with the people of Jannah lasts forever, by His promise, and is never followed by displeasure. And the road to it in this world is to seek His pleasure at the very moment when people's approval is pulling the other way.

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the people of Jannah answer with thanks instead of asking for more? What does that reveal about their hearts?
2. What makes people's approval so fickle? And what makes Allah's pleasure with the people of Jannah permanent?
3. Ibn Abi Jamrah drew from the hadith that each person is content with his own state despite the differences in rank. How does this idea help with the comparing we do on social media?
4. Why do we need the rule "without likening, without asking how, and without denial" when we talk about Allah's speech and His pleasure?
5. Describe a recent situation where you felt that people's approval and Allah's pleasure pointed in different directions. What did you choose?

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Are You Pleased?

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.077.activity.are-you-pleased-evening-answer" -->

For seven nights, before you sleep, write your answer to the question "Are you pleased?" in a private note, with three specific blessings from your day, one of them something that annoyed you before you saw the good in it. Then identify a situation you expect to face the next day in which the approval of your friends or followers might pull you away from what pleases Allah, and write down what you will choose in order to seek His pleasure alone, without telling anyone. The following night, record honestly: I did it, or I didn't, and what pulled at me. After the seventh night, write a private paragraph: Which kind of approval was I really chasing more? And what has changed?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.077.dua.grant-us-your-pleasure-and-your-salam" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّا نَسْأَلُكَ رِضَاكَ، فَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ تُسَلِّمُ عَلَيْهِمْ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَأَحِلَّ عَلَيْنَا رِضْوَانَكَ فَلَا تَسْخَطْ عَلَيْنَا بَعْدَهُ أَبَدًا.
>
> What it means: O Allah, it is Your pleasure we seek, not the approval of people. Make us people of Jannah who hear Your greeting of peace, and send down upon us Your good pleasure, never followed by displeasure.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of Abu Sa'id in sequence and mentions the additional wording in Muslim's narration and in the Book of Tawhid narration; explains the meaning of *uhillu* and why good pleasure is greater than every delight, from the words of Ibn Hajar, Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; presents Ibn Battal's answer and Ibn Abi Jamrah's inference; explains Ibn al-Qayyim's use of Al 'Imran 3:77 as evidence; applies the rule of affirmation without likening, without asking how, and without denial; and begins the "Are You Pleased?" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "Greeting of Peace," "Speech," and "Good Pleasure"; the three-column activity table; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Ibn Hajar's commentary on hadiths 6549 and 7518 in *Fath al-Bari*, an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 2829, chapter sixty-six of *Hadi al-Arwah*, the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Ya Sin 36:58 and at-Tawbah 9:72, and as-Sa'di's tafsir of ash-Shura 42:11. The teacher keeps in mind that seeing Allah has the next lesson to itself, and that the context of the Ya Sin passage was presented in an earlier lesson, and so does not expand on either.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When was the last time you felt that the approval of someone you love meant more to you than any gift from them?" Then read the introductory explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith in full, then map the stages of the conversation on the board: the call, the answer "Here we are," the question, the acknowledgment, the promise of something better, the polite question, and the descent of good pleasure; then read the words of Ibn Hajar, Ibn Battal and Ibn Abi Jamrah (8 minutes). Recite the ayah of Ya Sin and read its tafsir (3 minutes), then the ayah of Al 'Imran with Ibn al-Qayyim's words (3 minutes), then the ayah of at-Tawbah and its tafsir (3 minutes), then the ayah of ash-Shura and as-Sa'di's tafsir (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 3 and 4. The teacher points out that affirmation uses the words that have come in the texts without adding further description, and that the words "I will never be displeased with you after it, ever" are a promise of permanence given out of Allah's grace, as in Ibn Battal's answer.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes the first night's answer with three blessings (5 minutes) and identifies a situation expected the next day in which people's approval and Allah's pleasure pull against each other (5 minutes), then discusses with a partner, without revealing private details, how to keep the deed hidden (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give one piece of evidence for each of these: Allah's greeting of peace, His speech, and His good pleasure upon the people of Jannah, and state the rule by which we affirm them." Then close with question 5 as a silent reflection.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the hadith and the ayat of Ya Sin and at-Tawbah. Ask advanced learners to trace the hadiths about Allah's speaking to the people of Jannah that Ibn al-Qayyim gathered in chapter sixty-six and to distinguish the authentic ones among them, and to compare al-Bukhari's two wordings in the Book of Heart-Softeners and the Book of Tawhid.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Speech, the greeting of peace, and good pleasure are affirmed for Allah as they have come in the texts, without likening, without asking how, and without denial, and no one should strain to describe a voice, a form, or a place. Do not rely on the hadith in Ibn Majah about the shining light or on the report from 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz.[^8] Do not digress into the seeing of Allah, which has the next lesson to itself. Contentment with Allah's decree does not mean accepting injustice or keeping silent about harm, nor does it rule out grief and tears; someone going through a calamity is not asked to claim a contentment they do not feel, but is supported gently.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child tells how Allah calls the people of Jannah and they answer "Here we are!", how He asks them "Are you pleased?", and how He then tells them that He is pleased with them forever; names two blessings from the day and says "Alhamdulillah"; chooses a hidden good deed; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small box wrapped like a present; a sheet with a small sun drawn on it and no writing; coloring pencils; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the hadith in the same sentences without adding anything, and sends families a short note about the bedtime question: "What did Allah give you today?"

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Show the children the box and ask, "What is the best present you've ever gotten?" Then ask, "And what is the nicest thing anyone has ever said to you?" Read the explanation about grandma's words.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the report one sentence at a time. At "Here we are, our Lord, ready and glad!" the children repeat the sentence quietly. At "What could be better than that?" pause and ask, "What do you think will be better?" Then continue.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 3, 4 and 5, and say: "Allah speaks in the way that is right for Him. We don't know how, and we believe it."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child colors the first sun and practices the answer with the educator: "Allah gave me ... and .... Alhamdulillah. I'm pleased, my Lord." Then each child chooses a hidden good deed for tomorrow.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask, "What is the gift that is better than all of Jannah?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one blessing and the words "Alhamdulillah" are enough. Ask the older ones to tell the hadith to their family in four sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Allah, exalted is He, is never drawn in any image or symbol, and no light or beam is used to stand for Him; His voice and His place are not described. If a child asks, "How does Allah talk?", answer: "Allah speaks in the way that is right for Him. There is nothing like Him, and He hasn't told us how." Never tell a child that Allah is angry with them over a small mistake; connect His pleasure with love and good deeds, not with fear.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the hadith in sequence; explains *labbayka wa sa'dayk*, *uhillu*, *ridwani* and *fa la askhatu*; gives one Qur'anic proof that Allah greets the people of Jannah with peace and one proof that His pleasure is greater than every delight; expresses in a sentence the meaning of "There is nothing like Him"; and starts the Pleased Journal.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a small notebook for each student; a mushaf; a board with a seven-step staircase for the stages of the conversation.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the ayat at-Tawbah 9:72 and Ya Sin 36:58 on the board, prepares a sample page of the Pleased Journal, and sends families a notice about the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the story of Yusuf and his model, and ask: "Why did his dad's words mean more than a prize?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the two ayat (3 minutes), then the hadith, filling in the steps of the staircase: the call, the answer, the question, the acknowledgment, the promise, the polite question, good pleasure (6 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 2, 3 and 4. The teacher explains that the people of Jannah all gave the same answer despite their different ranks, and that we affirm Allah's speech and pleasure without comparing them to our own speech and pleasure.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes the first night's page with three blessings and chooses tomorrow's hidden deed, then practices with a partner telling the difference between "doing something because people will praise me" and "doing something because Allah loves it," using examples.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "Allah's pleasure is greater than the delights of Jannah because ..." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who are struggling, two blessings and one deed suggested by the teacher are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize the hadith, or to write a paragraph on the difference between the narration "I will give you" and the narration "Shall I not give you?"

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Allah, exalted is He, is never drawn or represented by anything, and students are not asked to imagine His voice. Students' journals are not shown to the class, and no student is asked about their hidden deed. Be considerate of any student going through a hard time: they are not required to write about a blessing in something painful if they cannot.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the conversation in the hadith and what it signifies that the people of Jannah answered with thanks; explains Ibn Hajar's words on why good pleasure is greater, and Ibn Abi Jamrah's inference; compares people's approval with Allah's pleasure; explains the rule of affirmation without likening, without asking how, and without denial; and begins a private log.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; three fictional scenario cards (a mocking comment you are being pushed to join in on; putting off a prayer to finish a game with friends; posting about a good deed to get praise).

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the scenarios so that they do not resemble particular students, and reads Ibn Hajar's commentary on hadith 7518 and as-Sa'di's tafsir of ash-Shura 42:11.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Say: "Write this down for yourself: Whose approval do you care about more than anyone else's? Don't answer out loud." Then read the explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at three moments: the question "Are you pleased?", the polite question, and the descent of good pleasure. Then read the terms and the ayah of ash-Shura.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Analyze the three scenarios in groups in light of questions 2 and 5, then discuss questions 3 and 4 as a class. The teacher points out that seeking Allah's pleasure does not mean looking down on people or being harsh with them; it means not putting their approval ahead of His pleasure.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes the first night's answer, identifies the situation expected tomorrow and what they will choose, and makes a plan to keep the deed hidden.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The difference between people's approval and Allah's pleasure is that ..." Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about the experience without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short study of chapter sixty-six of *Hadi al-Arwah* and to explain how Ibn al-Qayyim draws his conclusion from the ayat in which speech is withheld.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Private notes are never revealed. The lesson does not discuss how Allah speaks, or describe His speech in any way the texts have not, and any question about "how" is gently referred back to the ayah of ash-Shura. Do not digress into the seeing of Allah, which has the next lesson to itself. If a student discloses severe peer pressure, bullying, or harm, the teacher follows up privately and involves the family or the counselor in line with the safeguarding policy; contentment with Allah's decree does not mean accepting harm.
