# The Tree of Tuba

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Read the words of Allah, "Those who believe and do righteous deeds: Tuba is theirs, and a beautiful place of return" (ar-Ra'd 13:29), together with the ayah before it, and state al-Bukhari's definition of *Tuba* as "a *fu'la* form from everything good."
- Present the early scholars' views on the meaning of "Tuba is theirs" as at-Tabari transmits them (joy and delight, how excellent is what they have, a state others envy, the best of outcomes, good, Jannah itself, a tree in Jannah), and explain Ibn Kathir's remark that these views "are one thing, with no contradiction between them."
- Cite the hadith of the tree of Tuba in Ibn Hibban (7413), "A tree in Jannah that spans a hundred years' journey; the garments of the people of Jannah come forth from its blossom-sheaths," know that it is *hasan* by virtue of its supporting narrations, and add nothing to it that has not been established.
- Retell the two contrasting portraits in the hadith of al-Bukhari (2887): the slave of the dinar who, "if he is given, is pleased, and if he is not given, is displeased," and the servant for whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said "Tuba," because he fills his post completely wherever he is placed and never seeks to be noticed.
- Carry out the "Whatever Post I'm Given" activity: accepting an unseen task one did not choose, doing it fully for a set period without asking for praise or a swap, and checking one's heart each day.
- Remember the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to make us among the people of "Tuba and a beautiful place of return," and to grant us sincerity and contentment wherever He places us.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

*Tuba* is a short word, yet it carries the most beautiful good news a person could ever hear. It appears only once in the Qur'an, describing those who believe and do righteous deeds, while in the Sunnah it comes often from the lips of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, as praise for people marked by particular qualities.[^9] The early scholars differed over what it means: is it an Arabic word for goodness, sweetness and joy, a name for Jannah, or a tree within it?

This lesson pauses at three texts: the ayah that mentions *Tuba*, a *hasan* hadith describing the tree of Tuba, and an authentic hadith in al-Bukhari that sketches the kind of person who deserves *Tuba*. One question ties them together: who is the one to whom it is said, "Tuba for you"? We confine ourselves to what is established, since most of the many reports about Tuba's features in the books describing Jannah either stop at an early authority rather than reaching the Prophet or have weak chains, and we build nothing on them.[^8]

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-13-28-29" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Tuba Is Theirs, and a Beautiful Place of Return"

> **{الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَتَطْمَئِنُّ قُلُوبُهُمْ بِذِكْرِ اللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا بِذِكْرِ اللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ الْقُلُوبُ ۝ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ طُوبَىٰ لَهُمْ وَحُسْنُ مَآبٍ}** [الرعد: ٢٨-٢٩][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those who believe and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of Allah: truly, it is in the remembrance of Allah that hearts find rest. Those who believe and do righteous deeds: Tuba is theirs, and a beautiful place of return."** (ar-Ra'd 13:28-29)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tabari transmits several views on "Tuba is theirs": "How excellent is what they have" ('Ikrimah); "A state others will envy" (ad-Dahhak); "Joy and delight" (Ibn 'Abbas); "The best of outcomes for them, and it is a word from the speech of the Arabs" (Qatadah); and "Good for them" (Ibrahim an-Nakha'i). Others said it is one of the names of Jannah, and still others said it is "a tree in Jannah."[^2] After citing the first of these views, Ibn Kathir said: "These views are one thing, with no contradiction between them," and he explained "a beautiful place of return" (*husnu ma'ab*) as "a destination."[^3] As-Sa'di said: "That is, theirs is a good state and a beautiful place of return... and part of that is the tree of Tuba, which is in Jannah."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The two meanings do not clash. In its root sense, *Tuba* is everything good, pleasant and joyful, and the tree of Tuba is among the greatest of those good things. The ayah ties this goodness to two conditions, faith in the heart and righteous deeds in the limbs, and the ayah before it speaks of hearts that find rest in the remembrance of Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="ibnhibban-7413" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "A Tree in Jannah That Spans a Hundred Years' Journey"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ، أَنَّهُ قَالَ لَهُ رَجُلٌ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، مَا طُوبَى؟ قَالَ: **«شَجَرَةٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مَسِيرَةُ مِائَةِ سَنَةٍ، ثِيَابُ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ تَخْرُجُ مِنْ أَكْمَامِهَا»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: a man said to him, "O Messenger of Allah, what is Tuba?" He said: **"A tree in Jannah that spans a hundred years' journey; the garments of the people of Jannah come forth from its blossom-sheaths."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Athir said: "*Tuba* is a name of Jannah; it has also been said that it is a tree within it. Its origin is a *fu'la* form from *tib*, goodness."[^9] Ibn Kathir cites this hadith in his explanation of the ayah of ar-Ra'd.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith is *hasan* by virtue of its supporting narrations, and it tells us two things: how vast the tree is, and that the garments of the people of Jannah come forth from its *akmam*, the sheaths that enfold its blossoms. We stop there, and we add none of the descriptions found in reports that have not been established.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2887" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Tuba for a Slave Who Holds the Reins of His Horse"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«تَعِسَ عَبْدُ الدِّينَارِ وَعَبْدُ الدِّرْهَمِ وَعَبْدُ الْخَمِيصَةِ، إِنْ أُعْطِيَ رَضِيَ، وَإِنْ لَمْ يُعْطَ سَخِطَ، تَعِسَ وَانْتَكَسَ، وَإِذَا شِيكَ فَلَا انْتَقَشَ. طُوبَى لِعَبْدٍ آخِذٍ بِعِنَانِ فَرَسِهِ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، أَشْعَثَ رَأْسُهُ، مُغْبَرَّةٍ قَدَمَاهُ، إِنْ كَانَ فِي الْحِرَاسَةِ كَانَ فِي الْحِرَاسَةِ، وَإِنْ كَانَ فِي السَّاقَةِ كَانَ فِي السَّاقَةِ، إِنِ اسْتَأْذَنَ لَمْ يُؤْذَنْ لَهُ، وَإِنْ شَفَعَ لَمْ يُشَفَّعْ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Wretched is the slave of the dinar, the slave of the dirham, and the slave of the fine cloak (*khamisah*): if he is given, he is pleased, and if he is not given, he is displeased. May he be wretched and fall headlong, and when a thorn pricks him, may no one pull it out. Tuba for a slave who holds the reins of his horse in the path of Allah, his hair disheveled, his feet covered in dust: if he is in the guard, he is in the guard, and if he is in the rear, he is in the rear. If he asks permission, it is not granted him, and if he intercedes, his intercession is not accepted."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Right after the hadith, al-Bukhari said: "*Tuba* is a *fu'la* form from everything good."[^5] Ibn Hajar said that *ta'isa*, "wretched," is "the opposite of *sa'ida*," to be happy, and that "when a thorn pricks him, may no one pull it out" means "when a thorn strikes him, may he find no one to remove it." He quotes Ibn al-Jawzi on "if he is in the guard, he is in the guard": "The meaning is that he is obscure and unknown, not seeking to rise high," and at the end he says: "In it is the abandoning of love of leadership and fame, and the virtue of obscurity and humility." He also quotes others as holding that the intent is "a prayer for him to attain Jannah, because Tuba is its most famous and finest tree."[^6] Al-'Ayni said that the guard is at the front of the army and the rear guard (*as-saqah*) at its back, "and the meaning is that he carries out what he is ordered, and stays where he is stationed."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith sets two portraits side by side. The first is a heart that has become a slave to what it is given: its pleasure and its displeasure hang on the gift. The second is a slave of Allah who does not care where he is placed. At the front, he stands guard; at the back, he walks behind everyone else. Either way he fills his post completely, and he carries no weight in people's eyes. It was for this second man that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said "Tuba." And this is how we come to understand "those who believe and do righteous deeds": good work that does not wait for an audience.

The hadith also holds a practical lesson for anyone who serves in a home, a mosque or an organization: the work that benefits the group most is usually unseen, like guard duty, which no one notices until it is abandoned, or the rear guard, which protects the stragglers and the weak. The standard the hadith sets is not your place in the line, but how fully you carry out the post you are given, and whether your contentment stays the same whether you are given or not. This does not forbid seeking responsibility when one is capable of it, nor does it praise humiliation; what it condemns is letting gifts and status become the scale on which the heart weighs everything.

### Activity: Whatever Post I'm Given

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.054.activity.whichever-post-i-am-given" -->

Write down three unseen tasks in your home, mosque or workplace, the kind no one notices unless they are left undone. Do it fully for seven days, under three conditions: mention it to no one, do not ask to swap it, and do not let it slide if no one thanks you. Each night, answer two questions in a single line: Did I do it fully? And did I wait for someone to see me? On the seventh day, write a paragraph: Which of the two portraits in the hadith was closer to my heart this week?

Has anyone ever said "Well done!" to you, and it made you happy? In the Qur'an there is a word even lovelier than "Well done!" The word is *Tuba*. Allah says about the people who believe and do good deeds: "Tuba is theirs, and a beautiful place of return."[^1] That means: they will have goodness and joy, and at the end of the road a beautiful place waiting for them.[^4] And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Tuba is also the name of a tree in Jannah, a very, very big one, and the clothes of the people of Jannah come out of it.[^8] So who did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say "Tuba" to?

### An Authentic Hadith: The Man Who Stayed at His Post

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-2887" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report from Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2887" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about two men.[^5]

The first man loved money very, very much. When people gave him things, he was happy. When they didn't, he got angry. His heart had become like a servant to money. And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that this man was a loser.[^6]

The second man held his horse's reins, the straps you use to lead a horse, and he was working for Allah. His hair was messy, and his feet were dusty from walking so far. When he was told to guard the people at the front, he guarded them well. When he was told to walk at the very back of the line of travelers, he walked at the back and did not leave his spot.[^7] People didn't know who he was, and they didn't put him first, and he never asked them to look at him.[^6]

So what did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say about this man? He said: "Tuba" for him![^5]

Imagine that: the man nobody paid attention to, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave him the loveliest word of all. That's because Allah sees him wherever he is. And it's the same for you: when you line up the shoes at home and nobody sees you, Allah sees you.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every school team has two kinds of spots: the ones everybody sees, like the kid giving the presentation or scoring the goal, and the ones nobody sees, like the kid who gathers up the equipment after the game or tidies the classroom before the guests arrive. Most of us would rather have the first kind. But the Qur'an makes a promise to the people of good deeds: "Those who believe and do righteous deeds: Tuba is theirs, and a beautiful place of return."[^1] Some of the early Muslim scholars said *Tuba* means joy and delight; others said it means good things for them; and others said it is a tree in Jannah.[^2] Ibn Kathir said these explanations do not contradict one another.[^3] Once, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was asked, "What is Tuba?" He answered: "A tree in Jannah that spans a hundred years' journey; the garments of the people of Jannah come forth from its blossom-sheaths."[^8] So who deserves *Tuba*? The answer is in an authentic hadith about someone whose post nobody sees.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-13-28-29" -->

- **`A beautiful place of return (husnu ma'ab)`** — a good destination, a beautiful place the believer returns to in the Hereafter.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2887" -->

- **`The guard (al-hirasah)`** — protecting the army at its front so that no enemy can attack it by surprise.[^7]
- **`The rear guard (as-saqah)`** — the back of the army, the people who march at the very end.[^7]

### An Authentic Hadith: Tuba for the One at the Hidden Post

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-2887" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2887" audience="8-12" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, painted two pictures of two men with his words.[^5]

The first picture: a man the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, called "the slave of the dinar and the slave of the dirham." He wasn't a slave who could be bought and sold, but his heart had become a servant to money and fancy clothes: "If he is given, he is pleased, and if he is not given, he is displeased." His happiness and his anger are controlled by whatever he gets. And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Wretched is he," which means he has failed and ended up miserable.[^6]

The second picture: a man who has gone out with the Muslims in the path of Allah, holding his horse's reins. His hair is messy from exhaustion, and his feet are dusty from the road. An army has different posts: the guard at the front, to protect everyone, and the rear guard at the back.[^7] If this man is placed with the guard, he stays with the guard, and if he is placed in the rear guard, he stays in the rear guard. He doesn't complain, and he doesn't ask for a more famous spot; he does the job he was given, all of it.

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described him in a surprising way: if he asks permission, it isn't given to him, and if he intercedes for someone else, meaning he asks for something on their behalf, his request isn't accepted. In other words, people don't think he is important.[^6] So did he lose out? No! The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said about him: "Tuba for a slave..." Some scholars said that this is a prayer for him to reach Jannah, because Tuba is its most famous and finest tree.[^6]

Notice the difference. The first man always asks, "What am I going to get?" The second asks, "Did I do my part?" The first man gets upset when he isn't given anything; the second is content wherever he is, because he is working for Allah.

And here's the lesson for you: on a team, at home, or in class, sometimes you'll be given the spot nobody claps for. That is exactly where you can become one of the people of *Tuba*.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in an age that measures a person's worth by how visible they are: view counts, who stands front and center in the photo, whose name gets read out in the thank-yous. Wanting to be seen is perfectly natural. But the deeper question is this: can you do good work when no one is watching? The Qur'an promises *Tuba* to those who believe and do righteous deeds,[^1] and the early scholars differed over what the word means: joy, good, a state others envy, a name for Jannah, or a tree within it.[^2] Ibn Kathir reconciled these views by saying that they "are one thing, with no contradiction between them."[^3] In a hadith that is *hasan* through its supporting narrations, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was asked, "What is Tuba?" and he said: "A tree in Jannah that spans a hundred years' journey; the garments of the people of Jannah come forth from its blossom-sheaths."[^8] And in Sahih al-Bukhari there is a hadith that answers the question: who is the one to whom it is said, "Tuba for you"?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-2887" -->

- **`Tuba`** — al-Bukhari said it is "a *fu'la* form from everything good"; it has also been said to be a name of Jannah, or of a tree within it.[^5][^9]
- **`The rear guard (as-saqah)`** — the back of the army; the hadith mentions it together with the guard because these are two of the hardest posts.[^7]

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: Two Portraits, One Word

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-2887" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by al-Bukhari in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2887" audience="13+" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrates a hadith in which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, sets two portraits in front of you and leaves the choice to you.[^5]

The first portrait: "Wretched is the slave of the dinar, the slave of the dirham, and the slave of the *khamisah*." A *khamisah* is a luxurious cloak, so this covers money, clothes and appearances alike. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gives a precise sign of this kind of slavery: "If he is given, he is pleased, and if he is not given, he is displeased." The problem is not owning money; it is when your whole mood hangs on what you get. And he prays against such a person: "May he be wretched and fall headlong, and when a thorn pricks him, may no one pull it out." Ibn Hajar explains: when a thorn strikes him, may he find no one to remove it.[^6] A heart that lives on taking ends up alone when it is the one in need.

The second portrait: a slave holding the reins of his horse in the path of Allah, his hair disheveled, his feet dusty. An army has two grueling posts: the guard at the front and the rear guard at the back.[^7] This man doesn't bargain over his post: if he is in the guard, he is in the guard, and if he is in the rear, he is in the rear. Al-'Ayni put it this way: "He carries out what he is ordered, and stays where he is stationed."[^7] Then comes the detail that stings: if he asks permission, it is not granted, and if he intercedes, his intercession is not accepted. He has no clout, and nobody takes him into account. Ibn Hajar said: "In it is the abandoning of love of leadership and fame, and the virtue of obscurity and humility."[^6]

And it was for this man, the one people overlook, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Tuba." Ibn Hajar quotes some scholars as saying that the intent is a prayer for him to reach Jannah, "because Tuba is its most famous and finest tree."[^6]

Now think about the two portraits in today's terms. The first could be a young person who only helps out with an event if their name shows up somewhere, and who sulks when nobody thanks them. The second stacks the chairs after the party, stays until the last light is switched off, and never posts a word about it. The hadith does not forbid you from being seen; it asks you this: if your audience were taken away, would your work stay exactly the same?

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did Ibn Kathir reconcile the early scholars' views on "Tuba" instead of choosing just one of them?
2. What sign of "the slave of the dinar" does the hadith mention? Do you sometimes see it in yourself?
3. What is the difference between liking to be seen and quitting when you are not seen?
4. Why is "people don't think he is important" praise in this hadith and not a flaw?
5. What is the post nobody wants on your team, in your home, or at your school?

### Activity: Whatever Post I'm Given

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.054.activity.whichever-post-i-am-given" -->

On your team, in your family, or at your mosque, ask the person in charge to assign you the job everyone dodges, and let them be the one to choose it. Stick with it for seven days under three conditions: don't post anything about it, don't ask to swap it, and don't do it halfway if nobody thanks you. Every night, on a private sheet of paper, rate two questions from 1 to 5: How fully did I do it? And how much did it bother me that nobody noticed? At the end of the week, write a paragraph: When did the first portrait in the hadith pull me in? And when did I come closer to the second?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.054.dua.tuba-and-content-at-any-post" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ، طُوبَىٰ لَهُمْ وَحُسْنُ مَآبٍ، وَارْزُقْنَا الْإِخْلَاصَ وَالرِّضَا حَيْثُمَا أَقَمْتَنَا.

What it means: O Allah, make us among the people of faith and righteous deeds to whom You have promised goodness and a beautiful place of return. Free us from working for the eyes of others, and grant us contentment with whatever post You give us, whether everyone can see it or no one can.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner reads ar-Ra'd 13:28-29, presents the early scholars' views on *Tuba* as at-Tabari transmits them, and explains how Ibn Kathir reconciles them; states what is established about the description of the tree of Tuba and the grading of its hadith; analyzes the two portraits in al-Bukhari 2887 with the help of the commentaries of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni; and carries out an unseen task chosen by someone else for seven days, writing a review of their own heart.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with two columns, "Slave of what he is given" and "Slave wherever he is stationed"; cards with the seven early views on *Tuba*; an activity sheet with a seven-day chart; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on ar-Ra'd 13:29, the commentaries of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni on al-Bukhari 2887, and the hadith of Ibn Hibban 7413 with its editor's grading. Note that the editor graded the chain of the longer narration in Ahmad (11673) weak, and that the reports describing Tuba in at-Tabari and elsewhere either stop at early authorities or are weak, so they must not be presented as hadith. Prepare a suggested list of unseen tasks for learners who are unsure what to choose.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When was the last time someone said to you, 'Tuba for you'? And what does that word mean to you?" Gather the answers, then read the words of Allah: "Tuba is theirs, and a beautiful place of return."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the two ayat of ar-Ra'd. Hand out the cards with the early scholars' views to groups, and have each group classify its view as "linguistic meaning," "a name for Jannah," or "a tree" (7 minutes). Then read the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (3 minutes). Next, read the hadith of Ibn Hibban, pointing out the limits of what it establishes (3 minutes), then the hadith of al-Bukhari and its commentary (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Fill in the two columns on the board from the hadith. Discuss questions 3, 4 and 5. The teacher makes clear that the hadith does not condemn earning a living or legitimate visibility, but rather making one's pleasure and displeasure hang on what one is given, and that the "obscurity" it praises means giving up the pursuit of fame, not giving up work.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes their three unseen tasks (5 minutes); then partners swap lists and each chooses a task for the other (5 minutes); then each learner writes down the obstacle they expect during the week and how they will deal with it (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give two views on the meaning of *Tuba*, the sign the hadith gives for the slave of the dinar, and the task that was chosen for you." Then read the ayah one last time, and agree to review the paragraphs after a week, without requiring anyone to read theirs aloud.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the ayah, the hadith of al-Bukhari, and three of the early scholars' views. Ask advanced learners to compare the narration of Ibn Hibban with that of Ahmad, and to explain why Ahmad's chain is graded weak while the hadith is graded *hasan* by virtue of its supporting narrations.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not assert any description of the tree of Tuba beyond what is in the *hasan* hadith, and do not present reports from early authorities as the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. The hadith must not be understood as a call to accept injustice or to keep silent about the truth; what it means is giving up the pursuit of status. Make sure no one in the activity is assigned a task that involves hardship beyond their capacity or causes them embarrassment.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that *Tuba* means everything good and that it is the name of a tree in Jannah; tells the two men in the hadith apart; and does a small job chosen for them for three days without asking for praise.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Three picture cards with no words (shoes, spoons, a plant); a small cup and some beans; a set of reins or a short rope to stand in for a horse's reins; paper and crayons; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver draws the cards and makes sure the jobs are safe and suitable for the child's age. Practice telling the hadith without adding anything, and explaining *ta'isa*, "wretched," with the word "loser" without frightening the child.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Do you like it when someone tells you, 'Well done'? And do you still do good things when nobody says anything to you?" Then say: "Today we're going to learn a word that's even lovelier than 'Well done!'"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the explanation and the ayah, then the story. Hold the rope like a horse's reins when the second man is mentioned, and take two steps to "the back" to show what walking at the back of the line of travelers means.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 1, 2, 3 and 4. The caregiver stresses that Allah sees the small good things that nobody else sees.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child picks a face-down card, does the job for the first time together with the caregiver, drops the first bean into the cup, and starts drawing a picture of themselves.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Notice whether the child can say what *Tuba* means and tell what the second man did. Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the single sentence "Tuba for the one who does good while Allah sees him" and a job for just one day are enough. Ask older children to tell the story in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions. Do not describe fighting or the enemy in any detail; the idea of "guarding the people" is enough. Do not let the child come away thinking that what they ask for doesn't matter; the point is not to do good just for praise. Do not punish the child for forgetting a day.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student states the ayah, two explanations of the meaning of *Tuba*, and the hadith of the tree of Tuba; compares the two portraits in al-Bukhari's hadith; explains what the guard and the rear guard are; and does a hidden job drawn by lot for five days, recording it on a chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Small slips of paper and a bag for the drawing; a five-day chart with two columns; the term cards; a board with a simple drawing of a caravan on the move, with a front and a back.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes real hidden jobs in the classroom or at home, either with the students or beforehand, and makes sure they take about the same effort. Contact families so they can help follow up on the job without praising it over and over.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "On your team, who gets the applause? And who does the work nobody sees?" Write the answers in two columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation, the ayah and the early scholars' views (4 minutes), then the story, pausing at the drawing: where is the guard, and where is the rear guard? (5 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Ask questions 3, 4 and 5. The teacher explains that the hadith does not condemn being happy about a gift; it condemns letting all of our contentment hang on what we get.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The students write the jobs and fold the slips; each student draws one, makes their chart, and plans when they will do the job each day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes: "The sign of the slave of the dinar is..., if the second man is in the rear guard, he..., and my job is...". Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who are struggling sentence starters to complete. Ask advanced students to memorize the ayah and to look up the meaning of "a beautiful place of return" in another tafsir.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not act out the Companions or the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him. Do not show anyone's chart to the class, and do not compare students with one another. Do not put any job in the drawing that involves danger or cleaning with chemicals. Mention the army setting briefly, without describing any fighting.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen presents the early scholars' views on *Tuba* and how Ibn Kathir reconciled them, and knows the limits of what is established about the tree's description; analyzes the two portraits in the hadith in contemporary terms with the help of the commentaries of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni; and takes on the job everyone dodges for a week, measuring their own motivation on a private scale.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet of paper that is not collected; the text of the hadith on the board, divided into its phrases; cards for anonymous questions.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares two neutral contemporary examples: a volunteer who only works when there's a camera around, and a volunteer who stays after everyone has gone home. Be ready for the question "Is it haram to post about my charity work?" with a balanced answer: the hadith addresses making one's work depend on being seen, not every kind of visibility.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If every like notification disappeared tomorrow, which of the things you do would stop?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Read the ayah and the early scholars' views (4 minutes). Then the students read the hadith phrase by phrase, writing beside each phrase its meaning from the commentary (8 minutes). Then read the hadith of the tree of Tuba, pointing out its grading (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 12 minutes:** Groups discuss the two examples in light of questions 2, 3 and 4, then share their conclusions. The teacher makes the point that letting go of the love of fame is a kind of freedom, and that no one can stop the work of someone who doesn't need an audience.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student decides whom they will ask for the assignment, writes on their private sheet what they expect the job and the hardest moment to be, and sets up the seven-day scale.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes on an anonymous card: "A phrase from the hadith that moved me, and why." Read some of the cards aloud without names, then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private audio recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the narration in Ahmad 11673 and why its chain is graded weak.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to reveal the numbers on their scale. Point out that the hadith is not a call to accept humiliation or injustice, nor to hide one's needs from one's family, and that anyone who feels harmfully pushed aside in their group should talk to a trusted adult. Do not relate unestablished reports about Tuba's features as if they were hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
