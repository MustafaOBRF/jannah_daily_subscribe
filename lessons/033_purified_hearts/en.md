# Rancor Removed, Hearts Made Pure

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Define `rancor (al-ghill)` as the resentment that lies hidden in the chest, and show that Allah has promised to draw it out of the hearts of the people of Jannah in two ayat: al-A'raf 7:43 and al-Hijr 15:47.
- Set out in order how the believers are purified before entering Jannah and as they enter: the wrongs between them are settled on the qantarah (al-Bukhari 6535), and then they enter with hearts "as the heart of one man: no mutual hatred among them and no envy" (al-Bukhari 3254).
- Explain why the removal of rancor is a blessing even though the people of Jannah hold different ranks, as as-Sa'di sets it out.
- Narrate from the Qur'an the account of Yusuf, peace be upon him, and his brothers (Yusuf 12:15, 88-92, 100), and draw three steps from his forgiveness: dropping reproach, praying for the one who did wrong, and speaking of Allah's favor instead of the wrong that was done.
- Distinguish forgiving and letting go of rancor, on the one hand, from giving up one's rights or keeping quiet about serious harm, on the other.
- Carry out the "No Reproach Today" activity: turning a buried line of reproach into a response in the way of Yusuf, together with a private du'a for that person on three nights.
- Recite the lesson's bedtime du'a, asking Allah for a heart free of rancor and for a reunion in Jannah as brothers and sisters facing one another.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

*Ghill*, rancor, is the resentment that lies hidden in the chest:[^9] what is left over from an old quarrel, a buried envy, or a wound that never quite closed. Even a righteous believer may carry it. Part of the perfection of Jannah's bliss is that Allah does not let its people enter with these remnants; He Himself takes charge of drawing them out: `And We will have removed whatever rancor was in their breasts`. No place can be pure bliss while the heart still feels cramped by the people in it.

This lesson stays with the purifying of the heart itself. The settling of wrongs on the qantarah was covered in the lesson on how the believers enter Jannah; all we take from it here is that the purification comes before the entry. Then we return to this world, following the example of Yusuf, peace be upon him, who, when he finally held power over his brothers, said: `No reproach upon you today`.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-7-43" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And We Will Have Removed Whatever Rancor Was in Their Breasts"

> **{وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهِمُ الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي هَدَانَا لِهَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لِنَهْتَدِيَ لَوْلَا أَنْ هَدَانَا اللَّهُ ۖ لَقَدْ جَاءَتْ رُسُلُ رَبِّنَا بِالْحَقِّ ۖ وَنُودُوا أَنْ تِلْكُمُ الْجَنَّةُ أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ}** [الأعراف: ٤٣][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We will have removed whatever rancor was in their breasts. Rivers flow beneath them, and they will say, 'All praise belongs to Allah, who guided us to this. We would never have been guided had Allah not guided us. The messengers of our Lord did indeed come with the truth.' And they will be called: 'This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do.'"** (al-A'raf 7:43)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Naz'* means drawing something out, and *ghill* is the resentment that lies hidden in the chest.[^9] As-Sa'di says that Allah uproots whatever rancor and rivalry was in their hearts until they become loving brothers, and gives each of them such honor that he sees no bliss above the bliss he is in; so they are safe from envying and hating one another, because the causes of it are gone.[^1] It is reported through several chains that Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "I hope that Uthman, Talhah, al-Zubayr and I will be among those of whom Allah said: `And We will have removed whatever rancor was in their breasts`."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

In the ayah, the removal of rancor comes before the rivers, and before the praise of the people of Jannah. A pure heart is what makes bliss feel like bliss, and praise is the first thing a heart says once it has been made pure.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-15-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Brothers, on Couches, Facing One Another"

> **{وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَانًا عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ}** [الحجر: ٤٧][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We will have removed whatever rancor is in their breasts: brothers, on couches, facing one another."** (al-Hijr 15:47)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Mujahid said that `facing one another` (*mutaqabilin*) means none of them looks at the back of another's head. As-Sa'di said that their hearts remain sound, free of all malice and envy, clear toward one another and full of love; and their facing one another points to their visiting each other, their gathering together and their fine manners.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The fruit of removing rancor is something you can see: faces turned toward one another, not backs turned away. In this world, turning one's back on someone starts in the heart long before it shows in where we sit and how we speak.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6535" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Once They Have Been Refined and Purified"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«يَخْلُصُ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ مِنَ النَّارِ، فَيُحْبَسُونَ عَلَى قَنْطَرَةٍ بَيْنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، فَيُقَصُّ لِبَعْضِهِمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ مَظَالِمُ كَانَتْ بَيْنَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا، حَتَّى إِذَا هُذِّبُوا وَنُقُّوا أُذِنَ لَهُمْ فِي دُخُولِ الْجَنَّةِ، فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسُ مُحَمَّدٍ بِيَدِهِ لَأَحَدُهُمْ أَهْدَى بِمَنْزِلِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِنْهُ بِمَنْزِلِهِ كَانَ فِي الدُّنْيَا»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The believers will come safely through the Fire, and then they will be held back on a qantarah between Jannah and the Fire, where they will settle with one another the wrongs that passed between them in this world. Then, once they have been refined and purified, they will be given permission to enter Jannah. By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, each of them will find his way to his home in Jannah more surely than he ever found his way to his home in this world."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

In al-Bukhari's chain, one of the narrators recited the ayah of the removal of rancor before relating this hadith, and al-Baghawi cites it in explaining the ayah of al-A'raf, and Ibn Kathir in explaining the ayah of al-Hijr. That makes it the soundest guide to *when* the removal takes place: rights are settled, then hearts are refined and purified, then permission to enter is given.[^1][^2][^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

Rights are settled first; only then is whatever trace they left drawn out of the heart. This sets the boundaries of forgiveness in this world: letting go of resentment is one thing, and giving up a right is another.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3254" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Their Hearts Will Be as the Heart of One Man"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«أَوَّلُ زُمْرَةٍ تَدْخُلُ الْجَنَّةَ عَلَى صُورَةِ الْقَمَرِ لَيْلَةَ الْبَدْرِ، وَالَّذِينَ عَلَى آثَارِهِمْ كَأَحْسَنِ كَوْكَبٍ دُرِّيٍّ فِي السَّمَاءِ إِضَاءَةً، قُلُوبُهُمْ عَلَى قَلْبِ رَجُلٍ وَاحِدٍ، لَا تَبَاغُضَ بَيْنَهُمْ وَلَا تَحَاسُدَ، لِكُلِّ امْرِئٍ زَوْجَتَانِ مِنَ الْحُورِ الْعِينِ، يُرَى مُخُّ سُوقِهِنَّ مِنْ وَرَاءِ الْعَظْمِ وَاللَّحْمِ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The first group to enter Jannah will have the form of the moon on the night it is full, and those who follow close behind them will be like the most brilliantly shining star in the sky. Their hearts will be as the heart of one man: no mutual hatred among them and no envy. Each man will have two wives from among the *hur al-'in*, the marrow of whose shins will be seen through the bone and the flesh."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

`As the heart of one man` means one in agreement, love and the purity of their affection, not one in rank. They differ in their degrees, and yet they neither hate nor envy one another.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The society of Jannah has many ranks and a single heart.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-12-15" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "In the Depths of the Well"

> **{فَلَمَّا ذَهَبُوا بِهِ وَأَجْمَعُوا أَنْ يَجْعَلُوهُ فِي غَيَابَتِ الْجُبِّ ۚ وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَيْهِ لَتُنَبِّئَنَّهُمْ بِأَمْرِهِمْ هَٰذَا وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ}** [يوسف: ١٥][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"So when they took him away and agreed together to put him in the depths of the well, We revealed to him: 'You will surely tell them of this deed of theirs while they do not realize.'"** (Yusuf 12:15)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said that while he was in the well, Allah revealed to him that he would one day tell them of what they had done without their realizing who he was, and that this carried the glad tidings of his rescue and of his reunion with his family.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

Here is the wound, and here is the promise that comes true in the scene of forgiveness.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-12-88-92" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "No Reproach upon You Today"

> **{فَلَمَّا دَخَلُوا عَلَيْهِ قَالُوا يَا أَيُّهَا الْعَزِيزُ مَسَّنَا وَأَهْلَنَا الضُّرُّ وَجِئْنَا بِبِضَاعَةٍ مُزْجَاةٍ فَأَوْفِ لَنَا الْكَيْلَ وَتَصَدَّقْ عَلَيْنَا ۖ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَجْزِي الْمُتَصَدِّقِينَ * قَالَ هَلْ عَلِمْتُمْ مَا فَعَلْتُمْ بِيُوسُفَ وَأَخِيهِ إِذْ أَنْتُمْ جَاهِلُونَ * قَالُوا أَإِنَّكَ لَأَنْتَ يُوسُفُ ۖ قَالَ أَنَا يُوسُفُ وَهَٰذَا أَخِي ۖ قَدْ مَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا ۖ إِنَّهُ مَنْ يَتَّقِ وَيَصْبِرْ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُضِيعُ أَجْرَ الْمُحْسِنِينَ * قَالُوا تَاللَّهِ لَقَدْ آثَرَكَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا وَإِنْ كُنَّا لَخَاطِئِينَ * قَالَ لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ ۖ يَغْفِرُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ ۖ وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ}** [يوسف: ٨٨-٩٢][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Then, when they came in to him, they said, 'O 'Aziz, hardship has struck us and our family, and we have come with goods of little worth. So give us full measure, and be charitable to us. Indeed, Allah rewards the charitable.' He said, 'Do you know what you did to Yusuf and his brother, when you were ignorant?' They said, 'Is it really you? Are you Yusuf?' He said, 'I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. Allah has been gracious to us. Whoever is mindful of Allah and patient, Allah never lets the reward of those who do good go to waste.' They said, 'By Allah, Allah has truly preferred you over us, and we were indeed in the wrong.' He said, 'No reproach upon you today. Allah will forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of those who show mercy.'"** (Yusuf 12:88-92)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Bida'ah muzjah* means goods that are few and of poor quality. *La tathrib* means no rebuke and no blame. Ibn Kathir explains it: "and I will not bring up again the wrong you did me after today," and then Yusuf went further and prayed for their forgiveness. As-Sa'di said that he pardoned them completely, without shaming them by mentioning their past wrong, and prayed for forgiveness and mercy for them; and that this is the utmost of excellence.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The forgiveness came at a moment of complete power, and Yusuf joined three things in it: he credited the favor to Allah, `Allah has been gracious to us`; he dropped all reproach; and he prayed for them.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-12-100" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "After Satan Had Sown Discord Between Me and My Brothers"

> **{وَرَفَعَ أَبَوَيْهِ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ وَخَرُّوا لَهُ سُجَّدًا ۖ وَقَالَ يَا أَبَتِ هَٰذَا تَأْوِيلُ رُؤْيَايَ مِنْ قَبْلُ قَدْ جَعَلَهَا رَبِّي حَقًّا ۖ وَقَدْ أَحْسَنَ بِي إِذْ أَخْرَجَنِي مِنَ السِّجْنِ وَجَاءَ بِكُمْ مِنَ الْبَدْوِ مِنْ بَعْدِ أَنْ نَزَغَ الشَّيْطَانُ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَ إِخْوَتِي ۚ إِنَّ رَبِّي لَطِيفٌ لِمَا يَشَاءُ ۚ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْعَلِيمُ الْحَكِيمُ}** [يوسف: ١٠٠][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And he raised his parents up onto the throne, and they all fell down in prostration before him. And he said, 'O my father, this is the fulfillment of my dream of long ago; my Lord has made it come true. He was truly good to me when He brought me out of prison and brought you here from the desert, after Satan had sown discord between me and my brothers. My Lord is subtle in bringing about what He wills. He is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.'"** (Yusuf 12:100)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-'arsh* here means a raised seat of honor, a throne. Prostration as a greeting was permitted in the laws revealed before ours and was then forbidden in our Shari'ah. As-Sa'di, al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi note that Yusuf mentioned the prison and did not mention the well, out of generosity, so as not to embarrass his brothers after he had forgiven them.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Complete forgiveness is not satisfied with letting go of punishment; it lets go of reminders as well. Yusuf did not say, "after you threw me in"; he said, `after Satan had sown discord between me and my brothers`.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-59-10" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Put No Rancor in Our Hearts"

> **{وَالَّذِينَ جَاءُوا مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا رَبَّنَا إِنَّكَ رَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ}** [الحشر: ١٠][^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who came after them say, 'Our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who went before us in faith, and put no rancor in our hearts toward those who believe. Our Lord, You are Most Kind, Most Merciful.'"** (al-Hashr 59:10)[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said that the rancor this du'a asks to be kept away covers both little and much of it; and once rancor is gone, its opposite takes its place: love and sincere goodwill. He adds that the ayah shows this to be one of the rights that believers owe one another.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

What Allah completes in Jannah by drawing it out, the believer seeks in this world through du'a and inner struggle.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of al-A'raf and al-Hijr announce the blessing; the two hadiths in al-Bukhari explain its order and its fruit; the account of Yusuf shows what it looks like in this world; and the du'a of al-Hashr turns it into daily practice. As for the report that the people of Jannah drink from a spring at its gate and their rancor is drawn out, and the hadith "Rancor sits at the gates of Jannah like kneeling camels," we do not rely on either of them.[^11]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why does the removal of rancor come before the praise of the people of Jannah in the ayah of al-A'raf?
2. How can the people of Jannah hold different ranks and still feel no envy toward one another?
3. What is the difference between the settling of wrongs on the qantarah and the drawing of rancor out of the heart?
4. Why did Yusuf not mention the well when he spoke before his parents?
5. Does forgiveness mean keeping quiet about a right, or about harm that is still going on? Draw the distinction, with evidence.

### Activity: No Reproach Today

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.033.activity.no-reproach-today" -->

On a private sheet of paper, write the line of reproach that keeps replaying in your head about a believer who wronged you in an ordinary way, in a matter that is now over. Cross it out, and beneath it write a three-line response in the way of Yusuf: (1) a blessing from Allah you have seen despite what happened; (2) "No reproach": one reminder you will stop repeating; (3) a specific du'a for that person's forgiveness and good. Then draw three boxes, pray for them in private for three nights in a row, and check off a box each night. Do not choose serious or ongoing harm; that calls for protection and counsel, not for this activity.

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Sometimes someone takes our toy, or says something that makes us sad. Then something heavy stays in our heart, like a little stone. When that heavy feeling stays in the heart and turns into not liking someone, it is called `rancor`.

In Jannah, that stone doesn't stay in anyone's heart. Allah told us that He takes the rancor out of the hearts of the people of Jannah, so they sit together like brothers and sisters on beautiful couches, facing one another, loving each other.[^2] And our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that their hearts become like one heart: nobody dislikes anybody, and nobody is jealous of anybody.[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: Yusuf Forgives His Brothers

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-12-88-92" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

When Yusuf, peace be upon him, was a boy, his brothers took him far away from his father and threw him into a deep well. But Allah was with him, and told him that one day he would tell them what they had done.[^5]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-12-88-92" audience="4-7" -->

Many years went by, and Yusuf grew up to be an important man who handed out food to the people. Then came a hard time when there was very little food, and his brothers came to him, hungry. They didn't know who he was. They said, "Hard times have hit us and our families. Please give us food, and be kind and generous to us."

Yusuf said to them, "Do you know what you did to Yusuf and his brother?" They were amazed and said, "Are you Yusuf?!" He said, "I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. Allah has been kind to us."

They said, "By Allah, Allah has made you better than us, and we were wrong."

He could have been angry with them. But instead he said: `No reproach upon you today. Allah will forgive you.` That means: "I won't blame you today. I ask Allah to forgive you."[^6]

<!-- retelling:end -->

When his whole family came, Yusuf thanked his Lord for bringing him out of prison. He didn't mention the well, so his brothers wouldn't feel sad, and he said it was Satan who had come between them.[^7] That is what a clean heart is like: it forgives, and it doesn't keep bringing up the mistake.

### Bedtime Du'a

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Friends might fall out over a game, or brothers and sisters might fight over whose turn it is. Then the fight ends, but something stays behind in the heart: a little flash of annoyance every time you see that person. If that leftover feeling hardens into lasting dislike, it is called `rancor`.

Allah told us that He draws the rancor out of the hearts of the people of Jannah: `And We will have removed whatever rancor is in their breasts: brothers, on couches, facing one another`.[^2] And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that before the believers enter Jannah, they are stopped on a bridge called the qantarah, where the wrongs they did to one another in this world are settled, and once their hearts are clean, they are given permission to go in.[^3] Then they enter with hearts `as the heart of one man: no mutual hatred among them and no envy`, even though their ranks in Jannah are different.[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-12-88-92" -->

- **`Rancor (al-ghill)`** — resentment that hides in the chest and stays on after the disagreement is over.[^9]
- **`No reproach (la tathrib)`** — no blame, no scolding, and no bringing up the mistake again.[^6]
- **`Sowed discord (nazagha)`** — stirred up trouble and enmity between people by whispering to them.[^7]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: When Yusuf Had the Power, and Forgave

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-12-88-92" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

Yusuf, peace be upon him, was just a boy when his brothers took him away and agreed to throw him into `the depths of the well`, the dark bottom of a deep pit. At that terrible moment, Allah revealed to him that one day he would tell them what they had done, while they wouldn't even realize who he was.[^5] Long years passed. Allah rescued him, and he rose to a high position in Egypt.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-12-88-92" audience="8-12" -->

Then came years of hardship, and his brothers came before him without recognizing him. They said, "O 'Aziz, hardship has struck us and our family, and we've brought only a few poor goods. So give us full measure, and be charitable to us."

This was the moment Allah had promised him. Yusuf asked them: `Do you know what you did to Yusuf and his brother, when you were ignorant?` Stunned, they said, "Is it really you? Are you Yusuf?" He said, "I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. Allah has been gracious to us. Whoever is mindful of Allah and patient, Allah never lets the reward of those who do good go to waste."

They confessed: "By Allah, Allah has truly preferred you over us, and we were indeed in the wrong."

They needed him, and he had the power to punish them, or to remind them of everything that had happened. But he said: `No reproach upon you today. Allah will forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of those who show mercy.`[^6]

<!-- retelling:end -->

After the family was together again, Yusuf said: `He was truly good to me when He brought me out of prison`. He didn't say "out of the well," so as not to embarrass his brothers after he had forgiven them. Then he said: `after Satan had sown discord between me and my brothers`.[^7]

Notice the three steps in Yusuf's forgiveness: he spoke of Allah's blessing, he let go of blame, and he prayed for the ones who had done wrong. These steps are practice, here in this world, for the kind of heart the people of Jannah are given.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. What promise did Allah reveal to Yusuf while he was in the well? And when did it come true?
2. Name the three steps in Yusuf's forgiveness.
3. Why did Yusuf say "He brought me out of prison" and not "out of the well"?
4. A teammate apologized for a mistake, but some kids keep bringing it up every day. What do you do?
5. If someone hurts you in a serious way, or keeps hurting you, who should you tell? And why doesn't forgiving mean staying quiet about it?

### Activity: No Reproach Today

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.033.activity.no-reproach-today" -->

On a card, write a line of blame that keeps going around in your head about a brother, sister, or friend, over an ordinary thing that is already over. Cross it out, and underneath write three lines in the way of Yusuf: (1) a blessing from Allah you noticed; (2) "I won't remind them of this again"; (3) a du'a for them. Then draw three boxes, pray for them privately for three nights, and check off a box each night. Tell your mom or dad if the problem is bigger than an ordinary disagreement.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A conflict can be officially over: an apology in the group chat, a handshake, everyone back in the same classroom. And yet something lingers. You quietly keep tabs on their account. Their wins get under your skin. You've memorized the line you'll hit them with if the chance ever comes. That is `rancor`: resentment lying hidden in the chest.[^9]

No one enters Jannah carrying that weight. First the wrongs are settled on the qantarah; then whatever is in the chest is drawn out, and they enter with hearts as the heart of one man, with no hatred and no envy between them, even though their ranks differ.[^3][^4] As-Sa'di said that Allah gives each of them so much that he sees no bliss above his own, and so every cause of envy is cut off.[^1] But the work on the heart begins here, and the clearest example of it in the Qur'an is Yusuf, peace be upon him.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-12-88-92" -->

- **`Rancor (al-ghill)`** — resentment settled in the chest that stays on after the visible conflict has ended.[^9]
- **`Reproach (at-tathrib)`** — blaming and shaming someone by bringing up their wrong again after they have apologized.[^6]
- **`Facing one another (mutaqabilin)`** — their faces turned toward each other, with no one looking at the back of another's head.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Moment of Power

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-12-88-92" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an. It is not an imagined scene.**

It started with a deep wound: brothers taking their little brother away and agreeing together to throw him into the well. At the bottom of that well, a promise reached him from Allah: you will tell them of what they did, while they do not realize it.[^5] Then came years of exile and prison, and after them, a high position in Egypt.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-12-88-92" audience="13+" -->

Then the roles flip. The brothers come before `the 'Aziz`, the powerful minister of Egypt, in need: hardship has struck us and our family, and we have brought *muzjah* goods, meaning few and poor, so give us full measure and be charitable to us. This is the moment everyone who has ever been wronged dreams about: the other side is weak, and it's all in your hands.

Yusuf asks a single question: `Do you know what you did to Yusuf and his brother, when you were ignorant?` And they recognize him: "Is it really you? Are you Yusuf?" He says: "I am Yusuf, and this is my brother. Allah has been gracious to us. Whoever is mindful of Allah and patient, Allah never lets the reward of those who do good go to waste." And they confess: "By Allah, Allah has truly preferred you over us, and we were indeed in the wrong."

Then comes the verdict: `No reproach upon you today. Allah will forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of those who show mercy.` Ibn Kathir explains: that is, I will not bring up the wrong you did me after today; and then he went further and prayed for their forgiveness.[^6]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Even more striking is what he said later, in front of his parents: `He was truly good to me when He brought me out of prison`. He mentioned the prison and left out the well, out of generosity, so as not to embarrass his brothers, and then he put the split down to the fact that `Satan had sown discord between me and my brothers`.[^7] He didn't stop at forgiving; he shut the door on reminders too.

Think about the difference. Plenty of people say "I forgive you," then keep a screenshot of the conversation, or drop hints about the mistake in front of others. Yusuf's forgiveness teaches you that letting go of rancor is a decision of the heart that shows up in what you say. At the same time, it doesn't mean staying silent about bullying or ongoing harm: rights get settled, harm gets reported, and letting go of resentment is a separate thing.[^3]

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What made Yusuf's forgiveness greater than the forgiveness of someone who has no other option?
2. How does rancor show up on social media today, even after a "reconciliation"?
3. What is the difference between letting go of resentment and staying silent about bullying?
4. Why might not mentioning the wrong be harder than forgiving itself?
5. If your heart today were "as the heart of one man" with your classmates, what would change in the way you talk about one of them?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: No Reproach Today

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.033.activity.no-reproach-today" -->

Cross it out, and in its place write three lines in the way of Yusuf: (1) a blessing from Allah you came away with despite what happened; (2) one blaming habit you will stop, like dropping hints or reposting an old screenshot; (3) a specific du'a for them. Then pray for them privately for three nights in a row, and check off each night. If the harm is bullying or something dangerous, tell a trusted adult instead of doing this activity.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner defines *ghill* (rancor) linguistically and technically; sets out the stages of purification in order (the settling of wrongs, then the removal of rancor, then permission to enter), citing al-A'raf 7:43, al-Hijr 15:47, and al-Bukhari 6535 and 3254; draws three practical steps of forgiveness from Yusuf 12:15, 88-92 and 100; distinguishes letting go of resentment from giving up one's rights; and completes the "No Reproach Today" card.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a handout with the eight texts printed on it; a board with two columns headed "What Allah completes in Jannah" and "What we seek in this world"; paper and pens for the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and al-Qurtubi on al-A'raf 7:43 and al-Hijr 15:47, the tafsir of as-Sa'di and al-Baghawi on Yusuf 12:92 and 100, and Ibn Hajar's commentary on the hadith of the qantarah in the Book of Heart-Softeners (*Kitab ar-Riqaq*). The teacher also checks the status of the reports to be mentioned: the report from Ali (a Companion's statement transmitted through several chains), as-Suddi's statement about the spring at the gate of Jannah (the statement of a Successor, not relied upon), and the hadith "like kneeling camels" (not established).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Have you ever been at a lovely gathering, in a lovely place, and had it spoiled for you because someone you'd fallen out with was there?" Then: "That is why Allah made the removal of rancor part of the bliss of Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of al-A'raf and al-Hijr with their tafsir (6 minutes); then the hadiths of the qantarah and of the first group, pointing out that the narrator recited the ayah of the removal of rancor before relating the hadith (6 minutes); then the ayat of Yusuf 12:15, 88-92 and 100, and the ayah of al-Hashr (8 minutes). Write the texts into the two columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** Key questions: "How do the people of Jannah avoid envying one another when their ranks differ?" (as-Sa'di's explanation); "Why are wrongs settled and then rancor drawn out, rather than one of the two being enough?"; "What did Yusuf add in ayah 100 to the forgiveness of ayah 92?" Then the teacher discusses the limits of forgiveness: it does not mean clearing the wrongdoer of what he owes, nor staying in a harmful relationship.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Participants carry out the "No Reproach Today" activity individually and in silence (10 minutes); then anyone who wishes may name which of the three steps is hardest for them, without any details about the person (2 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Exit card: "Put the stages of purification before entering Jannah in order, with the evidence for each stage," and "State one difference between forgiving and giving up a right." Close by reciting al-Hashr 59:10 together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the ayah of al-Hijr, Yusuf 12:92, and the hadith of the qantarah. Ask advanced learners to gather the chains of the report from Ali from the tafsirs of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir and assess them, and to compare what the commentators say about when the removal of rancor takes place.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to disclose the person or the wrong. State clearly that the activity is for ordinary disagreements; that domestic, emotional or physical abuse calls for protection and specialist counsel; and that forgiveness is never forced on the one who was wronged. Yusuf and his brothers are not to be depicted in drawings or role-play. The mention of the dispute between Ali, Talhah and al-Zubayr, may Allah be pleased with them, must not be used to disparage any of them.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the hearts of the people of Jannah are clean, with no dislike in them; retells in two sentences that Yusuf forgave his brothers and said "No reproach upon you today"; says "I don't blame you. May Allah forgive you" to someone who apologizes; and makes du'a for someone who made them sad.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Drawing paper; a gray pencil and bright colors; star stickers; a small, clean stone; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver learns the simplified story well, and chooses a simple family situation in which siblings had a disagreement and one of them apologized.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The caregiver places the stone in the child's palm: "When someone makes us sad and we stay angry with them, something heavy sits in our heart, like this stone. In Jannah, Allah takes that heaviness out of every heart."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** Read the simplified story calmly and reverently, pausing at Yusuf's words `No reproach upon you today` and repeating them with the child.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 5 minutes:** "Did Yusuf get angry with his brothers when they came? What did he say? And why didn't he mention the well?" Then the child puts the stone to one side: "This is how we put our anger aside when we forgive."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Carry out the cloud-and-heart activity as described in the activity unit; the stars are added at home on the following nights.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What did Yusuf say to his brothers?" and "What do you say to someone who says sorry to you?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the heart and the sentence "I don't blame you" are enough. Ask older children to tell the story in their own words in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not depict Yusuf or any other prophet in drawings or role-play. Choose only a simple situation for the activity. Never tell a child that they must forgive someone who causes them real harm and keep quiet; instead, reassure them that they should always tell their parents. Do not make a child make up with someone in front of others.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the meaning of rancor (*ghill*), reproach (*tathrib*) and sowing discord (*nazgh*); retells the account of Yusuf in order, from the well to the forgiveness; names the three steps of forgiveness; puts in order what happens before entering Jannah (the qantarah, then hearts made pure); and completes the "No Reproach Today" card.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards for the three terms; five cards for the stages of the story (the well, the promise, the brothers' arrival, the confession, the forgiveness); the activity card; pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares three short school scenarios: a classmate who apologized but whose mistake keeps being brought up; a disagreement over a game; and repeated bullying (to distinguish what is forgiven in the heart from what must be reported).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "The fight is over, and you've shaken hands. Is everything over in your heart, too?" Then read al-Hijr 15:47.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Explain the terms, then read the story; students put the five stage cards in order and identify the point where the promise of ayah 15 comes true.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the three scenarios: "Where do we apply 'no reproach'? And where must we tell a teacher or a parent?" Make clear that forgiving a mistake does not mean staying silent about repeated harm.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Carry out the "No Reproach Today" activity as described in the activity unit; students keep their cards to follow through on the three nights.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each student writes the three steps from memory; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, present the story with picture cards that show no people. Ask advanced students to explain how Yusuf 12:15 connects to ayah 89.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Students are not asked to name the person on the card or in front of the class. Follow up on any student who discloses bullying or harm according to the safeguarding policy. Do not act out the prophets.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes Yusuf's forgiveness as forgiveness from a position of power that also lets go of reminders; connects it to the removal of rancor in Jannah and to the hadith of the qantarah; distinguishes letting go of resentment from reporting harm; identifies one digital or verbal blaming habit and stops it; and completes the activity with three nights of du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private activity sheet; three written cases: "Made up, then dropped hints in stories," "Apologized, but the screenshot is still saved," and "Ongoing bullying."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of as-Sa'di and al-Baghawi on ayah 100, and prepares a balanced answer to the question "Do I have to be friends again with someone who hurt me?": letting go of resentment is required; closeness is not an obligation where there is harm; and seeking one's right is permitted.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "'I forgive, but I don't forget': when is that wisdom, and when is it just rancor with good manners?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayat of Yusuf 12:15, 88-92 and 100 from the adults' academic section, then the ayah of al-Hijr and the hadith of the first group, focusing on "no mutual hatred among them and no envy."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the three cases in groups: what is dealt with by "no reproach," and what gets reported? Then ask: "Why did Yusuf mention the prison and not the well?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students carry out the "No Reproach Today" activity individually as described in the activity unit, and keep their sheets.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** A short paragraph: "How do I let go of rancor without giving up my rights or my safety?" Three days later, ask the students a general question, "Did you finish the three nights?", without asking for any details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For anyone who finds self-reflection difficult, identifying a single blaming habit is enough. Ask advanced students to summarize what as-Sa'di says on al-A'raf 7:43 about envy being absent despite differences in rank.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to disclose people or conflicts. Any sign of bullying or abuse is referred on according to the safeguarding policy. Forgiveness is not presented as an immediate duty for someone who has suffered serious harm, and no one is blamed for needing time.
