# Daylight in Jannah: Its Mornings and Its Evenings

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the Qur'anic scene in Surah Maryam (19:58-63): prophets who fall down in prostration, weeping, when the verses of the Most Merciful are recited to them; then a later generation who squandered the prayer and followed their desires; then the door of repentance left open; and finally the Gardens of Eden, where `they will hear no idle talk, only peace, and they will have their provision there morning and evening`.
- Explain what "morning and evening" mean in Jannah according to the commentators: times that follow one another, whose passing is recognized by changes of light, with no night and no darkness there; and keep what the text establishes distinct from scholarly reasoning about how it works, which belongs to the unseen.
- Set out the two views of the commentators on `they squandered the prayer`: abandoning it altogether, or neglecting its appointed times, as Ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, said.
- Use the hadith of Abu Hurayrah (al-Bukhari 3245) to show that the people of Jannah glorify Allah morning and evening, and the hadith of Abu Musa (al-Bukhari 574) to show the merit of the Fajr and Asr prayers, and connect the two to the care a believer gives his time in this world.
- Carry out the "Close the Gap to the Call" activity: measuring the gap in time between the moment a prayer comes due and the moment it is actually prayed, naming what causes the delay, and then shrinking that gap for Fajr and Asr through a commitment made in advance.
- Learn by heart the Prophet's du'a for sleeping and waking: *Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya* ("In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live") and *Al-hamdu lillahi alladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhi an-nushur* ("All praise is for Allah, who gave us life after He had caused us to die, and to Him is the rising") (al-Bukhari 6324).

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world we live inside a cycle that never stops: a morning that begins with striving, an evening that ends in fatigue, and a night that puts out the day and calls us to sleep. And as the cycle turns, lifetimes pass and strength drains away.

The Qur'an describes Jannah in these words: `and they will have their provision there morning and evening`. It speaks of mornings and evenings there. So does Jannah have a night and a day like ours? And why mention time at all in a home where no one ever grows tired? This lesson reads the ayah in its setting in Surah Maryam, a passage that opens with people who guarded the prayer and people who squandered it, and then asks: how should we treat our time here, while we hope for the times of that home?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-19-58-63" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And They Will Have Their Provision There Morning and Evening"

> **{أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ مِنْ ذُرِّيَّةِ آدَمَ وَمِمَّنْ حَمَلْنَا مَعَ نُوحٍ وَمِنْ ذُرِّيَّةِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْرَائِيلَ وَمِمَّنْ هَدَيْنَا وَاجْتَبَيْنَا ۚ إِذَا تُتْلَىٰ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُ الرَّحْمَٰنِ خَرُّوا سُجَّدًا وَبُكِيًّا ۩ ۝ فَخَلَفَ مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ خَلْفٌ أَضَاعُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَاتَّبَعُوا الشَّهَوَاتِ ۖ فَسَوْفَ يَلْقَوْنَ غَيًّا ۝ إِلَّا مَنْ تَابَ وَآمَنَ وَعَمِلَ صَالِحًا فَأُولَٰئِكَ يَدْخُلُونَ الْجَنَّةَ وَلَا يُظْلَمُونَ شَيْئًا ۝ جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ الَّتِي وَعَدَ الرَّحْمَٰنُ عِبَادَهُ بِالْغَيْبِ ۚ إِنَّهُ كَانَ وَعْدُهُ مَأْتِيًّا ۝ لَا يَسْمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغْوًا إِلَّا سَلَامًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ رِزْقُهُمْ فِيهَا بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا ۝ تِلْكَ الْجَنَّةُ الَّتِي نُورِثُ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا مَنْ كَانَ تَقِيًّا}** [مريم: ٥٨-٦٣][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those are the ones whom Allah blessed from among the prophets: of the descendants of Adam, and of those We carried with Nuh, and of the descendants of Ibrahim and Isra'il, and of those We guided and chose. Whenever the verses of the Most Merciful were recited to them, they fell down in prostration, weeping. But after them came a later generation who squandered the prayer and followed their desires; they will soon meet ruin. Except those who repent, believe, and do righteous deeds: they are the ones who will enter Jannah, and they will not be wronged in the least. Gardens of Eden, which the Most Merciful has promised His servants in the unseen; His promise is certain to come. They will hear no idle talk there, only peace, and they will have their provision there morning and evening. That is the Jannah which We give as an inheritance to those of Our servants who were God-fearing."** (Maryam 19:58-63)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, explains that the first ayah refers to the prophets as a whole: when they heard the speech of Allah, they fell into prostration out of humility, praise, and gratitude. Allah then mentions generations who came after them, squandered the prayer, and gave themselves over to the desires of this world. The early Muslims differed over what this "squandering" means. One view is that it means abandoning the prayer altogether, and this is the view Ibn Jarir preferred. The other is that it means neglecting its appointed times. Ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, was asked about the "guarding" of the prayer that the Qur'an mentions, and he said, "At its proper times." They said, "We always thought that referred only to abandoning it." He replied, "That would be disbelief." Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz said: "Their squandering was not that they abandoned it; rather, they squandered its time." On `except those who repent`, Ibn Kathir says that whoever turns back, Allah accepts his repentance and grants him a good end, and not the least of his deeds is diminished.[^1]

On `morning and evening`, Ibn Kathir says this means at times corresponding to mornings and evenings, not that there is any night or day there; rather, they are in times that follow one another, whose passing they recognize by changes of light and radiance. He reports from Qatadah: "There are two times there, a morning and an evening. There is no night there and no day; it is only brightness and light." And from Zuhayr ibn Muhammad: "There is no night in Jannah. They are in light forever. They know the length of the night by the lowering of curtains and the closing of doors, and the length of the day by their raising and opening." And from al-Hasan: "The Arabs counted a morning meal and an evening meal among the very best of living, so the Qur'an came down speaking to what they already felt to be bliss."[^1] Al-Baghawi mentions another view: that the phrase means ease of living and abundance of provision.[^3] As-Sa'di says that their provision continues whenever they wish for it, and that part of the fullness of its delight is that it comes at known times, so that its arrival is all the more welcome and its benefit complete.[^2]

Under this ayah Ibn Kathir also cites a hadith that contains the words "Not a single morning among the mornings of Jannah, and all of Jannah is mornings...", and he reports from Ibn Abi Hatim that it is a *munkar* (rejected) hadith, so it is not relied upon here.[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The commentators agree that there is no darkness in Jannah, and they differ over the form time takes there; how it works belongs to the unseen, for these are Gardens `which the Most Merciful has promised His servants in the unseen`. What is striking is that a passage that opens with people who squandered the prayer, an act of worship bound to set times, closes with a bliss that has times of its own. Whoever guards time for Allah here has been promised time there with no fatigue and no night. This connection is a teaching reflection offered by the lesson, not a view handed down from the commentators.

### "They Will Glorify Allah Morning and Evening"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«أَوَّلُ زُمْرَةٍ تَلِجُ الْجَنَّةَ صُورَتُهُمْ عَلَى صُورَةِ الْقَمَرِ لَيْلَةَ الْبَدْرِ، لَا يَبْصُقُونَ فِيهَا وَلَا يَمْتَخِطُونَ وَلَا يَتَغَوَّطُونَ، آنِيَتُهُمْ فِيهَا الذَّهَبُ، أَمْشَاطُهُمْ مِنَ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ، وَمَجَامِرُهُمُ الْأَلُوَّةُ، وَرَشْحُهُمُ الْمِسْكُ، وَلِكُلِّ وَاحِدٍ مِنْهُمْ زَوْجَتَانِ، يُرَى مُخُّ سُوقِهِمَا مِنْ وَرَاءِ اللَّحْمِ مِنَ الْحُسْنِ، لَا اخْتِلَافَ بَيْنَهُمْ وَلَا تَبَاغُضَ، قُلُوبُهُمْ قَلْبٌ وَاحِدٌ، يُسَبِّحُونَ اللَّهَ بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The first group to enter Jannah will have the look of the moon on the night it is full. They will not spit there, nor blow their noses, nor relieve themselves. Their vessels there will be of gold, and their combs of gold and silver; their censers will burn aloeswood, and their sweat will be musk. Each of them will have two wives, the marrow of whose shins can be seen through the flesh, so great is their beauty. There will be no disagreement among them and no hatred; their hearts will be as a single heart. They will glorify Allah morning and evening."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites this hadith in his commentary on the ayah of Surah Maryam as a witness to the succession of times in Jannah.[^1] *Al-aluwwah* is the aloeswood (oud) that is burned for its fragrance. In the narration of al-A'raj from Abu Hurayrah, al-Bukhari reports from Mujahid: "*Al-ibkar* is the first light of dawn, and *al-'ashi* is from the sun's decline until, I believe [he said], it sets." This explains the two words by what they mean in this world.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

Their glorifying Allah morning and evening is not a heavy duty laid upon them; it is a delight that renews itself as the times renew, in hearts that know no disagreement and no hatred. The times of Jannah, then, are times of remembrance, provision, and peace.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-574" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whoever Prays the Two Cool Prayers Will Enter Jannah"

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«مَنْ صَلَّى الْبَرْدَيْنِ دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever prays the two cool prayers will enter Jannah."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-bardan*, "the two cool ones," are the Fajr prayer and the Asr prayer. They are called this because they are prayed in the two cool ends of the day, its two edges, when the air is pleasant.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

Fajr is the prayer of the morning, and Asr the prayer of the evening. Whoever guards these two in this world, at the very times they weigh heaviest, when sleep or busyness pulls the other way, may hope to enter the Jannah whose people, we are told, receive their provision morning and evening.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of Surah Maryam present three portraits of time: prophets whose hours were filled with prostration; a later generation who squandered the prayer and its times for the sake of their desires; and the repentant, who turned back and entered a Jannah whose times are provision and peace, without any night. The hadith of Abu Hurayrah adds that those times are times of glorifying Allah, and the hadith of Abu Musa that the two prayers at the edges of the day, here in this world, are a road that leads there. So the practical question is this: where do our prayer times actually go?

### Activity: Close the Gap to the Call

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.039.activity.close-the-gap-to-the-call" -->

For seven days, record three things for every prayer: the time of the adhan, the time you actually began praying, and the reason for any delay (work, phone, sleep, conversation). For the first three days, simply observe, without changing anything. Then take Fajr and Asr and set one advance commitment for each that cuts off your most frequent cause of delay, such as an alarm ten minutes before Fajr, or shutting your laptop at the adhan for Asr, and keep it for the remaining four days. At the end, compare your average "gap" before and after the commitment, and write a paragraph: what did measuring reveal to you about your time?

In the morning the sun comes up, and we wake up and play. In the evening the sun goes down, and then the dark night comes, and we get tired and go to sleep.

But in Jannah there is no dark night, the scholars tell us. There is light all the time. And still, Allah tells us that the people of Jannah get their food and everything they love "morning and evening" (*bukratan wa 'ashiyya*). That means at a time like morning and a time like evening, and they never, ever get tired.[^1]

And our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah say "Subhan Allah" morning and evening.[^4]

### A Story from the Qur'an: Who Loves Prayer Time?

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-19-58-63" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In Surah Maryam, Allah tells us about some wonderful prophets who loved Him very much. Whenever they heard Allah's words, they bowed all the way down to the ground in sujud, and they cried because they loved Allah so much and stood in awe of Him.[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-19-58-63" audience="4-7" -->

Then, after them, came other people. These people let the prayer slip away. Some scholars said they kept putting it off until its time was gone, because they were busy with the things they wanted.[^1]

But Allah is so kind. If anyone comes back to Allah and says sorry, believes, and does good things, Allah lets them into Jannah, and not one of their good deeds gets lost.

And in that Jannah, they never hear any mean or silly talk. They only hear "Salam," peace. And their food and good things come to them morning and evening.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

So the prophets loved prayer time, and the people who let it slip away lost out. And whoever comes back to praying on time can hope that Allah will give them a Jannah where every time of day is beautiful.

### The Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him, for Sleeping and Waking

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.039.dua.in-your-name-i-die-and-live" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-6324" attribution="prophetic" -->

When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was ready to go to sleep, he would say:

> **بِاسْمِكَ اللَّهُمَّ أَمُوتُ وَأَحْيَا.**[^6]
>
> *"In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live."*
>
> And when he woke up, he would say: **الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا، وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ.**[^6]
>
> *"All praise is for Allah, who gave us life after He had caused us to die, and to Him is the rising."*
>
> What it means: In Your name, O Allah, I go to sleep and I wake up. And thank You, Allah, for waking us up after our sleep, and to You we will all go back on the Day of Resurrection.[^6]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Salim gets home from school, and the adhan for Asr goes off right in the middle of a game on his tablet. "Just one more minute," he says. Then one more minute... and when he finally looks up, he hears the adhan for Maghrib! He never meant to skip the prayer. The time just slipped through his fingers.

In this lesson we learn that our time in this world is a trust from Allah, and that Allah describes Jannah by saying its people will have their provision there `morning and evening`, even though there is no night there.[^1] Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that they glorify Allah there morning and evening,[^4] and he said: **"Whoever prays the two cool prayers will enter Jannah."** The "two cool prayers" are Fajr and Asr.[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-19-58-63" -->

- **`Morning and evening (bukratan wa 'ashiyya)`** — *bukrah* is the start of the day and *'ashi* is its end; in Jannah, the commentators say, these are two times recognized by light, not by a sun that rises and a night that falls.[^1]
- **`A later generation (khalf)`** — people who came after the righteous but did not follow in their footsteps.[^1]
- **`They squandered the prayer (ada'u as-salah)`** — some said it means they abandoned it; others, that they delayed it past its appointed times.[^1]
- **`Idle talk (laghw)`** — false, worthless speech that does no good.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: Three Generations and Time

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-19-58-63" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an; any explanation in it comes from the commentators.**

Surah Maryam was revealed in Makkah, and it tells of many prophets, among them Zakariyya, Yahya, 'Isa, Ibrahim, Ishaq, Ya'qub, Musa, Harun, Isma'il, and Idris, peace be upon them all. Then it describes them in a single ayah, which, as Ibn Kathir explains, is about all the prophets, not only the ones named in the surah: whenever the verses of the Most Merciful were recited to them, they fell down in prostration, weeping.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-19-58-63" audience="8-12" -->

Then Allah says: `But after them came a later generation who squandered the prayer and followed their desires`. The Companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud was asked about guarding the prayer, and he said: "At its proper times." In other words, one way of squandering the prayer is to put it off until its time has run out.[^1]

But the very next ayah opens a door: `Except those who repent, believe, and do righteous deeds: they are the ones who will enter Jannah, and they will not be wronged in the least`. Then it describes Jannah: there they hear no false talk, only peace, `and they will have their provision there morning and evening`.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Ibn Kathir said: there is no night or day there like the ones in this world; instead, there are times that follow one another, and the people of Jannah can tell them apart by the light.[^1]

One generation guarded its time for Allah, one generation squandered it, and some people turned back in repentance. And the promise at the end: a Jannah where every moment is provision and peace.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Nobody in your group ever says, "I'm going to skip the prayer." But the squad game starts just before Maghrib, the call drags on, and the "last episode" is never the last. In the end, Asr gets prayed right before sunset, or Fajr gets lost to sleep. The pressure here isn't anyone rejecting religion; it's a whole atmosphere that makes putting the prayer off feel normal.

This lesson reads ayat from Surah Maryam that describe a people who `squandered the prayer`, and then describe a Jannah whose people have their provision there `morning and evening`, with no night and no fatigue.[^1] And our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tells us that they glorify Allah there morning and evening.[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-19-58-63" -->

- **`Ruin (ghayy)`** — loss and evil, as Ibn Abbas and Qatadah explained it.[^1]
- **`Certain to come (ma'tiyya)`** — bound to happen, and the servants are heading toward it, as Ibn Kathir said; Allah's promise in the unseen will come true even though we cannot see it.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: A Generation That Squandered Time

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-19-58-63" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an; any explanation in it comes from the commentators.**

Surah Maryam moves through the lives of the prophets one after another, then pauses on a single picture that gathers them all: `Whenever the verses of the Most Merciful were recited to them, they fell down in prostration, weeping`. Ibn Kathir says they prostrated out of humility, praise, and gratitude for the blessings they had been given.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-19-58-63" audience="13+" -->

Then comes the turn: `But after them came a later generation who squandered the prayer and followed their desires; they will soon meet ruin`. Not all the commentators said these people abandoned the prayer. Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz said: "Their squandering was not that they abandoned it; rather, they squandered its time." And the reason, according to as-Sa'di, was that their concerns turned toward their desires, so they put those desires ahead of what they owed to Allah.[^1][^2]

Yet the ayat do not slam the door: `Except those who repent, believe, and do righteous deeds`. Ibn Kathir says that Allah accepts such a person's repentance and gives him a good end, and not the least of his deeds is diminished. Then comes the promise: the Gardens of Eden, with no idle talk there, only peace, `and they will have their provision there morning and evening`. Qatadah said: "There is no night there; it is only brightness and light."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice the contrast: a people who squandered the hours of this world for a passing desire, and a promise, to whoever turns back, of times that never run out and that no night ever puts out. Between those two pictures lies a decision that is yours to make: to be one of the `God-fearing` who inherit that Jannah.

<!-- story:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Among friends, why might *delaying* the prayer be more dangerous than openly announcing you're skipping it?
2. How does as-Sa'di explain the link between `followed their desires` and `squandered the prayer`?
3. In light of `except those who repent`, what would you say to a friend who feels he has "fallen too far behind" for coming back to make any difference?
4. Why would Allah mention morning and evening in a home that has no night?
5. Which prayer in your week needs a decision made in advance, rather than a burst of motivation in the moment?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: My Private Gap Log

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.039.activity.close-the-gap-to-the-call" -->

For seven days, keep a private notebook and record for each prayer: the adhan time, the time you prayed, the gap in minutes, and the reason: phone, game, sleep, friends. For the first three days, just record, and be honest. Then take Fajr and Asr and write, for each, a decision made in advance, one that kicks in before your moment of weakness, such as "I leave my phone outside my room at night" or "I tell my friends I'm praying Asr first." At the end, draw a simple line graph of your gap, and write a paragraph: who, or what, has been in charge of my time?

<!-- activity:end -->

### The Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him, for Sleeping and Waking

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.039.dua.in-your-name-i-die-and-live" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-6324" attribution="prophetic" -->

It is authentically reported that when the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was ready to go to sleep, he would say:

> **بِاسْمِكَ اللَّهُمَّ أَمُوتُ وَأَحْيَا.**[^6]
>
> *"In Your name, O Allah, I die and I live."*
>
> And when he woke up from his sleep, he would say: **الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا، وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ.**[^6]
>
> *"All praise is for Allah, who gave us life after He had caused us to die, and to Him is the rising."*
>
> What it means: In Your name, O Allah, I die when I fall asleep, and in Your name I live when I wake. All praise is for Allah, who gave us back our life after a sleep that resembles death, and to Him is the resurrection. So the last moment of your day and the first moment of your tomorrow are both in the name of Allah, and you wake to find your Fajr waiting for you.[^6]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene of Maryam 19:58-63 through its three portraits; presents the two views on `they squandered the prayer`, together with the report from Ibn Mas'ud and the statement of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz; explains what Ibn Kathir, Qatadah, Zuhayr ibn Muhammad, al-Hasan, and as-Sa'di said about mornings and evenings in Jannah, distinguishing what they agreed on (that there is no night) from what they differed on (how time works there); draws on al-Bukhari 3245 and 574; and designs a week of measuring the gap to the call.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf open to Surah Maryam; a board divided into three columns headed "The Prophets," "The Later Generation," and "The Repentant"; a weekly log sheet with the columns Prayer, Adhan Time, Time Prayed, Gap, and Reason; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Ibn Kathir's commentary on ayat 58-63 in full and prepares the report from Ibn Mas'ud, the statement of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, and the words of Qatadah and Zuhayr ibn Muhammad. Note that ayah 58 is a place of prostration of recitation (*sajdat at-tilawah*): if the teacher recites it, he prostrates and explains why. The teacher also prepares an example from his own life of a "gap" between the adhan and the prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If someone had logged for you yesterday when the adhan went off for each prayer and when you actually prayed it, how many minutes would the total come to?" Then: "Can you imagine a day with no night and no fatigue?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read ayat 58-63 in full while learners fill in the three columns (7 minutes). Then present the two views on "squandering" and the report from Ibn Mas'ud (4 minutes). Then the commentators' views on mornings and evenings, writing "Agreed upon" and "Differed over" on the board (5 minutes). Then the two hadiths, explaining the meaning of "the two cool prayers" (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss: why are people addressed in terms of mornings and evenings, which they already know? And why did as-Sa'di point to the wisdom of "known times" even though the provision never ceases? Make clear that linking the prayer times to the times of Jannah is a teaching reflection offered by the lesson, not a transmitted interpretation.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner fills in yesterday's row of the log from memory, names their most frequent cause of delay, then writes one advance commitment for Fajr and another for Asr, and swaps with a partner so that each suggests a practical improvement to the other's plan.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give two views on the meaning of 'they squandered the prayer'"; "According to the commentators, how are morning and evening recognized in Jannah?"; and "What is your commitment for Fajr?" Then read the du'a for sleeping and waking from al-Bukhari 6324.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, limit the study to ayat 59-60 and ayah 62, together with the hadith of the two cool prayers. Ask advanced learners to compare, in a table, the views on ayah 62 recorded by Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, and as-Sa'di, identifying the source of each view and its standing.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not get drawn into declaring any specific person a disbeliever over the prayer; that is not the subject of this lesson. Do not assert anything about the nature of time in Jannah beyond what the commentators have said. The hadith "Sleep is the brother of death, and the people of Jannah do not sleep" is reported by at-Tabarani and others; the lesson has not relied on it because its grading has not been verified. The hadith "Not a single morning among the mornings of Jannah" is *munkar* and must not be related as though it were established. Be considerate of anyone with a genuine reason for delay, such as shift work or illness.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the prophets used to prostrate when they heard Allah's words, that the people of Jannah receive their provision morning and evening, and that there is no dark night in Jannah; and takes part in the sand timer race for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small sand timer; a sheet of paper for each child; crayons or colored pencils; star stickers; a flashlight and a cloth cover to act out night and light; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the story calmly, draws a sample of the two suns (the morning sun and the low, slanting sun of Asr), and tries out the sand timer.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver switches the light off for a moment, then back on, and asks: "What do we do when night comes?" Then says: "Today we're going to learn about a place where there's no dark night."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the story, and has the children repeat «بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا» (*bukratan wa 'ashiyya*, "morning and evening"), pointing east for the morning and west for the evening.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions, and explain gently that people who let the prayer slip away can always come back to Allah, and that Allah loves those who come back.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The children draw the two suns and practice flipping the sand timer and reaching the prayer rug before the sand runs out, then take their sheet home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver notices whether the child can say "morning and evening" (*bukratan wa 'ashiyya*) and what it means. Then read the bedtime du'a together, and act out the waking du'a with a "waking up" motion.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one prayer with the family is enough. Ask older children to memorize «وَلَهُمْ رِزْقُهُمْ فِيهَا بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا» ("and they will have their provision there morning and evening").

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not make the child afraid of night or darkness; night in this world is a blessing for sleep and rest. Do not mention "ruin" (*ghayy*) or punishment. Never blame a child whose family does not pray Fajr on time; reach out to the family gently and let them choose what suits them.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the three generations in Maryam 19:58-63; defines the four terms; explains what morning and evening mean in Jannah; and fills in the gap chart for five days with one plan.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a gap-chart sheet for each student; a mushaf; a large wall clock or a picture of a clock; three cards reading "They Prostrated," "They Squandered," and "They Repented."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the day's adhan times on the board and prepares three short scenarios of a prayer being delayed (a game, homework, sleep).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads Salim's story from the introduction and asks: "How many minutes was the gap between the adhan for Asr and his prayer? And who took those minutes?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the whole story, arranging the three cards in order on the board and pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Kathir (9 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Hand the three scenarios out to groups. Each group writes down the "one more minute" that steals the time, then a practical plan to cut it off, and presents it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student sets up their chart as described in the activity unit, fills in today's row from memory, and agrees with the teacher on a review day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The prophets used to..., the later generation..., and the repentant..." and "In Jannah, morning and evening are recognized by...". Then read the bedtime du'a. After five days, anyone who wishes shares their two sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, limit the chart to Fajr and Asr. Ask advanced students to memorize Maryam 19:60-62 and explain, in their own words, what Ibn Kathir said about the light.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No student's chart is shown to classmates, and students are never compared by their numbers. Be considerate of a student whose family does not pray, and encourage without embarrassing. Do not state as certain either that there is sleep in Jannah or that there is none.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the link between following desires and squandering the prayer; distinguishes abandoning the prayer from neglecting its time; explains the door of repentance in `except those who repent`; describes time in Jannah in the words of the commentators; and completes the private gap log over seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a private notebook that is not collected; a mushaf; cards for anonymous answers; a simple graph sheet.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the commentaries of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on ayat 59-60 and prepares neutral examples of peer pressure on time (a multiplayer match, a livestream, staying up late).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When was the last time a prayer got pushed back because everyone was caught up in something?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the two terms, then the story, with three pauses: the prostration of the prophets; the statement of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and as-Sa'di's explanation; then the door of repentance and the description of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 2, and 3, followed by brief presentations. The teacher steers toward two points: a decision made in advance is stronger than a burst of motivation in the moment, and the door of repentance never closes, however long the delay has gone on.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student draws their log in their notebook and drafts the two advance decisions for Fajr and Asr, to be put into practice after the three days of observation, as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: what steals their time, the difference between abandoning the prayer and neglecting its time, and their first decision. A week later the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see notebooks. Then read the du'a for sleeping and waking.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find written logging difficult may keep private voice notes instead. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on Ibn Kathir's words, "times that follow one another, whose passing they recognize by changes of light and radiance," and on how this changes the way they picture time.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to reveal how many prayers they have missed. The lesson must not be used to shame any student's friends. Keep in mind that a teen struggling with a sleep disorder or emotional distress needs support, not blame. Do not assert unseen details about time in Jannah beyond what has been reported.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
