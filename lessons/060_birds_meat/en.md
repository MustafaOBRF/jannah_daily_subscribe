# Birds and Meat in Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Cite the two ayat that tell of the meat of birds and of meat in Jannah, "and the meat of fowl, whatever they desire" (al-Waqi'ah 56:21) and "And We shall keep them supplied with fruit and meat, whatever they desire" (at-Tur 52:22), and explain the meaning of "whatever they desire" from the tafsirs of Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari and as-Sa'di.
- Narrate the hadith of Anas (at-Tirmidhi 2542, hasan) in its proper order: the question about al-Kawthar, the description of the river, the birds whose necks are like the necks of camels, 'Umar's remark, "These must be living in luxury," and the reply of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, "Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do."
- Explain the hadith's key words (*al-juzur*, *na'imah*, *akalatuha*) as *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi* explains them, and show how the Prophet's reply moved attention away from the blessing and toward the one who enjoys it.
- State Ibn al-Qayyim's principle that nothing of the Hereafter exists in this world except the names, and refrain from describing how the food of Jannah is prepared beyond what the texts tell us.
- Distinguish the hadith the lesson relies on from other reports on the same subject that the lesson does not rely on, because they are weak or because their chains are disputed.
- Carry out the "Turning Chart" activity: recording moments of admiring a blessing or a creature, and turning each moment into hope and a deed that brings the learner closer to the people of Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In human life, meat is the mark of honor. It is what guests are welcomed with and what feasts are made of, and a poor man rejoices when it reaches his table after a long time without it. That is why the Qur'an names meat among the gifts with which Allah honors the people of Jannah, singles out the meat of birds in particular, and qualifies all of it with the words "whatever they desire."

This lesson takes up two ayat about meat and the meat of birds, and a hasan hadith about the birds of Jannah that came in answer to a question about al-Kawthar. Food and drink in general have a lesson of their own, as do wine and the drinks of Jannah, so we do not repeat them here. The lesson also stops where the text stops: it does not describe how this food is prepared beyond what has been reported.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-20-21" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And the Meat of Fowl, Whatever They Desire"

> **{وَفَاكِهَةٍ مِمَّا يَتَخَيَّرُونَ ۝ وَلَحْمِ طَيْرٍ مِمَّا يَشْتَهُونَ}** [الواقعة: ٢٠-٢١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And fruit, whatever they choose, and the meat of fowl, whatever they desire."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:20-21)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The two ayat continue the description of the foremost, those brought near to Allah, that began with "Immortal youths will circulate among them" (al-Waqi'ah 56:17). On the first, Ibn Kathir says: "That is, they will circulate among them with whatever fruits they choose." On the second, at-Tabari says: "And they will also circulate among them with the meat of fowl, whatever they desire, of the birds their souls long for." As-Sa'di says: "That is, of every kind of bird they desire, and of whatever sort of its meat they wish."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

Notice that the blessing comes to them; they do not go out in search of it. And it is measured by desire, not by need: there is no hunger there to be satisfied, only a wish to be honored. Notice too that the fruit is "whatever they choose" while the meat is "whatever they desire." This is the lesson's own observation on the wording, not a reported tafsir.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-22" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And We Shall Keep Them Supplied with Fruit and Meat"

> **{وَأَمْدَدْنَاهُمْ بِفَاكِهَةٍ وَلَحْمٍ مِمَّا يَشْتَهُونَ}** [الطور: ٢٢][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We shall keep them supplied with fruit and meat, whatever they desire."** (at-Tur 52:22)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "That is, We will furnish them with fruits and meats of every kind, whatever is delicious and desired." As-Sa'di says: "That is, We supplied the people of Jannah out of Our vast bounty and Our all-embracing provision," and he explains "and meat, whatever they desire" as "everything they ask for and their souls desire, the meat of birds and other things."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

*Imdad*, "supplying," is giving that keeps coming and never runs out, and its source is Allah's bounty, not anything the servant has earned. The ayah stands in the passage about the God-fearing whose offspring Allah joins to them, so the blessing there is the blessing of a family gathered together, not of a person alone.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2542" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Those Who Eat Them Live in Greater Luxury Than They Do"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: سُئِلَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: مَا الْكَوْثَرُ؟ قَالَ: **«ذَاكَ نَهْرٌ أَعْطَانِيهِ اللَّهُ -يَعْنِي فِي الْجَنَّةِ- أَشَدُّ بَيَاضًا مِنَ اللَّبَنِ، وَأَحْلَى مِنَ الْعَسَلِ، فِيهَا طَيْرٌ أَعْنَاقُهَا كَأَعْنَاقِ الْجُزُرِ»**. قَالَ عُمَرُ: إِنَّ هَذِهِ لَنَاعِمَةٌ. قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«أَكَلَتُهَا أَنْعَمُ مِنْهَا»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was asked, "What is al-Kawthar?" He said: **"That is a river Allah has given me (meaning in Jannah), whiter than milk and sweeter than honey. In it are birds whose necks are like the necks of camels."** 'Umar said, "These must be living in luxury!" The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Mubarakfuri explains that "meaning in Jannah" is the narrator's own comment; that "in it" means in that river or along its banks; that *al-juzur*, with *u* after the *j* and the *z*, is "the plural of *jazur*, which is the camel"; and that *la-na'imah* means "fattened and pampered," as in *an-Nihayah*. He gives the vowelling of *akalatuha* with *a* after the hamzah, the *k* and the *l*, as "the plural of *akil* (one who eats)... and the meaning is those who eat them," and also with a long first vowel and an *i* after the *k*, *akilatuha*.[^4] Ad-Dihlawi says that "These must be living in luxury" means "good, tender and plump, or living in comfort and ease."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The questioner asked about the river; the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described it and mentioned great, majestic birds beside it. 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, was struck by them, and the Prophet's reply turned his gaze from the birds to those who would eat them: whoever eats of them enjoys a greater bliss than they do. The most wonderful thing in Jannah, then, is not the food; it is the state of the one whom Allah has honored by admitting him there. That turns admiration for the description into a practical question: how do I become one of its people?

Notice also how the description works. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was speaking to people who knew milk and honey and camels, so he brought the unseen close to them through what they knew; then he used the comparatives "whiter" and "sweeter" so that the reality would remain above the example. The comparison opens a door for the imagination without setting a limit on the truth. From this we understand that the appetite for food is not blameworthy in itself, since Allah has promised its fulfillment to those He honors. What is blameworthy is for it to become a person's goal in this world, distracting him from the road to that abode.

<!-- evidence:end -->

After gathering the texts on the food and drink of Jannah, Ibn al-Qayyim says: "Nothing of what is in the Hereafter exists in this world except the names. As for the things named, the difference between them is beyond anything human beings know." And when he takes up the question of how meat is cooked there, he says: "The correct view is that it is roasted in Jannah by means that the Almighty, the All-Knowing has decreed for cooking it and making it good."[^5] So we do not imagine the food of Jannah being prepared in some worldly way, and we add nothing to the text. There are other reports on this subject that the lesson does not rely on, because their chains are weak or disputed.[^6]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the significance of the two ayat qualifying meat with the words "whatever they desire"?

2. What is the difference, as the lesson observes it, between describing the fruit as "whatever they choose" and the meat as "whatever they desire"? And why should we not treat this observation as a reported tafsir?

3. How did the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, "Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do," move attention from the blessing to the one who enjoys it?

4. What does Ibn al-Qayyim's principle, "Nothing of what is in the Hereafter exists in this world except the names," keep in check when we speak about the bliss of Jannah?

5. When does your admiration stop at the thing itself, and when does it turn into hope and action?

### A Hasan Hadith: The Birds by the River of al-Kawthar

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-2542" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. At-Tirmidhi reported it and said it is hasan (a good hadith). It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-2542" audience="4-7" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, tells us that one day someone asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, "What is al-Kawthar?"[^3]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that it is a river Allah gave him in Jannah. Its water is whiter than milk and sweeter than honey. And beside it are big birds with long necks, as long as a camel's neck![^3]

Can you picture it? A bird with a neck as long as a camel's! Then 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, said that these birds must have such a lovely, comfortable life: plump, happy and well looked after.[^3][^4]

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: «أَكَلَتُهَا أَنْعَمُ مِنْهَا»,[^3] which means: the people in Jannah who eat from them have an even happier, more comfortable life than the birds do.

The birds are beautiful, but even more beautiful is being one of the people of Jannah. So when you see something beautiful, say: "O Allah, make me one of the people of Jannah."

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On a school trip to the bird park, the students stopped in front of a long-necked bird strolling calmly beside the lake. "Wow, it's gorgeous!" one of them shouted. Another pulled out a phone to take a picture, and a third asked the guide what it was called and what it ate. Admiring Allah's creation is a beautiful thing. But what do we do with our admiration once we walk away?

Allah has told us that the people of Jannah will be given "the meat of fowl, whatever they desire,"[^1] and He says: "And We shall keep them supplied with fruit and meat, whatever they desire."[^2] In other words, out of His bounty He gives them gift after gift of whatever their hearts long for. And in a hasan hadith, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught us where to point our admiration.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="tirmidhi-2542" -->

- **`Al-juzur`** — the plural of *jazur*, a camel.[^4]
- **`Na'imah`** — plump, tender and fine, or living a life of comfort and luxury.[^4]
- **`Akalatuha`** — the plural of *akil*, "one who eats," so it means "the people who eat them."[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Hasan Hadith: "Those Who Eat Them Live in Greater Luxury"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-2542" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. At-Tirmidhi reported it and said it is hasan (a good hadith). It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-2542" audience="8-12" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was once asked a question: "What is al-Kawthar?"[^3]

He answered, "That is a river Allah has given me," and the narrator explains that he meant in Jannah. Then he described it: whiter than milk, sweeter than honey, and in it are birds whose necks are like the necks of *juzur*, that is, of camels.[^3][^4]

Everyone listening knew camels well, and knew just how long a camel's neck is. So picture a bird that size! Then 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, said, "These must be living in luxury," meaning they must be plump, fine birds with a life of comfort.[^3][^4]

That is when the answer came that changed the whole way of looking at it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: «أَكَلَتُهَا أَنْعَمُ مِنْهَا»,[^3] "Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do," meaning the people who eat from them enjoy a far greater bliss than the birds themselves.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not tell 'Umar, "Don't admire them." Instead, he carried his admiration to something greater: if the birds live in such comfort, imagine the comfort of the person Allah has honored by bringing him into Jannah! And then the question that really matters becomes: how do I become one of its people?

We do not know how the food of Jannah is prepared, because Allah has not told us. Ibn al-Qayyim said that nothing of what is in the Hereafter exists in this world except the names.[^5]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A short clip: a plate of grilled meat glistening under studio lights, the sound of a knife slicing through it, fast music. Then another clip, and a third. Half an hour later you haven't eaten a thing, but you've "liked" dozens of dishes. Admiration itself isn't a flaw. The real question is where it takes you. Does it stop at the image, or does it turn toward something that lasts?

The Qur'an speaks plainly about the blessing of meat in Jannah: "and the meat of fowl, whatever they desire,"[^1] and "And We shall keep them supplied with fruit and meat, whatever they desire."[^2] Desire there is honored without limit. And in a hasan hadith we see how the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, redirected the admiration of one of his closest Companions.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="tirmidhi-2542" -->

- **`Na'imah`** — some explain it as plump and pampered; others as fine and tender, or living in comfort.[^4]
- **`Akalatuha an'amu minha`** — "Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do," that is, whoever eats of them enjoys a greater bliss than they do. *Akalah* is the plural of *akil* (one who eats), just as *talabah* (students) is the plural of *talib*.[^4]
- **`Ya'ni fil-jannah ("meaning in Jannah")`** — a clarification added by the narrator, not part of the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Hasan Hadith: The Pampered Birds and Those Who Eat Them

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-2542" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. At-Tirmidhi reported it and said it is hasan (a good hadith). It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-2542" audience="13+" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was asked, "What is al-Kawthar?" It was a question about a special gift with which Allah had honored His Prophet.[^3]

He described it: a river Allah has given me, whiter than milk, sweeter than honey, and in it are birds whose necks are like the necks of *juzur*, that is, camels.[^3] An astonishing image for people who knew camels and knew how high a camel holds its head.

'Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, was there, and he said, "These must be living in luxury."[^3] A word of admiration for what he had just heard described.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: «أَكَلَتُهَا أَنْعَمُ مِنْهَا», "Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do."[^3] One short sentence, and the whole scene flips. The greatest value is not in the bird but in the human being who reaches the place where he eats from it. However great a blessing is, it is there to serve; the one truly honored is the person Allah has brought into His home.

Notice what did not happen. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not scold 'Umar for admiring the birds, and he did not turn the conversation into a long description of the food and how it is made. He lifted the admiration to a higher point. That is an approach we need today, when so much of what is put in front of us is designed to keep our admiration stuck on the image. The Prophet's turn of the gaze asks: and where are you among the people of real bliss?

Ibn al-Qayyim reminds us that the bliss of the Hereafter resembles the pleasures of this world in nothing but the names.[^5] The most mouthwatering thing you have ever seen in a clip shares nothing with what is there except a name.

For seven days, keep a private note that you do not post anywhere. Each day, record one moment of admiration, in real life or on a screen: a food, a place, something you own, or a living creature. Underneath it, write: Did my admiration stop at the thing, or did it turn? Then write your turning sentence, and one good deed you did that same day in the hope of being one of the people of Jannah. If you did not do one, leave the space blank and do not make one up. On the seventh day, write a paragraph: What captures my admiration most? And which turn do I want to become a habit?

<!-- activity:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.060.dua.among-those-who-eat-of-its-birds" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ الَّذِينَ يَأْكُلُونَ مِنْ طَيْرِهَا، وَأَعْطِنَا فِيهَا مِمَّا نَشْتَهِي، وَاجْعَلْنَا الْيَوْمَ مِنَ الشَّاكِرِينَ.
>
> *"O Allah, make us people of Jannah, who live in greater bliss than its birds and eat from them; give us there whatever our souls desire; and make us, today, among those who are grateful for Your blessings, so that admiring them never distracts us from You."*

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites al-Waqi'ah 56:20-21 and at-Tur 52:22 and explains "whatever they desire" from three tafsirs; narrates the hadith of at-Tirmidhi 2542 and explains its key words (*al-juzur*, *na'imah*, *akalatuha*) from *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi*; shows how the Prophet's reply moved the gaze from the blessing to the one who enjoys it; states Ibn al-Qayyim's principle about the names; distinguishes the hadith the lesson relies on from the reports it does not; and starts a seven-day Turning Chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with two columns headed "The Blessing" and "The One Who Enjoys It"; a two-column Turning Chart template; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsirs of Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari and as-Sa'di on al-Waqi'ah 56:20-21 and at-Tur 52:22, the commentary on the hadith in *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi*, and chapter forty-eight of *Hadi al-Arwah* together with the editor's notes on the hadiths about birds. The teacher bears in mind that food and drink in general, and wine, have lessons of their own, and that Ahmad's hadith describing the birds of Jannah as "like Bactrian camels" has a disputed chain and is not relied upon.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Where you come from, what food is served to honor a guest? And why?" Then connect the answers to the mention of meat among the blessings of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Recite the two ayat together with the context in al-Waqi'ah 56:17 (4 minutes), and read the statements of the commentators (4 minutes). Then read the hadith in full and explain its wording from *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi*, pointing out that "meaning in Jannah" is the narrator's comment (8 minutes). Then read Ibn al-Qayyim's statement (4 minutes). Fill in the two columns on the board as you go.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 2, 3 and 5. The teacher draws attention to the difference between the lesson's observation on the wording and a reported tafsir, and makes clear that admiring a blessing is perfectly legitimate; what is asked of us is to turn from it to the Giver and to action.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner fills in the first row of their chart with a moment of admiration from their day (7 minutes), then swaps with a partner so that each can suggest a sharper wording of the other's turn (5 minutes). The group agrees on a time to review the charts in a week (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "In two sentences, explain the meaning of 'Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do,' and name one report on this subject that the lesson does not rely on, and why." Then close with the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the two ayat, the hadith and Ibn al-Qayyim's statement. Ask advanced learners to trace the routes of the hadith as set out in the editor's notes to *Hadi al-Arwah* (including the narration that has "Abu Bakr" in place of "'Umar"), and to compare the gradings of at-Tirmidhi, al-Albani and Bashshar 'Awwad.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not add to the text's description of the food of Jannah, and do not elaborate on how the meat is prepared there; the most that may be said is what the lesson quotes from Ibn al-Qayyim. Do not rely on the report of the bird that "falls down before you roasted," nor on the report of Hudhayfah, nor on the mursal report of Qatadah.[^6] Do not say that this hadith describes the Basin on the Day of Judgment: the narrator made clear that it is in Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that in Jannah there is a river called al-Kawthar, whose water is whiter than milk and sweeter than honey, with birds beside it whose necks are like camels' necks; says that the people who eat from them have a happier life than the birds; draws a two-part picture; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A picture of a camel and a picture of a small bird, with no writing on them; a cup of milk and a spoonful of honey for tasting (after checking for allergies); sheets of paper folded in half; crayons; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the report without adding anything to it, makes sure the parents have agreed to the tasting, and prepares a finished sample drawing.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Show the picture of the small bird and ask, "Where have you seen birds?" Then show the picture of the camel and point out how long its neck is.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation, then the report. Pause at "whiter than milk and sweeter than honey," let the children taste a little milk and honey, and say: "The water of al-Kawthar is sweeter than this."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 3, 4 and 5, and teach the child the sentence: "O Allah, make me one of the people of Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child draws something they liked on the first half and a good deed they did today on the second half, then says the sentence together with the caregiver. Each child takes home two blank sheets to finish the remaining days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask: "Who has the happier life: the birds, or the people of Jannah?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one drawing and the sentence of the du'a are enough. Ask older children to tell the report to their family in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, and never make any image of Allah, the Exalted; do not act out the scene through role-play. Do not show pictures of slaughter, raw meat or cooked birds, and do not talk about slaughter. If a child asks, "How are the birds cooked in Jannah?" answer: "Allah hasn't told us that, and everything in Jannah is good and beautiful." Make allowances for any child who cannot have honey or milk because of an allergy.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the hadith in order; explains *al-juzur*, *na'imah* and *akalatuha*; cites the two ayat and the meaning of "whatever they desire"; explains how the Prophet's reply carried admiration to something greater; and starts a five-day Turning Strip.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a comic-strip sheet with ten panels; a mushaf; a picture of a long-necked bird with no writing on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the two ayat on the board and prepares a sample of two completed panels from the Turning Strip.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the picture of the bird and ask: "What do you say when you see something beautiful? And what do you do afterward?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the two ayat (4 minutes), then the story, pausing just before the Prophet's answer to ask, "What do you think he will say to 'Umar?" (5 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 3, 4 and 5. The teacher makes clear that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not criticize admiration but lifted it higher, and that how the food of Jannah is prepared belongs to the unseen, which we do not speak about without knowledge.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student draws the first two panels of their strip, writes the sentence "And even greater than that is...", and decides when they will complete the remaining days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The birds of Jannah live in luxury, but..." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide a strip with ready-made pictures to choose from. Ask advanced students to memorize the hadith, or to write a paragraph about the difference between "whatever they choose" and "whatever they desire."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the Companions are not drawn in the strip. Do not show pictures of slaughter or cooked birds. Never mock a student for admiring a food or a possession; admiration is not a sin. Do not give students any descriptions of the food of Jannah beyond what is in the text.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the Prophet's reply, "Those who eat them live in greater luxury than they do," as a redirection of admiration rather than a criticism of it; explains the hadith's key words and the fact that "meaning in Jannah" is the narrator's comment; applies Ibn al-Qayyim's principle about the names to food content online; and starts a seven-day private turning note.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; a written description of a typical food clip (the clip itself is not shown).

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes a description of a short food clip that includes the techniques it uses to grab attention (lighting, sound, pace), and reads the commentary in *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi* and Ibn al-Qayyim's statement.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "How many times this week were you impressed by something you saw on a screen? And what stayed in your heart from it?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the story, pausing at three moments: the description of the birds, 'Umar's remark, and the Prophet's reply. Then read the terms and Ibn al-Qayyim's statement.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** In groups, analyze the description of the clip in light of questions 2 and 3, then answer question 5 in private writing. The teacher makes clear that the turn does not mean suppressing admiration; it means lifting it up to the Giver and to action.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student designs their private note, records the first moment, and sets the honesty rule: a blank space is better than a made-up deed.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "What captures my admiration, and where do I want to turn it?" Then read the du'a. After a week, the teacher asks a general question about how the experience went.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on Ahmad's narration "like Bactrian camels," the disagreement among hadith scholars about its chain, and why the lesson does not rely on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show actual food clips in class, or any scenes of slaughter or butchering. Do not shame anyone for what they watch, and do not reveal anyone's private notes. Be mindful of students who struggle with eating disorders or body image: do not link the lesson to weight or dieting.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
