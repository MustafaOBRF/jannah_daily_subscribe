## Lesson Objectives And Outcomes

In this lesson, the learner discovers that Allah, Exalted is He, made the path to Jannah surrounded by hardships, and the path to the Fire surrounded by desires, just as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ told us. The learner comes to understand that these hardships are not pointless toil or meaningless difficulty, but rather the trial that Allah promised to everyone who believed before us, and that the religion, with all of this, remains ease without hardship, so long as its follower stays balanced and does not overburden himself. The learner reflects on the story of Ka'b ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, in the expedition of Tabuk — a true account, sound in its chain of narration, of one who chose difficult truthfulness over easy falsehood: he patiently bore the hardship of being shunned for fifty nights, and refused the comfort offered to him by the king of Ghassan, until Allah relieved him and sent down his acceptance of repentance as Qur'an recited to this day. Among the outcomes of the lesson is that every learner, according to his age, comes away with one practical distinction between a difficult path that leads to Allah's pleasure and an easy path that leads to His displeasure, and with one small step he commits to taking toward the first path.

### For Adults

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Allah, Mighty and Majestic, established a fixed law that never changes: the path to Jannah is surrounded by hardships, and the path to the Fire is surrounded by desires. So everything that weighs on the soul — prayer in the cold, truthfulness that costs its speaker a price, patience under harm, spending money or time — belongs to the "hardships" that surround Jannah; and everything that pulls the soul toward immediate comfort, even at the expense of the truth, belongs to the "desires" that surround the Fire. This is no contradiction with his ﷺ statement, "Indeed, the religion is ease" — rather it completes it. The ease of the religion is that Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity, and that the path is balanced and easy to walk for whoever aims straight and draws near, not that it is free of the difficulty by which the truthful is distinguished from the liar, and the patient from the anxious. Allah, Glorified is He, informed us that this trial is a fixed law for every believer who came before, until the hardship reached some of them so severely that they hastened to ask for Allah's help, and the answer came to them: Truly, the help of Allah is near. Among the clearest sound historical evidences of this law is the story of Ka'b ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, in his absence from the expedition of Tabuk, where he chose the hardship of truthfulness and its consequence over the desire of falsehood and its immediate comfort, so his outcome was that Allah sent down his acceptance of repentance as Qur'an recited until the Day of Judgment.

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2822" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->
عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «حُفَّتِ الْجَنَّةُ بِالْمَكَارِهِ، وَحُفَّتِ النَّارُ بِالشَّهَوَاتِ»[^1].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **"Jannah is surrounded by hardships, and the Fire is surrounded by desires."**[^1]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** Imam an-Nawawi, commenting on this hadith, said: the meaning of "surrounded" is that it was encircled, and the hardships were made to encircle it just as a wall encircles an orchard, so that no traveler reaches it except after crossing those hardships — just as the Fire was surrounded by desires, so that no one falls into it except by following his own whims.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This hadith is the foundation of the entire lesson: every act of obedience that weighs on the soul is one of the gates of Jannah, and every desire that is made attractive to the soul is one of the gates of the Fire, and the discerning believer is the one who measures his choice not by its immediate ease, but by its final outcome.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-214" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->
{أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ أَنْ تَدْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ وَلَمَّا يَأْتِكُمْ مَثَلُ الَّذِينَ خَلَوْا مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ ۖ مَسَّتْهُمُ الْبَأْسَاءُ وَالضَّرَّاءُ وَزُلْزِلُوا حَتَّى يَقُولَ الرَّسُولُ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مَعَهُ مَتَى نَصْرُ اللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا إِنَّ نَصْرَ اللَّهِ قَرِيبٌ} [الْبَقَرَةِ: ٢١٤][^2].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Or do you think that you will enter Jannah while such [trial] has not yet come to you as came to those who passed on before you? They were touched by poverty and hardship and were shaken until [even] the Messenger and those who believed with him said, 'When is the help of Allah?' Unquestionably, the help of Allah is near."** (al-Baqarah 2:214)[^2]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, mentioned that Allah, Exalted is He, informs His believing servants that He must try them, so that the truthful is distinguished from the liar, just as He tried the nations before them with poverty, sickness, and fear, until the hardship reached them so severely that they considered the help slow in coming, so the divine answer came to them with the glad tiding: Truly, the help of Allah is near.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This ayah clarifies that trial is neither a punishment nor a sign of anger, but a condition for entering Jannah that every believer before us has walked through, so let no one imagine that he will be exempted from it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-3-142" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->
{أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ أَنْ تَدْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ وَلَمَّا يَعْلَمِ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ جَاهَدُوا مِنْكُمْ وَيَعْلَمَ الصَّابِرِينَ} [آلِ عِمْرَانَ: ١٤٢][^3].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Or do you think that you will enter Jannah while Allah has not yet made evident those of you who fight in His cause and made evident those who are steadfast?"** (Al 'Imran 3:142)[^3]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: this is a rhetorical question of denial — meaning, do not think that you will enter Jannah by mere claim without a test, until it becomes clear in reality who struggled against his own self, his desires, and his enemy, and who remained patient in hardship.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** Allah here pairs "striving" and "patience" as two inseparable conditions for entering Jannah, and these are exactly what Ka'b ibn Malik walked through when he struggled against himself to avoid lying, and patiently bore the punishment of his truthfulness for fifty nights.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-39" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->
عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: «إِنَّ الدِّينَ يُسْرٌ، وَلَنْ يُشَادَّ الدِّينَ أَحَدٌ إِلَّا غَلَبَهُ، فَسَدِّدُوا وَقَارِبُوا وَأَبْشِرُوا، وَاسْتَعِينُوا بِالْغَدْوَةِ وَالرَّوْحَةِ وَشَيْءٍ مِنَ الدُّلْجَةِ»[^4].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet ﷺ, who said: **"Indeed, the religion is ease, and no one makes the religion a rigorous struggle except that it overwhelms him. So aim straight, draw near [to perfection], and be glad, and seek help through the morning, the evening, and a portion of the night."**[^4]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar, in Fath al-Bari, said: the meaning of "no one makes the religion a rigorous struggle except that it overwhelms him" is that whoever burdens himself with worship beyond his capacity becomes incapable and gives up; so he ﷺ commanded moderation: "aim straight" means seek what is correct and upright, "and draw near" means come as close to perfection as you can even if you do not fully attain it, and seek help in that through the times of freshness and energy.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This hadith is the safeguard that prevents understanding "hardships" as limitless self-imposed severity; the path is surrounded by moderate difficulty that every accountable person can bear, not by ruinous strain that destroys the one who carries it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-4418" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->
قَالَ كَعْبُ بْنُ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه: لَمْ أَتَخَلَّفْ عَنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فِي غَزْوَةٍ غَزَاهَا إِلَّا فِي غَزْوَةِ تَبُوكَ... وَاللَّهِ مَا كُنْتُ قَطُّ أَقْوَى وَلَا أَيْسَرَ حِينَ تَخَلَّفْتُ عَنْهُ فِي تِلْكَ الْغَزْوَةِ، وَلَمْ يَكُنْ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم يُرِيدُ غَزْوَةً إِلَّا وَرَّى بِغَيْرِهَا، حَتَّى كَانَتْ تِلْكَ الْغَزْوَةُ فِي حَرٍّ شَدِيدٍ وَسَفَرٍ بَعِيدٍ وَعَدُوٍّ كَثِيرٍ، فَجَلَّى لِلْمُسْلِمِينَ أَمْرَهُمْ لِيَتَأَهَّبُوا. فَطَفِقْتُ أَغْدُو لِأَتَجَهَّزَ مَعَهُمْ ثُمَّ أَرْجِعُ وَلَمْ أَقْضِ شَيْئًا، وَأَقُولُ فِي نَفْسِي: أَنَا قَادِرٌ عَلَيْهِ. فَلَمْ يَزَلْ يَتَمَادَى بِي حَتَّى أَسْرَعُوا وَتَفَارَطَ الْغَزْوُ، وَهَمَمْتُ أَنْ أَلْحَقَهُمْ فَلَمْ يُقَدَّرْ لِي ذَلِكَ.

وَلَمْ يَذْكُرْنِي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم حَتَّى بَلَغَ تَبُوكَ، فَقَالَ وَهُوَ جَالِسٌ فِي الْقَوْمِ: «مَا فَعَلَ كَعْبٌ؟». فَقَالَ رَجُلٌ مِنْ بَنِي سَلِمَةَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، حَبَسَهُ بُرْدَاهُ وَنَظَرُهُ فِي عِطْفِهِ. فَقَالَ مُعَاذُ بْنُ جَبَلٍ: بِئْسَ مَا قُلْتَ، وَاللَّهِ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، مَا عَلِمْنَا عَلَيْهِ إِلَّا خَيْرًا. فَسَكَتَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم.

قَالَ كَعْبٌ: فَلَمَّا بَلَغَنِي أَنَّهُ تَوَجَّهَ قَافِلًا حَضَرَنِي هَمِّي، وَطَفِقْتُ أَتَذَكَّرُ الْكَذِبَ وَأَقُولُ: بِمَاذَا أَخْرُجُ مِنْ سَخَطِهِ غَدًا؟ فَلَمَّا قِيلَ إِنَّهُ قَدْ أَظَلَّ قَادِمًا زَاحَ عَنِّي الْبَاطِلُ، وَعَرَفْتُ أَنِّي لَنْ أَخْرُجَ مِنْهُ أَبَدًا بِشَيْءٍ فِيهِ كَذِبٌ، فَأَجْمَعْتُ صِدْقَهُ. فَلَمَّا قَدِمَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم جَاءَهُ الْمُخَلَّفُونَ يَعْتَذِرُونَ إِلَيْهِ وَيَحْلِفُونَ لَهُ، فَقَبِلَ مِنْهُمْ عَلَانِيَتَهُمْ وَاسْتَغْفَرَ لَهُمْ وَوَكَلَ سَرَائِرَهُمْ إِلَى اللَّهِ. فَجِئْتُهُ، فَلَمَّا سَلَّمْتُ عَلَيْهِ تَبَسَّمَ تَبَسُّمَ الْمُغْضَبِ، ثُمَّ قَالَ: «تَعَالَ»، فَجَلَسْتُ بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ، فَقَالَ لِي: «مَا خَلَّفَكَ؟ أَلَمْ تَكُنْ قَدِ ابْتَعْتَ ظَهْرَكَ؟». فَقُلْتُ: بَلَى، إِنِّي وَاللَّهِ لَوْ جَلَسْتُ عِنْدَ غَيْرِكَ مِنْ أَهْلِ الدُّنْيَا، لَرَأَيْتُ أَنْ سَأَخْرُجُ مِنْ سَخَطِهِ بِعُذْرٍ، وَلَقَدْ أُعْطِيتُ جَدَلًا، وَلَكِنِّي وَاللَّهِ لَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ لَئِنْ حَدَّثْتُكَ الْيَوْمَ حَدِيثَ كَذِبٍ تَرْضَى بِهِ عَنِّي لَيُوشِكَنَّ اللَّهُ أَنْ يُسْخِطَكَ عَلَيَّ، وَلَئِنْ حَدَّثْتُكَ حَدِيثَ صِدْقٍ تَجِدُ عَلَيَّ فِيهِ إِنِّي لَأَرْجُو فِيهِ عَفْوَ اللَّهِ، لَا وَاللَّهِ مَا كَانَ لِي مِنْ عُذْرٍ، وَاللَّهِ مَا كُنْتُ قَطُّ أَقْوَى وَلَا أَيْسَرَ مِنِّي حِينَ تَخَلَّفْتُ عَنْكَ. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «أَمَّا هَذَا فَقَدْ صَدَقَ، فَقُمْ حَتَّى يَقْضِيَ اللَّهُ فِيكَ».

وَنَهَى رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم الْمُسْلِمِينَ عَنْ كَلَامِنَا أَيُّهَا الثَّلَاثَةُ مِنْ بَيْنِ مَنْ تَخَلَّفَ عَنْهُ، فَاجْتَنَبَنَا النَّاسُ وَتَغَيَّرُوا لَنَا حَتَّى تَنَكَّرَتْ فِي نَفْسِي الْأَرْضُ، فَلَبِثْنَا عَلَى ذَلِكَ خَمْسِينَ لَيْلَةً، لَا يُكَلِّمُنِي أَحَدٌ، حَتَّى إِنِّي تَسَوَّرْتُ يَوْمًا جِدَارَ حَائِطِ ابْنِ عَمِّي أَبِي قَتَادَةَ، وَهُوَ أَحَبُّ النَّاسِ إِلَيَّ، فَسَلَّمْتُ عَلَيْهِ، فَوَاللَّهِ مَا رَدَّ عَلَيَّ السَّلَامَ، فَقُلْتُ: يَا أَبَا قَتَادَةَ، أَنْشُدُكَ بِاللَّهِ هَلْ تَعْلَمُنِي أُحِبُّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ؟ فَسَكَتَ، فَعُدْتُ فَنَشَدْتُهُ فَسَكَتَ، ثُمَّ قَالَ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. فَفَاضَتْ عَيْنَايَ.

ثُمَّ بَيْنَا أَنَا أَمْشِي بِسُوقِ الْمَدِينَةِ إِذَا رَجُلٌ يَدْفَعُ إِلَيَّ كِتَابًا مِنْ مَلِكِ غَسَّانَ، فَإِذَا فِيهِ: أَمَّا بَعْدُ، فَإِنَّهُ قَدْ بَلَغَنِي أَنَّ صَاحِبَكَ قَدْ جَفَاكَ، وَلَمْ يَجْعَلْكَ اللَّهُ بِدَارِ هَوَانٍ وَلَا مَضْيَعَةٍ، فَالْحَقْ بِنَا نُوَاسِكَ. فَقُلْتُ لَمَّا قَرَأْتُهَا: وَهَذَا أَيْضًا مِنَ الْبَلَاءِ. فَتَيَمَّمْتُ بِهَا التَّنُّورَ فَسَجَرْتُهُ بِهَا.

حَتَّى إِذَا مَضَتْ أَرْبَعُونَ لَيْلَةً مِنَ الْخَمْسِينَ، أَتَانِي رَسُولُ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَقَالَ: إِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَأْمُرُكَ أَنْ تَعْتَزِلَ امْرَأَتَكَ. فَقُلْتُ: أُطَلِّقُهَا أَمْ مَاذَا؟ قَالَ: بَلِ اعْتَزِلْهَا وَلَا تَقْرَبْهَا. فَلَبِثْتُ عَشْرَ لَيَالٍ حَتَّى كَمَلَتْ خَمْسُونَ لَيْلَةً. فَلَمَّا صَلَّيْتُ الْفَجْرَ صُبْحَ خَمْسِينَ لَيْلَةً، وَأَنَا عَلَى ظَهْرِ بَيْتٍ مِنْ بُيُوتِنَا، وَقَدْ ضَاقَتْ عَلَيَّ نَفْسِي وَضَاقَتْ عَلَيَّ الْأَرْضُ بِمَا رَحُبَتْ، سَمِعْتُ صَوْتَ صَارِخٍ أَوْفَى عَلَى جَبَلِ سَلْعٍ بِأَعْلَى صَوْتِهِ: يَا كَعْبَ بْنَ مَالِكٍ، أَبْشِرْ! فَخَرَرْتُ سَاجِدًا، وَعَرَفْتُ أَنْ قَدْ جَاءَ فَرَجٌ. وَآذَنَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم بِتَوْبَةِ اللَّهِ عَلَيْنَا حِينَ صَلَّى الْفَجْرَ، فَذَهَبَ النَّاسُ يُبَشِّرُونَنَا... فَلَمَّا جَلَسْتُ بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ، وَهُوَ يَبْرُقُ وَجْهُهُ مِنَ السُّرُورِ، قَالَ: «أَبْشِرْ بِخَيْرِ يَوْمٍ مَرَّ عَلَيْكَ مُنْذُ وَلَدَتْكَ أُمُّكَ». قُلْتُ: أَمِنْ عِنْدِكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ أَمْ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ؟ قَالَ: «لَا، بَلْ مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ». قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، إِنَّ مِنْ تَوْبَتِي أَنْ أَنْخَلِعَ مِنْ مَالِي صَدَقَةً إِلَى اللَّهِ وَإِلَى رَسُولِهِ. قَالَ: «أَمْسِكْ عَلَيْكَ بَعْضَ مَالِكَ فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَكَ». قُلْتُ: فَإِنِّي أُمْسِكُ سَهْمِي الَّذِي بِخَيْبَرَ. وَقُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، إِنَّ اللَّهَ إِنَّمَا نَجَّانِي بِالصِّدْقِ، وَإِنَّ مِنْ تَوْبَتِي أَنْ لَا أُحَدِّثَ إِلَّا صِدْقًا مَا بَقِيتُ.

وَأَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ عَلَى رَسُولِهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: {لَقَدْ تَابَ اللَّهُ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ وَالْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ} إِلَى قَوْلِهِ {وَكُونُوا مَعَ الصَّادِقِينَ}[^5].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Ka'b ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, said: **"I never stayed behind from any expedition the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went on, except the expedition of Tabuk... By Allah, I had never been stronger or more comfortably placed than when I stayed behind from him on that expedition. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ never intended an expedition without disguising its true destination through indirection, until that expedition came in intense heat, a long journey, and a large enemy, so he made the matter clear to the Muslims so they could prepare. I kept setting out in the morning to get ready with them, then returning having accomplished nothing, telling myself: I am able to do it whenever I wish. This kept delaying me until they had set off quickly and the expedition had gone too far ahead, and I resolved to catch up with them, but it was not decreed for me.**
>
> **The Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not ask about me until he reached Tabuk, where he said, sitting among the people: 'What did Ka'b do?' A man from Banu Salimah said: 'O Messenger of Allah, his two cloaks and his admiring glances at his own sides have kept him back.' Mu'adh ibn Jabal said: 'What an evil thing you have said! By Allah, O Messenger of Allah, we know nothing of him except good.' So the Messenger of Allah ﷺ remained silent.**
>
> **Ka'b said: When I learned that he had set out returning, my anxiety overcame me, and I began to think of lying, saying: 'With what will I escape his displeasure tomorrow?' But when it was said he was about to arrive, falsehood left me, and I knew I would never escape it through anything containing a lie, so I resolved on truthfulness. When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ arrived, those who had stayed behind came to him offering excuses and swearing to him, so he accepted their outward statements, sought forgiveness for them, and left their inner reality to Allah. Then I came to him, and when I greeted him, he smiled the smile of one displeased, then said: 'Come.' I sat before him, and he said to me: 'What kept you back? Had you not purchased your mount?' I said: 'Yes indeed. By Allah, had I been sitting before anyone else in this world, I would surely have found a way out of his displeasure with some excuse, for I have been given skill in argument. But by Allah, I knew that if I told you today a lie that would please you, Allah would soon make you angry with me; and if I told you a truthful account that would displease you, I hope for Allah's pardon in it. No, by Allah, I had no excuse. By Allah, I had never been stronger or more comfortably placed than when I stayed behind from you.' The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'As for this one, he has spoken the truth. So get up until Allah decides your case.'**
>
> **The Messenger of Allah ﷺ then forbade the Muslims from speaking to the three of us out of all those who had stayed behind, so the people shunned us and changed toward us until the very earth seemed strange to me. We remained in that state for fifty nights, with no one speaking to me, until one day I climbed the wall of the orchard of my cousin Abu Qatadah, who was the dearest person to me. I greeted him, but by Allah he did not return my greeting. I said: 'O Abu Qatadah, I adjure you by Allah, do you not know that I love Allah and His Messenger?' He was silent. I asked him again, and he was silent, then he said: 'Allah and His Messenger know best.' My eyes overflowed with tears.**
>
> **Then, while I was walking in the market of Madinah, a man handed me a letter from the king of Ghassan, in which was written: 'To proceed: it has reached me that your companion has treated you harshly, and Allah has not placed you in a land of humiliation or loss, so join us and we will comfort you.' When I read it, I said: 'This too is part of the trial,' and I went straight to the oven and burned it in the fire.**
>
> **When forty of the fifty nights had passed, a messenger from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ came to me and said: 'The Messenger of Allah ﷺ commands you to keep away from your wife.' I said: 'Should I divorce her, or what?' He said: 'No, only keep away from her and do not go near her.' I remained like that for ten more nights until the fifty nights were complete. When I had prayed the dawn prayer on the morning of the fiftieth night, and I was on the roof of one of our houses, my soul had grown constricted and the earth, despite its vastness, had grown narrow to me, I heard the voice of a caller who had climbed Mount Sal' calling at the top of his voice: 'O Ka'b ibn Malik, rejoice!' I fell down in prostration, and I knew that relief had come. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ announced Allah's acceptance of our repentance when he had prayed the dawn prayer, and the people went out to give us the good news... When I sat before him, his face shining with joy, he said: 'Rejoice in the best day that has ever come to you since your mother gave birth to you.' I said: 'Is this from you, O Messenger of Allah, or from Allah?' He said: 'No, rather from Allah.' I said: 'O Messenger of Allah, part of my repentance is that I give up all my wealth as charity to Allah and His Messenger.' He said: 'Keep some of your wealth for yourself; that is better for you.' I said: 'Then I will keep my share that is at Khaybar.' And I said: 'O Messenger of Allah, Allah saved me only through truthfulness, and part of my repentance is that I will never speak anything but the truth for as long as I live.'**
>
> **And Allah revealed to His Messenger ﷺ: 'Allah has already turned in mercy to the Prophet, the muhajirun, and the ansar...' up to His statement 'and be with the truthful.'"**[^5]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar, in Fath al-Bari, said in his commentary on this long hadith: in it is the excellence of truthfulness even when its outcome outwardly appears severe, and that whoever chooses bitter truth over sweet falsehood, Allah grants him relief and a way out — and Ka'b himself, may Allah be pleased with him, declared exactly this, saying: "Allah saved me only through truthfulness."

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This hadith is a complete model of the meaning of "Jannah is surrounded by hardships": Ka'b's hardship was painful truthfulness, and the desire he refused was comfortable falsehood, and between them lay fifty nights of genuine trial, followed by the outcome — verses recited until the establishment of the Hour.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-117-119" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->
{لَقَدْ تَابَ اللَّهُ عَلَى النَّبِيِّ وَالْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ الَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُ فِي سَاعَةِ الْعُسْرَةِ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا كَادَ يَزِيغُ قُلُوبُ فَرِيقٍ مِنْهُمْ ثُمَّ تَابَ عَلَيْهِمْ ۚ إِنَّهُ بِهِمْ رَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ ﴿١١٧﴾ وَعَلَى الثَّلَاثَةِ الَّذِينَ خُلِّفُوا حَتَّى إِذَا ضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ الْأَرْضُ بِمَا رَحُبَتْ وَضَاقَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ أَنْفُسُهُمْ وَظَنُّوا أَنْ لَا مَلْجَأَ مِنَ اللَّهِ إِلَّا إِلَيْهِ ثُمَّ تَابَ عَلَيْهِمْ لِيَتُوبُوا ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ التَّوَّابُ الرَّحِيمُ ﴿١١٨﴾ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَكُونُوا مَعَ الصَّادِقِينَ ﴿١١٩﴾} [التَّوْبَةِ: ١١٧-١١٩][^6].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Allah has already turned in mercy to the Prophet and the muhajirun and the ansar who followed him in the hour of difficulty after the hearts of a party of them had almost inclined [to doubt], and then He turned to them in mercy. Indeed, He was to them Kind and Merciful. And [He also forgave] the three who were left behind [and regretted their error] to the point that the earth closed in on them in spite of its vastness and their souls confined them and they were certain that there was no refuge from Allah except in Him. Then He turned to them so they could repent. Indeed, Allah is the Accepter of Repentance, the Merciful. O you who have believed, fear Allah and be with those who are true."** (at-Tawbah 9:117-119)[^6]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, mentioned that "the three who were left behind" are Ka'b ibn Malik, Murarah ibn ar-Rabi', and Hilal ibn Umayyah, may Allah be pleased with them, and that Allah described the constriction of the world upon them in the most eloquent expression: "the earth closed in on them in spite of its vastness," then closed with the comprehensive command: "and be with those who are true," meaning: hold fast to truthfulness in every circumstance, for it is the means of salvation, just as Ka'b and his two companions were saved through it.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** These verses are the fruit of the hardship that Ka'b patiently bore: relief did not come through scheming or seeking a concession, but through steadfastness upon truthfulness until revelation itself descended, bringing him good news.

### For Little Ones (Ages 4-7)

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Our Prophet Muhammad ﷺ said: the path to Jannah has some hard things in it, but they lead to the most beautiful place. And the path to the Fire has some easy, tasty-seeming things in it, but they lead to a bad place. So telling the truth is sometimes hard, but it pleases Allah. And lying is easy, but it makes Allah upset.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.story" kind="story" -->
<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-4418" authenticated="true" -->

There was a Companion named Ka'b, who was late in going out with the Prophet ﷺ on a long journey, and he had no good reason for it. When the Prophet ﷺ came back, he asked him: "Why were you late?" Ka'b could have told a small lie, and the Prophet ﷺ would have believed him, but he said to himself: I will never lie. So he said to the Prophet ﷺ: "I had no excuse — I really was able to go." The Prophet ﷺ said: "This one has told the truth." But people stayed away from Ka'b for fifty days, and no one spoke to him, so Ka'b felt very sad. Then a man brought him a letter promising him comfort if he left the Prophet ﷺ, but Ka'b refused and burned the letter. And one morning, Ka'b heard a voice calling from the top of the mountain: "O Ka'b! Rejoice!" And he knew that Allah was pleased with him because he had been patient and truthful, so he fell down in prostration to Allah, full of joy. And this is why Allah sent down verses in the Qur'an that tell us: be with those who tell the truth.

### For Children (Ages 8-12)

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ told us that Jannah is surrounded by hardships — that is, by the things that tire the soul, like acts of obedience and sacrifices — and that the Fire is surrounded by desires — that is, the things that pull us quickly and pleasurably toward sin. So whoever wants Jannah must first cross the "wall of hardships," and whoever wants to be saved from the Fire must not be swept along behind its glittering "desires." Even so, Allah does not burden us; the religion is ease, and whoever aims straight, draws near, and is patient for a little while can manage it.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terminology" -->
<!-- terminology:start -->
**Hardships (al-Makarih):** the things that tire the soul among the duties of obedience, even though they are good for it.
**Desires (ash-Shahawat):** the immediate wishes that pull the soul toward sin and make it seem appealing.
**The Patient (as-Sabirun):** those who remain steadfast in obeying Allah despite the difficulty.
<!-- terminology:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.story" kind="story" -->
<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-4418" authenticated="true" -->

At the expedition of Tabuk, the Prophet ﷺ and the Muslims prepared for a long journey in intense heat. But Ka'b ibn Malik kept putting off getting himself ready, day after day, telling himself: I will catch up with them later. Until the entire army had gone, and he had no real excuse left at all.

When the Prophet ﷺ returned from the expedition, those who had stayed behind came to him with many excuses, and the Prophet ﷺ accepted their outward words. But Ka'b stood before the Prophet ﷺ and told the whole truth: "I had no excuse. By Allah, I was never stronger or more able to go out than I was that time." The Prophet ﷺ said: "As for this one, he has told the truth. So get up until Allah decides your case."

After that, the Prophet ﷺ ordered the Muslims not to speak to Ka'b and his two companions. So even the people closest to him stayed away from him, and this continued for fifty full nights, until Ka'b felt that the wide earth had become narrow around him. A messenger came to him from the king of Ghassan, offering him a chance to leave Madinah and live with him in comfort and honor, so Ka'b said: "This too is part of the trial," and he burned the letter in the oven.

Then on the morning of the fiftieth day, while Ka'b sat there in sadness, he heard a loud voice from the top of Mount Sal': "O Ka'b ibn Malik, rejoice!" So he fell down in prostration to Allah in thanks, and he knew that Allah had accepted his repentance. And when he went to the Prophet ﷺ, his face shining with joy, the Prophet said to him: "Rejoice in the best day that has ever come to you since your mother gave birth to you." And Ka'b said to himself afterward: "Allah saved me only through truthfulness." And Allah sent down verses in the Qur'an, still recited today, praising his truthfulness.

<!-- story:end -->

### For Teens (Ages 13+)

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The hadith "Jannah is surrounded by hardships, and the Fire is surrounded by desires" is not a passing description; it is a map for every decision you make. "Desire" in the hadith is far broader than physical craving alone; it is everything that tempts you toward immediate comfort at the expense of the truth: the lie that ends an embarrassing moment, the procrastination that delays a duty, flattering a friend at the expense of honesty. And "hardship" is the price the truthful person pays to remain on the side of truth. The story of Ka'b ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, at the expedition of Tabuk is a sound, authenticated example that choosing the difficult hardship may, in its moment, look like a loss, but its outcome with Allah cannot be compared to any immediate comfort.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.story" kind="story" -->
<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-4418" authenticated="true" -->

Ka'b ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, narrates his own story: he was among the senior Companions, present at the pledge of al-'Aqabah, and had never once stayed behind from any expedition. But at the expedition of Tabuk, while he was in the best state of strength and ease he had ever been in, he fell into something he had not expected: procrastination. Every day he told himself, "I am able to catch up with them at any time," until the whole army had gone without him having any real excuse.

When the Prophet ﷺ returned from the expedition, Ka'b found himself facing a real choice: he could invent an excuse as the other absentees had done, so the Prophet ﷺ would accept it outwardly and the matter would end immediately, or he could tell the whole truth and bear whatever consequence came, no matter what it was. Ka'b said of that moment: "I knew that if I told him today a lie that would please him, Allah would soon make him angry with me, and if I told him a truthful account that would displease him, I hoped for Allah's pardon in it." So he chose painful truthfulness, and said to the Prophet ﷺ: "I had no excuse." The Prophet ﷺ said: "As for this one, he has spoken the truth. So get up until Allah decides your case."

The immediate consequence was harsh: the Prophet ﷺ ordered that Ka'b and his two companions be socially shunned, so that no one would speak to them, and no one, not even those closest to them, would return their greeting. This continued for fifty complete nights, until that state was itself described in the Qur'an: "the earth closed in on them in spite of its vastness." And in the harshest moment of his isolation, another temptation came to him: a letter from the king of Ghassan offering him comfort and honor if he left the community that had rejected him. He could have justified accepting the offer on the grounds that he had been wronged, but he saw in the letter itself another test, and he burned it without hesitation.

Then, on the very morning of the fiftieth day, relief came: a voice called out to him with the good news from the top of Mount Sal'. Ka'b fell down in prostration in gratitude to Allah, then went to the Prophet ﷺ, who gave him the glad tidings, saying: "Rejoice in the best day that has ever come to you since your mother gave birth to you." Ka'b offered to give away all his wealth in gratitude to Allah, but the Prophet ﷺ advised him toward balance: "Keep some of your wealth for yourself; that is better for you" — another lesson that uprightness does not mean extremism. And Ka'b closed his experience by saying: "Allah saved me only through truthfulness, and part of my repentance is that I will never speak anything but the truth for as long as I live." And Allah sent down verses concerning him that are still recited in every mosque on the face of the earth to this day.

<!-- story:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

1. What is the difference between "desire" in its broad meaning in the hadith and physical craving alone?
2. How does Ka'b's rejection of the letter from the king of Ghassan illustrate the difference between lawful comfort and following one's whims?
3. When does a "concession" turn into an excuse for abandoning a duty?
4. Write down one decision you have been postponing, and identify its first step.
5. How does Ka'b's patience with the decree during the fifty nights connect to his patience in refraining from lying and his patience in holding to truthfulness?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->
<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.009.activity.fork-of-hardship-and-desire" -->

Identify one real decision that you are postponing, or that your friends are pressuring you about (a small lie to avoid embarrassment, procrastination on a duty, going along with the group at the expense of a principle). Write down the desire pulling you toward the easy option, and the hardship the right path requires, then commit to a first small step (no more than ten minutes) toward the right decision, and choose one person you trust to check in on you about it.

## Detailed Lesson Plans For Teachers

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="45" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The learner explains the relationship between the hadith "Jannah is surrounded by hardships" and the hadith "Indeed, the religion is ease" without contradiction, and applies the principle of "hardship versus desire" to a real situation in his own life.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
A copy of the lesson, a Mushaf for referring to the ayat, blank cards for the activity.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The teacher reviews the five ahadith and ayat before the lesson, and prepares one personal example of a "hardship" he chose over a "desire" to share when needed.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
Learners are asked: Have you ever chosen something difficult while knowing it was right? How did you feel afterward?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
The hadith of Muslim 2822 is presented, then the two ayat of al-Baqarah 214 and Al 'Imran 142, then the hadith of Bukhari 39 to clarify the limit of moderation, then Ka'b ibn Malik's hadith is read in full, with pauses at each turning point.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
The teacher discusses with the learners how every stop in Ka'b's story (the question, the shunning, the king's letter, the relief) was an opportunity to choose between a hardship and a desire, and connects this to the verses of repentance.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
The "This Week's Situation" activity is carried out as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
Each learner is asked to state orally one hardship and one desire from his own real life, and his understanding is assessed by how well he distinguishes between them.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
Beginners may be limited to the hadith of Muslim and Ka'b's story alone; advanced learners add a discussion of the limit of moderation in the hadith of Bukhari 39.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
The teacher should take care not to turn the activity into a public confession session about sensitive mistakes; whatever is shared should remain voluntary and general.
<!-- safeguards:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="20" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The child distinguishes between a difficult, correct action and an easy, wrong action in one situation from his day.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
Two drawn cards, coloring supplies.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The educator prepares two blank cards and the simplified story of Ka'b.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
The child is asked: Have you ever told the truth even though it was hard?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
The meaning of the hadith is stated in simple words: the path to Jannah has some hard things in it, but they are beautiful in the end.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
Ka'b's story is narrated in a lively voice, acting out the voice of the caller on the mountain to capture attention.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
The two-cards activity is carried out as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
Observe whether the child was able to classify one situation on his own onto the correct card.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
For younger children, the educator may be content with a single example acted out physically instead of written.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
Avoid describing the "shunning" in the story in a way that frightens the child about isolation; focus on the joy at the end.
<!-- safeguards:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="30" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The child explains the meaning of "hardships" and "desires," and applies this to three real stops in his own life.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
A sheet of paper for drawing the forked road, colored pens.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The educator reviews the three definitions in the terminology unit to explain them clearly.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
The children are asked: What is the difference between something difficult and right, and something easy and wrong?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
The three terms are explained, then Ka'b's story is told in full with its details.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
Every turning point in the story is discussed with the children: Why was telling the truth hard? Why did he refuse the king's letter?
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
The forked-road drawing activity is carried out as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
The drawings are assessed by how realistic the three stops written by each child are.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
For advanced students, add a discussion of the hadith of moderation (Bukhari 39) so they do not understand "hardship" as limitless severity.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
Let the child choose which of his stops to share publicly; he should not be required to disclose sensitive situations.
<!-- safeguards:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="35" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The teen analyzes a real, postponed decision in light of the principle of hardship and desire, and commits to a practical step toward it.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
A copy of the lesson, an individual commitment sheet.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The teacher prepares for a discussion of peer pressure and procrastination as contemporary examples of desire.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
Teens are asked: What is the hardest right decision you made this month?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
Ka'b's story is read in full, with focus on the moment he chose between comfortable falsehood and painful truthfulness.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
Discuss the difference between a lawful concession and following one's whims, and how patience with the decree (the fifty nights) and patience in refraining from sin (refusing to lie) are two sides of the same coin.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
The activity of identifying the postponed decision and its first step is carried out as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
The teacher follows up after a week to see whether the first step was carried out with the person each teen chose to check in on them.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
For anyone hesitant to share their decision publicly, allow them to write it privately and hand it only to the teacher.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
The teacher should be careful not to pressure any teen to disclose sensitive personal details in front of the group.
<!-- safeguards:end -->
