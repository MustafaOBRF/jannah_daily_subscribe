# The Gatherings of Jannah: Its People's Conversation and Laughter

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the report of Jabir ibn Samurah, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih Muslim (670): the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would stay seated in his place of prayer after the dawn prayer until the sun rose, and his Companions would talk, take up the affairs of the Jahiliyyah and laugh, while he, peace and blessings be upon him, smiled.
- State what an-Nawawi drew from the report: that remembrance of Allah after the dawn prayer is recommended, that it is permissible to tell stories of the Jahiliyyah, that laughing is permissible, and that it is better to keep to a smile.
- Describe the gatherings of the people of Jannah from the Qur'an: they turn to one another, asking each other how things were with them in this world and recalling how gracious Allah was to them (at-Tur 52:25-28); they are in a garden, "delighted," that is, made glad (ar-Rum 30:15); their faces are "laughing, rejoicing" ('Abasa 80:39); and on the Day of Resurrection they laugh at those who used to laugh at them (al-Mutaffifin 83:34-36).
- Tell the difference between laughter that draws hearts together in gratitude to Allah and laughter that wounds someone, whether absent or present.
- Carry out the "We Used to... Then Allah Was Gracious to Us" activity: preparing a true, two-part memory from one's past, telling it in a phone-free gathering with agreed rules, and writing down the blessings of Allah that came up in it.
- Learn by heart a lesson-authored bedtime du'a asking Allah to make our gatherings gatherings of goodness and remembrance, and to let us recall His blessings together in Jannah, laughing and rejoicing.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

No one's joy is complete when it is enjoyed alone. That is why the Qur'an does not describe the bliss of Jannah only as food and palaces; it describes its people together: turning toward one another, talking, their delight showing on their faces.

An earlier lesson looked at the scene in Surah at-Tur around the shared table. This lesson reads the same gathering from another angle, that of conversation and laughter: what do the people of Jannah talk about, and what is their laughter like? It begins with a real gathering in this world, related by Jabir ibn Samurah, may Allah be pleased with him: the gathering of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and his Companions after the dawn prayer.

<!-- unit:end -->

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-670" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Would Laugh, and He Would Smile"

> عَنْ سِمَاكِ بْنِ حَرْبٍ قَالَ: قُلْتُ لِجَابِرِ بْنِ سَمُرَةَ: أَكُنْتَ تُجَالِسُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ كَثِيرًا، كَانَ لَا يَقُومُ مِنْ مُصَلَّاهُ الَّذِي يُصَلِّي فِيهِ الصُّبْحَ أَوِ الْغَدَاةَ حَتَّى تَطْلُعَ الشَّمْسُ، فَإِذَا طَلَعَتِ الشَّمْسُ قَامَ، وَكَانُوا يَتَحَدَّثُونَ فَيَأْخُذُونَ فِي أَمْرِ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ فَيَضْحَكُونَ، وَيَتَبَسَّمُ ﷺ.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Simak ibn Harb said: I asked Jabir ibn Samurah, "Did you use to sit with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him?" He said: "Yes, often. He would not get up from the place where he prayed the dawn prayer (*as-subh*, or he said *al-ghadah*) until the sun had risen; and when the sun rose, he would get up. They would talk, and they would take up the affairs of the Jahiliyyah and laugh, and he, peace and blessings be upon him, would smile."[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi said: "It shows that remembrance of Allah after the dawn prayer is recommended, and that one should stay in one's place of prayer unless there is an excuse," and "it shows that it is permissible to tell stories of the Jahiliyyah and of other nations, and that laughing is permissible, though it is better to keep to a smile, as the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, did most of the time. They said: laughing a great deal is disliked."[^1] Al-Qurtubi said in *al-Mufhim* that devoting oneself to remembrance at that hour is "better and more fitting," but that this does not mean talking at that time is something to be avoided; and he rejected the view of those who placed the talking and the laughing at some other time.[^1] And 'Ali al-Qari said of their taking up the affairs of the Jahiliyyah that they did so "either by way of censure, or as a retelling of something that holds a benefit."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

A single gathering held both: remembrance until the sun came up, and then conversation that made the Companions laugh and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, smile. The subject was their own past, before Allah guided them. They spoke of it as people who had come out of it, so their laughter was gratitude, not nostalgia.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-25-28" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And They Will Turn to One Another, Asking Each Other"

> **{وَأَقْبَلَ بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ ۝ قَالُوا إِنَّا كُنَّا قَبْلُ فِي أَهْلِنَا مُشْفِقِينَ ۝ فَمَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا وَوَقَانَا عَذَابَ السَّمُومِ ۝ إِنَّا كُنَّا مِنْ قَبْلُ نَدْعُوهُ ۖ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْبَرُّ الرَّحِيمُ}** [الطور: ٢٥-٢٨][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And they will turn to one another, asking each other. They will say, 'Before this, when we were among our families, we lived in fear. But Allah was gracious to us and shielded us from the punishment of the scorching wind. Before this we used to call upon Him; He is truly the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.'"** (at-Tur 52:25-28)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: "They turn to one another, talking together and asking each other about their deeds and how things were with them in this world." As-Sa'di said that they ask one another "about the affairs of this world and how things went there."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The conversation of Jannah is a shared memory: "We used to..." and then "Allah was gracious to us." They do not boast of their deeds; they give the credit back to Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-30-14-15" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Be in a Garden, Delighted"

> **{وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ السَّاعَةُ يَوْمَئِذٍ يَتَفَرَّقُونَ ۝ فَأَمَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ فَهُمْ فِي رَوْضَةٍ يُحْبَرُونَ}** [الروم: ١٤-١٥][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And on the Day the Hour arrives, on that Day they will be divided. As for those who believed and did righteous deeds, they will be in a garden, delighted."** (ar-Rum 30:14-15)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tabari said: "*Al-habrah*, to the Arabs, means joy and happiness (*ghibtah*)," and he relates that Ibn 'Abbas explained *yuhbarun* as "they will be honored," and Mujahid and Qatadah as "they will enjoy bliss." After listing these views, Ibn Kathir said: "*Al-habrah* is broader than all of this."[^3]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-80-38-39" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Laughing, Rejoicing"

> **{وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ مُسْفِرَةٌ ۝ ضَاحِكَةٌ مُسْتَبْشِرَةٌ}** [عبس: ٣٨-٣٩][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Some faces on that Day will be shining, laughing, rejoicing."** ('Abasa 80:38-39)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that *musfirah*, "shining," means "radiant," and that "laughing, rejoicing" means "glad and joyful from the joy in their hearts; good cheer has appeared on their faces, and these are the people of Jannah." As-Sa'di said: "Joy and delight have appeared on them, because they know they have been saved and have won the bliss."[^4]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-83-34-36" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "So Today Those Who Believed Are Laughing at the Disbelievers"

> **{فَالْيَوْمَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنَ الْكُفَّارِ يَضْحَكُونَ ۝ عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ يَنْظُرُونَ ۝ هَلْ ثُوِّبَ الْكُفَّارُ مَا كَانُوا يَفْعَلُونَ}** [المطففين: ٣٤-٣٦][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"So today those who believed are laughing at the disbelievers, on raised couches, looking on. Have the disbelievers not been repaid for what they used to do?"** (al-Mutaffifin 83:34-36)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that they laugh "in return for the way those people laughed at them," meaning the wrongdoers of whom Allah said a few ayat earlier: "They used to laugh at those who believed." As-Sa'di said that their recompense is "of the same kind as their deeds," while the believers are "in the utmost ease and tranquility."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

This is the laughter of justice on the Day of Recompense, after long years of mockery that the believers bore with patience. It gives us no license today to mock anyone.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

Jabir's gathering is a small, earthly picture of the gathering in Jannah; this connection is the lesson's own, not part of the hadith: remembrance of Allah, then talk about the past, then laughter and smiles. In Jannah, its people ask one another how things were with them in this world, they recall how gracious Allah was to them, their faces are laughing and rejoicing, and they are in a garden, made glad. The difference is that laughter in this world needs limits: not too much of it, no mockery, and no ill word about anyone who is absent. So the practical question is this: what do our gatherings revolve around today, and what do we laugh at in them?

<!-- unit:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What rulings did an-Nawawi draw from the report of Jabir ibn Samurah?
2. Why did al-Qurtubi hold that the talking and laughing could take place in the same gathering, after the remembrance? And how does that change the way we understand the gathering?
3. What links the Companions' talk about the Jahiliyyah with the words of the people of Jannah: "Before this, when we were among our families, we lived in fear"?
4. How do you understand the laughter of the believers in Surah al-Mutaffifin without turning it into a justification for mockery in this world?
5. What would you like to be spoken of in your gatherings, and what would you like to be absent from them?

### Activity: We Used to... Then Allah Was Gracious to Us

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.070.activity.we-used-to-then-allah-favoured-us" -->

Within seven days, invite three or more family members or friends to a short, phone-free gathering, after a prayer or after a meal. Ask each person to prepare a true memory about themselves in two parts: "We used to..." (an old situation, a hardship, or a mistake that has become funny with time), and "Then Allah was gracious to us..." (the blessing that changed it). Before you begin, agree on three rules: nothing is told about anyone who is absent; no one is laughed at except over something they chose to tell about themselves; and the gathering ends with praise of Allah. Note down on a single sheet the blessings that are mentioned. Afterward, write a paragraph: Which blessing had you never noticed before? And did the laughter stay clean of mockery?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="3.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Do you like sitting with your family and hearing a story about when you were tiny? About how you couldn't walk yet, and then one day you were walking, and then running? Maybe you all laugh together and say, "Alhamdulillah, all praise is for Allah!"

In Jannah, its people sit together too. They turn to one another and talk, and they remember their days in this world, and they say that Allah was so kind to them.[^2] Allah told us that they are in a beautiful garden, "delighted," which means happy and glad,[^3] and that their faces are "laughing, rejoicing."[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Report: The Morning Gathering

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-670" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report told by the Companion Jabir ibn Samurah, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-670" audience="4-7" -->

A man named Simak asked the Companion Jabir ibn Samurah, "Did you use to sit with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him?" Jabir said, "Yes, lots of times."

Then he told him about it. After the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, prayed Fajr, he would stay sitting in the same place where he had prayed, until the sun came up. When the sun came up, he would stand.

His Companions would talk together. They would remember their old days, from before they knew Islam, and they would laugh. And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would smile.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Some scholars said they talked about those days to see what had been wrong with them, or to learn something from them.[^1] Just imagine: it was a lovely kind of laughter, because Allah had brought them out of those days into goodness. In Jannah, too, its people sit and remember, and they say: "Alhamdulillah, Allah has been so kind to us!"

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Short Questions

1. What did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, do after the Fajr prayer, until the sun came up?
2. What did his Companions do when they talked together? And what did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, do?
3. What are the faces of the people of Jannah like?
4. What happy story would you love to hear from your family?

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: When I Was Little

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.070.activity.we-used-to-then-allah-favoured-us" -->

Sit with your mom or dad for a little while, with no phone and no screen. Ask them for a story about you that starts with "You used to...": you used to not know how to walk, or you used to not know how to talk. Then you say: "And Allah blessed me, and now I can..." Draw a picture on a paper with two sides: one side is "When I was little," and the other side is "Now." You can laugh together at the story, but we never laugh at someone who isn't with us. At the end, everyone says together: "Alhamdulillah!"

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

At the end-of-year party, the teacher put up photos of the class from the very first day of school: one kid crying at the door, another hauling a backpack bigger than she was. Everyone burst out laughing, and the kids in the photos laughed hardest of all, because now they could see how much they had grown and learned. Then one student made a hurtful remark about the photo of a classmate who wasn't there. The room went quiet, and the whole mood changed.

This lesson is about the gatherings of the people of Jannah: how they turn to one another and talk about their days in this world, remembering how gracious Allah was to them, with faces "laughing, rejoicing."[^2][^4] It is also about a real gathering in this world that was full of conversation, laughter and smiles.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-670" -->

- **`The Jahiliyyah (al-jahiliyyah)`** — the time the Arabs lived through before Islam, when people worshipped idols and followed many wrong customs.
- **`Delighted (yuhbarun)`** — made glad and joyful; to the Arabs, *habrah* means joy, as at-Tabari said.[^3]
- **`Rejoicing (mustabshirah)`** — showing on the outside the joy that fills the heart, as Ibn Kathir said.[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Report: "They Would Laugh, and He Would Smile"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-670" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report told by Jabir ibn Samurah, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

Simak ibn Harb was one of the Tabi'un, the generation that came after the Companions. He wanted to know what it had been like to sit with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, so he asked someone who had seen it with his own eyes.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-670" audience="8-12" -->

Simak asked Jabir ibn Samurah, "Did you use to sit with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him?" He said: "Yes, often." Then he described it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would not get up from the place where he had prayed the dawn prayer until the sun had risen; when the sun rose, he would get up. The Companions would talk, and they would take up the affairs of the Jahiliyyah, remembering what they used to be like before Islam, and they would laugh, while the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, smiled.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

An-Nawawi said that this report shows that remembering Allah after the dawn prayer is recommended, and that laughing is allowed, though smiling is better, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did most of the time.[^1] Notice this: the Companions were not laughing at someone who wasn't there. They were laughing at their own old ways, the ones Allah had brought them out of. That is a lot like the conversation of the people of Jannah: "Before this, when we were among our families, we lived in fear. But Allah was gracious to us."[^2]

<!-- story:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a friends' group chat, the messages that get the most reactions are often an embarrassing photo of someone who wasn't there, or a clip that makes fun of somebody. The laughs come fast. But some of them are bought at another person's expense.

The Qur'an describes a different kind of gathering. The people of Jannah turn to one another, asking each other; Ibn Kathir said they are "talking together and asking each other about their deeds and how things were with them in this world," and they say: "But Allah was gracious to us."[^2] They are in a garden, "delighted," and *habrah*, to the Arabs, means "joy and happiness," as at-Tabari said.[^3] So what do people laugh about in a gathering like that?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-670" -->

- **`The affairs of the Jahiliyyah (amr al-jahiliyyah)`** — how the Arabs lived before Islam. The Companions spoke of it "either by way of censure, or as a retelling of something that holds a benefit," as 'Ali al-Qari said.[^1]
- **`Asking each other (yatasa'alun)`** — talking together and asking one another how things were with them in this world, as Ibn Kathir said.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Report: A Gathering Between Dawn and Sunrise

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-670" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report told by Jabir ibn Samurah, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih Muslim; any explanation in it comes from the scholars.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-670" audience="13+" -->

Simak ibn Harb, one of the Tabi'un, asked the Companion Jabir ibn Samurah: "Did you use to sit with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him?" He said: "Yes, often." Then he described the gathering. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would not get up from the place where he had prayed the dawn prayer until the sun had risen; once it rose, he would get up. "They would talk, and they would take up the affairs of the Jahiliyyah and laugh, and he, peace and blessings be upon him, would smile."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Think about who these people were. Some of them had worshipped idols for years; some had fought against Islam before they accepted it. And yet their past became something to talk and laugh about, in a gathering where the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, smiled, because they remembered it as people Allah had brought out of it. An-Nawawi said the report shows "that it is permissible to tell stories of the Jahiliyyah" and "that laughing is permissible, though it is better to keep to a smile."[^1]

This is close to what the people of Jannah say: "Before this, when we were among our families, we lived in fear. But Allah was gracious to us."[^2] And here is the lesson for you: the best laughter is about yourself and about how gracious Allah has been to you, not about someone who isn't there and can't defend themselves.

<!-- story:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why was the Companions' past something to laugh and smile about, rather than a source of shame to hide from?
2. What is the difference between laughing at your own past and laughing at a photo of a friend who isn't there?
3. Jabir's gathering moved from remembrance, to conversation, to laughter. What would that order look like in your own gatherings?
4. What does it mean to you that the faces of the people of Jannah are "laughing, rejoicing"?
5. After this lesson, what rule would you like to set for your group chat?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: We Used to... Then Allah Was Gracious to Us

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.070.activity.we-used-to-then-allah-favoured-us" -->

Within seven days, host a real gathering, not a group chat, with your siblings, relatives or friends you trust, with the phones put away. Prepare a true memory about yourself in two parts: "I used to..." (a habit you dropped, a fear you got past, an embarrassing moment that has become funny), and "Then Allah was gracious to me...". Ask the others to prepare one too. Agree on three rules: no stories about anyone who is absent; no laughing except at what someone chose to tell about themselves; and close with praise of Allah. Afterward, in a private note, rate each rule: Was it kept? Then write a paragraph: Which blessing did you hear about that changed how you see the person who shared it?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.070.dua.gatherings-of-good-rejoicing-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ مَجَالِسَنَا مَجَالِسَ خَيْرٍ وَذِكْرٍ، وَاجْعَلْنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ نَتَذَاكَرُ نِعْمَتَكَ عَلَيْنَا ضَاحِكِينَ مُسْتَبْشِرِينَ.
>
> What it means: O Allah, make our gatherings, whether face to face or on screens, gatherings of goodness and remembrance, free of mockery and backbiting. And let us recall together in Your Jannah how gracious You have been to us, laughing and rejoicing in Your favor, not in ourselves.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the report of Jabir ibn Samurah in Sahih Muslim 670 and states what an-Nawawi drew from it; explains al-Qurtubi's view that remembrance and conversation come together in the same gathering; describes the gatherings of the people of Jannah from at-Tur 52:25-28, ar-Rum 30:15 and 'Abasa 80:38-39; explains the laughter of the believers in al-Mutaffifin 83:34-36 through the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; and plans a "We Used to... Then Allah Was Gracious to Us" gathering within a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "Jabir's gathering," "The gatherings of Jannah" and "Our gatherings"; a blessings sheet for the host; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the commentary of an-Nawawi, al-Qurtubi and al-Qari on Muslim 670 and 2322; the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on at-Tur 52:25, 'Abasa 80:38-39 and al-Mutaffifin 83:34-36; and the tafsir of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir on ar-Rum 30:15. The teacher also reviews the lesson on food and drink (lesson 58) so as not to re-explain the table scene of at-Tur, and leaves the hadiths about visiting one another for their own lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Think of the last gathering where you laughed from the heart. What was the laughter about? And did you leave it feeling good about it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read Jabir's report and fill in the "Jabir's gathering" column: remembrance, conversation, laughter, smiles; then the statements of an-Nawawi, al-Qurtubi and al-Qari (8 minutes). Next, at-Tur 52:25-28, ar-Rum 30:14-15 and 'Abasa 80:38-39, filling in the second column (7 minutes). Then al-Mutaffifin 83:34-36 with the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the third and fourth questions. The teacher stresses that the link between Jabir's gathering and the gatherings of Jannah is a conclusion drawn by the lesson, and that the laughter in al-Mutaffifin is a recompense on the Day of Recompense, not a license to mock. Then fill in the "Our gatherings" column.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes out their memory in its two parts, decides whom they will invite and when, and words the three rules in language that suits their guests.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What did an-Nawawi draw from the report?", "What do the people of Jannah ask one another about?" and "What are the limits of laughter in our gatherings?" Then read the bedtime du'a, pointing out that it was written for this lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, Jabir's report and at-Tur 52:25-28 are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare Muslim's narration with those of at-Tirmidhi (2850) and an-Nasa'i (1358), and with what those narrations add about reciting poetry.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The report must not be taken as glorifying the sins of the Jahiliyyah; the Companions mentioned them in censure or to draw a lesson, as al-Qari said. Do not elaborate on the gatherings of Jannah beyond what is established: Anas's hadith about the people of Jannah talking together on their couches about the Day of Forgiveness, which Ibn Kathir cites from al-Bazzar, is not relied upon in this lesson.[^6] Be considerate of anyone whose past is painful or holds a sin they do not want to mention: it is enough for them to choose a light memory, and no one is asked to lift the veil with which Allah has covered them.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would sit after Fajr until the sun came up, and that his Companions would talk and laugh while he smiled; says that the people of Jannah sit together and talk, and that their faces are laughing; tells a "When I was little... Now" story and ends it with "Alhamdulillah"; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A picture of a sunrise (with no people in it); a sheet of paper divided into two sides for each child; crayons; the du'a card; a note for parents explaining the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the report in its short sentences, adding nothing, and prepares a gentle story from their own childhood to tell as an example.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Show the sunrise picture and ask: "When does the sun come up? Which prayer do we pray before it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the report, and the children smile along at the words "And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would smile."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions, then tell your example story in its two parts: "I used to... and Allah blessed me."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** Each child draws the two sides of their paper and tells the child next to them what they drew; then the two say together: "Alhamdulillah!"

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver watches whether the child mentions both parts and ends with "Alhamdulillah." Then read the du'a one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one drawing and the word "Alhamdulillah" are enough. Ask older children to tell the report in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not explain to the children in any detail the idol worship of the Jahiliyyah, and do not cover the ayat of al-Mutaffifin. Be mindful of a child who does not live with their parents; they can ask any relative who looks after them. And never laugh at a child's drawing or story.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the report of Jabir ibn Samurah in order; defines the three terms; says what the people of Jannah talk about; tells the difference between laughter that brings people together and laughter that hurts; and prepares their two-part memory and the activity chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; two cards labeled "Laughter that brings us together" and "Laughter that hurts"; a chart sheet for each student; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 2322 and Ibn Kathir's tafsir of at-Tur 52:25, and prepares short examples of moments of laughter from students' lives for sorting.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the story of the school party and asks: "When was the laughter lovely? When did the mood change? And why?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes); then the report, putting its events in order on the board: the question, the dawn prayer, sitting until sunrise, conversation, laughter, a smile (5 minutes); then the ayat of at-Tur (2 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Students sort the examples under the two cards and discuss the third and fourth questions. The teacher stresses that the Companions laughed at their own old ways, the ones Allah had brought them out of.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes their memory in its two parts, practices telling it to a classmate, and draws the two-column chart to take home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "In Jannah, people talk about..." Then read the du'a. After the week, anyone who wishes may share the blessing that surprised them.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, one line for each part is enough. Ask advanced students to memorize at-Tur 52:25-28 and to explain *yatasa'alun* in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No student is asked to tell an embarrassing memory they do not want to share. Be considerate of students who have been through loss, migration or illness, and let them choose a light memory. Classmates' stories or photos may not be used in the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes Jabir's report and explains an-Nawawi's statement on laughing and smiling; connects the Companions' talk about the Jahiliyyah with the words of the people of Jannah, "But Allah was gracious to us"; compares the laughter of the gathering with the laughter of group chats; and plans a real gathering and evaluates its rules.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; three invented messages from a friends' group chat (with no real names) for analysis; cards for anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the commentary of an-Nawawi and al-Qari on Muslim 670 and 2322, and the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and at-Tabari on at-Tur 52:25 and ar-Rum 30:15, and writes the invented messages so that none of them resembles any particular student.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the messages and asks: "Which message will get the most reactions? And who pays the price of the laughter in it?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the two terms, then the report, pausing three times: at "Yes, often," at the sitting until sunrise, and at "and laugh, and he would smile." Then read the ayat of at-Tur 52:25-28.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 2 and 5, followed by short presentations. The teacher draws out that laughing at oneself after Allah's blessing is a form of gratitude, while laughing at someone who is absent may be backbiting or mockery.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes their memory in its two parts, decides whom they will invite, and puts the three rules into their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: what happened in Jabir's gathering, what the people of Jannah talk about, and what their own new rule is. A week later the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see anyone's notes. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Anyone who finds writing difficult may record a private voice note instead. Ask advanced students to write a reflection on al-Qurtubi's view that remembrance and conversation can come together at the same time.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to reveal a past that involves sin; covering it comes first. Real student conversations are never shown. The ayat of al-Mutaffifin are never explained as permission to mock anyone. Be attentive to any student who is being bullied, and follow up with them privately.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
