# The Final Call of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the Qur'anic scene in Surah Yunus (10:7-10): one group content with this world and at ease in it, and another that believed and did righteous deeds, whom their Lord guided by their faith to the Gardens of Bliss, where their call begins with `Glory be to You, O Allah`, their greeting is `Peace`, and the close of their call is `Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds`.
- Explain the meaning of "call" (*da'wa*), glorification (*tasbih*) and praise (*hamd*) through the words of the commentators, and distinguish the view that ties glorification to the moment of asking for something from Ibn al-Qayyim's preferred view: that it is praise the people of Jannah are inspired with, opening in glorification and closing in praise.
- Draw on the hadith of Jabir (Muslim 2835) to show that the people of Jannah are inspired with glorification and praise as they are inspired with breath, that is, with no effort and no strain.
- Connect the praise of the people of Jannah on settling in their home with the removal of grief and weariness (Fatir 35:33-35), and connect that in turn with the merit of praise and glorification in this world (Muslim 223).
- Carry out the "Last Word Is Praise" activity: noticing what we actually say at the endings of our day, then closing each ending with a sentence of praise that names a specific blessing.
- Learn by heart the Prophet's bedtime du'a, `Praise be to Allah, who has fed us and given us drink...` (Muslim 2715), so that praise becomes the last word of the day.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every journey ends with a last word, and that word gives away what lies in the traveler's heart: a complaint, a sigh of relief, or praise. The Qur'an tells us the last word of the people of Jannah, spoken after the long journey of this world and the terrors of the Standing on the Day of Judgment: `And the close of their call is: "Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds."`

This lesson presents the Qur'anic scene in Surah Yunus and reads alongside it the ayat of Fatir on the praise the people of Jannah offer once they are settled, together with the hadith of Jabir describing how glorification and praise flow from their tongues as naturally as breath. Then it asks: if praise is the final word of the people of Jannah, what is our final word today?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-10-7-10" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And the Close of Their Call Is: Praise Be to Allah, Lord of All the Worlds"

> **{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ لَا يَرْجُونَ لِقَاءَنَا وَرَضُوا بِالْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَاطْمَأَنُّوا بِهَا وَالَّذِينَ هُمْ عَنْ آيَاتِنَا غَافِلُونَ ۝ أُولَٰئِكَ مَأْوَاهُمُ النَّارُ بِمَا كَانُوا يَكْسِبُونَ ۝ إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ يَهْدِيهِمْ رَبُّهُمْ بِإِيمَانِهِمْ ۖ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهِمُ الْأَنْهَارُ فِي جَنَّاتِ النَّعِيمِ ۝ دَعْوَاهُمْ فِيهَا سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ وَتَحِيَّتُهُمْ فِيهَا سَلَامٌ ۚ وَآخِرُ دَعْوَاهُمْ أَنِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ}** [يونس: ٧-١٠][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, those who do not hope to meet Us, who are content with the life of this world and feel secure in it, and those who are heedless of Our signs: those, their refuge is the Fire, for what they used to earn. Indeed, those who believed and did righteous deeds, their Lord guides them by their faith. Rivers will flow beneath them in the Gardens of Bliss. Their call therein is, 'Glory be to You, O Allah!', and their greeting therein is, 'Peace!' And the close of their call is: 'Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds.'"** (Yunus 10:7-10)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, explains that the first two ayat describe the wretched: those who do not hope to meet Allah, who are content with this world and whose souls have settled comfortably into it, and who are heedless of Allah's signs. Then Allah tells of the blessed. The *ba'* in `by their faith` (*bi-imanihim*) may be causal, meaning that He guides them, because of their faith in this world, along the Sirat until they pass safely through into Jannah; or it may be instrumental, as Mujahid said: their faith becomes a light for them by which they walk. On `And the close of their call`, Ibn Kathir says that it shows Allah is the One praised forever, at the beginning and at the end, in this world and in the Hereafter.[^1]

For al-Baghawi, `their call` (*da'wahum*) means their speech and their words, though some said it means their supplication; and `Glory be to You, O Allah` (*subhanaka Allahumma*) is a declaration of transcendence, declaring Allah free of every evil. In other words, they open their speech with glorification and seal it with praise.[^2] As-Sa'di says it means their worship of Allah there, which begins with glorification and ends with praise: religious obligations (*taklif*) have fallen away from them in the abode of reward, and what remains is the remembrance of Allah, sweeter to them than any food, which is to them as breathing is, with no effort and no strain.[^3]

At-Tabari reports from Ibn Jurayj, introduced only with "I was told," that when a bird they desired passed by the people of Jannah, they would say, "Glory be to You, O Allah," and the angel would bring them what they desired, and when they had eaten they would praise Allah; something similar is reported from Sufyan ath-Thawri. Ibn al-Qayyim comments that these authorities "captured part of the meaning without exhausting it," and that the ayah means something broader: the "call" here is a call of praise with which the people of Jannah are inspired, opening in glorification and closing in praise. They are inspired with both as they are inspired with breath, and these words are not confined to the moment of wanting something.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayat set two hearts side by side: one heart that grew comfortable with this world and so became heedless, and another that believed and acted and so was guided. The second journey does not end in silence, nor in a list of demands. It ends with Allah declared perfect at the start of their speech, with peace exchanged among them, and with praise at its close.

### "Praise Be to Allah, Who Has Removed All Grief from Us"

> **{جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا يُحَلَّوْنَ فِيهَا مِنْ أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَلُؤْلُؤًا ۖ وَلِبَاسُهُمْ فِيهَا حَرِيرٌ ۝ وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَذْهَبَ عَنَّا الْحَزَنَ ۖ إِنَّ رَبَّنَا لَغَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ ۝ الَّذِي أَحَلَّنَا دَارَ الْمُقَامَةِ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ لَا يَمَسُّنَا فِيهَا نَصَبٌ وَلَا يَمَسُّنَا فِيهَا لُغُوبٌ}** [فاطر: ٣٣-٣٥][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Gardens of Eden, which they will enter. They will be adorned there with bracelets of gold and with pearls, and their garments there will be silk. And they will say: 'Praise be to Allah, who has removed all grief from us. Indeed, our Lord is All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative, who out of His bounty has settled us in the Abode of Everlasting Stay, where no toil touches us and no weariness touches us.'"** (Fatir 35:33-35)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that `grief` (*al-hazan*) is fear of what one dreads: Allah lifted it from them and gave them rest from everything they used to fear among the worries of this world and the next. Ibn Abbas and others said that `All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative` means He forgave them many sins and rewarded them for few good deeds. On `out of His bounty`, Ibn Kathir explains their words as: He gave us this station out of His bounty, His grace and His mercy; our deeds were never worth it.[^5] As-Sa'di says it covers every kind of grief, so that no sorrow ever comes to them from any lack in their beauty, their food, or the permanence of their stay.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The praise of the people of Jannah is the praise of people who knew grief and weariness and then watched both disappear. And they credit their arrival to Allah's bounty, `out of His bounty`, not to anything they were owed in their own right.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2835" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Be Inspired with Glorification and Praise as They Are Inspired with Breath"

> عَنْ جَابِرٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: **«إِنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَأْكُلُونَ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ، وَلَا يَتْفُلُونَ، وَلَا يَبُولُونَ، وَلَا يَتَغَوَّطُونَ، وَلَا يَمْتَخِطُونَ»**. قَالُوا: فَمَا بَالُ الطَّعَامِ؟ قَالَ: **«جُشَاءٌ وَرَشْحٌ كَرَشْحِ الْمِسْكِ، يُلْهَمُونَ التَّسْبِيحَ وَالتَّحْمِيدَ كَمَا يُلْهَمُونَ النَّفَسَ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"The people of Jannah will eat and drink there, but they will not spit, nor pass urine, nor relieve themselves, nor blow their noses."** They asked, "Then what becomes of the food?" He said: **"A belch, and a sweat like the scent of musk. They will be inspired with glorification and praise as they are inspired with breath."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites this hadith in his commentary on the ayah of Yunus, and explains it by the fact that they see Allah's blessings upon them multiplying, renewed again and again and ever increasing, without end.[^1] In the narration of Abu az-Zubayr from Jabir in Muslim, the wording is "glorification and praise," and in another, "glorification and magnification (*takbir*)."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Breath is never forced and never tiresome, and life itself depends on it. So it is with their remembrance: it carries no burden and never breaks off. It is one of the delights of Jannah itself.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-223" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "And 'Praise Be to Allah' Fills the Scale"

> عَنْ أَبِي مَالِكٍ الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«الطُّهُورُ شَطْرُ الْإِيمَانِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ تَمْلَأُ الْمِيزَانَ، وَسُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ تَمْلَآنِ -أَوْ تَمْلَأُ- مَا بَيْنَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ، وَالصَّلَاةُ نُورٌ، وَالصَّدَقَةُ بُرْهَانٌ، وَالصَّبْرُ ضِيَاءٌ، وَالْقُرْآنُ حُجَّةٌ لَكَ أَوْ عَلَيْكَ، كُلُّ النَّاسِ يَغْدُو، فَبَائِعٌ نَفْسَهُ فَمُعْتِقُهَا أَوْ مُوبِقُهَا»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Malik al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Purification is half of faith. 'Praise be to Allah' (*al-hamdu lillah*) fills the Scale. 'Glory be to Allah' (*subhan Allah*) and 'Praise be to Allah' fill (or: fills) what lies between the heavens and the earth. Prayer is light, charity is proof, patience is radiance, and the Qur'an is an argument for you or against you. Every person sets out in the morning and sells his soul: either he sets it free, or he brings it to ruin."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, says that "fills the Scale" means the immensity of its reward. As for "fill (or: fills) what lies between the heavens and the earth," it may mean that if their reward were given a physical form, it would fill all that lies between them. The reason for their great merit is what they contain: declaring Allah free of every imperfection in saying "Glory be to Allah," and entrusting oneself to Him and confessing one's need of Him in saying "Praise be to Allah."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The two phrases that make up the call of the people of Jannah in the Hereafter are the very phrases that weigh most heavily on a servant's Scale in this world. Whoever trains the tongue to say them here is practicing the language of the home he hopes to reach.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of Yunus open at a fork in the road: contentment with this world and heedlessness, or faith and action. The believer's journey closes with glorification, peace and praise, the praise of someone from whom Allah, out of His bounty, has lifted grief and weariness. The hadith of Jabir shows that this remembrance is, there, like breath and not a duty; and the hadith of Abu Malik shows that these same two phrases fill the Scale here. Jannah is the home where praise is the last word spoken, and this world is the training ground where we learn to close our own affairs with it.

### Activity: The Last Word Is Praise

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.035.activity.last-word-is-praise" -->

For seven days, pick out five recurring "endings" in your day: finishing a meal, finishing work, arriving after a trip, the end of an irritating situation, and the end of the day. For the first two days, simply observe: what is the first word or thought that comes out of you at each ending? Write it down honestly. Then, for the next five days, close each ending with a sentence of praise that names a specific blessing still in your hands, such as, "Praise be to Allah, who brought me here safely." At the end of the week, compare the two columns and write a paragraph: at which endings was praise hardest, and why?

When we finish eating, we say, "Alhamdulillah," which means "Praise be to Allah." When we get home after a long trip, we say, "Alhamdulillah." It is a beautiful thing to say when something comes to an end.

And guess what? The people of Jannah say "Alhamdulillah" too! The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that they say "Subhan Allah" and "Alhamdulillah" so easily, just the way we breathe without getting tired.[^7]

Today we are going to hear a story from the Qur'an about the very last thing the people of Jannah say.

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Last Word in Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-10-7-10" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In Surah Yunus, Allah tells us about two kinds of people.

The first kind did not look forward to meeting Allah. They were happy with this world and nothing else, and they did not notice all of Allah's signs around them: the sun, the rain, and the trees.[^1]

The second kind believed in Allah and loved Him, and they did good things: they prayed, they told the truth, and they helped others. In this world they sometimes got tired, and sometimes they felt sad.

So what happened to these believers? Allah says that He guides them by their faith. That means He shows them the way, until they reach beautiful gardens with rivers flowing through them.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-10-7-10" audience="4-7" -->

And when they get there, what do they say? The first thing they say is, `Subhanaka Allahumma`, which means, "How great and perfect You are, O Allah!" They greet each other with, `Salam`, which means "Peace!" And the last thing they say is, `Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil 'alamin`, "Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds."[^1]

They also say, "Praise be to Allah, who has taken all our sadness away!" No more sadness after that day, and no more getting tired.[^5]

<!-- retelling:end -->

So the believers started their journey with faith, and they finished it with praise. And we can start learning the words of the people of Jannah right now.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Picture the last day of a school trip. The bus is heading back and everyone is worn out. Somebody says, "That went by way too fast!" Somebody else grumbles about the heat. And one kid says, "Alhamdulillah, that was a great trip, and we all made it back safe." That last word shows how each of them saw the whole trip.

The Qur'an tells us the last word of the people of Jannah after the whole journey of this life: "Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds."[^1] And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that they are inspired with glorification and praise the way they are inspired with breath.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-10-7-10" -->

- **`Their call (da'wahum)`** — their words and speech (some scholars said: their supplication); here it is praise of Allah.[^2][^4]
- **`Glory be to You (subhanaka)`** — words that declare Allah perfect, meaning: You, O Allah, are far above every flaw and every evil.[^2]
- **`They are inspired (yulhamun)`** — the words flow from their tongues with no tiredness and no effort, the way breath flows.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: Two Roads, Two Endings

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-10-7-10" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an; any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

In Surah Yunus, a surah revealed in Makkah, Allah describes two groups.

The first group does not look forward to meeting Allah. They are content with this world and feel completely at home in it, as if it were everything, and they ignore Allah's signs. Ibn Kathir explains that they ignored Allah's signs in the universe, so they never thought about them, and ignored His commands, so they never acted on them. And so their ending was the Fire, because of what they used to earn.[^1]

The second group believed and did righteous deeds. Their lives were not always easy; they felt afraid, sad, and tired, just like everyone else. But Allah guides them by their faith. Mujahid said that their faith becomes a light they walk by, until they reach the Gardens of Bliss, with rivers flowing beneath them.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-10-7-10" audience="8-12" -->

Then Allah describes what they say there: `Their call therein is, "Glory be to You, O Allah!", and their greeting therein is, "Peace!" And the close of their call is: "Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds."` Al-Baghawi said: they open their words with glorification and close them with praise.[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

In Surah Fatir, we hear one of the things they say in praise: `Praise be to Allah, who has removed all grief from us`. The fear they lived through is over, the tiredness is over, and they say it all came `out of His bounty`.[^5]

Both groups lived in the very same world. The first made it the end of the road; the second made it a road to Allah, and so their last word was praise.

### The Bedtime Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.035.dua.praise-for-food-and-shelter" provenance="sunnah" source_id="muslim-2715" attribution="prophetic" -->

Whenever the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, lay down in his bed, he would say:

> **الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَطْعَمَنَا وَسَقَانَا، وَكَفَانَا وَآوَانَا، فَكَمْ مِمَّنْ لَا كَافِيَ لَهُ وَلَا مُؤْوِيَ.**[^9]
>
> It means: Praise be to Allah, who provided us with food and drink, took care of everything we need, and gave us a shelter to rest in. How many people there are who have no one to take care of their needs, and no place to shelter them![^9]

<!-- bedtime-dua:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Notice how things end around you: a game ends with a sarcastic comment, a school day ends with "Finally, it's over," and a group chat ends with everyone complaining about everything. In a setting like that, griping becomes the shared language, and praise starts to sound out of touch with reality.

But the Qur'an tells us that the people of Jannah, after a journey full of real fear and real exhaustion, close with this word: `Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds`,[^1] and that praise fills the Scale in this world.[^8] So the question is not whether life is tiring. The question is: what word will you end it with?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-35-33-35" -->

- **`Grief (al-hazan)`** — fear of what one dreads, and everything they used to worry about from the troubles of this world and the next, as Ibn Kathir explains.[^5]
- **`Weariness (lughub)`** — exhaustion; *nasab* (toil) and *lughub* are both kinds of tiredness, and the point is that tiredness is removed from both their bodies and their souls, as Ibn Kathir explains.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Last Word

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-10-7-10" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an; any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah Yunus was revealed in Makkah, during years when the believers were few, the mockery never let up, and all the world's power was in other hands. Against that backdrop, the Qur'an maps out two roads.

The first road belongs to people who do not hope to meet Allah, who are content with the life of this world and feel secure in it. Al-Hasan al-Basri said, as Ibn Kathir quotes him: "By Allah, they didn't even adorn it or raise it up, and still they were content with it." In other words, they accepted this world just as it is, made it the ceiling of their dreams, and tuned out Allah's signs. The end of that road: `those, their refuge is the Fire, for what they used to earn`.[^1]

The second road belongs to those who believed and did righteous deeds in the middle of all that pressure. In Makkah they had no influence and no comfort, but they had faith, and by it their Lord guides them. Ibn Kathir says that He may guide them because of it across the Sirat until they make it over, or that their faith may become a light for them to walk by.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-10-7-10" audience="13+" -->

Then the scene cuts to the end of the road: rivers flowing beneath them in the Gardens of Bliss, and speech with no idle talk in it. It opens with `Glory be to You, O Allah`, their greeting is `Peace`, and it closes with `Praise be to Allah, Lord of all the worlds`. Ibn al-Qayyim affirms that this is praise they are inspired with as they are inspired with breath, with no obligation involved.[^1][^4]

<!-- retelling:end -->

In Surah Fatir, their praise reveals what lies behind it: `Praise be to Allah, who has removed all grief from us`. They remember the fear and the exhaustion, and then they credit their arrival to Allah's bounty, not to themselves: `who out of His bounty has settled us in the Abode of Everlasting Stay`.[^5]

Notice the irony: the one who felt secure in this world lost it and lost the Hereafter too, while the one who never made it his goal ended with praise. And that last word of his did not appear out of nowhere. It was a language he had trained in here.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What is the difference between enjoying this world and "feeling secure in it," as the ayah puts it?
2. Why is praise hard in a setting where griping is the shared language?
3. What does it mean that the people of Jannah mention the grief that has been removed from them while they are praising Allah?
4. How is sincere praise different from denying a problem or pretending everything is amazing?
5. Which recurring ending in your week do you want to give a new last word?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Last-Word Log

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.035.activity.last-word-is-praise" -->

For seven days, keep track of your "endings" in a private notebook: the end of a class or a test, the end of a game or match, the end of a group chat, and the end of the day. For the first three days, just record the last word you actually said or typed. Then, for the remaining four days, close each ending with a sincere sentence of praise that names a blessing that is still there despite the disappointment, without denying the problem. At the end, write a paragraph: how did the way you see your day change when your last word changed?

<!-- activity:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.bedtime-dua" kind="bedtime_dua" -->

### The Bedtime Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.035.dua.praise-for-food-and-shelter" provenance="sunnah" source_id="muslim-2715" attribution="prophetic" -->

It is authentically reported that whenever the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, lay down in his bed, he would say:

> **الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَطْعَمَنَا وَسَقَانَا، وَكَفَانَا وَآوَانَا، فَكَمْ مِمَّنْ لَا كَافِيَ لَهُ وَلَا مُؤْوِيَ.**[^9]
>
> It means: Praise be to Allah, who has fed us and given us drink, who has taken care of what concerns us, and who has given us a home where we can rest at ease; for how many people find no one to take care of them and no shelter to turn to. Let the last words of your day be praise that knows the worth of small blessings others are living without.[^9]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene of Yunus 10:7-10 with its two groups and two endings; explains `their call` and `Glory be to You, O Allah` through the words of al-Baghawi and as-Sa'di; distinguishes the report of Ibn Jurayj and ath-Thawri from Ibn al-Qayyim's preferred view, noting that Ibn Jurayj's report, introduced with "I was told," cannot be built upon; draws on Muslim 2835, Muslim 223 and Fatir 35:33-35; and designs a week of "The Last Word Is Praise."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for reference to Surahs Yunus and Fatir; a board with two columns, "The First Road" and "The Second Road"; an activity sheet with a seven-day chart in two columns ("What I Said" and "My Praise Sentence"); an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the four pieces of evidence and their references, and reviews Ibn Kathir's commentary on Yunus 10:7-10 and Fatir 35:34 and the final chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher keeps in mind that the ayat of az-Zumar 39:74 and al-A'raf 7:43 on the praise of the people of Jannah were studied in lessons thirty and thirty-one, so here they are only mentioned in passing. The teacher also prepares a short personal example of an "ending" whose last word changed.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Think of the last trip or long project you finished. What was the first thing you said when it was over?" Then: "If the last words of each day last week had been recorded, what would you find?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read Yunus 10:7-10 in full, and have learners fill in the two columns on the board with the traits of each group (7 minutes). Then present the commentators' views on "call" and glorification, and lay out the difference between the narrower view and Ibn al-Qayyim's preferred view (5 minutes). Then Fatir 35:33-35 and the meaning of grief, toil and weariness (4 minutes). Then the two hadiths, Muslim 2835 and 223 (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion: Why was praise the close of their call and not its opening? How does `out of His bounty` hold together deeds and mercy? What is the difference between remembrance that takes effort in this world and remembrance that is inspired in Jannah, and how does practice move the first toward the second?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner identifies their five endings and writes a draft sentence of praise for each one that names a specific blessing. Then they swap the hardest ending on their list with a partner, and each suggests to the other a wording that is sincere and unforced.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What is the opening and the close of the call of the people of Jannah? And what does 'as they are inspired with breath' mean?" and "Which ending will you start with tomorrow?" Then read the bedtime du'a from Muslim 2715 and explain it.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, Yunus 10:9-10 and the hadith of Jabir are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the ayat about the praise of the people of Jannah (Yunus 10:10, Fatir 35:34, az-Zumar 39:74, al-A'raf 7:43) into a table: When was the praise said? For which blessing?

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not add to the description of the delights of Jannah beyond what has been reported, and build nothing on unsourced reports about servants and banquets. Praise must not be understood as denying pain or forbidding complaint to Allah; what is asked for is praise together with an honest acknowledgment of reality. Be mindful of anyone going through loss or severe hardship, and do not ask them to offer praise publicly.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the first words of the people of Jannah are "Subhanaka Allahumma" and the last are "Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil 'alamin"; mentions that Allah took away their sadness; and closes three daily moments with "Alhamdulillah" while naming a blessing.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A long strip of paper for each child; coloring pencils; star stickers; a nature picture (a river and trees) with no writing on it; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the story in a calm voice, and draws a sample Alhamdulillah strip with the three pictures ahead of time.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver gives each child a small piece of fruit, and after they eat it asks, "What do we say now?" and cheers for whoever says, "Alhamdulillah."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the story while showing the nature picture, and asks the children to repeat `Subhanaka Allahumma` and then `Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil 'alamin`.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What's the first word? What's the last word? What did Allah take away from them?" Gently explain that the people of Jannah remember Allah as easily as breathing, and invite the children to take a deep breath together.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The children draw the three pictures on the strip as described in the activity unit, practice the sentence "Alhamdulillah for..." by naming something they love, and take the strip home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver notices whether the child can say the two phrases and a sentence of praise that names a blessing. Then read the bedtime du'a together, one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, saying "Alhamdulillah" at the end of a meal is enough. Ask older children to memorize the final ayah: `وَآخِرُ دَعْوَاهُمْ أَنِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ`.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the first group only briefly, without frightening the children or describing the Fire. Do not compare children by how many stars they have. Be gentle with a child who is living with sadness at home, and reassure them that Allah takes sadness away from the believers.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student compares the two groups in Yunus 10:7-10; defines the three terms; explains the ayah of Fatir 35:34; and fills in the Endings Tracker for five days and writes a reflection.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; an Endings Tracker chart for each student; a mushaf; two large cards reading "At ease with this world" and "Guided by his Lord through his faith."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes ayat 7 and 9 side by side to bring out the contrast, and prepares three short school scenarios with different endings (losing a match, finishing a test, the end of a trip).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the bus example from the introduction and asks, "Which of the three comments sounds most like yours usually does? Why?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the whole story, pausing at the traits of the first group, at the words of the people of Jannah, and at the ayah of Fatir, and pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of the commentators (9 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher assigns the three scenarios to groups. Each group writes a likely complaint, then a sincere sentence of praise that names a blessing still there, and presents it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student sets up their chart as described in the activity unit, fills in the first day's row about an ending that happened today, and agrees with the teacher on a review day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The people of Jannah open their words with... and close them with...". Then read the bedtime du'a. After five days, anyone who wishes shares their two sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide the chart with pictures instead of writing in the first column. Ask advanced students to memorize Yunus 10:9-10 and explain the meaning of "they are inspired" in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the punishment of the first group in detail. Do not blame a student for anything written in the "What I Said First" column; honesty there is the point. Make clear that praise does not mean we never feel sad or never ask for help.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the meaning of "being content with this world and feeling secure in it" and contrasts it with lawful enjoyment; explains how the people of Jannah mention grief while praising Allah; distinguishes sincere praise from denying a problem; and completes the Last-Word Log over seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a private notebook that is not collected; a mushaf; small cards for anonymous answers; neutral text snippets (written out on paper) showing group chats that end in griping.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral examples of "the shared language of griping," reviews al-Hasan al-Basri's statement in Ibn Kathir's commentary on Yunus 10:7, and keeps in mind the difference between praise and toxic positivity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the written chat endings and asks, "If this were the last message of someone's day, what would it say about them?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the two terms, then the story, with three pauses: the setting in Makkah, al-Hasan's statement about being content with this world, and the end of the road in Yunus 10:10 and Fatir 35:34-35.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Small-group discussion of questions 1, 3 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher draws out that the people of Jannah did not deny the grief they had lived through; they praised Allah for removing it, and sincere praise names the blessing without lying about the pain.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student identifies their four endings in their notebook and writes a draft sentence of praise for each, to try out after the three observation days, as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: what makes griping easy, the difference between praise and pretending, and which ending they will start with. The teacher follows up a week later with a general question, without asking to see notebooks. Then read the bedtime du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may keep a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on Ibn al-Qayyim's words, "they are inspired with both as they are inspired with breath," and on how the habit of praise can move from effort to second nature.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to disclose their worries. Point out that anyone going through severe emotional distress needs someone to support them, and that praise is no substitute for seeking help. Do not declare Jannah certain for any specific person, and build nothing on unsourced reports in describing its delights.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
