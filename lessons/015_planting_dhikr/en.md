# The Plantings of Paradise and Its Remembrances

## Lesson Objectives And Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Explain the hadith "Two words beloved to the Most Merciful, light on the tongue, heavy on the scale," and show that the heaviness meant here is the heaviness of value and reward, not the heaviness of physical effort.
- Connect the ayah `And the lasting good deeds are better with your Lord in reward and better in [one's] hope for the future` (Al-Kahf 18:46) to Ibn Kathir's tafsir, which explains the lasting good deeds as the five phrases, including glorifying and praising Allah.
- Recount the hadith "Whoever says: Subhan Allah wa bihamdih, a palm tree is planted for him in Paradise," and show that this planting is real and unseen, not restricted to any specific number of times.
- Distinguish between three deviations in dealing with easy remembrance: belittling it because it is small, being heedless of its meaning while repeating it, and abandoning it after starting.
- Distinguish between measuring the value of a deed by how visible it is or how much effort it costs, and the correct measure — sincerity of heart and consistency of habit — as shown in the stories of Sami, Huda, and Karim.
- Practice a daily habit of saying "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" during repeated moments not usually counted as deeds, tracking the count and connecting it to the three pieces of evidence (the ayah of Al-Kahf, the hadith of Abu Hurairah, and the hadith of Jabir).

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

People tend to judge a good deed by how much effort it took, assuming that a great reward can only come from something long or difficult. But revelation reveals a different economy of reward: a short, easy remembrance on the tongue may be heavy on the scale, permanent in its effect, never interrupted even when its speaker leaves this world. The phrase `Subhan Allah wa bihamdih` ("Glory be to Allah, and praise be to Him") costs almost no effort at all, and yet a real palm tree is planted with it in Paradise every time it is said sincerely.

This understanding protects the heart from three deviations:

1. **Belittling it:** thinking that an easy deed does not deserve to count as worship, and so abandoning it in pursuit of some "bigger" deed that people can see and count.
2. **Heedlessness:** repeating the remembrance with the tongue while the heart is absent, so that it becomes mere sound without meaning instead of a remembrance that plants something good.
3. **Giving up:** starting with enthusiasm, then dropping the habit after a few days, and so losing the accumulated reward that is built through consistency, not through a single burst of effort.

For this reason, this lesson brings together the ease of the words, the magnitude of the reward, and the condition of sincerity and consistency — without either vanity over how easy the deed is or belittling its true worth.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-18-46" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### The Lasting Good Deeds Are Better With Your Lord

> **الْمَالُ وَالْبَنُونَ زِينَةُ الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا ۖ وَالْبَاقِيَاتُ الصَّالِحَاتُ خَيْرٌ عِنْدَ رَبِّكَ ثَوَابًا وَخَيْرٌ أَمَلًا.** [الكهف: ٤٦][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Wealth and children are [but] adornment of the worldly life. But the lasting good deeds are better with your Lord for reward and better for [one's] hope."** (Al-Kahf 18:46)[^1]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Ibn Kathir mentions in his tafsir of this ayah that a group of the Companions and the Followers, among them Uthman ibn Affan and Ibn Abbas, may Allah be pleased with them, explained `the lasting good deeds` as the five phrases: `La ilaha illallah` ("There is no god but Allah"), `Subhan Allah` ("Glory be to Allah"), `Al-hamdu lillah` ("Praise be to Allah"), `Allahu Akbar` ("Allah is the Greatest"), and `La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah` ("There is no power nor might except with Allah"). This interpretation was also reported with a chain going back to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in a number of narrations.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah contrasts the outward, fading adornment of this world with the lasting good deeds, which Allah describes as **better** with Him — not because of how much effort they take in this world, but because of their permanence and their acceptance in His sight. Glorifying and praising Allah are among these lasting good deeds; they are two phrases that cost neither money nor a long time, and yet Allah has described them as better in reward and better in hope.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-7563" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### Light on the Tongue, Heavy on the Scale

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«كَلِمَتَانِ حَبِيبَتَانِ إِلَى الرَّحْمَنِ، خَفِيفَتَانِ عَلَى اللِّسَانِ، ثَقِيلَتَانِ فِي الْمِيزَانِ: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ، سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ الْعَظِيمِ».**[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurairah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Two words are beloved to the Most Merciful, light on the tongue, heavy on the scale: Subhan Allah wa bihamdih [Glory be to Allah, and praise be to Him], Subhan Allah al-Azim [Glory be to Allah, the Magnificent]."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The hadith brings together two descriptions a person might assume contradict one another: lightness on the tongue, and heaviness on the scale. Scholars have explained that the heaviness here is the heaviness of value and reward, not the heaviness of physical effort; the word comes out without any cost, and Allah places it on the scale with an immense weight, purely out of His grace.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith demolishes the idea that reward is measured by how tiring a deed is. Allah's love for these two words is not conditioned on their difficulty, but on the sincerity and devotion of whoever says them.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-3464" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### A Palm Tree Planted in Paradise for Every Glorification

> عَنْ جَابِرٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«مَنْ قَالَ: سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ الْعَظِيمِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ، غُرِسَتْ لَهُ نَخْلَةٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ».**[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Whoever says: Subhan Allah al-Azim wa bihamdih [Glory be to Allah, the Magnificent, and praise be to Him], a palm tree is planted for him in Paradise."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The planting here is real, not merely symbolic; it belongs to the unseen matters that we believe in as they have come to us, without picturing for it any exact earthly image. The hadith does not restrict the reward to any specific number, so every sincere glorification is an independent cause for a new planting, according to the vastness of Allah's mercy and grace.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith links an easy phrase to a lasting effect that grows in a home that never perishes. The lesson is not in how small the word appears in this world, but in the standing Allah gives it in the Hereafter — and this is the very meaning of His words: `better with your Lord for reward`.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### An Educational Story: Sami's Seed

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.015.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an educational fictional story, not a hadith or historical report.**

Grandma gave Sami a tiny little seed, so small he could barely see it, and told him, "Plant it in the garden." Sami looked at his sister's big pot full of roses and said, "My seed is so small — it won't make anything worth noticing!" But he planted it anyway, just as his grandmother had asked.

That evening, as he got ready for bed, his father said to him, "Did you know that every time you say: Subhan Allah wa bihamdih, Allah plants a palm tree for you in Paradise?" Sami was amazed. "A small word makes a huge palm tree?!" His father said, "Yes — just like your tiny seed that will grow big one day, but the palm tree of Paradise is even greater and never dies."

From that evening on, Sami began saying "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" often, smiling as he said it, and he never again said, "This is too small to matter."

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

You might think that a deed deserving a great reward must be hard or visible to other people, like cleaning up or collecting donations. But Allah has told us that an easy phrase like "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" plants an entire palm tree in Paradise — a phrase no one sees and that costs almost no effort at all. Value with Allah is not measured by how tiring a deed is, but by the sincerity of the heart and the consistency of the habit.

### An Educational Story: The Board of Visible Deeds

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.015.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an educational fictional story, not a hadith or historical report.**

Huda organized a "Good Deeds Board" at her school, where big deeds were recorded: cleaning the classroom, collecting donations, organizing the library. Every morning she waited long minutes for the bus, feeling bored, thinking that this time was "wasted" and not worth recording on any board.

One day, her teacher asked the class: "Do you know of a deed no one sees, and yet it plants an entire palm tree in Paradise for whoever does it?" She then told them the hadith of "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih." Huda realized that her empty waiting minutes had been a missed opportunity — not because they were unimportant, but because she had not known their true worth.

The next day, she began sincerely saying "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" while waiting for the bus, without writing it down on any board that people could see. She did not stop taking part in the visible deeds on the board, but she learned that her most precious deeds might well be the ones hidden from everyone except Allah.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

In a world that often measures achievement by what gets posted and seen, you might feel that a deed no one notices does not "count," and that easy remembrance is more like some outdated habit that doesn't suit someone who wants to accomplish "real" things. Revelation corrects this picture: the value of a deed with Allah is not measured by how visible it is or how much effort it costs, but by its sincerity and consistency; and the heaviest thing on the scale may be the lightest thing on the tongue.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Educational Story: What Never Gets Posted

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.015.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an educational fictional story, not a hadith or historical report.**

Karim was in the habit of documenting every activity he did — a sports practice, a school project, a volunteer initiative — and posting photos that showed his effort. He felt that this documentation gave him the identity of "a productive person." One day, a friend mocked him, saying, "I saw your lips moving while you waited for class — is that glorifying Allah? That doesn't count as an achievement!"

Karim felt embarrassed, and thought about dropping this quiet habit since it added nothing "visible." But he remembered what he had learned about two hadiths: one describing the phrase "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" as light on the tongue and heavy on the scale, and the other saying it plants a palm tree in Paradise. He asked himself: "Should I abandon a deed the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described with this weight, just because a friend didn't understand its value?"

Karim decided to keep up his quiet glorification without explaining it to anyone or documenting it, and to distinguish between what he shares with people and what belongs only to him and his Lord. He did not stop his visible projects, but he stopped assuming that the value of a deed is measured by how many people see it.

<!-- story:end -->

### A Prophetic Report That Demolishes the Measure of Visibility

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-3464" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-3464" audience="13+" -->

Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever says: Subhan Allah al-Azim wa bihamdih, a palm tree is planted for him in Paradise."[^4] And in the authentic, agreed-upon hadith of Abu Hurairah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described this phrase and its companion phrase as "two words beloved to the Most Merciful, light on the tongue, heavy on the scale."[^3]

Together, the two hadiths take apart a common assumption: that reward follows visible effort. The phrase costs its speaker no fatigue, and the hadith does not require that anyone see it, and yet revelation described it as among the heaviest things placed on the scale and the most lasting thing planted in Paradise.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-18-46" -->

- **`Al-Baqiyat as-Salihat`** ("the lasting good deeds") — righteous deeds and remembrances whose reward remains and never perishes, including glorifying and praising Allah.
- **`Al-Mizan`** ("the scale") — the scales in which deeds are weighed on the Day of Judgment; their heaviness means the greatness of a deed's worth with Allah, not physical heaviness.
- **`Al-Ghaflah`** ("heedlessness") — the heart's inattention to the meaning of a remembrance while the tongue repeats it.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains how remembrance combines ease and heaviness on the scale, connects the ayah of Al-Kahf with the hadiths of Abu Hurairah and Jabir, diagnoses belittling, heedlessness, and giving up, and carries out a daily remembrance habit that can be measured.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the three pieces of evidence; a simple three-day tracking chart; paper and pens; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prints the three texts, prepares a blank tracking-chart template, and reviews Ibn Kathir's tafsir on the five phrases so as to explain it accurately.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What is the lightest deed you know, and what is the heaviest deed you know? Could the same deed be both light and heavy at once?" Gather responses without correcting them immediately.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Groups read Al-Kahf 18:46, the hadith of Bukhari 7563, and the hadith of At-Tirmidhi 3464. They underline the words describing ease and lightness, and circle the words describing reward, permanence, and planting.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains the difference between the heaviness of effort and the heaviness of value, connects the lasting good deeds to glorifying and praising Allah, then presents the three deviations — belittling, heedlessness, and giving up — and asks for a real-life example of each.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners begin the "Planting of Glorification" activity, choosing their three moments and, if possible, practically trying the first day within the session, agreeing to continue recording for the remaining days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Explain how a word can be light on the tongue and heavy on the scale, and name one of the three deviations and how you avoid it." The teacher closes with the lesson's du'a, noting that it is his own educational composition.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a beginner ready-made suggested moments for the activity, and assign an advanced learner to connect both hadiths to the ayah of Al-Kahf in a three-line explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not use the hadith to diminish the value of other deeds, and do not push someone prone to obsessive doubts toward compulsively counting remembrances; instead, guide them toward balanced remembrance with a present heart, without extreme tallying.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="30" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" and knows that it plants a palm tree in Paradise, distinguishes that a small word can have a huge effect, and practices saying it several times during the day.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A drawing of a leafless palm tree; small green paper leaves or stickers; a real small seed for display if available; a du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares the palm tree drawing and small leaves in advance, and practices explaining the meaning of "Subhan Allah" and "bihamdih" in two short sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The teacher shows a small seed and asks: "Will this seed make something big one day?" Accepts the children's answers, then connects them to the lesson's topic.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The teacher narrates Sami's fictional story, then the true hadith story, and the children repeat with him: "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 6 minutes:** The teacher clarifies that every glorification plants a palm tree in Paradise, and that a small word does not mean a small effect, using the comparison between the seed and the palm tree.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 9 minutes:** Each child says "Subhan Allah wa bihamdih" and sticks a green leaf on the palm tree drawing, repeating this several times, then the children count the leaves together at the end of the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What happens in Paradise when we say this word? Is it a small word or a word with a big effect?" Then read the du'a once, slowly.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Allow group repetition for a child who cannot say the phrase alone, and give an advanced child the task of counting the leaves and announcing the total.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not tell the child that forgetting the glorification one day loses him a previous reward, and do not turn the counting into a competition between children; keep the activity a source of joy and reassurance.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student defines the meaning of "ghurisat," "Subhan Allah," and "bihamdih," clarifies how a phrase can be both light and heavy at once, and sets up a tracking chart for a daily remembrance habit.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of both hadiths; terminology cards; a three-day tracking chart; drawing paper for a palm-tree garden.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes both hadiths on the board, prepares a tracking-chart template, and readies a sample drawing of a palm-tree garden to illustrate the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Does every important deed have to be hard or visible to people?" Gather student opinions before presenting Huda's story.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Students read both hadiths in full, and identify the words describing ease and lightness and the words describing reward and permanence.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher explains the three terms, then retells Huda's story, showing how it changed her view of "wasted" time.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 14 minutes:** Students begin the tracking chart for two chosen moments, and each draws a small palm-tree garden representing their expected total for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes a sentence connecting the lightness of the remembrance to the heaviness of its reward, one or two students share theirs, then the teacher reads the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a struggling student ready-made moments for the tracker, and assign an advanced student to explain why reward is not measured by effort alone, citing both hadiths.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not let the comparison turn into ranking students by their commitment to remembrance, and do not ask them to announce their private acts of worship in front of the class.

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 55 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the idea of measuring value by visibility versus sincerity, explains the meaning of the lasting good deeds and the scale, and sets up a private tracker for a hidden remembrance habit for one week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A file with the three pieces of evidence; terminology cards; a private weekly-tracker template; exit slips.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the wording of both hadiths, and prepares a neutral example about the pressure of posting and social documentation without pointing to any specific student.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher poses the question: "Does a good deed need to be seen by someone to have value?" Opens a short discussion before reading Karim's story.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Three groups read Al-Kahf 18:46, the hadith of Abu Hurairah, and the hadith of Jabir. Each group extracts from every text: the word indicating ease, and the word indicating lasting reward.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** The teacher explains the terms "the lasting good deeds," "the scale," and "heedlessness," and discusses how the two hadiths together confront the idea that real achievement is only what gets posted and seen.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Students design their own private weekly tracker for a hidden remembrance habit, and write an introductory paragraph explaining why they chose their three moments.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** The student writes a three-line answer to the opening question, drawing on both hadiths, then identifies one private step they need not announce to anyone.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a struggling student three ready-made moments to choose from, and assign an advanced student to discuss how documenting a deed to motivate others differs from documenting it to prove oneself.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not use the lesson to judge anyone who documents their deeds publicly, and do not turn remembrance into compulsive counting that triggers obsessive doubt; guide anyone showing such signs toward balanced remembrance and appropriate support.
