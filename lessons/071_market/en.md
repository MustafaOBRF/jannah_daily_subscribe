# The Market of Jannah and Its Outings

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of the market of Jannah (Sahih Muslim 2833) in its proper sequence: the outing to the market every Friday, the north wind, the increase in beauty and radiance, the return to their families, and the praise each side gives the other.
- Explain, from an-Nawawi's commentary, that the market is a gathering place where the people of Jannah come together and that "every Friday" means the span of a week, not an actual week, and give al-Qadi 'Iyad's explanation of why the north wind is singled out.
- Show that the authentic hadith mentions no buying or selling in the market, and that the reports which describe such details were not relied upon in this lesson because they are weak.
- Connect the market of Jannah with the marketplace of this world through the ayat of Friday, "and leave off trade," and the ayat "men whom neither trade nor sale distracts," as Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di explained them.
- Carry out the activity "What Did We Bring Back on Friday?": setting a specific intention for some increase before Friday, then, on returning, sharing with one's family what each side gained, and recording it over four Fridays.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world, the market is a place of give and take: buying and selling and haggling, crowds and fatigue, and very often a place where the heart drifts into heedlessness. That is why it is striking that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us there is a market in Jannah. What is this market? What happens there? And what do its people carry home with them when they return?

This lesson deals with the market of Jannah, the outing to it and the return from it, and then comes back to the marketplace of this world to ask: how can Friday become a day of increase for us? The beauty and qualities of the people of Jannah, their visits to one another, their gatherings, the garden in which they "are made joyful," and the seeing of Allah, exalted is He, each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2833" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "In Jannah There Is a Market"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ لَسُوقًا يَأْتُونَهَا كُلَّ جُمُعَةٍ، فَتَهُبُّ رِيحُ الشَّمَالِ فَتَحْثُو فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ وَثِيَابِهِمْ، فَيَزْدَادُونَ حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، فَيَرْجِعُونَ إِلَى أَهْلِيهِمْ وَقَدِ ازْدَادُوا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، فَيَقُولُ لَهُمْ أَهْلُوهُمْ: وَاللَّهِ لَقَدِ ازْدَدْتُمْ بَعْدَنَا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، فَيَقُولُونَ: وَأَنْتُمْ وَاللَّهِ لَقَدِ ازْدَدْتُمْ بَعْدَنَا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Indeed, in Jannah there is a market that they come to every Friday. The north wind blows and scatters over their faces and their clothes, and they grow in beauty and radiance. Then they go back to their families, having grown in beauty and radiance, and their families say to them, 'By Allah, you have grown in beauty and radiance since you left us!' And they say, 'And you too, by Allah, have grown in beauty and radiance since we left!'"**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi said: "What is meant by the market is a gathering place for them, where they gather just as people gather at the market in this world. And the meaning of 'they come to it every Friday' is: in the measure of every Friday, that is, a week; there is no actual week there, since there is no sun, no night and no day." He also cites al-Qadi 'Iyad as saying that the wind of Jannah is singled out as the north wind "because, for the Arabs, it is the wind of rain," and that in another hadith it is named *al-muthirah*, "the stirrer," "because it stirs up into their faces the musk of the soil of Jannah and the other delights that it stirs up."[^2] In Imam Ahmad's narration of the hadith by way of Hammad ibn Salamah, it adds: **"In it are dunes of musk, and when they go out to it, the wind blows."**[^3] Ibn al-Qayyim, in *Hadi al-Arwah*, devoted a chapter "on the market of Jannah and what Allah, exalted is He, has prepared in it for its people," and opened it with this hadith.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith traces a complete movement: going out to a gathering, then a gift that comes down on them while they are there, then the return home. Nothing is said here about buying, selling or prices; what happens in this market is an increase that Allah grants them. Then comes the loveliest touch of all: those who stayed at home have grown more beautiful too, and each side hurries to praise the other, swearing by Allah as they do. Going out is a joy, coming home is a joy, and no one's happiness shrinks because someone else has received more.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-62-9-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Leave Off Trade"

> **{يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِذَا نُودِيَ لِلصَّلَاةِ مِنْ يَوْمِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَاسْعَوْا إِلَىٰ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَذَرُوا الْبَيْعَ ۚ ذَٰلِكُمْ خَيْرٌ لَكُمْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ ۝ فَإِذَا قُضِيَتِ الصَّلَاةُ فَانْتَشِرُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ وَابْتَغُوا مِنْ فَضْلِ اللَّهِ وَاذْكُرُوا اللَّهَ كَثِيرًا لَعَلَّكُمْ تُفْلِحُونَ ۝ وَإِذَا رَأَوْا تِجَارَةً أَوْ لَهْوًا انْفَضُّوا إِلَيْهَا وَتَرَكُوكَ قَائِمًا ۚ قُلْ مَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ خَيْرٌ مِنَ اللَّهْوِ وَمِنَ التِّجَارَةِ ۚ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرُ الرَّازِقِينَ}** [الجمعة: ٩-١١][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"O you who believe, when the call is made for the prayer on the day of Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade. That is better for you, if only you knew. Then, when the prayer is over, disperse through the land and seek of Allah's bounty, and remember Allah often so that you may succeed. Yet when they saw some trade or amusement, they rushed off to it and left you standing. Say: What is with Allah is better than amusement and than trade, and Allah is the best of providers."** (al-Jumu'ah 62:9-11)[^4]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Ibn Kathir said: "Friday was called *al-Jumu'ah* only because it is derived from *al-jam'*, gathering, for the people of Islam gather on it once every week in the great places of worship." As-Sa'di said of "and leave off trade": "That is, give up selling when the call to prayer is made, and go to it." On the tenth ayah he said: "Since being occupied with trade is a likely cause of heedlessness of the remembrance of Allah, Allah commanded that He be remembered often." On the eleventh ayah he mentioned that a caravan carrying goods arrived in Madinah on a Friday while the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was delivering the sermon, and the people rushed off to it; and he explained "Say: What is with Allah" as meaning "the reward and recompense for whoever holds fast to good and keeps himself patiently in the worship of Allah."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

Friday in this world is a weekly gathering, and the market of Jannah is a gathering "in the measure of every Friday." These ayat do not forbid trade; they actually command us to disperse and seek Allah's bounty after the prayer. What they do is put the market in its proper place: it must not come before the call to prayer, and it must not make us forget the remembrance of Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-24-36-38" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Give Them More from His Bounty"

> **{فِي بُيُوتٍ أَذِنَ اللَّهُ أَنْ تُرْفَعَ وَيُذْكَرَ فِيهَا اسْمُهُ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُ فِيهَا بِالْغُدُوِّ وَالْآصَالِ ۝ رِجَالٌ لَا تُلْهِيهِمْ تِجَارَةٌ وَلَا بَيْعٌ عَنْ ذِكْرِ اللَّهِ وَإِقَامِ الصَّلَاةِ وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ ۙ يَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا تَتَقَلَّبُ فِيهِ الْقُلُوبُ وَالْأَبْصَارُ ۝ لِيَجْزِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ أَحْسَنَ مَا عَمِلُوا وَيَزِيدَهُمْ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ يَرْزُقُ مَنْ يَشَاءُ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ}** [النور: ٣٦-٣٨][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"In houses that Allah has permitted to be raised up, and in which His name is remembered, He is glorified there morning and evening by men whom neither trade nor sale distracts from the remembrance of Allah, the establishing of prayer and the giving of zakah. They fear a Day when hearts and eyes will be turned about, so that Allah may reward them for the best of what they did and give them more from His bounty. And Allah provides for whom He wills without measure."** (an-Nur 24:36-38)[^5]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

As-Sa'di said: "These men, even if they trade and buy and sell, there is nothing wrong in that. But these things do not distract them, in the sense of their putting them first and preferring them over 'the remembrance of Allah, the establishing of prayer and the giving of zakah.'" And of "and give them more from His bounty" he said: "A great increase beyond the reward that corresponds to their deeds." Ibn Kathir mentions from Ibn Mas'ud that he "saw some people of the market who, when the call to prayer was made, left their trading and rose to go to the prayer," and said: these are among those whom Allah mentioned in His Book.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The lesson brings these two texts together as a connection, not as tafsir: whoever does not let the markets of this world distract him from the remembrance of Allah has been promised that Allah will "give them more from His bounty," and in the market of Jannah its people see that increase with their own eyes. They come home having grown in beauty and radiance.

<!-- evidence:end -->

As for the other details of the market, such as the claim that "nothing is bought or sold in it," or that it contains forms which anyone who desires one may enter, these come in reports that the lesson does not rely on because they are weak.[^7] So we affirm what is authentic: a weekly gathering, a wind that scatters over them, an increase in beauty and radiance, and a happy return home to their families. Beyond that, we hold back.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between what happens in the markets of this world and what the authentic hadith tells us about the market of Jannah?
2. Why did an-Nawawi say that "every Friday" means the span of a week rather than an actual week? And what does this kind of precision teach us about how we understand matters of the unseen?
3. What is the significance of the fact that those who stayed at home also grew in beauty and radiance? And what does the praise each side gives the other teach us?
4. How do the ayat of al-Jumu'ah and an-Nur put the market in its proper place without forbidding trade?
5. What did you bring back from last Friday? And what distracted you from it?

### Activity: What Did We Bring Back on Friday?

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.071.activity.what-we-brought-back-on-friday" -->

For four Fridays in a row: before you leave for the Friday prayer, or before its time if you stay at home, write down one specific increase you intend to come back with: a benefit from the sermon that you will put into practice, a form of remembrance, a family tie to strengthen, or an act of charity. When you return, sit with your family for five minutes. The one who went out shares what they brought back, and the one who stayed home shares what they gained while the other was away. Then each side honestly names one increase they noticed in the other, without exaggeration and without comparison. Record all this in a four-column table: the Friday, what I intended, what I brought back, what I noticed in my family. After the fourth Friday, write a paragraph: has Friday become a season of increase in our home? And what trade or amusement had been crowding it out?

### An Authentic Hadith: The Lovely Wind in the Market of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2833" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by Muslim in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2833" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that there is a market in Jannah, and the people of Jannah come to it every Friday.[^1]

When they all gather there, a lovely wind blows over them. It is called the north wind.[^1]

The wind scatters sweetness over their faces and their clothes, and they become even more beautiful than they were before.[^1][^2]

Then they go back to their homes and their families, more beautiful than before.[^1]

And their families say to them, "By Allah, you have become more beautiful since you left us!"[^1]

And they say to their families, "And you too, by Allah, have become more beautiful since we left!"[^1]

How lovely is that! Everyone is happy for everyone else, and says something kind to them. Nobody is sad, and nobody is jealous.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Fridays in Salma's house have a special flavor. Her father and brother go to the mosque, and she stays home with her mom, reading Qur'an and remembering Allah. Last week, her brother wanted to stay behind to finish a game on his tablet. His father told him, "Jumu'ah matters more. The game can wait." When the two of them got back, Salma asked, "What did the khatib say?" Her brother shared a beautiful lesson from the sermon, and she shared what she had read. Everyone felt that the house had become a lovelier place.

Allah says about Friday: "hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade,"[^4] which means: when the call to prayer is made, stop buying and selling and go to it. And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that in Jannah there is a gathering a little like Friday, in a place he called a "market."

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2833" -->

- **`The market of Jannah (suq al-jannah)`** — a gathering place where the people of Jannah come together, the way people gather at the markets of this world.[^2]
- **`Every Friday (kulla jumu'ah)`** — about once every week's worth of time, because Jannah has no sun and no night and day like ours.[^2]
- **`Scatters (tahthu)`** — the wind sprinkles over them the sweet scents and delights of Jannah that it carries.[^2]
- **`Leave off trade (dharu al-bay')`** — stop buying and selling when the call to the Friday prayer is made.[^4]

### An Authentic Hadith: They Come Home More Beautiful

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2833" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by Muslim in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2833" audience="8-12" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Indeed, in Jannah there is a market that they come to every Friday."[^1] The scholars explain that the market here is a gathering place for the people of Jannah, and that "every Friday" means about once a week's worth of time.[^2]

When they have gathered, the north wind blows. The Arabs loved this wind, because it drove the clouds and the rain before it.[^2] The wind scatters over their faces and their clothes, sprinkling them with the sweet scents of Jannah, and they grow in beauty and radiance.[^1][^2]

Then they go back to their families, having grown in beauty and radiance. Their families are the first to speak: "By Allah, you have grown in beauty and radiance since you left us!"[^1]

And they answer: "And you too, by Allah, have grown in beauty and radiance since we left!"[^1] So the ones who stayed at home had grown more beautiful as well!

Notice three things. The hadith says nothing about buying, selling or money. Everyone in the story gained something. And each side was quick to praise the other and be happy for them. Allah says of His servants whom trade does not keep from remembering Him: "and give them more from His bounty."[^5]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Haul videos, weekend flash sales, the shopping cart that fills up and then gets abandoned, aimless laps around the mall. These days you don't have to go out to the market; it lives in your pocket. And so often we come back from it with more stuff but a poorer heart: comparison, regret over what we didn't buy, and time we'll never get back.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that there is a market in Jannah from which its people come home more beautiful than when they set out, and they come home to families who have grown more beautiful too.[^1] The Qur'an does not forbid the marketplace of this world, but it describes the people of faith as "men whom neither trade nor sale distracts from the remembrance of Allah," and promises that He will "give them more from His bounty."[^5] As-Sa'di said: "even if they trade and buy and sell, there is nothing wrong in that," but they do not put it ahead of the remembrance of Allah and the prayer.[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2833" -->

- **`The market of Jannah (suq al-jannah)`** — a gathering place for the people of Jannah, where they come together the way people gather at a market; the authentic hadith mentions no buying or selling in it.[^2]
- **`Every Friday (kulla jumu'ah)`** — the span of a week, since there is no actual week there, with no sun, no night and no day.[^2]
- **`Does not distract them (la tulhihim)`** — does not keep them busy, or lead them to put it ahead of the remembrance of Allah.[^5]

### An Authentic Hadith: What Did They Bring Back from the Market?

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2833" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report narrated by Muslim in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2833" audience="13+" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Indeed, in Jannah there is a market that they come to every Friday."[^1] An-Nawawi said that the market means "a gathering place for them, where they gather just as people gather at the market in this world," and that "every Friday" is the span of a week, "and there is no actual week there, since there is no sun, no night and no day."[^2]

There, the north wind blows. An-Nawawi cites al-Qadi 'Iyad as saying that the Arabs used to long for this wind, because it was the wind that brought the rain.[^2] It scatters over their faces and their clothes, and they grow in beauty and radiance.[^1] In Imam Ahmad's narration, that market has "dunes of musk," and when they go out to it, the wind blows.[^3]

Then they go back to their families. Before the returning ones can say a word, their families get in first, with an oath: "By Allah, you have grown in beauty and radiance since you left us!" And they answer with an oath of their own: "And you too, by Allah, have grown in beauty and radiance since we left!"[^1]

Look at the details the hadith leaves out: no prices, no haggling, no list of what they bought. And look at the details it includes: a gathering, a gift from Allah, a return home, and praise passed both ways with no comparison in it. Those who went out gained, those who stayed gained, and each is delighted by what the other gained.[^1] As for the reports that add further descriptions of the market, such as that nothing is bought or sold in it, the lesson does not rely on them because their chains are weak.[^7]

### Discussion Questions

1. Why was the praise the two sides exchanged in the hadith free of comparison? And how is it different from the "before and after" culture on social media?
2. What do you usually bring back from the "digital marketplace"? And what do you bring back from Jumu'ah?
3. As-Sa'di said there is "nothing wrong" in trade as long as it does not distract from the remembrance of Allah. Where is the line between enjoying shopping and being consumed by it?
4. Why does it matter that we separate what is authentic in the descriptions of the market from what is not?
5. What increase do you want your family to notice in you after next Friday?

### Activity: What Did We Bring Back on Friday?

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.071.activity.what-we-brought-back-on-friday" -->

For four Fridays: before the time of Jumu'ah, write in a private note one specific increase you intend to come back with, from the sermon if you attend, or from an act of worship at home if you stay. During the hour before the prayer, close your shopping and deals apps. After the prayer, ask someone in your family, "What did you bring back?" and answer the question yourself too. Then each of you names one increase you noticed in the other, honestly and without comparison. Keep a table: what I intended, what I brought back, what I noticed in my family, what tried to distract me. On the fourth Friday, write a paragraph: what changed in our home once Friday became a day of increase?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.071.dua.not-distracted-gather-us-in-the-market-of-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ لَا تَجْعَلْ شَيْئًا يُلْهِينَا عَنْ ذِكْرِكَ، وَاجْمَعْنَا فِي سُوقِ الْجَنَّةِ، نَزْدَادُ فِيهَا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، وَنَرْجِعُ إِلَى أَهْلِينَا فَرِحِينَ.
>
> *"O Allah, let nothing distract us from remembering You. Gather us in the market of Jannah, where we grow in beauty and radiance, and let us come home to our families full of joy."*

What it means: O Allah, let no trade, no amusement and no screen keep us from remembering You. Gather us in the market of Jannah, where we grow in beauty and radiance by Your bounty, and let us come home to our families full of joy, each of us glad for what the other has gained.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates Muslim 2833 in its proper sequence; explains the meaning of the market, of "every Friday" and of the north wind from the words of an-Nawawi and al-Qadi 'Iyad; mentions the addition in Ahmad's narration, "in it are dunes of musk"; explains al-Jumu'ah 62:9-11 and an-Nur 24:36-38 from Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; distinguishes what is authentic in the descriptions of the market from what the lesson does not rely on; and begins the activity "What Did We Bring Back on Friday?"

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with two columns, "The market of this world" and "The market of Jannah as in the hadith"; the four-column activity table; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 2833, the sixtieth chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* together with the editor's footnotes, and the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Jumu'ah 62:9-11 and an-Nur 24:36-38. The teacher keeps in mind that the beauty of the people of Jannah, their visits to one another, their gatherings, the garden, and the seeing of Allah each have their own lesson, and does not expand on them.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Describe the last time you came back from the market. What did you bring back? And how did you feel?" Write the answers in the "The market of this world" column.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Muslim in full twice, then map its sequence on the board: going out, the wind, the increase, the return, the mutual praise (5 minutes). Read an-Nawawi's commentary and what he cites from al-Qadi 'Iyad, then Ahmad's narration, pointing out that "every Friday" is a measure of time, not an actual week (5 minutes). Recite the ayat of al-Jumu'ah and read their tafsir (5 minutes), then the ayat of an-Nur and their tafsir (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 3 and 4. The teacher points out that the hadith mentions no buying or selling, that what has been reported about such things is not established, and that connecting "and give them more from His bounty" with the increase in beauty in the market is an educational connection made by the lesson, not tafsir of the ayah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner draws up their table and writes their intention for next Friday (5 minutes), agrees with their family on a time for the short sitting after the prayer (5 minutes), and practices with a partner how to give sincere praise without exaggeration (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name three things that are authentic in the description of the market of Jannah, and one thing the lesson does not rely on, and why." Then close with the question: what will you intend for next Friday?

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith and the ayah "and leave off trade" are enough. Advanced learners are asked to trace the reports in the sixtieth chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* and the editor's judgments on them, and to explain which of them are attributed to the Prophet (*marfu'*) and which stop at a Companion (*mawquf*).

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Safeguards:** Do not describe the market with anything that is not established, and do not rely on the reports of the "forms" or on the narration of Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab from Abu Hurayrah.[^7] Do not digress into the seeing of Allah, exalted is He, or visiting Him; that has its own lesson, which presents what is authentic. Be considerate of anyone for whom Friday prayer is not obligatory or who cannot attend it: their part in the activity is an act of worship at home. Do not let the mutual praise become empty flattery.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child tells how the people of Jannah go to its market every Friday, a lovely wind blows and they become more beautiful, and they go home to their families and each says something kind to the other; says a kind word to someone in their family on Friday; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small hand fan, or a sheet of paper for fanning; a piece of cloth with a light scent on it; a sheet of paper with an open door drawn on it and no writing; star stickers; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the report without adding anything, makes sure the scent contains nothing that could cause an allergy, and sends families a short message about the Friday activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Who has been to the market? What did you see there?" and listen.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation, then the report. When the wind is mentioned, gently fan the children with the scented cloth; then pause at the families' words and ask, "What do you think the ones coming home will say?" and then carry on.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 2, 4 and 5, and teach the children the sentence "You came back more beautiful!" to say to one another.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child colors the open door, chooses the good thing they will do on Friday, and takes the sheet home to stick the stars on.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask: "What do the people of Jannah say to each other when they come home?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, coloring the door and saying the kind sentence are enough. Older children are asked to tell the report to their family in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Safeguards:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, nor any image of Allah, exalted is He, and do not draw the people of Jannah with specific faces. If a child asks, "What do they buy at the market?" answer: "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, didn't tell us that they buy anything. He told us that they gather together and become more beautiful." Do not compare the children's looks, and do not comment on their appearance.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the hadith in its sequence; explains "the market of Jannah," "every Friday," "scatters" and "leave off trade"; compares the market of Jannah as the hadith describes it with the markets of this world; and starts a Friday Notebook.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a small notebook or folded sheet for each student; a mushaf; a board with a two-column comparison table.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes al-Jumu'ah 62:9 on the board, prepares a sample Friday Notebook, and sends families a note about the activity with a suggestion for a short sitting after the Friday prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read Salma's story and ask: "What would her brother have missed if he had stayed home playing?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Jumu'ah and its explanation (3 minutes), then the story, pausing before the reply of those who came home: "What do you expect them to say?" (6 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Students fill in the table comparing the two markets, and questions 2, 3 and 5 are discussed. The teacher makes clear that buying and selling are permitted, and that what is blameworthy is letting them keep us from the prayer and the remembrance of Allah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student makes a Friday Notebook with its three columns, writes their intention for next Friday, and practices with a partner asking and answering "What did you bring back?"

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes the sentence: "The market of Jannah is different from the markets of this world because ..." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, two columns are enough: "What I intended" and "What I brought back." Advanced students are asked to memorize the hadith, or to write a paragraph about the ayah "men whom neither trade nor sale distracts."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Safeguards:** Do not let girls or younger children feel left out of the activity because they do not attend Jumu'ah; the hadith itself mentions that the ones who stayed at home also gained. Do not show students' notebooks to the class. Do not add anything to the description of the market that is not in the hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes what the hadith does and does not say in its description of the market; explains an-Nawawi's words on the meaning of the market and of "every Friday"; applies the ayah "neither trade nor sale distracts them" to digital shopping habits; distinguishes the authentic reports from those that are not; and begins a personal Friday table.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that will not be collected; three cards with imagined scenarios about shopping habits (scrolling through deals before bed, buying things to keep up with friends, delaying the prayer to finish placing an order).

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the three scenarios so that none of them resembles any particular student, and reads an-Nawawi's commentary and the sixtieth chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* with its footnotes.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "How many times this week did you open a shopping app without needing anything? Don't answer out loud." Then read the explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at three moments: the wind, the return, and the mutual praise. Students then write two lists: what the hadith mentions and what it does not. Then read the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Groups analyze the three scenarios in light of questions 2 and 3, and then the class discusses question 4. The teacher points out that permissible enjoyment is not blameworthy, and that the standard is the one as-Sa'di gave: that trade is not put ahead of the remembrance of Allah and the prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student draws up their personal table, writes their intention for next Friday, and decides which app they will close in the hour before the prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes the sentence: "After Friday, I want my family to notice in me ..." Then read the du'a. After four Fridays, the teacher asks a general question about how the experience went.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private audio recording instead. Advanced students are asked to do a short research piece on the reports about the market of Jannah in at-Tirmidhi and *Hadi al-Arwah*, explaining why they were not relied upon.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Safeguards:** Do not reveal the private notes. Do not turn the lesson into commentary on students' looks, clothes or possessions. Be considerate of a student living through difficult family circumstances: the "family" in the activity can be whichever relative or trusted friend they choose. If a student discloses compulsive spending, debt or peer pressure, the teacher follows up privately and involves the family or the counselor in line with the safeguarding policy.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
