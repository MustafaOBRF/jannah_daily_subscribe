# The Form, Age, and Beauty of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of Abu Hurayrah in the two Sahihs (al-Bukhari 3326 and Muslim 2841): Allah created Adam sixty cubits tall, everyone who enters Jannah will be in the form of Adam, and human stature has kept diminishing until now.
- Explain, from al-'Ayni and what he relays from Ibn at-Tin and al-Qurtubi, what "in the form of Adam" means and how long the cubit is, and recognize that converting the height into meters is the lesson's own approximation.
- Cite the hadith of the market of Jannah (Muslim 2833) and show that the beauty of the people of Jannah keeps increasing, that the increase reaches them and their families alike, and that they praise one another without rivalry or envy.
- Cite the hadith of Mu'adh on the age of the people of Jannah (at-Tirmidhi 2545), state how the scholars graded it and the disagreement over its chain, and explain its wording.
- Connect all of this to the hadith "Allah does not look at your forms and your wealth" (Muslim 2564), and so understand that beauty there is a gift from Allah, not an arena for comparison, and that the measure in this world is the heart and its deeds.
- Carry out the "Mirror of Gifts and the Polished Heart" activity: thanking Allah for what the body does, choosing one quality of the heart to polish for a set period, and then exchanging with family members what each has seen grow more beautiful in the other.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world people are deeply preoccupied with how they look: whether they are tall or short, what age shows on their faces, and what picture of them others carry away. This lesson brings the report of the Truthful One, peace and blessings be upon him, about the form of the people of Jannah: in what form they enter it, at what age, and how their beauty grows once they are inside.

The lesson stays within what the authentic texts state and describes no feature for which no report has come. The strength of the people of Jannah and the purity of their bodies are treated in a separate lesson, and the market of Jannah itself in another; the market hadith appears here only for what it says about growing in beauty and loveliness.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3326" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Everyone Who Enters Jannah Will Be in the Form of Adam"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«خَلَقَ اللَّهُ آدَمَ وَطُولُهُ سِتُّونَ ذِرَاعًا، ثُمَّ قَالَ: اذْهَبْ فَسَلِّمْ عَلَى أُولَئِكَ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ، فَاسْتَمِعْ مَا يُحَيُّونَكَ، تَحِيَّتُكَ وَتَحِيَّةُ ذُرِّيَّتِكَ. فَقَالَ: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ، فَقَالُوا: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ، فَزَادُوهُ: وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ. فَكُلُّ مَنْ يَدْخُلُ الْجَنَّةَ عَلَى صُورَةِ آدَمَ، فَلَمْ يَزَلِ الْخَلْقُ يَنْقُصُ حَتَّى الْآنَ»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Allah created Adam, and his height was sixty cubits. Then He said: 'Go and greet that company of angels, and listen to how they return your greeting, for it will be your greeting and the greeting of your descendants.' So he said, 'Peace be upon you (as-salamu 'alaykum).' They said, 'Peace be upon you, and the mercy of Allah (as-salamu 'alayka wa rahmatullah).' So they added for him: 'and the mercy of Allah.' Everyone who enters Jannah will be in the form of Adam, and humankind has kept diminishing ever since, until now."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Explaining the words "Everyone who enters Jannah will be in the form of Adam," al-'Ayni says: "That is, everyone whom Allah the Exalted grants entry into Jannah will enter it in the form of Adam in comeliness and beauty," and he notes that no one will enter it with the defects and deficiencies he had in this world (summarized). On "humankind has kept diminishing," he says: "That is, in height. He meant that each generation is shorter in stature than the generation before it, until the diminishing of height ended with this ummah, and the matter settled there." He relays from Ibn at-Tin that the cubit here is the cubit we know, adding, "and the first view is better known," and he relays from al-Qurtubi: "Allah the Exalted will return the people of Jannah to the frame of their origin, who is Adam."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith describes Adam's creation and his greeting, then closes with news that concerns his descendants: whoever of them enters Jannah is returned to the first, complete form of their father. A cubit is roughly half a meter, which makes sixty cubits about thirty meters (some hundred feet); this is the lesson's own approximation to help us picture it, not a figure handed down in the sources. What matters most is that this form is a gift given to everyone who enters, not the reward of effort spent on the body, and not a rank some attain while others miss it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2833" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "You Have Grown More Beautiful and Lovely Since You Left Us"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ لَسُوقًا يَأْتُونَهَا كُلَّ جُمُعَةٍ، فَتَهُبُّ رِيحُ الشَّمَالِ فَتَحْثُو فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ وَثِيَابِهِمْ، فَيَزْدَادُونَ حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، فَيَرْجِعُونَ إِلَى أَهْلِيهِمْ وَقَدِ ازْدَادُوا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، فَيَقُولُ لَهُمْ أَهْلُوهُمْ: وَاللَّهِ لَقَدِ ازْدَدْتُمْ بَعْدَنَا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا، فَيَقُولُونَ: وَأَنْتُمْ وَاللَّهِ لَقَدِ ازْدَدْتُمْ بَعْدَنَا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"In Jannah there is a market to which they come every Friday. The north wind blows and scatters over their faces and their clothes, and they grow in beauty and loveliness. Then they go back to their families, having grown in beauty and loveliness, and their families say to them, 'By Allah, you have grown in beauty and loveliness since you left us!' And they reply, 'And you too, by Allah, have grown in beauty and loveliness since we left!'"**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi said: "What is meant by the market is a gathering place for them, where they come together just as people in this world gather at a market. 'They come to it every Friday' means at intervals the length of a Friday, that is, a week; there is no actual week there, since the sun, the night, and the day are absent."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The beauty of the people of Jannah is not a fixed amount received once and for all; it keeps increasing. Notice that the increase reached both sides, those who went to the market and those who stayed at home, and each hurried to praise the other, and swore by Allah to it. No one grows at anyone else's expense, and beauty never turns into a race or a source of envy; it becomes a joy that is shared.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2545" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Aged Thirty or Thirty-Three Years"

> عَنْ مُعَاذِ بْنِ جَبَلٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«يَدْخُلُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ الْجَنَّةَ جُرْدًا مُرْدًا مُكَحَّلِينَ، أَبْنَاءَ ثَلَاثِينَ أَوْ ثَلَاثٍ وَثَلَاثِينَ سَنَةً»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Mu'adh ibn Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The people of Jannah will enter Jannah free of body hair, beardless, their eyes as if lined with kohl, aged thirty or thirty-three years."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Mubarakfuri explains that "free of body hair, beardless, their eyes as if lined with kohl" describes them "by natural make," that the word "or" reflects "doubt on the part of the narrator," and that other reports state "aged thirty-three" without hesitation. Ad-Dihlawi says: "As the people of this world are at that age, since it is the age of a person's full strength and maturity."[^6] And Ibn al-Qayyim, reconciling the mention of thirty with thirty-three, says: "When the Arabs give an estimate with a number that has a few units over it, they have two ways: sometimes they mention the extra units for precision, and sometimes they drop them."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

At-Tirmidhi, al-Albani, and others graded this hadith *hasan* (good), while some scholars who examined its chain judged the chain weak. The lesson therefore cites it together with an account of its status and builds nothing on it beyond its general meaning: the people of Jannah are in the fullness of youth and strength, with neither the frailty of childhood nor the weakness of old age.[^5]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2564c" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "But He Looks at Your Hearts and Your Deeds"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَنْظُرُ إِلَى صُوَرِكُمْ وَأَمْوَالِكُمْ، وَلَكِنْ يَنْظُرُ إِلَى قُلُوبِكُمْ وَأَعْمَالِكُمْ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Allah does not look at your forms and your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Mulla 'Ali al-Qari explains "does not look" as meaning "a look of regard," "at your forms: since no account is taken of their beauty or their ugliness."[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith keeps everything before it in proportion. The beautiful form of Jannah is a reward that is given, whereas what a person is held to account for in this world is the heart and its deeds. Whoever neglects the heart for the sake of appearance has turned the scales upside down, and whoever mocks another for a body he never chose has found fault with something Allah does not even weigh.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### Activity: The Mirror of Gifts and the Polished Heart

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.062.activity.gift-mirror-and-heart-polish" -->

Head a first column "Mirror of Gifts" and list five blessings in your body, describing each by what it does rather than how it looks (for example, "eyes I read with," "hands I serve with"), and praise Allah for each one. Head a second column "Polishing the Heart," choose a quality of the heart or a deed you want to grow more beautiful, such as patience, truthfulness, or giving up backbiting, and check it off every evening for seven days. In the margin, note every time you commented on someone's appearance, even as a compliment, and turn the comment into praise for a quality of character or a deed. On the seventh day, ask someone in your family, "What have you seen grow more beautiful in me this week?" and tell them what you have seen grow in them.

### An Authentic Hadith: Our Very Tall Father Adam

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3326" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3326" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Allah created our father Adam, peace be upon him, and that he was sixty cubits tall.[^1] That is very, very tall! Taller than lots of houses, and taller than lots of trees.

Then Allah said to Adam: Go and say salam to those angels, and listen to what they say back to you. That will be your greeting, and the greeting of your children after you.[^1]

So Adam went and said, "As-salamu 'alaykum: peace be upon you." And the angels answered, "As-salamu 'alayka wa rahmatullah: peace be upon you, and the mercy of Allah." They gave him back something extra and lovely: "and the mercy of Allah."[^1]

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us some happy news: everyone who goes into Jannah will look like our father Adam, tall and beautiful and complete.[^1] But people in this world grew shorter, and shorter, and shorter, until they were as tall as we are today.

So if Allah is going to give us a beautiful look in Jannah, what do we do here and now? We make our hearts beautiful with love, and we make our words beautiful with salam.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In class, the students lined up to have their heights measured. One kid laughed at a shorter classmate. Another announced, "I'm the tallest!" A third said nothing at all, his face turning red. Our height, our looks, and our features are gifts from Allah that none of us picked for ourselves. So why would we make them a reason to show off, or to make fun of someone?

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah will enter it in the form of their father Adam, and that their beauty keeps growing there. He also told us that in this world Allah does not look at our appearance, but at our hearts and our deeds.[^8]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-3326" -->

- **`Cubit (dhira')`** — the length from the elbow to the fingertips, roughly half a meter (about a foot and a half).[^2]
- **`In the form of Adam ('ala surat Adam)`** — with his looks, his beauty, and his height, just as Allah first created him.[^2]
- **`Diminishing (yanqus)`** — people's height getting shorter generation after generation, until it settled where we know it today.[^2]

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: In the Form of Our Father Adam

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3326" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3326" audience="8-12" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke about how human beings began: Allah created Adam, peace be upon him, and he was sixty cubits tall.[^1] A cubit is about half a meter, so that makes him around thirty meters tall, roughly the height of a ten-story building. That is an estimate to help us picture it.

Then Allah told him to go to a group of angels, greet them with salam, and listen to their reply, because their greeting would become his greeting and the greeting of his descendants. So Adam said, "As-salamu 'alaykum," and they replied, "As-salamu 'alayka wa rahmatullah." They had added "and the mercy of Allah" for him.[^1]

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, ended with news about us, the children of Adam: "Everyone who enters Jannah will be in the form of Adam, and humankind has kept diminishing ever since, until now."[^1] In other words, people grew shorter generation after generation, and when a believer enters Jannah, Allah gives him back the first, complete form of his father.

The scholars explain that the believer enters Jannah in the form of Adam in looks and beauty, with none of the flaws or shortcomings he had in this world left on him.[^2]

Another authentic hadith says that the people of Jannah go to a market there, where a wind blows over them and they grow in beauty and loveliness. When they go back to their families, the families have grown more beautiful too, so each says to the other, "You have grown in beauty and loveliness since you left us!"[^3]

So beauty there is a gift for everyone, and each person is happy for themselves and for everyone else. Here in this world, we get ready for it with the things Allah looks at: the heart and our deeds.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A photo gets posted after the lighting is tweaked, then a filter reshapes the face, then the likes start counting up. And underneath it all runs a silent question: Am I good-looking enough? So much of the pressure around you turns appearance into a race: who is tallest, who is thinnest, whose face looks youngest.

The texts this lesson studies flip that race on its head. The people of Jannah enter it in the form of their father Adam, in the prime of youth; their beauty grows and never fades; and the increase reaches all of them, so no one pulls ahead of anyone else. In this world, meanwhile, Allah "does not look at your forms and your wealth, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds."[^8]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="tirmidhi-2545" -->

- **`Free of body hair (jurdan)`** — plural of *ajrad*, one whose body has no hair on it.[^6]
- **`Beardless (murdan)`** — plural of *amrad*, one who has no beard.[^6]
- **`As if lined with kohl (mukahhalin)`** — their eyes look as though darkened with kohl by nature, not because anyone applied it.[^6]

### An Authentic Hadith: The Original Form

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-3326" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true report recorded by al-Bukhari and Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3326" audience="13+" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrates a hadith that begins at humanity's very first moment: Allah created Adam, and he was sixty cubits tall.[^1] The first human being, created whole and complete in a single act.

Then came the first social lesson in human history: Go and greet that company of angels, and listen to how they greet you back, for it will be your greeting and the greeting of your descendants. Adam gave the salam, and they answered him with something fuller than what he had said: "As-salamu 'alayka wa rahmatullah: peace be upon you, and the mercy of Allah."[^1] The very first meeting between Adam and anyone else was built on peace and honor, not on comparison.

Then the hadith jumps to the end of the road: "Everyone who enters Jannah will be in the form of Adam, and humankind has kept diminishing ever since, until now."[^1] Human height, in other words, shrank generation after generation, and then Allah returns whoever enters Jannah to that original form in comeliness and beauty.[^2] Everyone is restored to one and the same complete form, so bragging about height or features loses all meaning.

In the hadith of Mu'adh, which at-Tirmidhi and al-Albani graded *hasan* while some scholars who examined its chain judged the chain weak, the people of Jannah enter it "aged thirty or thirty-three years,"[^5] that is, at the age of full strength.[^6]

Then comes the authentic hadith of the market: they grow in beauty and loveliness, and go back to their families, who have grown more beautiful as well, and they trade compliments under oath: "By Allah, you have grown in beauty and loveliness since you left us!"[^3]

Compare that with the world around you. Here, beauty is usually measured by comparison: when one person rises, someone else feels smaller. There, beauty is a gift that lifts everyone together, and each person is glad at what has increased in someone else. And the training for that starts now: learning to see beauty in hearts and deeds, and to praise others without envy.

<!-- retelling:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What does it mean to you that everyone in Jannah is restored to the complete form of Adam?

2. Why does the lesson cite the hadith on the age of the people of Jannah together with the disagreement over its chain?

3. What is the difference between beauty measured by comparison and beauty that grows in everyone together?

4. How do filters and like-counts shape the way you see yourself? Answer without naming anyone.

5. Which quality of the heart do you want Allah to see growing more beautiful in you?

### Activity: The Mirror of Gifts and the Polished Heart

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.062.activity.gift-mirror-and-heart-polish" -->

Keep a private note that you do not post anywhere. First, write your "Mirror of Gifts": five blessings in your body, described by what they do rather than how they look, and praise Allah for them. Then, for "Polishing the Heart," choose one quality you want to grow more beautiful over seven days, such as truthfulness, forgiveness, or guarding your tongue, and check it off every evening. Each day, also record how many times you compared your appearance with someone else's, in front of a mirror or a screen, without blaming yourself: the goal is simply to notice. On the seventh day, write a paragraph: What did you notice? Then tell a friend or relative something you have seen growing more beautiful in their character or their actions.

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.062.dua.purify-hearts-increase-in-beauty" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ لَكَ الْحَمْدُ عَلَى مَا خَلَقْتَنَا، فَطَهِّرْ قُلُوبَنَا وَزَيِّنْ أَعْمَالَنَا، وَاجْمَعْنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ نَزْدَادُ فِيهَا حُسْنًا وَجَمَالًا.
>
> *"O Allah, all praise is Yours for the way You created us. So purify our hearts, adorn our deeds, and gather us in Your Jannah, growing there in beauty and loveliness."*

What it means: O Allah, all praise is Yours for the way You made us, so let us neither look down on what You have given us nor envy others for what You have given them. Purify our hearts, for they are where Your gaze falls; make our deeds beautiful; and gather us with those we love in Your Jannah, where we keep growing in beauty and loveliness.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates al-Bukhari 3326 and explains "in the form of Adam" and "humankind has kept diminishing" from al-'Ayni's commentary; explains the hadith of the market (Muslim 2833) and the exchange of praise in it; states the status of at-Tirmidhi 2545 and the disagreement over it; connects all of this to Muslim 2564 on Allah looking at hearts and deeds; and begins the seven-day "Mirror of Gifts and the Polished Heart" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a board with two columns headed "A gift that is given" and "A deed we are held to account for"; a two-column activity sheet; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-'Ayni's commentary on the hadith in *'Umdat al-Qari*, an-Nawawi's commentary on the hadith of the market, *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi* and *Lama'at at-Tanqih* on the hadith of Mu'adh, and the thirty-ninth chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* together with its editor's footnotes. The teacher keeps in mind that the strength of the people of Jannah, the purity of their bodies, and the market of Jannah each have their own lessons, and that the lesson does not rely on the report "seven cubits wide" or the report "with the beauty of Yusuf."[^10]

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "How many minutes a day do people spend attending to their appearance? And how many on setting their hearts right?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Adam in full with al-'Ayni's commentary, and point out that the conversion into meters is an approximation (7 minutes). Then the hadith of the market with an-Nawawi's commentary (5 minutes). Then the hadith of Mu'adh, with an account of its grading, the disagreement over it, and Ibn al-Qayyim's remark on the number (4 minutes). Then Muslim 2564 with al-Qari's explanation (4 minutes). Fill in the two columns on the board as you go.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 3, 4, and 5. Steer the group toward three points: the form of Jannah is a gift to everyone who enters; the increase in the market hadith is mutual; and the measure in this world is the heart and its deeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes the "Mirror of Gifts" column (5 minutes), chooses a "Polishing the Heart" quality and sets a time for the evening check-in (5 minutes), and arranges with a relative to ask the seventh-day question (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two hadiths from the lesson and state the grading of each, and explain in one sentence why beauty in Jannah is not an arena for competition." Close with the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, limit the session to the hadith of Adam, the hadith of the market, and Muslim 2564. Ask advanced learners to trace the routes of the hadith of Mu'adh in the footnotes of the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah*, and to compare the gradings of at-Tirmidhi, al-Albani, Ahmad Shakir, and Bashshar 'Awwad with the editor's verdict.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Add nothing to the description of the features of the people of Jannah beyond the texts, and do not present weak reports as established.[^10] Do not use the lesson to comment on the bodies, weights, or ages of anyone present. Be mindful of those living with illness, disability, or old age: present the report as glad tidings that every pain will be gone, never as a reproach for their condition in this world. Do not go into the rulings concerning women and spouses in Jannah; they have their own lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Adam, peace be upon him, was sixty cubits tall and that the people of Jannah will be in his form; says Adam's greeting and the angels' reply; thanks Allah for one part of the body by naming what it does; makes a two-sided paper mirror; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A measuring tape or a long rope; large paper circles; crayons; star stickers; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher practices retelling the report without adding to it, prepares a sample paper mirror, and measures his or her own forearm in advance to show the children.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Hold out your forearm and say, "This is one cubit." Then ask, "How many cubits tall do you think our father Adam was?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation, then the report, pausing at the greeting so the children can repeat it: "As-salamu 'alaykum," "As-salamu 'alayka wa rahmatullah." If possible, stretch the rope out across the yard so they can picture the height.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 3, 4, and 5, and stress: "Everyone in Jannah is beautiful, and nobody shows off to anybody."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child draws himself or herself on the front, thanks Allah for their eyes and hands, draws a heart on the back, and takes star stickers home to finish there.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask, "Who will the people of Jannah look like?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the drawing and the sentence of praise are enough. Ask older children to retell the report to their family in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not measure the children's heights in front of their classmates or compare them with one another, and make no comment on any child's appearance. Do not draw Adam, peace be upon him, or the angels, or any image of Allah the Exalted, and do not act out the scene as role-play. If a child with a disability or illness asks, answer: "In Jannah there is no pain and no tiredness, and Allah gives the people there the most beautiful form."

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith of Adam in sequence; explains "cubit," "in the form of Adam," and "diminishing"; cites the hadith of the market and the exchange of praise in it; explains why mocking someone's natural appearance is not allowed; and begins the five-day mirror activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; the two-half mirror sheet; a picture of a ten-story building with no writing on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the hadith on the board and prepares a completed sample of both halves of the mirror.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the height-measuring scene from the explanation and ask, "What would you have done if you were in that class?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story with the picture of the building to help picture the height (6 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes), then the paragraph on the hadith of the market (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 3, 4, and 5. Explain that a person's natural appearance is a gift its owner never chose, and that praise for character lasts longer than praise for looks.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student writes the "Mirror of Gifts" half, chooses a "Polishing the Heart" quality, and decides when they will color in their squares.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "In Jannah everyone grows more beautiful, so I…" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who are struggling a ready-made list of qualities and blessings to choose from. Ask advanced students to memorize the hadith, or to write a paragraph on the difference between praising someone's looks and praising their deeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not measure anyone's actual height in class, and use no nicknames that refer to appearance. Gently follow up with any student who seems hurt by being mocked for their looks. Do not draw Adam, peace be upon him, or the angels.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the hadith of Adam and the hadith of the market as a picture of beauty that does not rest on comparison; explains the wording of the hadith of Mu'adh and states its status; applies Muslim 2564 to the culture of photos and filters; and begins a seven-day private log.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; a written description of a filter-edited post (without showing any real images).

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-'Ayni's and an-Nawawi's commentaries and *Tuhfat al-Ahwadhi*, and writes a description of a typical post and the editing done to it.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask, "What makes a person edit a photo before posting it?" Listen without judgment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at three moments: the complete creation, the greeting, and the return to the original form. Then the hadith of Mu'adh with its grading, then the hadith of the market.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** In groups, analyze the description of the post in light of questions 3 and 4, then have students answer question 5 in private writing. Guide them to see that moderate care for one's appearance is legitimate; what is blameworthy is letting looks become the measure of a person's worth.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes the "Mirror of Gifts," chooses a "Polishing the Heart" quality, and adopts the rule of noticing without self-blame.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "How does the news about Jannah change the way I see my own image and other people's?" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about how the experiment went.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private audio recording instead. Ask advanced students to research the report "seven cubits wide" briefly and why the lesson does not rely on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Show no images of real people, and never expose anyone's private notes. Be mindful of students who struggle with eating disorders or body-image anxiety: do not link the lesson to weight or dieting, and refer any worrying case to the appropriate counselor. Do not go into descriptions relating to spouses or the hur; they have their own lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
