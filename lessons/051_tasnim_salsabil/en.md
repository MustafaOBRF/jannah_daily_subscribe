# Tasnim, Salsabil, and the Sealed Nectar

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite Allah's words "They will be given to drink of a pure, sealed nectar, whose seal is musk. So for this let all who would compete, compete. And its blend is of Tasnim, a spring from which those brought near will drink" (al-Mutaffifin 83:25-28), along with the two ayat of camphor (al-Insan 76:5-6) and the two ayat of ginger and Salsabil (al-Insan 76:17-18).
- Name the drinks and springs of Jannah that the Qur'an mentions (the sealed nectar, Tasnim, camphor, ginger, and Salsabil) and explain the meaning of each name using the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di and what they report from the early generations.
- Explain that Tasnim is the highest drink of Jannah, which those brought near drink pure and which is blended for the Companions of the Right, and that rank in this drink follows being first to do good, as Allah says: "And the forerunners, the forerunners: those are the ones brought near" (al-Waqi'ah 56:10-11).
- Narrate the account of Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, when he set out to outdo Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, in charity (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3675, Sunan Abi Dawud 1678), and draw from it the etiquette of competing in good: racing ahead without envy, and acknowledging the merit of the one who got there first.
- Carry out the "Race to the Spring Without Envy" activity: choosing a good deed with a partner, tracking it for a set period, asking whoever is ahead what helped them, and then making du'a for them.
- Learn by heart the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for nearness to Him, for a drink from Tasnim and the sealed nectar, and for the ability to race toward good while loving it for others.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world, drinks have names that tell people where they come from, what they taste like, and how highly they are prized, and some are served only to the guest of honor. The Qur'an, too, names the drinks and springs of Jannah one by one: the sealed nectar, Tasnim, camphor, ginger, and Salsabil. Yet it never mentions them simply to describe pleasure. Each time, they are tied to the kinds of people who will drink them: "the righteous" (*al-abrar*) and "those brought near" (*al-muqarrabun*).

Three truths emerge from the texts of this lesson:

1. **Known names for unseen realities:** We believe in what Allah has named and described, we understand the meanings of the words, and we do not strain to picture how they are.
2. **The drink has ranks:** Tasnim is the highest drink of Jannah. Those brought near drink it pure, and for the rest of the people of Jannah it is blended.
3. **The way to it is a race in this world:** The command to compete comes right in the middle of the description of the nectar itself, and those brought near are the ones who were first to do good.

The rivers and springs of Jannah in general were covered in an earlier lesson; the four rivers have a lesson of their own, and the wine and drinks of Jannah will be the subject of a later lesson.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-83-22-28" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "A Sealed Nectar Whose Blend Is of Tasnim"

> **{إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ لَفِي نَعِيمٍ ۝ عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ يَنْظُرُونَ ۝ تَعْرِفُ فِي وُجُوهِهِمْ نَضْرَةَ النَّعِيمِ ۝ يُسْقَوْنَ مِنْ رَحِيقٍ مَخْتُومٍ ۝ خِتَامُهُ مِسْكٌ ۚ وَفِي ذَٰلِكَ فَلْيَتَنَافَسِ الْمُتَنَافِسُونَ ۝ وَمِزَاجُهُ مِنْ تَسْنِيمٍ ۝ عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا الْمُقَرَّبُونَ}** [المطففين: ٢٢-٢٨][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Surely the righteous will be in bliss, on raised couches, gazing. You will recognize in their faces the radiance of bliss. They will be given to drink of a pure, sealed nectar, whose seal is musk. So for this let all who would compete, compete. And its blend is of Tasnim: a spring from which those brought near will drink."** (al-Mutaffifin 83:22-28)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said, "*Al-rahiq* is one of the names of wine," and he reports this from Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and others. On "whose seal is musk" (*khitamuhu misk*) he reports from Ibn Mas'ud, "Its blend is musk," and from Ibrahim and al-Hasan, "Its finish is musk." On "So for this let all who would compete, compete," he said, "Let those who race for such things take pride in it, seek more of it, and race toward its like." Of Tasnim he said, "It is the noblest and highest drink of the people of Jannah," and on "a spring from which those brought near will drink": "Those brought near drink it pure, and it is blended for the Companions of the Right." He attributes this to Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Masruq and Qatadah.[^2]

As-Sa'di mentions two possible meanings of the "seal." Either the drink is "sealed against anything entering it that would lessen its delight or spoil its taste," and its seal is musk; or whatever is left at the bottom of the cup, the dregs usually poured away in this world, will be musk in Jannah. On competing, he said: "That is, let them race one another in hastening toward it through the deeds that lead to it." And of Tasnim he said: "It is the highest drink of Jannah without exception, and that is why it is pure for those brought near, who are the highest of creation in rank, and blended for the Companions of the Right."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

Notice the order: first the nectar, then the command to compete, then Tasnim, the drink of those brought near. The call to the race comes immediately before the mention of the highest drink of all. The lesson on thrones and couches paused on the scene of the gathering in this same passage; here, we pause on the drink.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "A Cup Whose Blend Is Camphor"

> **{إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِنْ كَأْسٍ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا ۝ عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا عِبَادُ اللَّهِ يُفَجِّرُونَهَا تَفْجِيرًا}** [الإنسان: ٥-٦][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Surely the righteous will drink from a cup whose blend is camphor: a spring from which the servants of Allah drink, making it gush forth in abundance."** (al-Insan 76:5-6)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said, "The coolness and pleasant fragrance of camphor are well known, together with the delight that is added to that in Jannah," and he reports from al-Hasan: "The coolness of camphor in the fragrance of ginger."[^5] As-Sa'di said that it is blended with camphor "to cool it and soften its sharpness," and that the camphor of Jannah "is free of every impurity and every spoiling element found in the camphor of this world."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world camphor is known for its coolness and its scent. So a familiar name was borrowed for a delight whose true nature we do not know, while every flaw that clings to it here is denied.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-17-18" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "A Spring There Called Salsabil"

> **{وَيُسْقَوْنَ فِيهَا كَأْسًا كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا زَنْجَبِيلًا ۝ عَيْنًا فِيهَا تُسَمَّىٰ سَلْسَبِيلًا}** [الإنسان: ١٧-١٨][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And there they will be given to drink a cup whose blend is ginger: a spring there called Salsabil."** (al-Insan 76:17-18)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said, "Sometimes their drink is blended with camphor, which is cool, and sometimes with ginger, which is warm, so that it is perfectly balanced," and then, "As for those brought near, they drink of each of them pure, as Qatadah and others said." On Salsabil he said, "That is, the ginger is a spring in Jannah called Salsabil," and he reports from Mujahid: "It was given that name for the smoothness of its flow and the swiftness of its current." He mentions that Ibn Jarir held that the name covers all of these meanings.[^5] As-Sa'di said: "It was given that name for its smoothness, its delight and its beauty."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The same meaning recurs across the two surahs: the righteous have a fine, blended drink, and those brought near have it pure. The people of Jannah differ in rank, and every one of them is in a bliss that lacks nothing.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-10-12" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Those Are the Ones Brought Near"

> **{وَالسَّابِقُونَ السَّابِقُونَ ۝ أُولَٰئِكَ الْمُقَرَّبُونَ ۝ فِي جَنَّاتِ النَّعِيمِ}** [الواقعة: ١٠-١٢][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And the forerunners, the forerunners: those are the ones brought near, in the Gardens of Bliss."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:10-12)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir lists several views on who the forerunners are, then says, "All of these views are correct, for what is meant by the forerunners is those who hasten to do good deeds as they were commanded," and he concludes, "for the recompense matches the deed." As-Sa'di said: "That is, those who were foremost in this world in good deeds are the foremost in the Hereafter in entering the Gardens."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

If Tasnim is the drink of those brought near, and those brought near are the forerunners, then the practical question becomes: which good deed will I race to today?

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-3675" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Today I Will Outdo Abu Bakr"

> عَنْ عُمَرَ بْنِ الْخَطَّابِ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **أَمَرَنَا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم أَنْ نَتَصَدَّقَ، فَوَافَقَ ذَلِكَ عِنْدِي مَالًا، فَقُلْتُ: الْيَوْمَ أَسْبِقُ أَبَا بَكْرٍ إِنْ سَبَقْتُهُ يَوْمًا. قَالَ: فَجِئْتُ بِنِصْفِ مَالِي، فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «مَا أَبْقَيْتَ لِأَهْلِكَ؟» قُلْتُ: مِثْلَهُ. وَأَتَى أَبُو بَكْرٍ بِكُلِّ مَا عِنْدَهُ، فَقَالَ: «يَا أَبَا بَكْرٍ، مَا أَبْقَيْتَ لِأَهْلِكَ؟» قَالَ: أَبْقَيْتُ لَهُمُ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ. قُلْتُ: وَاللَّهِ لَا أَسْبِقُهُ إِلَى شَيْءٍ أَبَدًا.**[^9]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, said: **"The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded us to give charity, and it so happened that I had some wealth at the time. I said to myself, 'Today I will outdo Abu Bakr, if ever there is a day I outdo him.' So I brought half of my wealth, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said, 'What have you left for your family?' I said, 'The same amount.' Then Abu Bakr brought everything he had, and he said, 'Abu Bakr, what have you left for your family?' He said, 'I have left them Allah and His Messenger.' I said, 'By Allah, I will never get ahead of him in anything.'"**[^9]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tirmidhi said after narrating it, "This is a *hasan sahih* hadith," and al-Albani graded it *hasan*. Abu Dawud narrated it with similar wording in the Book of Zakah, and at the end of his version Umar says, "I will never compete with you over anything again."[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The account shows an open contest between two Companions in an act of obedience that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had commanded. Umar announced his intention to get ahead; he gave half his wealth and left the same amount for his family. When he saw what Abu Bakr had brought, he acknowledged that Abu Bakr had beaten him. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not criticize their rivalry, and he asked each of them what he had left for his family. As for giving away all one's wealth, that reflected the particular certainty of Abu Bakr; it is not asked of everyone.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of al-Mutaffifin name the nectar and Tasnim and make competing the way to them. The ayat of al-Insan name camphor, ginger and Salsabil and show that the righteous have a blended drink. The ayat of al-Waqi'ah show who those brought near are: the forerunners. Then the account of Umar and Abu Bakr arrives as a living example of a rivalry that knows its limits: racing ahead in obedience, rejoicing for the one who wins, and protecting the rights of one's family.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between the drink of the righteous and the drink of those brought near, as Ibn Kathir reports it from the Companions and their Successors?
2. What are the two possible meanings that as-Sa'di gives for "whose seal is musk"?
3. Why does the command "So for this let all who would compete, compete" come in the middle of the description of the drink?
4. In light of the account of Umar and Abu Bakr, how can one compete in doing good while keeping the heart free of envy?
5. In which area of your life do you compete most today: this world or the Hereafter? And what could you shift from one to the other?

### Activity: Race to the Spring Without Envy

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.051.activity.race-to-the-spring-without-envy" -->

Choose a partner from your family or friends, and agree on one good deed to race each other in for ten days, such as the Duha prayer, a small daily act of charity, or memorizing ayat. Set up a two-column chart where each of you honestly records what you have done. On the fifth day, whoever is behind asks whoever is ahead, "What has helped you?" and takes up one piece of that advice. On the last day, each of you writes the other a one-sentence word of praise and a du'a for them by name. Then write two lines for yourself: What did I learn about competing? And was I glad when my partner got ahead of me?

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Aws and the Race to Wudu'

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.051.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

It was a hot day. Aws and his little sister Rahaf played in the yard until they were worn out. Mom called, "Come inside, you two! I made you some orange juice!"

Mom poured Aws a cup of just juice. For Rahaf, she poured a little juice and then added cold water to it, because Rahaf was still little.

Aws asked, "Why is my cup only juice, and Rahaf's has water in it?"

Dad laughed and said, "That reminds me of Jannah. In Jannah there is a spring called Tasnim, and it is the best drink there. The people brought near to Allah drink it pure, and for the others in Jannah some of it is mixed in. And all of them are happy."

Aws said excitedly, "I want to be one of the people brought near! How?"

Dad said, "The people brought near are the ones who hurry to be first in doing good."

Just then, the adhan began. "I'm going to be first to make wudu'!" Aws shouted, and off he ran. Rahaf ran after him, and Aws bumped her with his shoulder so he could get there first. Rahaf fell onto the grass and cried.

Aws stopped at the door. He had gotten there first, but his heart didn't feel happy.

Aws went back to his sister, held out his hand, and said, "I'm sorry, Rahaf. Come on, let's go together."

Dad patted both their heads and said, "In the race to do good, we never push anyone. In the race to do good, we can all get there, and Jannah is wide enough for everyone."

Aws made wudu', and then he helped Rahaf make hers. That evening, Aws drew a long path that ended at a blue spring of water, and Dad wrote on it: "Aws and Rahaf's Race to Do Good."

### A True Account: Umar and Abu Bakr Race Each Other

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="tirmidhi-3675" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-3675" audience="4-7" -->

**This really happened. Imam at-Tirmidhi recorded it, and it is not a made-up story.**

Umar and Abu Bakr were two Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.

One day, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told his Companions to give charity, which means giving some of their money to people in need. Umar had some money, so he said, "Today I'm going to get ahead of Abu Bakr!"

Umar brought half of his money. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him: "What have you left for your family?" That means: what did you keep for the people at home? Umar said, "The same amount," which means the other half.

Then Abu Bakr brought everything he had! The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him the same question, and Abu Bakr said, "I have left them Allah and His Messenger."

Then Umar said, "By Allah, I will never get ahead of him in anything."[^9]

Umar admitted that his friend had gotten there first. And that is what we learn: in the race to do good, we try our best, we admit it when someone beats us, and we're happy for them.

The Qur'an calls some of the drinks of Jannah by name. The people of Jannah "will be given to drink of a pure, sealed nectar, whose seal is musk," and this nectar is blended from a spring called Tasnim, "a spring from which those brought near will drink."[^1] Scholars explain that Tasnim is the highest drink in Jannah: those brought near drink it pure, and it is blended for the Companions of the Right.[^2] Jannah also has a cup whose blend is camphor, and a cup whose blend is ginger, from a spring called Salsabil.[^4] And here's the amazing part: right between the nectar and Tasnim, Allah says, "So for this let all who would compete, compete."[^1] That's the race truly worth running.

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Jumanah and the Memorization Board

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.051.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

At the summer Qur'an circle at the mosque, the teacher hung up a big board of the girls' names, each with a star for every surah of Juz' 'Amma she had memorized. Ten-year-old Jumanah was always out in front.

In the third week, a new girl named Leen joined, and her stars piled up fast. By Thursday, she had passed Jumanah.

Jumanah felt something heavy settle in her chest. When Leen recited Surah al-Mutaffifin for the teacher, she slipped on a word. Jumanah thought about calling out, "You got it wrong!" in front of everyone.

But the teacher got there first. She gently corrected the word, then told the circle, "Listen to this ayah: 'whose seal is musk. So for this let all who would compete, compete.' Allah loves for us to race to Jannah. But be careful: racing toward good doesn't mean tripping someone else up. It means speeding up yourself." Then she told them the story of Umar and Abu Bakr.

Jumanah went quiet. On the way home, she asked her mom, "Is it wrong that I feel sad because Leen got ahead of me?"

Her mom said, "Wishing to be like her in doing good is a beautiful thing. Wishing that she would fall, that is what spoils the heart."

The next day, Jumanah asked Leen, "How do you memorize so fast?"

Leen smiled. "I listen to the surah ten times before bed, then go over it again after Fajr."

Jumanah tried it. Soon the two were reviewing together after class, quietly correcting each other.

At summer's end, Leen finished Juz' 'Amma three days before Jumanah. When Leen stood up for her prize, Jumanah clapped first. Then Jumanah finished, too.

In her notebook, Jumanah wrote: "Leen got there first, but I got there too. And the spring that those brought near drink from is enough for everyone."

### A True Account: Umar Sets Out to Outdo Abu Bakr

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="tirmidhi-3675" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-3675" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true account narrated by at-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud from Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not a made-up story.**

Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, tells us that one day the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told the Companions to give charity. As it happened, Umar had some money then, so he said, "Today I'll outdo Abu Bakr, if there's ever going to be a day I outdo him."

So Umar brought half of his wealth. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him: "What have you left for your family?" He said, "The same amount," meaning the other half.

Then Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, brought everything he had. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him: "Abu Bakr, what have you left for your family?" He said, "I have left them Allah and His Messenger."

Then Umar said, "By Allah, I will never get ahead of him in anything."[^9]

Notice three things. Umar wanted to get ahead in an act of obedience, not in money or fame. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, didn't tell them to stop competing; he asked each of them about his family. And when Abu Bakr came out ahead, Umar said so openly.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in a world of leaderboards: points in a game, followers, streaks in an app, grades in class. Competition itself isn't a flaw: the Qur'an commands it, but it also marks out the arena. Allah describes the sealed nectar given to the righteous, then says, "So for this let all who would compete, compete," and then mentions Tasnim, "a spring from which those brought near will drink."[^1] As-Sa'di said that Tasnim is "the highest drink of Jannah without exception, and that is why it is pure for those brought near."[^3] And those brought near are "the forerunners." As-Sa'di said: "Those who were foremost in this world in good deeds are the foremost in the Hereafter in entering the Gardens."[^8] So the question isn't "Should I compete?" It's "Which leaderboard am I competing on?"

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Kinan and the Leaderboard

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.051.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Fifteen-year-old Kinan sat at number one on an online game's leaderboard for his city. That spot was part of how his friends saw him: "Kinan? He's number one in the game."

Late one Thursday night, a notification popped up: his friend Malik had passed his score. Kinan decided to stay up until he took the top spot back. He played until three in the morning, then crashed, and slept through Fajr.

He woke up late and wiped out. In the car on the way to the mosque for Jumu'ah, his dad was listening to Surah al-Mutaffifin, and the ayah came on: "So for this let all who would compete, compete." Without turning around, his dad said, "You know, that ayah is a direct command to compete, and it comes in the description of the drink of Jannah."

Kinan didn't answer. But the ayah stuck in his head.

After the prayer, he texted Malik: "Congrats on number one. I've got another challenge for you: thirty days, who prays Fajr at the mosque more? And we log it honestly." Malik laughed and wrote back, "Deal. And I'm going to beat you at this one too."

In the first week, Malik was winning, five days to three. One night Kinan stayed up late, missed Fajr at the mosque, and prayed it at home. That evening he opened the chart, his finger hovering over the box. Nobody would ever know if he put "Yes." He hesitated, then typed: "No."

And he thought: If I cheat in the race for the Hereafter, then what am I even racing for?

Halfway through the month, he asked Malik, "How do you wake up every single day?" Malik said, "I leave my phone outside my room after Isha." Kinan tried it, and his whole sleep changed.

The month ended with Malik in the lead: twenty-seven days to twenty-two. Kinan messaged him: "You beat me. May Allah bless you. Make du'a for me." He never got back to number one in the game, and it no longer bothered him the way it used to.

In his notes, he wrote: "Lost on two leaderboards this month. But on the second one, losing was winning."

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.authenticated-story" kind="authenticated_story" -->

### A True Account: "I Will Never Get Ahead of Him in Anything"

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="tirmidhi-3675" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-3675" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true account recorded in Jami' at-Tirmidhi and Sunan Abi Dawud, and at-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan sahih*. It is not an imagined scene.**

One day, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told his Companions to give charity. Umar ibn al-Khattab happened to have some wealth at the time, and he made up his mind: "Today I will outdo Abu Bakr, if ever there is a day I outdo him." His words "if ever there is a day I outdo him" imply that Abu Bakr had always been ahead of him before.

Umar brought half of his wealth, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him: "What have you left for your family?" He said, "The same amount." Then Abu Bakr brought everything he had, and the Prophet asked him: "Abu Bakr, what have you left for your family?" He said, "I have left them Allah and His Messenger." And Umar said, "By Allah, I will never get ahead of him in anything."[^9]

Look closely at three moments: an openly declared intention to get ahead in an act of obedience; a question from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about the family's rights, before any word of praise; and Umar's frank admission that his companion had beaten him. This shows that praiseworthy competition keeps the heart whole. As for giving away all one's wealth, that was particular to Abu Bakr's situation and his certainty, which is exactly why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him about his family.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-83-22-28" -->

- **`Let them compete (fal-yatanafas)`** — a command to race for something precious; in as-Sa'di's words, "let them race one another in hastening toward it through the deeds that lead to it."[^3]
- **`Pure (sirf)`** — undiluted, not mixed with anything else; this describes how those brought near drink from Tasnim.[^2]
- **`Blend (mizaj)`** — what is mixed into a drink, like the camphor, ginger and Tasnim in the drink of the righteous.[^5]

### Discussion Questions

1. Why had the number-one spot in the game become part of Kinan's identity? And which leaderboards shape your own identity?
2. Why was the moment he typed "No" in the chart the most important moment in the story?
3. In light of the account of Umar and Abu Bakr, what makes competition praiseworthy or blameworthy?
4. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked each of the two Companions about his family. What does that question teach us about the limits of competition?
5. What does it mean that losing a race for the Hereafter can still be winning?

### Activity: Race to the Spring Without Envy

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.051.activity.race-to-the-spring-without-envy" -->

Pick a friend you trust, and agree on a fourteen-day challenge in one good deed, such as praying Fajr on time or reading one page of the Qur'an a day. Before you start, write down two rules: we log honestly even when no one is watching, and neither of us mocks the other. On the seventh day, whoever is behind asks whoever is ahead about one habit that has helped them, and tries it. At the end, send your partner a message praising one specific thing they did, make du'a for them by name in your sujud, and then write yourself a paragraph: Did my feelings change when they got ahead of me, or when I got ahead of them?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.051.dua.bring-us-near-give-us-tasnim" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ قَرِّبْنَا إِلَيْكَ، وَاسْقِنَا مِنْ عَيْنِ التَّسْنِيمِ وَمِنْ رَحِيقٍ خِتَامُهُ مِسْكٌ، وَاجْعَلْنَا نُسَابِقُ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ وَنُحِبُّهُ لِإِخْوَانِنَا.
>
> *"O Allah, bring us near to You, give us to drink from the spring of Tasnim and from a nectar whose seal is musk, and make us race toward good and love it for our brothers and sisters."*

This du'a holds together the goal, the path, and the heart: nearness to Allah is the goal, racing toward good is the path, and loving good for others is the guard that keeps the race from turning into envy.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites the ayat of al-Mutaffifin (83:22-28), al-Insan (76:5-6, 17-18) and al-Waqi'ah (56:10-12); names the five drinks of Jannah and explains their meanings using the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; distinguishes the pure drink of those brought near from the blended drink of the righteous; narrates the account of Umar and Abu Bakr (at-Tirmidhi 3675) and draws from it three guidelines for praiseworthy competition; and begins the race activity with a partner.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with a three-column table headed "Name," "Meaning," and "For whom?"; a printed two-column activity chart covering ten days; small cards for praise and du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Mutaffifin (83:25-28), al-Insan (76:5, 17-18) and al-Waqi'ah (56:10-11); reads the hadith in Jami' at-Tirmidhi (Book of Virtues) and Sunan Abi Dawud (Book of Zakah) together with the scholars' gradings of it; and prepares the opening question.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What was the last thing you competed in? And when did you feel that competing had spoiled your heart?" Then read "So for this let all who would compete, compete" without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of al-Mutaffifin and their tafsir, then the ayat of al-Insan, while learners fill in the table: the sealed nectar, Tasnim, camphor, ginger, Salsabil. Then read the ayat of al-Waqi'ah to link those brought near with the forerunners, and then the hadith in its own wording together with at-Tirmidhi's grading.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss: Why does the command to compete come in the middle of the description of the drink? What is the difference between healthy aspiration (*ghibtah*) and envy (*hasad*) in the account of Umar? Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, ask each of them about his family? Learners draw out three guidelines: the arena is obedience to Allah; the heart stays at peace with the one who gets ahead; and the rights of those we provide for are not neglected.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains the activity as described in the activity unit; each learner chooses a partner and a good deed, and the pair write down the rule of honest record-keeping.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes the names of the five drinks, the difference between pure and blended, and one guideline for competition taken from the account of Umar. Then the lesson's du'a is recited, and those who wish agree to share their two lines after ten days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayat of al-Mutaffifin and the hadith are enough. Advanced learners compare at-Tirmidhi's wording with Abu Dawud's, and read Ibn Kathir's survey of the views on who the forerunners are and explain how he reconciles them.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not strain to describe the taste or nature of these drinks; we believe in the names and meanings exactly as they have come to us. Do not let anyone conclude from Abu Bakr's example that everyone must give away all their wealth: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked about the family, and Abu Bakr's situation was a special one. Do not say that anyone who drinks the blended drink is deprived; all the people of Jannah are in complete bliss. Under "They will be given to drink of a pure, sealed nectar," Ibn Kathir mentions a hadith recorded by Ahmad: "Any believer who gives a believer a drink when he is thirsty, Allah will give him to drink on the Day of Resurrection from the sealed nectar." Its chain includes Atiyyah al-Awfi, and the narrator says of it, from Abu Sa'id, "I think he attributed it to the Prophet." It has therefore not been relied upon in this lesson, and the same applies to the report from Abu al-Darda' about the seal that Ibn Jarir cites.[^10] There is a slight difference of opinion over the grading of the hadith of Umar, mentioned in the References.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child names two drinks of Jannah (Tasnim and Salsabil), knows that those brought near are the ones who hurry to do good, retells how Umar was happy for Abu Bakr when Abu Bakr got ahead, applies the rule "I hurry to do good, and I don't push anyone," and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Two clear cups, one with only juice and the other with juice mixed with water; a large sheet of paper with two paths drawn on it, both ending at a blue spring of water; stickers; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the story of Aws and the account of Umar in a calm voice, and draws the two paths in advance.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Show the two cups and ask, "What's different about them?" Then say, "In Jannah there is a spring called Tasnim, and the people brought near drink from it pure."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell the story of Aws, then say, "And this next one really happened," and tell the account of Umar and Abu Bakr. The child repeats: "a spring from which those brought near will drink."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask, "Was Aws happy when he pushed Rahaf? What did he do afterward?" Then repeat together with the child: "I hurry to do good, I don't push anyone, and I'm happy for whoever gets there first."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The child and a partner choose the small good deed as described in the activity unit, the child puts the first sticker on their path, and practices saying, "Well done, may Allah bless you."

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child, "What is the name of the spring that the people brought near drink from?" and "What did Umar say?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the name Tasnim and the sentence "I'm happy for whoever gets there first" are enough. Older children name all three drinks and retell the story of Aws in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not turn the race into a physical one inside the classroom, and do not compare children with one another publicly. Make sure a child who has fewer stickers is never made to feel ashamed. Do not use the word "wine" with children when describing the nectar; it is enough to say it is a delicious drink in Jannah. Say that all the people of Jannah are happy, and never portray any of them as left out.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student names the drinks of Jannah mentioned in the lesson and explains the meanings of Tasnim, Salsabil and the sealed nectar; narrates the account of Umar and Abu Bakr; distinguishes between wishing to do good like others and wishing that others would lose what they have; and begins a seven-day race with a partner.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The ayat and the hadith written out; the four term cards; the activity chart; three scenario cards: "Your friend beat you on a test," "Your brother has memorized more than you," "A classmate won a prize you wanted."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes two columns on the board, "Good wishing" and "Wishing that spoils the heart," and reviews as-Sa'di's words on competing.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Have you ever felt something heavy in your chest because someone got ahead of you? What did you do?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the ayat of al-Mutaffifin and ask students to notice where the ayah about competing comes. Then tell the account of Umar and Abu Bakr and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read the story of Jumanah, pausing at the moment she wanted to expose Leen's mistake. Then students work in pairs on the scenario cards, writing for each one a response that fits praiseworthy competition.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student chooses a partner and a good deed as described in the activity unit, and draws their chart ending at a spring of water.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** The student completes: "Those brought near are..." and "If my friend gets ahead of me in doing good, I will..." Then the du'a is read. A week later, students share their praise cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who need support ready-made sentences for the praise and the du'a. Advanced students write a short dialogue between Jumanah and Leen that explains the ayah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not post public leaderboards of students in this activity; the chart stays between the two partners. Do not encourage students to correct one another publicly in a way that embarrasses. Look out for any student without a suitable partner, and let a parent or the teacher be that student's partner.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes how leaderboards shape their identity; explains the ayah about competing and its position between the mention of the nectar and of Tasnim; links those brought near with the forerunners through as-Sa'di's words; draws from the account of Umar and Abu Bakr the guidelines of praiseworthy competition; and commits to honesty in a record nobody else is checking.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a sheet titled "My Leaderboards" on which each student privately lists the areas they compete in; the fourteen-day activity chart; unnamed cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews as-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Mutaffifin (83:26, 28) and al-Waqi'ah (56:10), and the wording of the hadith in at-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Students privately write down three "leaderboards" where their position matters to them. Then ask: "Which of these will still matter in ten years? And which after death?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayat of al-Mutaffifin with the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di, then the ayat of al-Waqi'ah, then the account of Umar and Abu Bakr in its own wording, and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read the story of Kinan, and have groups discuss: "Why was that box in the chart a real test? And what moved Kinan from one leaderboard to another?" Then ask: "What's the difference between losing to Malik in the game and losing to him at Fajr?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes down the two challenge rules as described in the activity unit and decides on a partner and a good deed, without being asked to announce either.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** On an unnamed card, the student writes: "The leaderboard I want to compete on is... because..." Then the du'a is read. Two weeks later, anyone who wishes shares their paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A student who cannot find a partner may compete against their own record from the previous week. Advanced students gather the views on the meaning of "the forerunners" in Ibn Kathir and explain the reasoning behind his statement, "All of these views are correct."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not condemn games or sports in themselves; discuss instead what competing in them takes from one's time, one's prayer and one's heart. Never ask anyone to reveal their record in front of the class, and never embarrass anyone who has missed a prayer. Point out that recording the truth, even when it shows a shortfall, is the heart of the activity.
