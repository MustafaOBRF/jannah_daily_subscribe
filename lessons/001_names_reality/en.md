# The Names of Jannah and Its Reality

## Lesson Objectives And Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- State that the names of Jannah in the Qur'an — such as `al-Jannah`, `Dar as-Salam`, and `al-Firdaws` — are not empty synonyms, but that each name reveals a real attribute of that abode.
- Recite Allah's statement, `And Allah invites to the Home of Peace` (Yunus 10:25), and His statement, `Those are the inheritors, who will inherit al-Firdaws` (al-Mu'minun 23:10-11), and explain what each of these two names reveals.
- Narrate the sacred hadith (hadith qudsi), "I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined," and explain that the reality of Jannah is greater than the utmost reach of human imagination.
- Narrate the story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami, may Allah be pleased with him, when he asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to be his companion in Jannah, and explain that this tremendous hope is sought through real action, not wishing alone.
- Distinguish between a person imagining the best they know of this world, and the reality that Allah has named and that surpasses that imagination — without this distinction canceling either hope or action.
- Carry out the "Imagine, Then Exceed" activity: describing the best thing the learner can imagine, then comparing it to one of the names of Jannah and the sacred hadith, and connecting that to one concrete action.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We often assume the Qur'anic names of Jannah — `al-Jannah`, `Dar as-Salam`, `al-Firdaws` — are mere synonyms for rhetorical variety. But reflecting on their Qur'anic contexts shows each name reveals a real attribute of that abode, not just a beautiful title. Naming it `Dar as-Salam` tells us complete safety is inherent to it, not a passing description. Naming it `al-Firdaws` tells us it is the highest and most central of Jannah's stations.

A deeper question remains: can we imagine this abode's reality the way we imagine the most beautiful place we know in this world, simply adding a bigger palace and a clearer river? The sacred hadith answers clearly: Jannah's reality is not an extension of the best thing a person has known; it is beyond what all of humanity's combined imagination could ever reach.

This corrects two opposite errors:

1. **Reducing Jannah to an enlarged earthly image:** thinking it merely the best garden or palace one knows, only bigger.
2. **Turning Jannah's greatness into a vague abstraction with no practical effect:** thinking that exceeding imagination makes it a theoretical luxury that changes nothing in one's behavior today.

So the names of Jannah point to real attributes we hope for, and their surpassing our imagination should increase our longing and action, not our withdrawal. The story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami, may Allah be pleased with him, embodies this: he asked for something immense, so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, guided him to a concrete action by which it could be attained.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-10-25" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Allah Invites to the Home of Peace"

> **وَاللَّهُ يَدْعُو إِلَىٰ دَارِ السَّلَامِ وَيَهْدِي مَنْ يَشَاءُ إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُسْتَقِيمٍ.** [يونس: ٢٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And Allah invites to the Home of Peace and guides whom He wills to a straight path."** (Yunus 10:25)[^1]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

The exegetes explain that `Dar as-Salam` (the Home of Peace) is Jannah, so named because its people are safe there from every affliction, worry, sorrow, and death, and because `as-Salam` is one of Allah's names, so attaching the abode to Him is an honorific attribution. The invitation is general, addressed to all people; guidance to the straight path is specific to whomever Allah wills.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

This name is fact, not poetic description: whoever enters this abode attains complete safety unlike anything known in this world. The ayah makes this safety an open invitation from Allah Himself, not a distant prize of doubtful attainment.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-23-10" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Those Are the Inheritors, Who Will Inherit al-Firdaws"

> **أُولَـٰئِكَ هُمُ الْوَارِثُونَ * الَّذِينَ يَرِثُونَ الْفِرْدَوْسَ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ.** [المؤمنون: ١٠-١١][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those are the inheritors, who will inherit al-Firdaws; they will abide therein eternally."** (al-Mu'minun 23:10-11)[^3]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

These ayat are preceded by a description of the successful believers: humility in prayer, turning away from idle talk, paying zakah, guarding their private parts, fulfilling trusts and covenants, and safeguarding their prayers. Allah made their reward the inheritance of `al-Firdaws`, the highest and most central part of Jannah, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained elsewhere.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The name `al-Firdaws` reveals that Jannah has levels, and its highest level is attained not by mere wishing but by the specific deeds already named in context. The name is thus tied directly to practical attributes, not detached from them.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3244" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Have Prepared for My Righteous Servants What No Eye Has Seen"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«قَالَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى: أَعْدَدْتُ لِعِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحِينَ مَا لَا عَيْنٌ رَأَتْ، وَلَا أُذُنٌ سَمِعَتْ، وَلَا خَطَرَ عَلَى قَلْبِ بَشَرٍ، فَاقْرَءُوا إِنْ شِئْتُمْ: {فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَا أُخْفِيَ لَهُمْ مِنْ قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ جَزَاءً بِمَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ}».**[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Allah, Most High, said: I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined. So recite, if you wish: {No soul knows what joy has been kept hidden for them as a reward for what they used to do}."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

This is an agreed-upon (muttafaq 'alayh) sacred hadith (hadith qudsi): Allah, Most High, informs us that He has prepared for His righteous servants bliss that no sight or hearing has perceived and that has never crossed a human heart's imagination. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, then cited the ayah of Surah as-Sajdah confirming this meaning.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith governs everything we learn about Jannah's names and attributes: what we hear of it is true and moving, yet never encompasses the whole reality. However beautifully we describe Jannah using the best we know of this world, its reality remains greater than our description — directing the heart toward longing and action, not mere contentment with imagination.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-489" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Ask You for Your Companionship in Jannah"

> عَنْ رَبِيعَةَ بْنِ كَعْبٍ الْأَسْلَمِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **كُنْتُ أَبِيتُ مَعَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَأُتِيهِ بِوَضُوئِهِ وَحَاجَتِهِ، فَقَالَ لِي: «سَلْ». فَقُلْتُ: أَسْأَلُكَ مُرَافَقَتَكَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ. قَالَ: «أَوْ غَيْرَ ذَٰلِكَ؟». قُلْتُ: هُوَ ذَاكَ. قَالَ: «فَأَعِنِّي عَلَىٰ نَفْسِكَ بِكَثْرَةِ السُّجُودِ».**[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: **"I used to spend the night with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and I would bring him his water for wudu and see to his needs. He said to me: 'Ask.' I said: I ask you for your companionship in Jannah. He said: 'Or something else?' I said: That is it. He said: 'Then help me for your sake by prostrating often.'"**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Rabi'ah, may Allah be pleased with him, used to serve the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Offered the chance to ask for whatever he wished, he did not ask for wealth or status, but for the greatest of all things: to be the Prophet's companion, peace and blessings be upon him, in Jannah itself. The Prophet sought to clarify his request, and when Rabi'ah persisted, he guided him to the practical path toward it: abundant prostration.

#### Lesson Explanation

This encounter ties the greatest hope to the simplest visible action: prostration after prostration. Rabi'ah was left neither with mere wishing nor without a path; his tremendous hope was paired with a deed every Muslim can do daily — a model for anyone who has heard the names of Jannah, knows its reality surpasses imagination, and asks: so what is the action?

<!-- evidence:end -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Hana's Most Beautiful House

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.001.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Hana loved building houses out of colorful blocks in her grandmother's garden. One day, Hana built a big house with a red roof, and said proudly, "This is the most beautiful house in the whole world!"

A little while later, her cousin came and built a house slightly bigger than hers, and everyone clapped for him. Hana felt sad, and wanted to knock down her cousin's house so that hers would stay the most beautiful.

Before she could do it, her grandmother sat beside her and said gently, "Did you know, Hana, that Allah named Jannah `Dar as-Salam`? That means a home with no fighting and no sadness in it. And He told us that Jannah is more beautiful than every house you have ever imagined, even if you built the biggest house of blocks in the whole world." Hana smiled, left her cousin's house as it was, and said to him, "Your house is beautiful. Come, let's build a big house together."

That night, Hana drew a small house next to a big heart, and said to her mother, "This is my house, and this is the heart, because I didn't fight with my cousin. And the house of Jannah is more beautiful than all of this!"

### A True Story: Rabi'ah Asks for Something Great

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-489" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-489" audience="4-7" -->

There was a righteous man named Rabi'ah who used to serve the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. One day the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him, "Ask for whatever you want." Rabi'ah did not ask for a toy or for money. Instead he said, "I want to be with you in Jannah." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him, "Do you want something else?" Rabi'ah said, "No, I only want this." So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him, "Then help yourself by prostrating to Allah often."[^5] That means: every time Rabi'ah prostrated to Allah a lot, he came closer to his great request.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Adam's Dream-House Project

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.001.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

The teacher assigned the class a project titled: "Describe Your Dream House," due on Monday and to be presented in front of everyone. Adam wanted to win the class's admiration, so he began writing about an imaginary house with a swimming pool, a private elevator, and a huge playground garden.

On presentation day, his classmate Faisal stood up and read an exaggerated description of a palace with golden towers, and the class clapped for him enthusiastically. Adam felt that his simple description would not impress anyone, and thought about adding exaggerated details like his classmate's, to get the same admiration, even if those details did not reflect what he truly thought.

Moments before his turn, Adam remembered what his father had explained to him over the weekend: that Allah told us His Jannah contains "what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined," and that its name `al-Firdaws` means it is higher than anything we can imagine. Adam asked himself, "Do I need to exaggerate to prove something, when the greatest reality already surpasses all my exaggeration?"

Adam stood up and read his simple, honest description exactly as he had written it, then added one final sentence: "But more beautiful than all of this is `al-Firdaws`, which Allah described as being beyond anything we could ever imagine." The class did not clap for him with the same enthusiasm they had for Faisal, but Adam felt an inner peace, because he had not lied to be admired, and he learned that the greatest truth does not need exaggeration for the heart to believe it.

### The Hadith Story: Rabi'ah's Great Request

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-489" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-489" audience="8-12" -->

Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that he used to serve the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, bringing him his water for wudu and attending to his needs. One night, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him, "Ask for whatever you wish." Rabi'ah did not ask for wealth or status, but said, "I ask you that I may accompany you in Jannah." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him, "Or do you want something else?" Rabi'ah insisted: "That is my request." So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him, "Then help me for your sake by prostrating to Allah often."[^5]

Rabi'ah's request was far greater than any worldly request, yet the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not leave him with mere wishing; he tied it to a deed he could do every day: prostration.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

In a world where half of life is lived on a screen, and worth is sometimes measured by how many people see your picture and react to it, the names of Jannah remind us of an entirely different standard. `Dar as-Salam` tells you there is a real peace that needs no documentation and no likes. And `al-Firdaws` tells you that the highest stations are attained through sincere action, not the best photo you can post. And the sacred hadith, "what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined," demolishes the idea that the utmost you could aspire to is the best version of your life that you can picture; the promised reality is greater than any image, whether you publish it to people or keep it hidden.

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Layla's Private Vision Board

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.001.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Layla decided to make a "vision board" for a school project about her future goals, and to post a picture of it on her account to share with her friends. She gathered pictures of a luxurious house, a car, and travels, and began thinking: "What if I add goals bigger than what I actually aspire to, so the board looks more impressive?"

That night, Layla happened to scroll past a lesson she had heard before about the names of Jannah, and paused at the sacred hadith: "I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined." She thought: "Every image I add to my board, however beautiful, is something a person has imagined or made. But this promise speaks of something that has never even crossed a human heart."

Layla decided not to delete her worldly vision board — pursuing her studies and success is a legitimate goal — but in a private corner of her notebook, one she would never share with anyone, she added the words: "My first goal: to be among the people of `al-Firdaws`." She wrote nothing beneath it for display, but wrote one practical step, for herself alone: to keep her Fajr prayer on time this week.

Layla posted her worldly vision board as it was, without exaggeration or falsification, but she understood that the greatest hope in her life was not the one displayed on a screen, but the one she carried in her private prostration — like Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b when he asked for something no one else had asked for.

### A Prophetic Encounter That Redefines Ambition

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-489" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-489" audience="13+" -->

Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami, may Allah be pleased with him, reported that he used to spend the night near the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, serving him with his wudu water and his needs. One night the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him, "Ask." Rabi'ah said, "I ask you for your companionship in Jannah." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Or something else?" Rabi'ah said, "That is it." So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him, "Then help me for your sake by prostrating often."[^5]

Notice that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, sought clarification from Rabi'ah twice before answering him, as though making sure this was his true ambition and not merely a passing remark. And once he was certain of the sincerity of his request, he did not leave him at the threshold of a wish, but turned it into a simple, practical program anyone could follow: abundant prostration. So the greatest ambition a person could ask for is tied to the simplest deed one can maintain in private, not in public display before others.

- **`Muraafaqatak` ("your companionship")** — for a person to ask to be near the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Jannah — one of the greatest things one could ever ask for.
- **`al-Firdaws`** — the highest and most central of Jannah's stations, mentioned in the Qur'an paired with practical attributes, not mere wishing.
- **`Qurratu A'yun`** ("coolness/comfort of the eyes") — a bliss that fills the heart and eye with complete joy, mentioned in the Qur'an in the context of what is hidden from people as a reward for their deeds.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did Layla consider exaggerating her vision board, and what changed her decision?
2. How does the sacred hadith challenge the idea that the best thing you can aspire to is the best image you can imagine or post?
3. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, seek clarification of Rabi'ah's request twice before guiding him to the deed?
4. What is the difference between a goal you display to people and a goal you carry alone in your private prostration?
5. Which name of Jannah in this lesson do you need to make your personal compass this week?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Imagine, Then Exceed

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.001.activity.imagine-then-exceed" -->

Write a private paragraph (one you will not show anyone) describing the best future you can imagine for yourself. Then re-read the sacred hadith, "what no eye has seen...", and choose one name of Jannah from this lesson. Write a reflection paragraph explaining how this reality reorders your worldly ambition without canceling it, then close with one private, practical step — one you will not announce to anyone — following the model of "abundant prostration" in the story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b.

<!-- activity:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ دَارِ السَّلَامِ، وَبَلِّغْنَا فِي الْجَنَّةِ مَا لَا عَيْنٌ رَأَتْ وَلَا أُذُنٌ سَمِعَتْ وَلَا خَطَرَ عَلَىٰ قَلْبِ بَشَرٍ.
>
> *"O Allah, make us among the people of the Home of Peace, and grant us in Jannah what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined."*

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the ayat of `Dar as-Salam` and `al-Firdaws` and the sacred hadith "what no eye has seen," distinguishes between imagining Jannah as an enlarged earthly image and understanding it as a reality beyond imagination, and connects the story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b to one practical action.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the two ayat and two hadiths; a model of the "Imagine, Then Exceed" activity; paper and pens; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the grading and source of each hadith, and prepares a neutral example of the difference between people's imagining of complete comfort and what revelation has described, without delving into speculative details about Jannah's sensory attributes.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If someone asked you to describe the most beautiful place that could possibly exist, what would you describe?" Gather brief answers before presenting the evidence.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Groups read the two ayat and two hadiths in full, and each group identifies: the attribute revealed by each name, and the significance of the reality surpassing everything an eye has seen, an ear has heard, or that has crossed a heart.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains that the names of Jannah are real attributes, not titles, and that the reality surpassing imagination does not mean vagueness but rather directs one toward greater longing and action, citing the story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners individually complete the "Imagine, Then Exceed" activity, then whoever wishes shares their closing paragraph with their small group, without being required to share personal details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two of the names of Jannah in this lesson, and state the attribute each one reveals, then write the action you chose for this week." The teacher closes by reading the du'a, noting that it is his own educational composition.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give a beginner learner a ready list of names to choose from in the activity, and assign an advanced learner to compare the meanings of `Dar as-Salam` and `al-Firdaws` in more detail, discussing why each ayah was paired with its particular name in its context.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one should be asked to describe detailed sensory particulars about Jannah from their own imagination; adhere strictly to what is stated in the text. The activity must not be used to judge who has bigger worldly goals than others; instead, direct the discussion toward sincerity of intention and action.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="30" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child states the meaning of the name `Dar as-Salam` in a simple sentence, understands that Jannah is more beautiful than anything they can imagine, and connects that to one simple act of peace.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Paper and coloring supplies for drawing; a du'a card in clear handwriting; simple pictures of houses and gardens to spark imagination.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares drawing paper and practices narrating the story of Hana and the hadith of Rabi'ah in short, clear sentences suited to the child's age.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What is the most beautiful house or garden you have ever imagined?" Listen enthusiastically to the children's answers, then tell the story of Hana.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The teacher narrates the story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b simply, pausing after his request to ask: "What did Rabi'ah ask the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, for?"

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 6 minutes:** The teacher explains that Allah named Jannah `Dar as-Salam` because there is no fighting in it, and that it is more beautiful than anything we can imagine.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 9 minutes:** Each child draws the most beautiful house they can imagine, then adds a small heart and says one sentence about an act of peace they will do tomorrow. The adult helps whoever needs help, without comparing the children's drawings.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What does the name `Dar as-Salam` mean?" Then read the du'a slowly so the children can repeat what they are able to.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A child who finds drawing difficult may simply describe their house in words, and an advanced child is given the chance to explain in a full sentence why Jannah is more beautiful than their drawing.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Jannah must not be described with sensory details invented by the teacher that are not found in the text; keep strictly to what has been reported. No child's drawing should be compared to another's in front of the group.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="45" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student summarizes the meaning of two of Jannah's names, explains the meaning of the sacred hadith "what no eye has seen," distinguishes between exaggerating to impress others and being truthful in description, and completes the "Imagine, Then Exceed" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The full text of the two ayat and two hadiths; term cards; a model "My Dream House" card; pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prints the texts and terms, and prepares a completed example of the "My Dream House" card to display before asking students to begin.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Display two phrases: "An exaggerated description meant to impress others" and "A simple, honest description." Ask: "Which one did Adam choose in the end, and why?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Students read the sacred hadith and the hadith of Rabi'ah in full, underline the sentence describing the reality surpassing imagination, and circle the deed the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, guided him to.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher explains the three terms, then builds the class statement: "The names of Jannah are real attributes, and its reality is greater than all our imagination."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 14 minutes:** Students fill out the "My Dream House" card, then write the two comparison sentences with a name of Jannah, then each student shares one action for this week with a classmate, without sensitive details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences: "The name I chose is..." and "The action I will begin with is...". The teacher closes by reading the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A ready list of names and attributes is provided for students who are struggling, and advanced students are assigned to compare the two ayat and explain why the inheritance of `al-Firdaws` is paired with specific practical attributes in its context.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No student should mock a classmate regarding the details of their imagined house, before or after revision, and no student should be asked to announce embarrassing personal goals in front of the class.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="55" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 55 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the difference between an ambition displayed to people and a private ambition built on the reality of revelation, explains the significance of the sacred hadith and the story of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b, and completes the "Imagine, Then Exceed" activity with a private practical step.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A file of the four pieces of evidence; term cards; private activity papers that are not collected from students; exit slips.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the grading of the two hadiths, and prepares a neutral example of the pressure of posting and comparison on social media, without referring to any specific student.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher poses a question: "Is a goal no one sees worth less than a goal that is posted?" Open a brief discussion before reading Layla's story.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Two groups read the ayah of `Dar as-Salam` and the sacred hadith "what no eye has seen," and two other groups read the ayah of `al-Firdaws` and the hadith of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b. Each group identifies: the attribute their text reveals, and its relation to public display versus private ambition.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** The teacher explains the terms `muraafaqatak`, `al-Firdaws`, and `qurratu a'yun`, and discusses how these texts confront the temptation to measure worth by views and likes.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Students write their private paragraph about the best future they can imagine, then the reflection paragraph and private step, with reassurance that these papers are personal and will not be collected or displayed.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** The student writes a three-line response to the opening question, drawing on the four pieces of evidence, without needing to share it with anyone.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A struggling student is given three ready examples of private goals to choose from, and an advanced student is assigned to discuss how a Muslim balances a legitimate worldly ambition with a higher otherworldly ambition without contradiction.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Private activity papers must not be collected or read aloud in class. The lesson must not be used to judge those who publicly post their goals; direct the discussion instead toward sincerity of inner intention. Take care that the reflection does not turn into a hurtful comparison between students' ambitions.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
