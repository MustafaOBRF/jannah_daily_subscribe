# The People of Jannah Remember This World: "Had It Not Been for the Favor of My Lord"

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the conversation in Surah as-Saffat (37:50-61) in its proper order: the people of Jannah ask one another about their lives in this world; one of them recalls a companion who used to fill him with doubt about the resurrection; he looks down and sees him in the midst of the Blazing Fire; he reproaches him and thanks his Lord for His favor; and then he rejoices in eternal life: "This is truly the supreme triumph."

- Explain the meaning of *qarin*, "companion," through the two views of Mujahid and Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn Kathir's reconciliation of them, together with as-Sa'di's explanation of "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord" as Allah keeping him firm upon Islam.

- Use the hadith in al-Bukhari (6569) to show that everyone who enters Jannah is shown the seat in the Fire that would have been his had he done evil, "so that he may be all the more grateful," with Ibn Hajar's explanation of what gratitude means here.

- Explain how the people of Jannah inherit the homes left behind by those who abandoned the purpose for which they were created, drawing on al-Mu'minun 23:10-11, the hadith in Ibn Majah (4341), and Ibn Kathir's reasoning.

- Distinguish gratitude for being saved from gloating, and distinguish what the Qur'an tells us about a companion who died in denial from what we may never do: pass judgment on the final end of any living person.

- Carry out the activity "The Story I Will Tell in Jannah": writing a story of steadfastness as though telling it to one's brothers and sisters in Jannah, pinpointing the decision point that will make it come true, and acting on it for a set period.

- Learn by heart the Prophetic du'a: «يَا مُقَلِّبَ الْقُلُوبِ ثَبِّتْ قَلْبِي عَلَى دِينِكَ», "O Turner of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion."

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The people of Jannah do not forget this world. They talk it over in their gatherings: what their lives were like, what they went through, and who was with them along the way. Among the most striking of these conversations that the Qur'an recounts is that of a man from the people of Jannah who remembers a companion who used to sow doubt in him about the resurrection. He looks down, sees him in the midst of the Blazing Fire, and says: "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord, I would have been among those brought [to it]." Authentic hadith adds two further meanings: everyone who enters Jannah is shown the seat that would have been his had he done evil, and every person has two homes, so that the people of Jannah inherit the home of whoever abandoned the purpose for which he was created.

So this lesson brings together three connected themes: remembering this world, seeing the people of the Fire, and inheriting homes. All three flow into a single meaning: gratitude for guidance and for steadfastness. How the righteous fare with those who used to mock them, as described in Surah al-Mutaffifin, was covered in an earlier lesson.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-37-50-61" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then He Looked and Saw Him in the Midst of the Blazing Fire"

> **{فَأَقْبَلَ بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ ۝ قَالَ قَائِلٌ مِنْهُمْ إِنِّي كَانَ لِي قَرِينٌ ۝ يَقُولُ أَإِنَّكَ لَمِنَ الْمُصَدِّقِينَ ۝ أَإِذَا مِتْنَا وَكُنَّا تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا أَإِنَّا لَمَدِينُونَ ۝ قَالَ هَلْ أَنْتُمْ مُطَّلِعُونَ ۝ فَاطَّلَعَ فَرَآهُ فِي سَوَاءِ الْجَحِيمِ ۝ قَالَ تَاللَّهِ إِنْ كِدْتَ لَتُرْدِينِ ۝ وَلَوْلَا نِعْمَةُ رَبِّي لَكُنْتُ مِنَ الْمُحْضَرِينَ ۝ أَفَمَا نَحْنُ بِمَيِّتِينَ ۝ إِلَّا مَوْتَتَنَا الْأُولَىٰ وَمَا نَحْنُ بِمُعَذَّبِينَ ۝ إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ ۝ لِمِثْلِ هَٰذَا فَلْيَعْمَلِ الْعَامِلُونَ}** [الصافات: ٥٠-٦١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Then they will turn to one another, asking each other questions. One of them will say, 'I had a companion who used to say, "Are you really one of those who believe it? When we have died and become dust and bones, are we really going to be brought to account?"' He will say, 'Will you look down?' Then he will look down and see him in the midst of the Blazing Fire. He will say, 'By Allah, you very nearly brought me to ruin! Had it not been for the favor of my Lord, I would have been among those brought [to it]. So are we not to die, except for our first death, and are we not to be punished? This is truly the supreme triumph.' For the like of this, then, let all who strive, strive."** (as-Saffat 37:50-61)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says of "asking each other questions": "that is, about their circumstances: how they had been in this world and what they used to endure in it," and this takes place in their gatherings, "while they are sitting on couches." On *qarin*, "companion," he cites Mujahid: "meaning a devil," and Ibn 'Abbas: "He is the idolater who has a companion among the people of faith in this world." He then says: "There is no contradiction between what Mujahid and Ibn 'Abbas said," because a devil may be from the jinn, whispering within the soul, or from humankind, "saying words that the ears hear." He explains that the companion asked his question "by way of astonishment, denial, and dismissing it as far-fetched." *La-madinun* means "brought to account" according to Mujahid and as-Suddi, and "repaid for our deeds" according to Ibn 'Abbas. *Muttali'un* means "looking down from above," and *sawa' al-jahim* means "the middle of the Blazing Fire." On "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord," he says: "But He bestowed His favor [upon me], had mercy on me, guided me to faith, and directed me to His oneness." On the final ayah, he cites Qatadah's view that it is spoken by the people of Jannah, and Ibn Jarir's view that it is the speech of Allah the Exalted.[^1]

As-Sa'di explains that the man of Jannah says to his brothers: "This is my story, and this is my news: me and my companion. I remained a believer who affirmed the truth, and he remained a denier who rejected the resurrection." On "Will you look down?" he says: "so that we may look at him, and our delight and joy in what we are in may grow." The man said what he said to his companion "reproaching him for his state, and thanking Allah for His favor in saving him from his scheming," and as-Sa'di explains "the favor of my Lord" as being "in that He kept me firm upon Islam." He also points out that the first ayah leaves unstated what they ask one another about, and "this shows that they ask one another about everything they delight in talking about."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The whole conversation takes place in a gathering of warmth and happiness, not one of fear. The man does not credit his rescue to his own intelligence or to the strength of his arguments, but to the favor of his Lord. And the companion was not an open enemy. He was a close friend who tossed out a mocking question: you still believe all that? A believer may hear that same voice today from a friend, from a screen, or from the whisperings of his own heart.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6569" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "So That He May Be All the More Grateful"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: **«لَا يَدْخُلُ أَحَدٌ الْجَنَّةَ إِلَّا أُرِيَ مَقْعَدَهُ مِنَ النَّارِ لَوْ أَسَاءَ، لِيَزْدَادَ شُكْرًا، وَلَا يَدْخُلُ النَّارَ أَحَدٌ إِلَّا أُرِيَ مَقْعَدَهُ مِنَ الْجَنَّةِ لَوْ أَحْسَنَ، لِيَكُونَ عَلَيْهِ حَسْرَةً»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"No one enters Jannah without being shown the seat in the Fire that would have been his had he done evil, so that he may be all the more grateful. And no one enters the Fire without being shown the seat in Jannah that would have been his had he done good, so that it may be a source of anguish for him."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On "had he done evil," Ibn Hajar says: "that is, had he done an evil deed, namely disbelief, and so become one of the people of the Fire." On "so that he may be all the more grateful," he says: "that is, more joyful and content. It is expressed through what necessarily accompanies it, because whoever is pleased with something gives thanks to the one who did it for him." To those who objected that Jannah is an abode of reward, not of gratitude, al-'Ayni replied that "the gratitude is not by way of obligation, but by way of delight."[^3]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-23-10-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "It Is They Who Are the Inheritors"

> **{أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْوَارِثُونَ ۝ الَّذِينَ يَرِثُونَ الْفِرْدَوْسَ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [المؤمنون: ١٠-١١][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"It is they who are the inheritors, who will inherit al-Firdaws, abiding in it forever."** (al-Mu'minun 23:10-11)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

These two ayat come after the qualities of the successful believers listed at the start of the surah: humility in prayer, turning away from idle talk, paying zakah, guarding their chastity, keeping their trusts, and guarding their prayers. As-Sa'di says that al-Firdaws is "the highest part of Jannah, its middle and its best," or that what is meant is "all of Jannah, so that the believers in general are included, according to their degrees and ranks, each according to his state."[^4]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="ibnmajah-4341" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Two Homes"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«مَا مِنْكُمْ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا لَهُ مَنْزِلَانِ: مَنْزِلٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَمَنْزِلٌ فِي النَّارِ، فَإِذَا مَاتَ فَدَخَلَ النَّارَ وَرِثَ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ مَنْزِلَهُ، فَذَلِكَ قَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: {أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْوَارِثُونَ}»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"There is not one of you who does not have two homes: a home in Jannah and a home in the Fire. If he dies and enters the Fire, the people of Jannah inherit his home. That is the meaning of His words, exalted is He: 'It is they who are the inheritors.'"**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites this hadith under the ayah and then says: "So the believers inherit the homes of the disbelievers, because they were [all] created to worship Allah the Exalted. When these believers carried out the worship required of them, and those others abandoned what they had been commanded, the very thing for which they were created, these believers secured the share those others would have had if they had obeyed their Lord, Mighty and Majestic is He."[^4] Ibn Hajar cites al-Qurtubi: "It may be that attaining Jannah is called inheritance in the sense that they alone are given it, to the exclusion of others; so it is inheritance by way of metaphor."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith reveals how vast Allah's mercy is: every human being has a home in Jannah prepared for him, should he believe and obey, and no one is denied it except the one who turns away from the purpose for which he was created. Whoever inherits that home has wronged no one by taking it. He has inherited what its owner left behind.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The people of Jannah talk over their life in this world; Allah shows them what He saved them from; and they inherit what others left behind. All of this increases their joy and their gratitude. But none of it turns that gratitude into gloating here in this world. The Qur'an told us about a companion who died in denial, but we do not know how any living person's life will end, and we do not condemn any particular individual to the Fire. Instead we call him to the truth and pray for him. A believer's steadfastness is a blessing he cannot guarantee for himself, which is why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would often say: «يَا مُقَلِّبَ الْقُلُوبِ ثَبِّتْ قَلْبِي عَلَى دِينِكَ», "O Turner of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion."[^6] The practical question, then: What voice is sowing doubt in me today, and what story do I want to tell about it in Jannah?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What do the people of Jannah ask one another about, according to Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di? And what does it signify that the ayah leaves the subject of their questions unstated?

2. How did Ibn Kathir reconcile Mujahid's view that the companion was a devil with Ibn 'Abbas's view that he was an idolater?

3. Why did the man credit his rescue to "the favor of my Lord" rather than to himself?

4. What does "so that he may be all the more grateful" mean according to Ibn Hajar, and how did al-'Ayni answer the objection that Jannah is an abode of reward?

5. How does the hadith of the two homes bring together the vastness of Allah's mercy and His justice? And why may we never condemn a particular living person to the Fire?

### Activity: The Story I Will Tell in Jannah

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.075.activity.the-story-i-will-tell-in-jannah" -->

On a private page, write a story of steadfastness that you hope to tell your brothers and sisters in Jannah, in the past tense: "I had... and he used to tell me... and Allah kept me firm when...". The "companion" may be a friend, an account you follow, a habit, or the whisperings of your own soul; do not name anyone. Then pinpoint the decision point that will make this ending true: a situation that will come up again this week in which you face that voice, and write down what you will do when it does. Act on it for seven days, and each day note: Did the situation come up? What did I do? On the seventh day, write a paragraph: Where did I see "the favor of my Lord" in my steadfastness? And if the companion is a person, write a du'a asking Allah to guide them.

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

Have you ever heard your grandpa or grandma tell stories about when they were little? They say, "We used to walk such a long way to school!" Then they smile and say, "Alhamdulillah."

In Jannah, the believers sit together on beautiful seats too, happy, and they talk about their days in this world: what life was like, and how Allah helped them.[^1] The Qur'an tells us the story of one of them.

### From the Qur'an: "Had It Not Been for the Favor of My Lord"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-37-50-61" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In a surah called "as-Saffat," Allah tells us about a man from the people of Jannah.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-37-50-61" audience="4-7" -->

The man sat with his brothers in Jannah, and they were happy, talking together. He said, "In the world, I had a friend. He used to say to me, 'Do you really believe that when we die and turn into dust, we'll come back and be asked about what we did?'" His friend didn't believe in the Day of Judgment, and he wanted the man to stop believing too. But the man stayed a believer.

Then Allah showed him his old friend from far away. The friend wasn't in Jannah. He was in the Fire. The man said to him, "By Allah, you almost ruined me!" Then he said beautiful words of thanks: «وَلَوْلَا نِعْمَةُ رَبِّي لَكُنْتُ مِنَ الْمُحْضَرِينَ». That means: "If Allah hadn't been so kind to me and kept me strong in Islam, I would have been there with you." Then he was filled with joy and said: here we will never die, ever. «إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ», "This is truly the greatest win of all!"[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Look: the man didn't say, "I'm so smart." He said, "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord." Today, we choose friends who love what is good. And if someone says something to us that pulls us away from Allah, we don't follow them, and we say, "Alhamdulillah for Islam!"

<!-- story:end -->

### Activity: My Beautiful Story

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.075.activity.the-story-i-will-tell-in-jannah" -->

Fold a sheet of paper in half with your mom or dad. On the first half, draw a time when someone asks you to do something wrong, like "Take the toy without asking," or "Don't get up to pray, keep playing!" Then draw yourself choosing what is good. On the second half, draw yourself in a beautiful garden, telling this story to your family and saying, "Alhamdulillah, Allah helped me." Then pick, together, something that keeps happening at home. For three days, every time it happens and you choose what is good, say, "Alhamdulillah, Allah helped me," and put a star on your paper.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

At recess, Yusuf got up to pray Dhuhr in a corner of the classroom. His classmate Ziyad laughed and said, in front of everyone, "You still believe all that? Who says there's anything after you die?" Yusuf's face went red, and he wished he had just stayed in his seat. Then he remembered that he was praying for Allah, not for Ziyad, and he finished his prayer.

At home he asked his dad, "Will I remember this day in Jannah, in sha' Allah?" His dad said, "The people of Jannah remember their days in this world, and they tell the stories to one another.[^1] And there's a story in the Qur'an that's a lot like yours."

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-37-50-61" -->

- **`Constant companion (qarin)`** — a companion who never leaves your side; Ibn 'Abbas said he was an idolater who was the believer's friend in this world, Mujahid said he was a devil, and Ibn Kathir brought the two views together.[^1]
- **`Looking down (muttali'un)`** — looking down from above, seeing along with me; Ibn Kathir explained it as "looking down from above."[^1]
- **`The midst of the Blazing Fire (sawa' al-jahim)`** — the middle of the Fire, as Ibn Kathir reported from Ibn 'Abbas and others.[^1]
- **`Bring me to ruin (la-turdin)`** — destroy me; as-Sa'di said: through the doubts you planted in me.[^2]

### From the Qur'an: Two Friends, Two Endings

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-37-50-61" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an, and any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah as-Saffat describes one of the gatherings of the people of Jannah.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-37-50-61" audience="8-12" -->

«فَأَقْبَلَ بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ», "Then they will turn to one another, asking each other questions": they ask each other about their lives in this world as they sit on their couches. One of them says: «إِنِّي كَانَ لِي قَرِينٌ», "I had a companion." He used to ask me, amazed and mocking: Are you really one of those people who believe that once we've died and become dust and bones, we'll be raised up and repaid for what we did? As-Sa'di said it is as if he were telling his brothers: "This is my story, and this is my news: me and my companion."

Then he says to his brothers: «هَلْ أَنْتُمْ مُطَّلِعُونَ», "Will you look down?" He looks down and sees his companion in the middle of the Fire. He says to him: «تَاللَّهِ إِنْ كِدْتَ لَتُرْدِينِ», "By Allah, you very nearly brought me to ruin!", meaning: you almost destroyed me, if I had listened to you. Then he thanks his Lord: «وَلَوْلَا نِعْمَةُ رَبِّي لَكُنْتُ مِنَ الْمُحْضَرِينَ», "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord, I would have been among those brought [to it]." As-Sa'di explained that this favor was Allah keeping him firm upon Islam. Then he rejoices that he will never die again and never be punished, and says: «إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ ۝ لِمِثْلِ هَٰذَا فَلْيَعْمَلِ الْعَامِلُونَ», "This is truly the supreme triumph. For the like of this, let all who strive, strive."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that everyone who enters Jannah is shown the place that would have been his had he done evil, "so that he may be all the more grateful."[^3] And he told us that every person has a home in Jannah and a home in the Fire. Whoever turns away from faith leaves his home in Jannah behind, and the people of Jannah inherit it.[^5]

Notice: the man didn't win because his arguments were stronger than his companion's. He won because Allah kept him firm. Yusuf didn't need to beat Ziyad in a debate either. It is enough for him to stay firm and answer kindly, "Yes, I do believe, and I pray good things for you," and then ask Allah to keep him steady. And we never say that Ziyad, or anyone else, belongs in the Fire, because we don't know how anyone's life will end.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

These days the doubt may not come from someone arguing with you face to face. It may come from a short clip mocking belief in the Hereafter, from a group chat laughing at "the religious ones," or from a thought that keeps coming back to you at night: Is any of this real? And the most dangerous voice is usually not the loudest one. It is the closest: a friend you love, an account you check every single day.

The Qur'an takes you to the end of the story: a gathering in Jannah where a man remembers a companion who used to say exactly that to him. That ending holds three lessons: steadfastness is a gift from Allah, gratitude is not the same as gloating, and your own story is being written right now.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-37-50-61" -->

- **`Constant companion (qarin)`** — the companion who sticks close to you; Ibn Kathir said he may be from the jinn, whispering within the soul, or from humankind, saying words the ears can hear.[^1]
- **`Brought to account (la-madinun)`** — called to account, or repaid for our deeds, as Ibn Kathir reported from the scholars of tafsir.[^1]
- **`Those brought [to it] (al-muhdarin)`** — those brought into the punishment, as as-Sa'di said: "into the punishment along with you."[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: The Voice That Almost Ruined Him

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-37-50-61" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an, and any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

The scene in Surah as-Saffat: the people of Jannah on their couches, turning to one another and talking.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-37-50-61" audience="13+" -->

One of them says: «إِنِّي كَانَ لِي قَرِينٌ ۝ يَقُولُ أَإِنَّكَ لَمِنَ الْمُصَدِّقِينَ ۝ أَإِذَا مِتْنَا وَكُنَّا تُرَابًا وَعِظَامًا أَإِنَّا لَمَدِينُونَ», "I had a companion who used to say, 'Are you really one of those who believe it? When we have died and become dust and bones, are we really going to be brought to account?'" Ibn Kathir said he used to say this "by way of astonishment, denial, and dismissing it as far-fetched." Notice the technique: it isn't an argument. It's a sneering question designed to make you feel like the only naive person in the room.

Then he says to the people sitting with him: «هَلْ أَنْتُمْ مُطَّلِعُونَ», "Will you look down?" «فَاطَّلَعَ فَرَآهُ فِي سَوَاءِ الْجَحِيمِ», "Then he looks down and sees him in the midst of the Blazing Fire." And he says to him: «تَاللَّهِ إِنْ كِدْتَ لَتُرْدِينِ ۝ وَلَوْلَا نِعْمَةُ رَبِّي لَكُنْتُ مِنَ الْمُحْضَرِينَ», "By Allah, you very nearly brought me to ruin! Had it not been for the favor of my Lord, I would have been among those brought [to it]." As-Sa'di said he spoke these words "reproaching him for his state, and thanking Allah for His favor in saving him from his scheming," and that the favor was that Allah "kept me firm upon Islam." Then he closes with the joy of eternal life: «إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ ۝ لِمِثْلِ هَٰذَا فَلْيَعْمَلِ الْعَامِلُونَ», "This is truly the supreme triumph. For the like of this, let all who strive, strive."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

An authentic hadith completes the picture: everyone who enters Jannah is shown the seat in the Fire that would have been his had he done evil, "so that he may be all the more grateful," which Ibn Hajar explained as joy and contentment.[^3] And every person has a home in Jannah and a home in the Fire; whoever dies having turned away, the people of Jannah inherit his home there.[^5]

Keep two limits in mind. The Qur'an told us about a companion who died in denial, but you do not know how your living friends' lives will end, so never condemn anyone to the Fire; pray for them instead. And the man in Jannah did not say, "I was smarter than him." He said, "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord." Steadfastness isn't guaranteed by self-confidence. It is something you ask Allah for.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why was the companion's question mockery rather than an argument? Where do you hear questions in that same style today?

2. What is the difference between the gratitude of the man in Jannah and gloating over others?

3. What does it mean that steadfastness is a "favor" from Allah and not just a personal achievement?

4. How does the hadith "so that he may be all the more grateful" change the way you look at the sins Allah has saved you from?

5. Who is the "companion" closest to you today: a person, an account, or a thought? And what is the first step in dealing with it?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Drafting My Story in Jannah

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.075.activity.the-story-i-will-tell-in-jannah" -->

In a private note, write a paragraph in the past tense, as if you were telling it to your friends in Jannah: "I had a companion... he used to tell me... and one day... Allah kept me firm." The companion might be a person, an account, or a recurring thought; don't write any names. Then pinpoint this week's decision point: the situation in which you will run into this voice, and what you will do: reply with one calm sentence, mute the account, or ask a knowledgeable person you trust. Act on it for seven days, and each day log it in one word: held firm, stumbled, or didn't come up. At the end, rewrite the paragraph with what actually happened, and add one line, "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord...", along with a du'a for guidance for the owner of the voice, if it is a person.

<!-- activity:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.bedtime-dua" kind="bedtime_dua" -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.075.dua.turner-of-hearts-make-my-heart-firm" provenance="sunnah" source_id="tirmidhi-2140" attribution="prophetic" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to say this du'a often, and Anas asked him, "We believe in you and in what you have brought. Are you afraid for us?" He said, "Yes." If that was how he feared for his own Companions, we need this du'a even more.

> **يَا مُقَلِّبَ الْقُلُوبِ ثَبِّتْ قَلْبِي عَلَى دِينِكَ**[^6]
>
> *"O Turner of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion."*
>
> What it means: O You in whose hand is the turning of hearts, make my heart firm upon Your religion, so that no doubt, no desire, and no mockery can sway it.[^6]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the conversation in as-Saffat 37:50-61 in order; presents the two views of Mujahid and Ibn 'Abbas on *qarin* and Ibn Kathir's reconciliation of them; explains "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord" through the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; draws evidence from al-Bukhari 6569 with the commentary of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni; explains the inheritance of homes through the ayah of al-Mu'minun and the hadith of Ibn Majah 4341, including its grading; distinguishes gratitude from gloating; and begins the seven-day activity "The Story I Will Tell in Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with three columns headed "Remembering this world," "Seeing what Allah saved them from," and "Inheriting homes"; the activity sheet; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on as-Saffat 37:50-61 and al-Mu'minun 23:10-11, and the commentary of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni on al-Bukhari 6569, and reviews the grading of Ibn Majah 4341 in the References. The teacher decides in advance to describe the scene of looking down using the Qur'an's own words alone, without the details found in reports this lesson does not rely on.[^7]

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "If you were sitting with your brothers and sisters in Jannah, which story from your life would you most want to tell?" Listen to two or three answers without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of as-Saffat in full, then pause at three stations: the questioning, the companion and his mocking question, and the looking down and the thanks, with the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (10 minutes). Then the hadith of al-Bukhari 6569 and its commentary (4 minutes). Then the ayah of al-Mu'minun, the hadith of the two homes, Ibn Kathir's reasoning, and al-Qurtubi's statement (6 minutes). Fill in the three columns on the board during the reading.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions three and five. The teacher makes clear that the Qur'an's report about a companion who died in denial does not permit passing judgment on any particular living person; that seeing what Allah saved them from increases the people of Jannah in gratitude and contentment, not in hardness of heart; and that steadfastness is sought from Allah through du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes a draft of their story in the past tense and pinpoints the decision point and what they will do at it. Anyone who wishes may share their decision point only, never the private story.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What are the two views on the meaning of *qarin*?", "What does 'so that he may be all the more grateful' mean?" and "Why do we not condemn a living person to the Fire?" Then recite the du'a «يَا مُقَلِّبَ الْقُلُوبِ», explaining that it is a du'a of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, ayat 50-57 and the hadith of the two homes are enough. Ask advanced learners to trace the difference of opinion between Qatadah and Ibn Jarir over who speaks the words "For the like of this, let all who strive, strive," and to explain why this lesson does not rely on the Isra'iliyyat stories of the two partners that Ibn Kathir cites under the ayah.[^7]

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not dwell on descriptions of the Fire, and do not narrate the unrelied-upon reports about what the man saw.[^7] Do not pronounce the Fire on any specific person, and do not let the lesson become material for mocking non-Muslims or learners' relatives who have not been guided; direct the class instead toward praying for them and treating them well. Be gentle with anyone who has lost a relative whose state they do not know: reassure them of Allah's justice and mercy, and remind them that judgment belongs to Allah alone. Anyone struggling with genuine doubts should be listened to respectfully and referred to someone who can answer with knowledge; they must not be treated like the denying companion.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah talk about their days in this world; retells in their own words that a man had a friend who did not believe in the Day of Judgment, and that the man stayed a believer and thanked Allah; repeats «وَلَوْلَا نِعْمَةُ رَبِّي»; chooses what is good in a situation that keeps happening and says, "Alhamdulillah, Allah helped me"; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A sheet of paper folded in half for each child; crayons; star stickers; two cushions or two chairs facing each other to stand for the gathering where people talk; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the story in a calm, reassuring voice, prepares two examples of simple situations from children's lives, and sends home a note to parents explaining the three-day activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The educator sits on the chair facing the children and tells a short memory from their own childhood that ends with "Alhamdulillah," then asks: "Has your grandpa or grandma ever told you a story from when they were little?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The educator reads the introduction and then the story from the Qur'an, and the children repeat «وَلَوْلَا نِعْمَةُ رَبِّي», then «إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَهُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ».

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions, then have two children act out a situation: one says, "Don't get up to pray, keep playing!" and the other answers kindly, "I'll pray and then come back," and everyone says, "Alhamdulillah, Allah helped him."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child draws both halves of the paper with the educator's help and takes it home to add stars for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The educator observes whether each child can say what the man said to thank Allah, and what they will say if someone invites them to do something wrong. Then read the du'a one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, it is enough that the man stayed a believer and said, "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord." Ask older children to retell the whole story, from the gathering to "the supreme triumph."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the Fire in a single word, as the story does, with no description, picture, or frightening sound, and never threaten a child with it. Never tell a child that a friend or relative of theirs will be in the Fire. If a child asks about a relative who is not Muslim or who has died, answer calmly: "Allah is the Most Merciful of those who show mercy and the Most Just of judges, and we pray for people to be guided."

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the conversation in as-Saffat in order; defines the four terms; states in their own words the two hadiths "so that he may be all the more grateful" and the two homes; composes a kind, steady answer for a pressure situation; and begins the five-day activity "My Story Card."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; four cards for the scenes of the story (the gathering, the companion and his question, the looking down, the thanks and the joy); the three-box activity card; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on as-Saffat 37:50-61 and prepares three examples of familiar school pressure situations, each with a kind, steady answer.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the story of Yusuf and Ziyad and asks: "How did Yusuf feel? And why did he finish his prayer?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the story from the Qur'an while putting the scene cards in order, pointing out the difference between the wording of the ayah and the words of the scholars of tafsir (6 minutes), then the two hadiths (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss questions three and five. The teacher explains that steadfastness is a gift from Allah, that a kind answer is stronger than an angry argument, and that we never say anyone belongs in the Fire.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The teacher presents one of the prepared examples; then each student fills in the boxes on their card and chooses the situation that keeps happening.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The man in Jannah said: Had it not been for..." and "My steady answer is...". Then read the du'a. After five days, anyone who wishes may share their final sentence.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give students who are struggling a ready-made answer to choose from three options. Ask advanced students to memorize ayat 55-61 and explain "For the like of this, let all who strive, strive" in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe what the man saw in the Fire beyond the words "the middle of the Fire." Do not allow real classmates to be named on the cards or in discussion. Point out that a steady answer does not mean picking a fight or mocking back. If a student reveals repeated bullying because of their religion, listen to them privately and inform the person responsible.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the conversation in as-Saffat and identifies the companion's technique (mockery, not argument); explains *qarin*, *la-madinun*, and *al-muhdarin*; distinguishes gratitude from gloating, and the Qur'an's report about a dead man from judging a living one; connects steadfastness with the Prophetic du'a; and begins the seven-day activity "Drafting My Story in Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; cards for anonymous answers; a sheet with two columns: "Argument" and "Mockery."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on these ayat and Ibn Hajar's commentary on al-Bukhari 6569, prepares two examples of content that mocks faith (with no names and no links), and identifies a trustworthy person or service to refer anyone with serious questions to.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What's the most mocking line about religion you've ever heard or read? Was it an argument, or was it mockery?" Students write anonymous answers, which are sorted into the two columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the story from the Qur'an with three pauses: the companion's technique, the man's gratitude, and the joy of eternal life; then the two hadiths with Ibn Hajar's commentary.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 2, and 5, followed by brief reports. The teacher guides students toward three points: a serious question deserves respect and an answer sought from people of knowledge; mockery is not answered with mockery; and steadfastness is something we ask Allah for.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes a draft of their own private story and pinpoints the decision point and what they will do at it.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: how Ibn Kathir reconciled the two views about the companion, why the man said "Had it not been for the favor of my Lord," and what their decision point is this week. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see anyone's notes. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may record a private voice note instead. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on as-Sa'di's remark that leaving the subject of their questions unstated shows that they ask one another "about everything they delight in talking about."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the punishment, and do not narrate the unrelied-upon reports.[^7] Do not encourage a student to cut off family members or friends who are not practicing; encourage steadfastness, kindness, and praying for them instead. Distinguish between someone who mocks and someone struggling with sincere doubt: the second is to be embraced and helped. If it emerges that a student is facing intense pressure or isolation because of their religion, the teacher follows up confidentially in line with the child protection policy.
