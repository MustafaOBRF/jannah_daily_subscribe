# The Food and Drink of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Read the scene in Surah at-Tur (52:17-28) and retell it in order: the righteous in gardens and bliss, delighting in what their Lord has given them; the call to them, "Eat and drink with wholesome delight for what you used to do"; and then their turning to one another to talk, recalling how they feared Allah among their families in this world, how He showed them His favor, and how they used to call upon Him.
- Explain the meaning of *hani'an*, "with wholesome delight," from the words of at-Tabari and as-Sa'di: food and drink enjoyed with gladness and joy, with no fear of harm or ill effect, and with nothing to spoil it and no end to it.
- Show from the hadith of Jabir in Sahih Muslim (2835) that the people of Jannah truly eat and drink, and that their food leaves them as a belch and a sweat like the fragrance of musk, with no waste; together with an-Nawawi's statement that their bliss shares nothing with the bliss of this world except the name and the basic form.
- Connect at-Tur with al-Mursalat (77:41-44), where the same call is made, and reconcile "for what you used to do" with the hadith of Aishah in Sahih al-Bukhari (6467): deeds are a cause, but entry into Jannah is by Allah's mercy.
- Carry out the "Question at the Table" activity: for a set period, closing a daily shared meal with a question each person asks another about a good deed or a du'a from their day, recording the answers honestly, and reading them back at the end of the period.
- Learn by heart a lesson-authored bedtime du'a asking Allah to make our food a help in obeying Him, and to feed us and give us drink in Jannah with wholesome delight.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In this world, food is a blessing that never comes without some toil. We earn it by working, we cook it with effort, and afterward it may sit heavy in the stomach or even make us ill; and it always leaves waste behind that has to be cleaned away. In Jannah there is real food and real drink, but with none of that.

An earlier lesson looked at the first hospitality the people of Jannah receive when they enter it. This lesson looks at their food and drink from then on, forever: how the Qur'an describes it, how it passes from them without any harm, and what the people of Jannah talk about as they enjoy their bliss.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-17-28" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Eat and Drink with Wholesome Delight for What You Used to Do"

> **{إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي جَنَّاتٍ وَنَعِيمٍ ۝ فَاكِهِينَ بِمَا آتَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ وَوَقَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ عَذَابَ الْجَحِيمِ ۝ كُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا هَنِيئًا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ ۝ مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ سُرُرٍ مَصْفُوفَةٍ ۖ وَزَوَّجْنَاهُمْ بِحُورٍ عِينٍ ۝ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَاتَّبَعَتْهُمْ ذُرِّيَّتُهُمْ بِإِيمَانٍ أَلْحَقْنَا بِهِمْ ذُرِّيَّتَهُمْ وَمَا أَلَتْنَاهُمْ مِنْ عَمَلِهِمْ مِنْ شَيْءٍ ۚ كُلُّ امْرِئٍ بِمَا كَسَبَ رَهِينٌ ۝ وَأَمْدَدْنَاهُمْ بِفَاكِهَةٍ وَلَحْمٍ مِمَّا يَشْتَهُونَ ۝ يَتَنَازَعُونَ فِيهَا كَأْسًا لَا لَغْوٌ فِيهَا وَلَا تَأْثِيمٌ ۝ وَيَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ غِلْمَانٌ لَهُمْ كَأَنَّهُمْ لُؤْلُؤٌ مَكْنُونٌ ۝ وَأَقْبَلَ بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ يَتَسَاءَلُونَ ۝ قَالُوا إِنَّا كُنَّا قَبْلُ فِي أَهْلِنَا مُشْفِقِينَ ۝ فَمَنَّ اللَّهُ عَلَيْنَا وَوَقَانَا عَذَابَ السَّمُومِ ۝ إِنَّا كُنَّا مِنْ قَبْلُ نَدْعُوهُ ۖ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْبَرُّ الرَّحِيمُ}** [الطور: ١٧-٢٨][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, the righteous will be in gardens and bliss, delighting in what their Lord has given them, and their Lord will have shielded them from the punishment of the Blazing Fire. 'Eat and drink with wholesome delight for what you used to do.' They will recline on couches set in rows, and We will pair them with *hur* of wide, lovely eyes. And those who believed, and whose descendants followed them in faith, We will join their descendants to them, and We will not deprive them of anything of their deeds; every person is held in pledge for what he has earned. And We will keep supplying them with fruit and meat, whatever they desire. There they will pass to one another a cup that brings no idle talk and no sin. And young servants of theirs will circulate among them, as if they were pearls kept hidden. And they will turn to one another, asking each other. They will say, 'Before this, among our families, we used to live in fear. So Allah showed us His favor and shielded us from the punishment of the scorching wind. Before this, we used to call upon Him. Indeed, He is the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.'"** (at-Tur 52:17-28)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that *fakihin* means "they take their delight in the bliss Allah has given them, in every kind of pleasure, in food and in drink," and that being saved from the Fire is "a blessing in its own right." On "Eat and drink with wholesome delight," he says: "This in return for that, as a favor and a kindness from Him." *Yatasa'alun* means "they talk together and ask one another about their deeds and their states in this world," and *mushfiqin* means "afraid of our Lord, fearful of His punishment and His penalty."[^1]

At-Tabari says of *hani'an*: "You need fear no harm and no ill effect from anything you eat or drink there."[^2] As-Sa'di says that *hani'an* means "enjoying that food and drink with gladness and joy." On their words "we used to live in fear," he says: "So out of fear of Him we left sins behind, and for the same reason we mended our faults"; and "we used to call upon Him" "covers both the du'a of worship and the du'a of asking."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The scene is a meal followed by a conversation. The people of Jannah do not talk about the food. They talk about the road that brought them to it: fearing Allah while they were at home among their families, a du'a that never stopped, and then Allah's favor. The talk at the table has become thanks.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-77-41-44" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Fruits of Whatever They Desire"

> **{إِنَّ الْمُتَّقِينَ فِي ظِلَالٍ وَعُيُونٍ ۝ وَفَوَاكِهَ مِمَّا يَشْتَهُونَ ۝ كُلُوا وَاشْرَبُوا هَنِيئًا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ ۝ إِنَّا كَذَٰلِكَ نَجْزِي الْمُحْسِنِينَ}** [المرسلات: ٤١-٤٤][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, the righteous will be amid shade and springs, and fruits of whatever they desire. 'Eat and drink with wholesome delight for what you used to do.' Indeed, this is how We reward those who do good."** (al-Mursalat 77:41-44)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "This is said to them as an act of kindness toward them."[^4] As-Sa'di says that *hani'an* means "with nothing to spoil it or cloud it; and its delight is not complete until the food and drink are free of every blight and every lack, and until they are certain that it will never be cut off and never come to an end."[^3]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2835" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "A Belch, and a Sweat Like the Fragrance of Musk"

> عَنْ جَابِرٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: **«إِنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَأْكُلُونَ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ، وَلَا يَتْفُلُونَ، وَلَا يَبُولُونَ، وَلَا يَتَغَوَّطُونَ، وَلَا يَمْتَخِطُونَ»**. قَالُوا: فَمَا بَالُ الطَّعَامِ؟ قَالَ: **«جُشَاءٌ وَرَشْحٌ كَرَشْحِ الْمِسْكِ، يُلْهَمُونَ التَّسْبِيحَ وَالتَّحْمِيدَ كَمَا يُلْهَمُونَ النَّفَسَ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jabir, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"The people of Jannah will eat and drink there, but they will not spit, they will not urinate, they will not relieve themselves, and they will not blow their noses."** They asked, "Then what becomes of the food?" He said: **"A belch, and a sweat like the fragrance of musk. Glorifying and praising Allah will come to them as naturally as breathing."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi says: "The position of the people of the Sunnah and of Muslims at large is that the people of Jannah eat and drink there." They enjoy it "with an enjoyment that is everlasting and has no end," in the same manner as the people of this world enjoy things, except for the difference between the two in delight and in excellence, a delight "which shares nothing with the bliss of this world except the name and the basic form," and except that they do not urinate, relieve themselves, blow their noses or spit.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Companions asked a practical question: where does the food go? The answer came back: a belch, and a sweet-smelling sweat. This is *hani'an* explained in practice: no harm while eating, and none afterward. And then glorifying and praising Allah come without any effort at all, the way breath comes.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6467" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Unless Allah Covers Me"

> عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«سَدِّدُوا وَقَارِبُوا، وَأَبْشِرُوا، فَإِنَّهُ لَا يُدْخِلُ أَحَدًا الْجَنَّةَ عَمَلُهُ»**. قَالُوا: وَلَا أَنْتَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟ قَالَ: **«وَلَا أَنَا، إِلَّا أَنْ يَتَغَمَّدَنِيَ اللَّهُ بِمَغْفِرَةٍ وَرَحْمَةٍ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Aim for what is right, and come as close to it as you can, and rejoice in good news, for no one's deeds will admit him into Jannah."** They said, "Not even you, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: **"Not even me, unless Allah covers me with forgiveness and mercy."**[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The *ba'* in "for what you used to do" (*bima kuntum ta'malun*) is the *ba'* of cause, not the *ba'* of price, which is why Ibn Kathir says "as a favor and a kindness from Him." The people of Jannah say so themselves: "So Allah showed us His favor," and they close with "Indeed, He is the Most Kind, the Most Merciful."

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of at-Tur and al-Mursalat describe real food and real drink, and their people are told, as an act of kindness: "Eat and drink with wholesome delight." The hadith of Jabir explains how the food is wholesome: no waste and no harm, only glorification and praise, as natural as breathing. The ayat of at-Tur show what the people of Jannah talk about as they enjoy their bliss: their fear of Allah among their families, their du'a, and Allah's favor upon them. And the hadith of Aishah sets the balance right: we work and we hope, and the grace belongs to Allah. So the practical question is this: what do we talk about at our own tables today?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What does *hani'an* mean according to at-Tabari and as-Sa'di, and how does the hadith of Jabir explain it?

2. According to Ibn Kathir, what did the people of Jannah ask one another about? And why did they mention that they used to live in fear "among our families" in particular?

3. According to an-Nawawi, what does the food of Jannah have in common with the food of this world, and how does it differ?

4. How do we reconcile "for what you used to do" with the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him, "No one's deeds will admit him into Jannah"? And what is the significance of their saying, "So Allah showed us His favor"?

5. What do people usually talk about at your table? And what would you like to hear talked about there?

### Activity: The Question at the Table

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.058.activity.turn-and-ask-at-the-table" -->

For seven days, choose one daily meal you share with your family or with the people you live with. Begin it in the name of Allah and close it with praise of Him. Then ask the people with you one fixed question, the same every day: "What good did you do today for the sake of Allah?" or "What did you ask Allah for today?" Answer it yourself as well. In a small notebook, record your own answer only, and if you have no honest answer, leave the line blank. Make at least one day connected to food itself: feeding someone in need, sharing a meal with a neighbor, or cutting back on waste. On the seventh day, read your notebook and write a paragraph: how did the talk at the table change, and where did you see Allah's favor upon you?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

What is the yummiest food you love? Here in this world we eat and drink, and we get full and happy. But sometimes our tummy hurts when we eat too much, and afterward we need to go to the bathroom and wash our hands.

In Jannah there is real food and real drink, tastier than anything we have ever had. The Companions once asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: where does the food of the people of Jannah go? He told them that they never need the bathroom at all. The food just leaves them as a little burp, and as a sweat that smells like musk, which is a beautiful perfume. And they say "SubhanAllah" and "Alhamdulillah" easily, the way we breathe.[^5]

### From the Qur'an: What the People of Jannah Talk About as They Eat

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-52-17-28" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a real scene from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In a surah called "at-Tur," Allah tells us about the people who loved Him and were afraid of disobeying Him, and what they will be like in Jannah.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-52-17-28" audience="4-7" -->

They are in beautiful gardens, happy with everything their Lord has given them. And they are told: "Eat and drink with wholesome delight." That means: eat and drink and be happy, with no tiredness and no tummy ache, because of the good things you used to do. And Allah gives them all the fruit and meat they love.

Then they turn to each other and start talking. They say: "Back in the world, when we were with our families, we were afraid of making Allah angry. And we used to make du'a to Him all the time. So Allah was kind to us, and He kept us safe from the Fire. He is the Most Kind, the Most Merciful."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Look: while the people of Jannah are eating, they remember Allah and thank Him. You can do that too. At your table you can say "Alhamdulillah," and tell your family about a good thing you did today.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On a long school trip, the students ate a lot of fast food. On the way home, some of them started complaining of stomachaches, and the bus kept pulling over to look for restrooms. The food had been delicious, but it was not what the Qur'an calls *hani'an*: a pleasure with nothing to spoil it.

This lesson is about the food and drink of the people of Jannah. We will read a scene from Surah at-Tur, learn the word *hani'an*, and find out what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said when the Companions asked him, "Then what becomes of the food?" In other words, where does it go? He said: "A belch, and a sweat like the fragrance of musk. Glorifying and praising Allah will come to them as naturally as breathing." So there is no bathroom and no discomfort, just a light burp and a sweat that smells like musk, and words of praise that flow from their tongues the way breath flows.[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-52-17-28" -->

- **`Delighting (fakihin)`** — happy and enjoying what Allah has given them, as as-Sa'di explains.[^3]
- **`With wholesome delight (hani'an)`** — with no fear of any harm or ill effect, as at-Tabari explains.[^2]
- **`Asking each other (yatasa'alun)`** — talking together and asking one another about their deeds and their lives in this world, as Ibn Kathir explains.[^1]
- **`Living in fear (mushfiqin)`** — afraid of Allah and in awe of Him, so that they gave up sins because of it, as as-Sa'di explains.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: "Before This, Among Our Families, We Used to Live in Fear"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-52-17-28" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a real scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

Just before this scene, Surah at-Tur talks about the people who denied the truth. Then it turns to the righteous, the people who feared Allah and obeyed Him. So what will they find?[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-52-17-28" audience="8-12" -->

"Indeed, the righteous will be in gardens and bliss, delighting in what their Lord has given them." Ibn Kathir explains that they enjoy every kind of pleasure Allah has given them, in food and in drink. And Allah has saved them from the Fire, which is a blessing all by itself.

Then they are told: "Eat and drink with wholesome delight for what you used to do." Allah keeps supplying them with fruit and meat, whatever they desire, and they pass around a cup that brings no foolish talk and no sin.

And after that: "And they will turn to one another, asking each other." What do they talk about? They say: "Before this, among our families, we used to live in fear. So Allah showed us His favor and shielded us from the punishment of the scorching wind. Before this, we used to call upon Him. Indeed, He is the Most Kind, the Most Merciful." As-Sa'di explains that they had been afraid, and they say: "So out of fear of Him we left sins behind, and for the same reason we mended our faults." And they used to call upon Allah and draw near to Him at all times.[^1][^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice: they feared Allah at home, among their own families, and not only in front of other people. And they did not say, "We earned this." They said: "So Allah showed us His favor." Our deeds are a cause, but the grace belongs to Allah, just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "No one's deeds will admit him into Jannah."[^6]

<!-- story:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.058.dua.food-a-help-to-obey-feed-us-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ مَا نَأْكُلُ وَنَشْرَبُ عَوْنًا لَنَا عَلَى طَاعَتِكَ، وَأَطْعِمْنَا وَاسْقِنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ هَنِيئًا بِرَحْمَتِكَ.
>
> *"O Allah, make what we eat and drink a help for us in obeying You, and feed us and give us drink in Your Jannah with wholesome delight, by Your mercy."*

What it means: O Allah, let everything we eat and drink give us strength to worship You and obey You. And in Jannah, give us food and drink that is wholesome and brings no harm at all, by Your mercy and not because of our deeds alone.

<!-- bedtime-dua:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A photo of a beautifully plated dish under perfect lighting racks up thousands of likes. The photo doesn't show what the dish cost, or how heavy your stomach felt afterward, or how much of it ended up in the trash. Nor does it show what the people around the table talked about, or whether they talked at all, each of them looking down at a phone.

The Qur'an describes the food of Jannah with one word that sums up the whole difference: *hani'an*, "with wholesome delight." At-Tabari explains it: "You need fear no harm and no ill effect from anything you eat or drink there."[^2] And in Sahih Muslim, the Companions asked, "Then what becomes of the food?" The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "A belch, and a sweat like the fragrance of musk. Glorifying and praising Allah will come to them as naturally as breathing."[^5] An-Nawawi says that this is real eating and real drinking, but it shares nothing with the bliss of this world "except the name and the basic form."[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-52-17-28" -->

- **`Living in fear (mushfiqin)`** — afraid of Allah and dreading His punishment, as Ibn Kathir explains.[^1]
- **`The scorching wind (as-samum)`** — a punishment of fierce, searing heat, as as-Sa'di explains.[^3]
- **`We used to call upon Him (nad'uhu)`** — covering both the du'a of worship and the du'a of asking, meaning every act that draws one near to Allah as well as asking Him, as as-Sa'di explains.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### From the Qur'an: A Conversation at the Table of Bliss

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-52-17-28" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a real scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

As-Sa'di notes that after Allah mentions the punishment of the deniers in Surah at-Tur, He mentions the bliss of the righteous, "so that hearts stay between fear and hope."[^3]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-52-17-28" audience="13+" -->

"Indeed, the righteous will be in gardens and bliss, delighting in what their Lord has given them, and their Lord will have shielded them from the punishment of the Blazing Fire. 'Eat and drink with wholesome delight for what you used to do.'" Ibn Kathir comments: "This in return for that, as a favor and a kindness from Him." Then the ayat mention the couches set in rows, the believing descendants whom Allah joins to their parents, the fruit and the meat, and a cup "that brings no idle talk and no sin."[^1]

Then comes the moment that completes the picture: "And they will turn to one another, asking each other." Ibn Kathir explains: "They talk together and ask one another about their deeds and their states in this world." And their answer was: "Before this, among our families, we used to live in fear. So Allah showed us His favor and shielded us from the punishment of the scorching wind. Before this, we used to call upon Him. Indeed, He is the Most Kind, the Most Merciful."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice the words "among our families": their fear of Allah lived at home, where there was no audience and no likes. As-Sa'di puts their words this way: "So out of fear of Him we left sins behind, and for the same reason we mended our faults."[^3] And notice that they did not present their deeds as a price paid. They said: "So Allah showed us His favor." This fits the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him: "No one's deeds will admit him into Jannah," unless Allah covers him with forgiveness and mercy.[^6]

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the people of Jannah speak specifically about fearing Allah "among our families"? What is the difference between taqwa at home and taqwa in front of other people?

2. An-Nawawi said that the bliss of Jannah shares nothing with the bliss of this world "except the name and the basic form." What does that mean for a food whose name we already know?

3. How do you reconcile "for what you used to do" with their words, "So Allah showed us His favor"?

4. What do conversations at your family table usually revolve around? What would this scene change about them?

5. What du'a would you want to be making, so that you are among those who "used to call upon Him"?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Question at the Table

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.058.activity.turn-and-ask-at-the-table" -->

For seven days, choose one daily meal where you sit down with your family, and keep your phone away from it. Begin it in the name of Allah and close it with praise of Him. Then ask one member of your family a fixed question: "What's the best thing you did today?" or "What did you ask Allah for today?", and really listen. Then, in a private note that no one else sees, record your own answer to the same question, honestly and without dressing it up, and add a column: "Was it for Allah, or for people to see?" On the seventh day, read your note and write a paragraph: if one day in Jannah we were asking each other about these days of mine, what would I want to be able to say?

<!-- activity:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.058.dua.food-a-help-to-obey-feed-us-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ مَا نَأْكُلُ وَنَشْرَبُ عَوْنًا لَنَا عَلَى طَاعَتِكَ، وَأَطْعِمْنَا وَاسْقِنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ هَنِيئًا بِرَحْمَتِكَ.
>
> *"O Allah, make what we eat and drink a help for us in obeying You, and feed us and give us drink in Your Jannah with wholesome delight, by Your mercy."*

What it means: O Allah, make every bite and every sip a strength for obeying You, never for disobeying You. And in Your Jannah, feed us and give us drink with wholesome delight, with nothing to spoil it and no end to it, by Your mercy before our deeds.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner retells the scene of at-Tur 52:17-28 in order; explains *hani'an* from the words of at-Tabari and as-Sa'di; uses the hadith in Muslim 2835 and an-Nawawi's statement to show that the people of Jannah truly eat and that their eating brings no harm; explains, from Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di, what the people of Jannah asked one another about; reconciles "for what you used to do" with al-Bukhari 6467; and begins seven days of the Question at the Table.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board divided into two columns, "Food in This World" and "Food in Jannah"; a card for each learner on which to write their table question; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and at-Tabari on at-Tur 52:17-28, the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Mursalat 77:43, and an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 2835. The teacher also reviews the lesson on the first hospitality (lesson 34) so as not to repeat its subject, and leaves the details of fruit, wine and meat to the lessons devoted to them.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Think of a meal that was delicious but not wholesome. What spoiled it?" Then: "What do people usually talk about at their tables?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read at-Tur 52:17-28 in full, and have the learners divide it into three stations: bliss and the call, then the table, then the conversation (8 minutes). Then al-Mursalat and the commentators' explanations of *hani'an* (4 minutes). Then the hadith of Jabir and an-Nawawi's statement, filling in the "Food in Jannah" column (5 minutes). Then the hadith of Aishah (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 2 and 4. The teacher stresses that the *ba'* in "for what you used to do" is causal, and that the people of Jannah themselves attributed their salvation to Allah's favor. Then ask: what makes fear of Allah "among our families" more genuine?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses their daily meal and their fixed question, writes their answer for today, and decides on one food-related deed to carry out during the week, such as feeding someone in need or cutting back on waste.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What does *hani'an* mean?", "Where does the food of the people of Jannah go?" and "What did the people of Jannah ask one another about?" Then read the bedtime du'a, pointing out that it is lesson-authored.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, limit the reading to at-Tur 52:17-19 and 25-28 and the hadith of Jabir. Ask advanced learners to gather the causes of bliss mentioned in at-Tur and al-Mursalat: taqwa, doing good, fear of Allah, and du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not elaborate on the food of Jannah beyond what is established. The lesson does not rely on the hadith of Anas about the people of Jannah visiting one another and talking about the Day of Forgiveness, which Ibn Kathir cites from al-Bazzar while pointing out the weakness of its chain, nor on reports in books on the description of Jannah about the capacity of its people for eating.[^7] Explain "and We will pair them with *hur* of wide, lovely eyes" briefly to adults, without digression. Be considerate of anyone who suffers from a digestive illness or an eating disorder, and of anyone who has no family to share meals with: they can ask a friend or a roommate. Never present deeds as a price that earns a reward by right.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child can say that the people of Jannah are told "Eat and drink with wholesome delight," that they talk about how they feared Allah and made du'a, and that they do not need the bathroom; asks and answers the dinner question; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small play table with empty plates for role-play; small pieces of paper and crayons; a small box for each child; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the scene calmly and without frightening the children, and sends parents a short note explaining the three-day activity and the dinner question.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The children sit around the little table, and the educator asks: "What do we say before we eat? What do we say after? And what do we talk about while we eat?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The educator reads the introduction and then the Qur'anic scene. The children repeat "Eat and drink with wholesome delight," and when the scene reaches the people of Jannah talking with one another, each child turns to the child beside them.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions, and stress that the people of Jannah thanked Allah and said that He had been kind to them.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** In pairs around the little table, the children practice the dinner question; then each child draws a good thing they did today and puts it in their box to take home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The educator watches whether each child asks the question and answers it. Then read the du'a together, one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, saying "Bismillah" and "Alhamdulillah" and making one drawing is enough. Ask older children to retell in their own words what the people of Jannah said.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not explain the punishment of the scorching wind or anything about the Fire to the children; it is enough that Allah kept them safe from it. Mention the bathroom gently and without making fun. Never embarrass a child who can't think of a good deed to share; simply saying "Alhamdulillah" is enough.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the scene in its three stations; defines the four terms; tells the difference between "delicious" and *hani'an*; states the Prophet's answer, peace and blessings be upon him, about the food; and records the Question at the Table for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; three station cards: "Bliss and the Call," "The Table," "The Conversation"; a small table for each student; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on at-Tur 52:17-28, and decides how to explain briefly, without digression, what the ayat mention about the couches, the descendants and the cup.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the school-trip story and asks: "Was the food delicious? Was it *hani'an*? What's the difference?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes); then the Qur'anic scene, arranging the station cards on the board and pointing out the difference between the words of the ayah and the words of the commentators (6 minutes); then the hadith of Jabir (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss questions 2 and 3; then the teacher explains that the people of Jannah said, "So Allah showed us His favor": deeds are a cause, and entry into Jannah is by Allah's mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student chooses their fixed question and their meal, practices asking and answering with a classmate, and then writes their first answer, for today.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The food in Jannah is *hani'an* because..." Then read the du'a. After five days, anyone who wishes may share the answer that made them happiest.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, three days and ayat 17-19 and 25-28 are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize at-Tur 52:25-28 and to explain *mushfiqin* in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never require students to show their tables. Be considerate of a student whose family does not eat together: allow them to ask a brother or sister, a relative, or a friend. Never mock anyone's family food, or how little of it there is.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the scene and connects taqwa at home with the conversation of the people of Jannah; explains *hani'an* and an-Nawawi's statement about the reality of Jannah's bliss; reconciles "for what you used to do," "So Allah showed us His favor" and the hadith of Aishah; and begins seven days of the Question at the Table with their private note.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; a food photo posted on social media; cards for anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and at-Tabari on at-Tur 52:17-28 and an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 2835, and chooses a food photo that promotes nothing forbidden and no particular brand.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the photo and asks: "What does it show? What doesn't it show? And what do you usually talk about over food at home?" The students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the Qur'anic scene, with three pauses: the call, *hani'an*; then *yatasa'alun*, "asking each other"; then "among our families" and "So Allah showed us His favor."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 3 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher draws attention to the fact that private taqwa at home is what the people of Jannah mentioned, and that mercy comes before deeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student chooses their meal and their question, sets up their private note, and writes its first line, about today.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: the meaning of *hani'an*, what the people of Jannah asked one another about, and their own table question. After a week, the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see anyone's notes. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may keep private voice notes. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on as-Sa'di's words: "So out of fear of Him we left sins behind, and for the same reason we mended our faults."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never ask anyone to reveal their deeds or their shortcomings in front of the class. Be considerate of anyone struggling with an eating disorder or body image, and never link food with guilt or punishment; and of anyone whose family is going through conflict or separation, who may ask a trusted friend instead. Never judge anyone to be among the righteous or not.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
