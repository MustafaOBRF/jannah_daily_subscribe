# The Rivers of Jannah Flow Without Channels

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Relate what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw as he walked in Jannah (al-Bukhari 6581): a river whose banks were domes of hollowed pearl; how he asked Jibril, "What is this?" and was told, "This is al-Kawthar, which your Lord has given you"; and how its mud was intensely fragrant musk.
- Cite the authentic hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, showing that this river flows although no channel was ever cut for it, that its soil is musk, and that its pebbles are pearls (Musnad Ahmad 13578).
- Distinguish between the authentic words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; the statement of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, which is his own and not the Prophet's: "they flow freely over the surface of the ground"; and Masruq's explanation of "and water poured forth."
- Explain how Ibn al-Qayyim reconciled the words of Allah, "beneath which rivers flow," with rivers that run over the surface of the ground: they flow beneath the palaces, the chambers and the trees, not beneath the ground of Jannah.
- Discover, in the activity "A River Without a Channel," how much the water of this world depends on a channel and a bank; then build a week-long habit of asking "What is this?" about the signs of Allah one sees, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked, while stopping where the text stops.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for greater certainty through His signs, for the good manners to ask about what we do not know, and for a drink from the flowing rivers of Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Nobody in this world has ever seen a river without a bed. Water either carves a valley for itself or people dig it a canal, and then they build it a bridge and shore up its banks. Even so, it floods and drowns, or sinks away and dries up, and it drags mud and gravel along with it. No wonder so many of us picture the rivers of Jannah the same way: trenches cut into the earth.

The lessons "The Rivers and Springs of Jannah" and "The Four Rivers of Jannah" dealt with where the rivers come from and what kinds there are. This lesson pauses on a single feature: how do they flow, and what are their banks made of? The answer comes from an authentic hadith of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, describing al-Kawthar, and from a statement of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, that is his own and not the Prophet's. The books on the description of Jannah contain other reports about the sources of the rivers and their courses, but the hadith verifiers have judged some of their chains weak, so we build nothing on them.

Masruq, one of the senior Successors (the generation after the Companions), said of the words of Allah "and water poured forth": "Rivers that flow without a channel."[^9] This is a Successor's interpretation; it is a welcome support, but we do not treat it as a foundation.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6581" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "While I Was Walking in Jannah"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«بَيْنَمَا أَنَا أَسِيرُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، إِذَا أَنَا بِنَهَرٍ حَافَتَاهُ قِبَابُ الدُّرِّ الْمُجَوَّفِ، قُلْتُ: مَا هَذَا يَا جِبْرِيلُ؟ قَالَ: هَذَا الْكَوْثَرُ الَّذِي أَعْطَاكَ رَبُّكَ. فَإِذَا طِينُهُ، أَوْ طِيبُهُ، مِسْكٌ أَذْفَرُ»**. شَكَّ هُدْبَةُ.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"While I was walking in Jannah, I suddenly came upon a river whose two banks were domes of hollowed pearl. I said, 'What is this, O Jibril?' He said, 'This is al-Kawthar, which your Lord has given you.' And there was its mud (or: its scent): intensely fragrant musk."** Hudbah was unsure which of the two words was said.[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Hajar said: "It was mentioned earlier, in the tafsir of Surat al-Kawthar, that this took place on the night he was taken on the Night Journey."[^6] Al-'Ayni said that *qibab* (domes) is "the plural of *qubbah*, a kind of building"; that *ad-durr* is "the plural of *durrah*, which is a pearl"; and that *al-mujawwaf* means "hollow."[^7] *Adhfar* means of a sweet scent that is strong and pervasive.[^8] The uncertainty between *tinuhu*, "its mud," and *tibuhu*, "its scent," comes from Hudbah, al-Bukhari's teacher, so al-Bukhari preserved both words and did not choose between them.[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith holds three features: two banks lined with pearl domes that are hollow inside; mud that is not sludge to be stepped around but musk to be breathed in; and the question the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked about what he saw: "What is this?" Whoever comes across something wondrous does not stop at amazement; they ask someone who knows.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="ahmad-13578" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "It Was a River Flowing, Though No Channel Had Been Cut for It"

> عَنْ ثَابِتٍ، عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه، أَنَّهُ قَرَأَ هَذِهِ الْآيَةَ: {إِنَّا أَعْطَيْنَاكَ الْكَوْثَرَ}، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«أُعْطِيتُ الْكَوْثَرَ، فَإِذَا هُوَ نَهَرٌ يَجْرِي وَلَمْ يُشَقَّ شَقًّا، فَإِذَا حَافَتَاهُ قِبَابُ اللُّؤْلُؤِ، فَضَرَبْتُ بِيَدِي إِلَى تُرْبَتِهِ، فَإِذَا مِسْكَةٌ ذَفِرَةٌ، وَإِذَا حَصَاهُ اللُّؤْلُؤُ»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Thabit reported from Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, that he recited this ayah: "Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar," and then said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"I was given al-Kawthar, and there it was: a river flowing, though no channel had been cut for it. Its two banks were domes of pearl. I struck my hand down into its soil, and it was fragrant musk, and its pebbles were pearls."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Its chain is authentic; it comes by way of Hammad ibn Salamah from Thabit from Anas. By this same route, Ahmad in another place and Ibn Hibban record that it is a river that **"flows over the surface of the ground."**[^2] Ibn al-Qayyim cites this narration in his chapter on the rivers of Jannah and the course along which they flow.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

This narration states outright something al-Bukhari's narration does not mention: the river flows although no channel was ever cut for it. It is not a trench dug into the ground. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, adds that he struck his hand down into its soil and found it to be musk, and found its pebbles to be pearls. The riverbed, the banks and the mud are all part of the bliss: water running over the face of the ground, lined with domes of pearl.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="athar-anas-rivers-on-surface" kind="athar" mode="canonical" -->

### "Perhaps You Think the Rivers of Jannah Are Trenches in the Ground"

> عَنْ مُعَاوِيَةَ بْنِ قُرَّةَ، عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: "لَعَلَّكُمْ تَظُنُّونَ أَنَّ أَنْهَارَ الْجَنَّةِ خُدُودٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ، لَا وَاللَّهِ، إِنَّهَا لَسَائِحَةٌ عَلَى وَجْهِ الْأَرْضِ، حَافَّتَاهَا خِيَامُ اللُّؤْلُؤِ، وَطِينُهَا الْمِسْكُ الْأَذْفَرُ". قَالَ الرَّاوِي: قُلْتُ: يَا أَنَسُ، مَا الْأَذْفَرُ؟ قَالَ: "الَّذِي لَا خِلْطَ لَهُ".[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Mu'awiyah ibn Qurrah reported that Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "Perhaps you think the rivers of Jannah are trenches cut into the ground. No, by Allah! They flow freely over the surface of the ground. Their two banks are tents of pearl, and their mud is musk, *adhfar*." The narrator said: I asked, "O Anas, what is *adhfar*?" He said: "That which has nothing mixed into it."[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

This is the statement of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him: it is *mawquf*, stopping with him, and is not the wording of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It was also narrated as the Prophet's own words through a narrator whose reliability is unknown, who went against two truthful narrators who both narrated it as Anas's statement; so al-Mundhiri and the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah* judged the version stopping with Anas to be the correct one.[^3] A description like this is not usually something a person says from his own opinion, yet it remains the statement of a Companion, and its meaning agrees with the authentic hadith of the Prophet above.

#### Lesson Explanation

Anas begins by correcting a picture in our minds: "Perhaps you think..." Our imagination is built out of what we see in this world, and revelation corrects it. The rivers there are *sa'ihah*: water spread out in the open, running over the surface of the ground. And in the student's question, "What is *adhfar*?", lies a lesson in the manners of learning: whoever does not understand a word asks about it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-100" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And He Has Prepared for Them Gardens Beneath Which Rivers Flow"

> **{وَالسَّابِقُونَ الْأَوَّلُونَ مِنَ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ وَالَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُمْ بِإِحْسَانٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ وَأَعَدَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي تَحْتَهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا أَبَدًا ذَلِكَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ}** [التوبة: ١٠٠][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And the first forerunners among the Emigrants and the Helpers, and those who followed them in excellence: Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him, and He has prepared for them gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will abide forever. That is the supreme triumph."** (at-Tawbah 9:100)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim argued from how often the Qur'an repeats this description that the rivers in Jannah are "real," that they are "flowing, not still," and that they run "beneath their chambers, their palaces and their orchards." He then answered those who thought that flowing without a channel rules out the rivers being "beneath them," saying: "For the rivers of Jannah, even though they flow without a channel, are beneath the palaces, the dwellings and the chambers, and beneath the trees; and He, glorified and exalted is He, did not say: from beneath its ground."[^5] He also said: "This is one of the signs of the Lord, exalted is He: that He makes rivers flow of kinds that in this world are not customarily made to flow, and makes them flow without a channel."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The two texts do not contradict each other: the rivers flow "beneath" the dwellings and the trees, and they flow "over the surface of the ground," not through cracks cut into it. The ayah also tells us for whom these gardens were prepared: the first forerunners, and those who followed them in excellence. The way to the rivers of Jannah, then, is to follow in excellence.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Which features of al-Kawthar come together from the narrations of al-Bukhari and Ahmad?
2. What does "though no channel had been cut for it" mean? And why is al-Bukhari's narration not enough on its own to establish this feature?
3. How do the authentic words of the Prophet, the statement of Anas that stops with him, and the statement of Masruq differ in their weight as proof?
4. How did Ibn al-Qayyim reconcile "beneath which ... flow" with rivers that flow over the surface of the ground?
5. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked, "What is this?" and the student asked, "What is *adhfar*?" What do you see every day that you have never once asked about?
6. Where does the text stop in describing the rivers? And how do you keep your imagination from running past it?

### Activity: A River Without a Channel, and the Question "What Is This?"

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.052.activity.river-without-a-channel-what-is-this" -->

Set a flat tray at a slight tilt, pour a cup of water onto it, and try to make the water run in a single straight line from one end to the other without digging it a channel or giving it an edge. Note down what you needed to make that work, and what the water did when it was left with no channel. Then write two columns: "What the water of this world needs" and "What the text describes of the rivers of Jannah." After that, carry a small notebook for seven days: whenever you see one of the signs of Allah, in water or anywhere else, say "Subhan Allah," write down one "What is this?" question, and look for its answer from a scholar or in a reliable tafsir. At the end of the week, write down one question whose answer turned out to be "The text does not go into detail on this," and a paragraph on what you learned from stopping at the limits of the text.

### An Authentic Hadith: The River the Prophet Saw, Peace and Blessings Be Upon Him

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6581" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. It is in Sahih al-Bukhari, and it is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6581" audience="4-7" -->

One amazing night, Allah showed our Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, Jannah.[^6] As the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was walking there, he saw a beautiful river.[^1]

All along both sides of the river stood big domes made of pearl. A dome is like a round house. And these domes were empty inside, with lots of room in them.[^1][^7]

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked Jibril, "What is this, O Jibril?" And Jibril said, "This is al-Kawthar, which your Lord has given you."[^1]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, put his hand down into the soil of the river, and it was musk that smelled wonderful! And its pebbles, the little stones at the bottom, were pearls![^2]

This river flows along, and nobody ever dug a path for it in the ground.[^2]

Did you notice? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw something amazing, so he asked, "What is this?" to find out. You can do that too. When you see something amazing, say "Subhan Allah," and then ask your dad or mom, "What is this?"

### Activity: A River Without a Channel, and the Question "What Is This?"

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.052.activity.river-without-a-channel-what-is-this" -->

With your dad or mom, put a little sand in a tray. Pour in a small cup of water and watch: did the water run along in one line, or did it spread out? Now draw a path in the sand with your finger and pour the water again. This time the water ran along, but it dug into the sand and turned muddy. Say: "Subhan Allah! The river of Jannah flows without a dug-out path." And for the next two days, every time you see water (rain, a faucet, or a puddle), say "Subhan Allah" and ask, "What is this?" Your dad or mom will draw you a star on a sheet of paper for every question.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In science class you learn that a river carves its own bed: the water runs for years and years, wearing away soil and rock, until it has cut a deep valley. People build dams and banks for rivers and dig canals for them, and even so, a river can overflow and flood, or dry up in the summer. So some of us might imagine that the rivers of Jannah are like that too: ditches in the ground. Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, said, in his own words: "Perhaps you think the rivers of Jannah are trenches cut into the ground. No, by Allah! They flow freely over the surface of the ground."[^3] And an authentic hadith from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, makes this clear.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6581" -->

- **`Domes of hollowed pearl (qibab ad-durr al-mujawwaf)`** — domes made of pearl that are empty on the inside.[^7]
- **`Intensely fragrant musk (misk adhfar)`** — musk with a scent that is both lovely and strong.[^8]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="ahmad-13578" -->

- **`No channel had been cut for it (lam yushaqq shaqqan)`** — nobody dug it a bed in the ground.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: "What Is This, O Jibril?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6581" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account recorded by al-Bukhari, with some of its details reported in the Musnad of Ahmad with an authentic chain. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6581" audience="8-12" -->

On the night of the Isra' and Mi'raj, the Night Journey and the Ascension, Allah showed His Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, some of His signs, and Jannah was one of them.[^6] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described what he saw: "While I was walking in Jannah, I suddenly came upon a river whose two banks were domes of hollowed pearl."[^1]

Picture the scene the way he described it: a river running along, and on both its banks, domes of pearl, every one of them hollow inside.[^7] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not walk past this sight in silence. He asked, "What is this, O Jibril?" And Jibril answered, "This is al-Kawthar, which your Lord has given you."[^1]

In another authentic narration, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "There it was: a river flowing, though no channel had been cut for it," meaning that no bed had been dug for it in the ground. Then he said: "I struck my hand down into its soil, and it was fragrant musk, and its pebbles were pearls."[^2] In other words, its soil is sweet-smelling musk, and its little stones are pearls.

Now compare that with a river in this world. Here, water digs into the ground and carries mud along with it. There, the river runs over the surface of the ground with no channel at all, and its mud is musk. The scholars have said that this is one of the signs of Allah: that He makes rivers flow without a channel.[^5]

And notice what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did: he saw something amazing, so he asked someone who knew. Later, when Anas described the rivers, his student asked him, "What is *adhfar*?" and Anas explained it to him.[^3] An honest question is the key to knowledge.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Type "rivers of Jannah" into any search engine and you'll get a wall of AI-generated images: golden waterfalls, hyper-detailed palaces, colors cranked all the way up. They look convincing, but whoever made them saw nothing at all; they just stitched together pictures from this world. Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, flagged this very mistake centuries ago: "Perhaps you think the rivers of Jannah are trenches cut into the ground. No, by Allah! They flow freely over the surface of the ground."[^3] Those are his own words, a statement that stops with him. What has come authentically from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, himself is a story of a question and an answer, and it is worth stopping to look at.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="ahmad-13578" -->

- **`No channel had been cut for it (lam yushaqq shaqqan)`** — no trench was dug for it in the ground; it flows over the surface instead.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="athar-anas-rivers-on-surface" -->

- **`Mawquf`** — a statement that stops with a Companion and is not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^3]
- **`Sa'ihah`** — water out in the open, spread wide, running over the surface of the ground.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### An Authentic Hadith: A Question Asked in Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6581" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account recorded by al-Bukhari, with further detail in a narration in the Musnad of Ahmad whose chain is authentic. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6581" audience="13+" -->

The night of the Isra'. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, is walking in Jannah.[^6] In his words: "While I was walking in Jannah, I suddenly came upon a river whose two banks were domes of hollowed pearl."[^1] A sight no eye in this world has ever seen: two banks lined with pearl domes, each one hollow inside.[^7]

And here is the moment worth pausing on. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not settle for admiring it. He asked: "What is this, O Jibril?" The answer came: "This is al-Kawthar, which your Lord has given you."[^1] A personal gift from his Lord, and he came to know it by asking.

Nor did he stop at looking. In a narration with an authentic chain, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, describes the river as "flowing, though no channel had been cut for it," and says: "I struck my hand down into its soil, and it was fragrant musk, and its pebbles were pearls."[^2] No trench, no sludge, no ordinary gravel. Water running over the face of the ground, a riverbed of pearl, and soil of musk.

Years later, Anas was describing the rivers to his students when one of them asked, "O Anas, what is *adhfar*?" He said: "That which has nothing mixed into it."[^3] A simple question about a single word, and it preserved that word's meaning for us.

Notice the limits, too. The text mentions the banks, the soil and the pebbles; it does not describe every detail. Ibn al-Qayyim said that rivers flowing without a channel are one of the signs of the Lord, exalted is He.[^5] A believer affirms what is authentically established, and does not add anything from their own imagination and pass it off as fact.

So here is the lesson for you. In a world that hands you a ready-made image of everything, be the one who asks: "What is this? And how do you know?" Ask people who actually know, go back to the source, and keep separate what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, what a Companion said, and what some designer imagined.

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the Prophet's question, "What is this?", matter so much in the story?
2. What did Ahmad's narration add to al-Bukhari's?
3. Why do we keep Anas's words "Perhaps you think..." separate from the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, even though they mean the same thing?
4. How do you deal with an image or a video about Jannah that adds details not found in the texts?
5. What question have you been putting off for a long time because you were too embarrassed to ask it?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: A River Without a Channel, and the Question "What Is This?"

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.052.activity.river-without-a-channel-what-is-this" -->

Try this in two minutes: pour a little water onto a flat, tilted surface and try to make it run in a single line with no channel or edge, then note what happened. Next, pick a widely shared image or description of "the rivers of Jannah" and sort its details into three groups: "In an authentic hadith," "A Companion's statement," and "Imagination, not in any text." Then, for seven days, write one question a day in your private notebook, starting with "What is this?", about one of the signs of Allah or one of the texts about Jannah. Take it to a trustworthy teacher or a recognized tafsir, and write down the answer and where it came from. On the seventh day, write a paragraph: Which question changed the way you see things? And where did you find that the text was silent, so you stayed silent too?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.052.dua.certainty-asking-rivers-of-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ زِدْنَا بِآيَاتِكَ يَقِينًا، وَعَلِّمْنَا أَنْ نَسْأَلَ عَمَّا نَجْهَلُ، وَاسْقِنَا مِنْ أَنْهَارِ جَنَّتِكَ الْجَارِيَةِ.
>
> *"O Allah, increase us in certainty through Your signs, teach us to ask about what we do not know, and give us to drink from the flowing rivers of Your Jannah."*

What it means: O Allah, let Your signs, in Your creation and in Your revelation, increase us in certainty. Grant us the courage to ask about what we do not know, and the good manners to ask well, and give us to drink from the flowing rivers of Your Jannah.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner states the features of al-Kawthar from the narrations of al-Bukhari 6581 and Ahmad 13578, and shows that its flowing without a channel is established as the Prophet's own words in Ahmad's narration, whose chain is authentic; distinguishes the Prophet's words from the statement that stops with Anas and from the interpretation of Masruq, a Successor; explains Ibn al-Qayyim's reconciliation of "beneath which ... flow" with rivers that run over the surface of the ground; and carries out the tray experiment and keeps the "What is this?" notebook for seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a flat tray, a cup of water and a towel; a board divided into three columns: "Authentic words of the Prophet," "Stops with a Companion," and "Interpretation of a Successor"; a small notebook for each learner; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 6581 and 4964 with the commentaries of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni; Musnad Ahmad 13578 and 12542 and Ibn Hibban 6471 with al-Arna'ut's gradings; the forty-seventh chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* and the section that follows it, with the editor's notes; and the chapter "The Description of How Its Rivers Flow" in Abu Nu'aym's *Sifat al-Jannah*. The teacher should bear in mind that the origin of the rivers and the four rivers each have an earlier lesson of their own, so their material is not repeated here; and that the report from Abu Musa that the rivers "gush out into a hollow" was judged weak by the editor, so it is not used.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Describe a river you know. How does it flow? And what keeps it in place?" Write the answers on the board (a riverbed, banks, mud, floods, drought), then ask: "Do you think the rivers of Jannah are like that?" Leave the question hanging.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Bukhari's hadith with the commentary of Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni (5 minutes); then Ahmad's narration, asking the learners to pick out what it adds (5 minutes); then the statement of Anas, explaining its status and placing it on the board alongside the statement of Masruq (5 minutes); then the ayah of at-Tawbah 9:100 and Ibn al-Qayyim's words (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 2, 3 and 4. Make clear that the authentic words of the Prophet are the foundation; that a Companion's statement is mentioned with a clear note that it stops with him; and that a Successor's interpretation is a welcome support. Then discuss question 6: disciplined imagination follows the text and never runs ahead of it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** A volunteer carries out the tray experiment in front of everyone, and each learner fills in their two columns (8 minutes). Then each learner writes their first "What is this?" question in their notebook, and pairs share a reliable source they will turn to (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two features established in the Prophet's own words, one feature that comes in the statement of Anas, and how Ibn al-Qayyim reconciled the texts." Then agree to review the notebooks in a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, al-Bukhari's hadith and Ahmad's narration are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the routes of the hadith of Hammad from Thabit from Anas and compare their wordings ("though no channel had been cut for it," "not cut out," "flows over the surface of the ground"), and to review why the version stopping with Anas was judged correct.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe Jibril, peace be upon him, in any physical form, and do not speak about what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw beyond what has been reported. Do not present the statement of Anas as a hadith of the Prophet. Do not attribute the weak reports about the sources of the rivers to the texts. Do not let the lesson be taken as belittling the means Allah has placed in this world: digging canals and managing water well are righteous deeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child retells that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw a river in Jannah with domes of pearl on both sides, and asked Jibril, "What is this?"; says that the river of Jannah flows without a dug-out path and that its soil is musk; and says "Subhan Allah" and asks "What is this?" on seeing water.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A plastic tray with sand in it; a small cup of water; a towel; a shiny white bead that looks like a pearl; the star sheet; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver sets up the tray somewhere easy to clean, practices telling the account without adding anything to it, and prepares the star sheet for the two days.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Where does the water go when we turn on the faucet? And where does a river flow?" Then show the child the bead: "Imagine a big dome made of pearl, like this."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation and then the account. Pause at the Prophet's question, "What is this?", and repeat it together with the child; then pause at the word "hollowed" and show an empty cup to make it clear.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 1, 2 and 4, and stress that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked so that he would know, and that asking is a beautiful thing to do.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child pours water onto the smooth sand and then into the dug-out path, describes what happened, and says: "Subhan Allah! The river of Jannah flows without a dug-out path."

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Notice whether the child can name two things about the river. Then read the du'a together and explain the star sheet for the next two days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the sentence "The river of Jannah has pearls on both sides" and pouring the water once are enough. Ask older children to tell the account to a brother or sister in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or Jibril, peace be upon him, and do not act out their roles. Supervise water play closely, never leave a child near deep water, and do not invite the child to go near a real river or pond. Do not add anything to the description of Jannah that has not been reported.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith with its features (domes of hollowed pearl, al-Kawthar, mud of musk, flowing without a channel, pebbles of pearl); tells apart the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the words of Anas; compares a river of this world with the river of Jannah in a table; and writes five "What is this?" questions along with the sources of their answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Sand trays for the groups; cups of water; towels; the experiment sheet with its table; the "What is this?" notebook; two large cards: "The Prophet said, peace and blessings be upon him" and "Anas said."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher tries both experiments beforehand to find the right amount of water, and prepares a photo of a real river and of a valley carved by a river, with no writing on them.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the photo of the valley and ask: "Who dug this valley?" Then read the explanation and ask: "And the rivers of Jannah?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at three moments: the sight, the question, and the hand struck into the soil. Then the teacher holds up the two cards, and the students sort the sentences: which come from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and which from Anas.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Ask questions 2, 3 and 4. Make clear that a Companion's statement is respected and passed on, but is never attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The groups carry out both experiments and write down their observations and the table; then each student writes their first question in the "What is this?" notebook.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes: "Two features of the river of Jannah from the hadith, and the difference between the Prophet's words and a statement that stops with a Companion, in one sentence." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide a half-completed table. Ask advanced students to look into why the narration is unsure between "its mud" and "its scent," and whose uncertainty it was.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or Jibril, peace be upon him. Wipe up the floor right away so no one slips. Guide the students to ask their parents, their teachers and reliable sources, not strangers on the internet.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the account in the hadith as a model of asking and verifying; explains what Ahmad's narration adds; defines *mawquf* and applies it to the statement of Anas; sorts the details of a widely shared picture of Jannah into authentic, a Companion's statement, and imagination; and commits to keeping the "What is this?" notebook for seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a flat surface and a cup of water; a written description, prepared by the teacher, of a widely shared image of "the rivers of Jannah" (without showing the image itself if it depicts people); the three-way sorting sheet; private notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes a neutral description of a popular image that mixes authentic details with imagined ones, and prepares for the question "Is it wrong to imagine Jannah?" with the answer that imagination which stirs longing is permissible, as long as it is not presented as fact.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When was the last time you believed an image or a video and then found out it was fake?" Listen, then read the explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at the question, at the hand struck into the soil, and at the student's question to Anas. Then read the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Discuss questions 2, 3 and 4 in small groups, then bring the discussion together around one rule: "Affirm what is authentic, attribute every statement to the one who said it, and stop where the text stops."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** The two-minute water experiment; then sorting the details of the written description into the three groups; then each student writes their first question in their private notebook.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a short paragraph: "What I learned from the Prophet's question, 'What is this?'" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about how the experience went, without asking to see the notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the routes of the narration of Hammad from Thabit from Anas and their wordings.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show images that depict the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, the angels or the Companions. Do not mock anyone's imagination; correct it gently with the text. Point out that knowledge is sought from trustworthy teachers and well-known sources, and that anyone who comes across misleading or harmful content should tell a trusted adult.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
