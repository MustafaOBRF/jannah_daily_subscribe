# The Clothing and Jewelry of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the account of the silk garment given to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, whose softness amazed the Companions, and his words: "The handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jannah are better than it and softer" (al-Bukhari 3802 and 2615, and Muslim 2468), and explain what it shows in an-Nawawi's words: the humblest garment of Jannah is better than the finest garment of this world.
- Describe the clothing and jewelry of the people of Jannah from al-Kahf 18:31, al-Hajj 22:23 and al-Insan 76:21: green garments of *sundus* and *istabraq*, clothing of silk, and bracelets of gold, silver and pearls, noting that the commentators differ over the meaning of *sundus* and *istabraq*.
- State the ruling for this world precisely: silk and gold are unlawful for men and lawful for women (at-Tirmidhi 1720); the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave the reason for the prohibition in his words, "for they are theirs in this world and ours in the Hereafter" (al-Bukhari 5426); the scholars have understood the warning "he will not wear it in the Hereafter" (al-Bukhari 5834) in several ways; and the addition "he will not enter Jannah" is a statement of Ibn az-Zubayr, not of the Prophet.
- Explain the hadith "The adornment of the believer will reach as far as the water of wudu reaches" (Muslim 250) through the scholars' two views on the meaning of "adornment": the jewelry of Jannah, or the light of wudu.
- Carry out the "Adornment Where Wudu Reaches" activity: a complete, unhurried wudu at set times over a set number of days, with a map of the parts of wudu marked after each one, followed by reflection on an adornment built through obedience rather than display.
- Recite the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for the garment of taqwa in this world and for the adornment and garments of Jannah in the Hereafter.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In every age, clothing and jewelry catch the eye: a soft fabric, a striking color, a bracelet that gleams. The Qur'an does not ignore this pull. It tells us that the people of Jannah will wear clothing of silk and green garments of *sundus* and *istabraq*, and will be adorned with bracelets of gold, silver and pearls. Two careful questions remain. How are we to understand this promise when silk and gold are forbidden to men in this world? And how should a believer look at the clothes that people admire?

This lesson pauses at four meanings: the measure of the "handkerchiefs" in the account of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh; the description of the clothing and jewelry of Jannah in the Qur'an; the ruling on silk and gold in this world; and the adornment that reaches the places touched by wudu. The furnishings and couches of Jannah have a lesson of their own next to this one, and the appearance and beauty of the people of Jannah are taken up in a later lesson.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3802" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jannah Are Better Than It"

> عَنِ الْبَرَاءِ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **أُهْدِيَتْ لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ حُلَّةُ حَرِيرٍ، فَجَعَلَ أَصْحَابُهُ يَمَسُّونَهَا وَيَعْجَبُونَ مِنْ لِينِهَا، فَقَالَ: «أَتَعْجَبُونَ مِنْ لِينِ هَذِهِ؟ لَمَنَادِيلُ سَعْدِ بْنِ مُعَاذٍ خَيْرٌ مِنْهَا، أَوْ أَلْيَنُ».** وَفِي رِوَايَةِ مُسْلِمٍ: **«لَمَنَادِيلُ سَعْدِ بْنِ مُعَاذٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ خَيْرٌ مِنْهَا وَأَلْيَنُ»**.[^1]
>
> وَعَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **أُهْدِيَ لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ جُبَّةُ سُنْدُسٍ، وَكَانَ يَنْهَى عَنِ الْحَرِيرِ، فَعَجِبَ النَّاسُ مِنْهَا، فَقَالَ: «وَالَّذِي نَفْسُ مُحَمَّدٍ بِيَدِهِ، لَمَنَادِيلُ سَعْدِ بْنِ مُعَاذٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ أَحْسَنُ مِنْ هَذَا».** وَقَالَ سَعِيدٌ عَنْ قَتَادَةَ عَنْ أَنَسٍ: **إِنَّ أُكَيْدِرَ دُومَةَ أَهْدَى إِلَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of al-Bara', may Allah be pleased with him, who said: **A silk garment (*hullah*) was given to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, as a gift, and his Companions began to touch it, marveling at how soft it was. He said: "Do you marvel at the softness of this? The handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh are better than it," or he said, "softer."** And in the narration of Muslim: **"The handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jannah are better than it and softer."**[^1]
>
> On the authority of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: **A cloak (*jubbah*) of *sundus* was given to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, as a gift, and he used to forbid silk. The people marveled at it, and he said: "By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, the handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jannah are finer than this."** And Sa'id narrated from Qatadah from Anas: **Ukaydir of Dumah sent it as a gift to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "The scholars said: this points to the great rank of Sa'd in Jannah, and to the fact that the humblest of his garments there is better than this, because the handkerchief is the humblest of garments: it is meant for dirt and rough use, so everything else is finer. And in it is the affirmation of Jannah for Sa'd."[^10] Mulla 'Ali al-Qari quotes al-Khattabi as saying of the handkerchiefs: "If the humblest of them is like this, what do you suppose the finest of them is like?"[^11]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to forbid silk, and yet he did not scold his Companions for admiring it, nor did he belittle the gift. Instead, he lifted their gaze. The comparison here is not between one garment and another, but between the finest thing in this world and the humblest thing in Jannah: a cloth you wipe your hands on is better than a king's robe.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-18-31" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### Green Garments of *Sundus* and *Istabraq*

> **{أُولَٰئِكَ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهِمُ الْأَنْهَارُ يُحَلَّوْنَ فِيهَا مِنْ أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَيَلْبَسُونَ ثِيَابًا خُضْرًا مِنْ سُنْدُسٍ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٍ مُتَّكِئِينَ فِيهَا عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ ۚ نِعْمَ الثَّوَابُ وَحَسُنَتْ مُرْتَفَقًا}** [الكهف: ٣١][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Those are the ones who will have the Gardens of Eternity, with rivers flowing beneath them. They will be adorned there with bracelets of gold, and they will wear green garments of *sundus* and *istabraq*, reclining there on canopied couches. What an excellent reward, and what a fine place of rest!"** (al-Kahf 18:31)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "*Sundus* is fine, delicate garments, like shirts and the like; as for *istabraq*, it is thick brocade with a sheen to it."[^12] As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, put it the other way around: *sundus* "is the thick kind of brocade, and *istabraq* is the fine kind."[^13]

#### Lesson Explanation

What the two agree on is that *sundus* and *istabraq* are both kinds of the finest silk, one delicate and one heavy, and that their color is green. The ayah brings adornment, clothing and ease together in a single scene.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-22-23" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Their Clothing There Will Be Silk"

> **{إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُدْخِلُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ يُحَلَّوْنَ فِيهَا مِنْ أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَلُؤْلُؤًا ۖ وَلِبَاسُهُمْ فِيهَا حَرِيرٌ}** [الحج: ٢٣][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Allah will admit those who believe and do righteous deeds into gardens beneath which rivers flow. They will be adorned there with bracelets of gold, and with pearls, and their clothing there will be silk."** (al-Hajj 22:23)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "In contrast to the garments of the people of the Fire that were cut out for them, the clothing of these people is of silk, its *istabraq* and its *sundus*."[^14]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah mentions pearls alongside gold, and wording close to it comes in Surah Fatir (ayah 33). The reward depends on faith and righteous deeds, not on what a servant of Allah wore in this world.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-21" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And They Will Be Adorned with Bracelets of Silver"

> **{عَالِيَهُمْ ثِيَابُ سُنْدُسٍ خُضْرٌ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٌ ۖ وَحُلُّوا أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَسَقَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًا طَهُورًا}** [الإنسان: ٢١][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Upon them will be green garments of *sundus*, and *istabraq*. They will be adorned with bracelets of silver, and their Lord will give them a pure drink."** (al-Insan 76:21)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "That is, bracelets of silver are placed on their hands, on the men among them and the women alike."[^15] Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "This is the description of the righteous (*al-abrar*). As for those brought near (*al-muqarrabun*), it is as He said: {They will be adorned there with bracelets of gold, and with pearls}."[^16]

#### Lesson Explanation

The adornment of Jannah, then, belongs to men and women alike. Ibn Kathir made silver the mark of the righteous and gold the mark of those brought near; this is his own interpretation, which we mention without adding anything to it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-1720" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Wearing Silk and Gold Has Been Made Unlawful for the Males of My Ummah"

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«حُرِّمَ لِبَاسُ الْحَرِيرِ وَالذَّهَبِ عَلَى ذُكُورِ أُمَّتِي، وَأُحِلَّ لِإِنَاثِهِمْ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Wearing silk and gold has been made unlawful for the males of my Ummah, and lawful for their females."**[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ruling for this world is clearly defined: men may not wear silk or adorn themselves with gold, and women may. Silk and gold are themselves among the delights of Jannah, as the ayat above show, so forbidding them to men in this world does not mean that they are evil in themselves. The details of the exceptions belong to the books of fiqh and to people of knowledge.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5426" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "For They Are Theirs in This World and Ours in the Hereafter"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنِ أَبِي لَيْلَى: **أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا عِنْدَ حُذَيْفَةَ فَاسْتَسْقَى، فَسَقَاهُ مَجُوسِيٌّ، فَلَمَّا وَضَعَ الْقَدَحَ فِي يَدِهِ رَمَاهُ بِهِ، وَقَالَ: لَوْلَا أَنِّي نَهَيْتُهُ غَيْرَ مَرَّةٍ وَلَا مَرَّتَيْنِ -كَأَنَّهُ يَقُولُ: لَمْ أَفْعَلْ هَذَا- وَلَكِنِّي سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: «لَا تَلْبَسُوا الْحَرِيرَ وَلَا الدِّيبَاجَ، وَلَا تَشْرَبُوا فِي آنِيَةِ الذَّهَبِ وَالْفِضَّةِ، وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا فِي صِحَافِهَا، فَإِنَّهَا لَهُمْ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَلَنَا فِي الْآخِرَةِ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of 'Abd ar-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: **They were with Hudhayfah when he asked for something to drink, and a Magian brought him a drink. When the man placed the cup in his hand, Hudhayfah threw it at him and said: "Had I not forbidden him more than once or twice..." (as if to say: I would not have done this) "...but I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: 'Do not wear silk or brocade (*dibaj*), do not drink from vessels of gold and silver, and do not eat from plates made of them, for they are theirs in this world and ours in the Hereafter.'"**[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith also mentions vessels of gold and silver, which have a lesson of their own. The point here is the reason the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave: "theirs in this world and ours in the Hereafter." When a believer leaves what he has been forbidden, he is not being deprived; he is saving it for a home that lasts.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5834" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whoever Wears Silk in This World Will Not Wear It in the Hereafter"

> عَنْ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: **«مَنْ لَبِسَ الْحَرِيرَ فِي الدُّنْيَا لَمْ يَلْبَسْهُ فِي الْآخِرَةِ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of 'Umar, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever wears silk in this world will not wear it in the Hereafter."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Mulla 'Ali al-Qari said: "It is understood to refer to one who holds it to be lawful; or as a stern deterrent and threat; or to a period before he enters Jannah, for the clothing of the people of Jannah there is silk."[^17] As for the addition "and whoever does not wear it in the Hereafter will not enter Jannah," Ibn Hajar made clear that it is "interpolated into the report (*mudraj*), and it is a statement of Ibn az-Zubayr (*mawquf*)."[^18]

#### Lesson Explanation

It is therefore a severe warning to the man who wears silk in this world, and it may not be turned into a means of judging a particular person as someone who will not enter Jannah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-250" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Adornment of the Believer Will Reach as Far as the Water of Wudu Reaches"

> عَنْ أَبِي حَازِمٍ قَالَ: **كُنْتُ خَلْفَ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ وَهُوَ يَتَوَضَّأُ لِلصَّلَاةِ، فَكَانَ يَمُدُّ يَدَهُ حَتَّى تَبْلُغَ إِبْطَهُ، فَقُلْتُ لَهُ: يَا أَبَا هُرَيْرَةَ، مَا هَذَا الْوُضُوءُ؟ فَقَالَ: يَا بَنِي فَرُّوخَ، أَنْتُمْ هَاهُنَا؟ لَوْ عَلِمْتُ أَنَّكُمْ هَاهُنَا مَا تَوَضَّأْتُ هَذَا الْوُضُوءَ، سَمِعْتُ خَلِيلِي ﷺ يَقُولُ: «تَبْلُغُ الْحِلْيَةُ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِ حَيْثُ يَبْلُغُ الْوَضُوءُ»**.[^9]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hazim, who said: **I was behind Abu Hurayrah while he was making wudu for the prayer, and he was extending the washing of his arm until it reached his armpit. I said to him, "Abu Hurayrah, what is this wudu?" He said, "Sons of Farrukh, are you here? Had I known you were here, I would not have made this wudu. I heard my beloved friend, peace and blessings be upon him, say: 'The adornment of the believer will reach as far as the water of wudu reaches.'"**[^9]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites the hadith under the ayah of al-Hajj that describes the jewelry of the people of Jannah,[^14] and Ibn 'Uthaymin said: "The adornment on the Day of Resurrection is worn by men and women alike."[^19] Al-Mubarakfuri said: "What is meant by the adornment is the *ghurrah* and *tahjil*, that is, light and whiteness; it has also been said that it is adornment in Jannah, but that is unlikely."[^19] An-Nawawi relates from al-Qadi 'Iyad, in summary, that Abu Hurayrah disliked being seen by ordinary people, lest they think that what he added was the required obligation.[^20]

#### Lesson Explanation

On either view, the places that wudu reaches are where the believer's adornment will be, whether as jewelry or as light. The promised adornment is tied to an act of worship done every day, one that a man, a woman and a child can all manage.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, choose the handkerchief, of all things, for the comparison?
2. How does Ibn Kathir's view of *sundus* and *istabraq* differ from as-Sa'di's? And what do they agree on?
3. How does a believer reconcile the prohibition of silk and gold for men in this world with the promise of both to the people of Jannah?
4. In what ways has the hadith "he will not wear it in the Hereafter" been understood? And why may it not be used to pass judgment on a particular person?
5. What are the two views on the meaning of "adornment" in the hadith of wudu? And what do they have in common?
6. What do you find yourself admiring in the way other people dress? And how can you lift your gaze the way the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, lifted the gaze of his Companions?

### Activity: Adornment Where Wudu Reaches

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.045.activity.adornment-where-wudu-reaches" -->

Choose one prayer each day for seven days, and make a complete, unhurried wudu for it, paying close attention to the places people most often skimp on: the elbows, the ankles, between the fingers and toes, and the heels. Draw a chart with a row for each of the seven days and a column for each part of wudu, and put a mark against every part you washed thoroughly. On the seventh day, teach someone in your family a thorough wudu, and then write three lines: Which place had you been neglecting? What is the difference between an adornment displayed to people and an adornment built in private? And how do you now understand the hadith of the handkerchiefs?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

Look at your clothes. Some are new, some are old, and some are so soft you love to touch them. Jannah has clothes more beautiful than all the clothes in this world!

Allah told us that the people of Jannah wear green clothes made of soft, shiny silk, and that they wear bracelets of gold, of silver and of pearls.[^3][^5]

Silk is a very, very soft cloth. In this world, Allah made silk and gold for girls and women, and boys and men don't wear them now.[^6] But in Jannah, all its people, men and women, wear silk and bracelets.[^15]

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that a believer's adornment reaches as far as the water of his wudu reaches.[^9]

### A True Account: The Soft Robe and the Handkerchiefs of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3802" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, told by two Companions, al-Bara' ibn 'Azib and Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with them. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3802" audience="4-7" -->

One day, a present came for the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: a robe made of silk.

It was a very soft robe. The Companions touched it with their hands, and they were amazed at how soft it was.

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to teach men not to wear silk.

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to them, "Are you amazed at how soft this is?" He meant: do you really like how soft this robe is?

Then he told them something wonderful. There was a Companion named Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, and he had handkerchiefs in Jannah. A handkerchief is a little piece of cloth we wipe our hands with. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that Sa'd's handkerchiefs in Jannah were better than this robe, and softer.[^1][^2]

Think about it with me: the softest robe in this world is not as good as one little handkerchief in Jannah! So what must the big, beautiful clothes of Jannah be like? The Companions loved the soft robe, so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, reminded them of something even more beautiful: Jannah.[^10]

### Activity: Adornment Where Wudu Reaches

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.045.activity.adornment-where-wudu-reaches" -->

With your mom or dad, draw a picture of a boy or a girl, and together color in the places that wudu reaches: the face, the hands and arms, the head, and the feet. Every time you make a whole, calm wudu without rushing, your mom or dad puts a small shiny sticker on the hand in the picture, like a bracelet. ### A True Account: "Are You Amazed at How Soft This Is?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3802" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by al-Bara' ibn 'Azib and Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with them, in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3802" audience="8-12" -->

Ukaydir, the ruler of Dumah, sent the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, a gift: a garment of silk. In Anas's narration, it was a cloak of *sundus*, which is a very fine silk.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to forbid men to wear silk.

The Companions began running their hands over the garment, amazed at how beautiful and soft it was. They had never seen fabric that soft before.

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to them, "Are you amazed at how soft this is?" And then he swore an oath: "By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, the handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jannah are finer than this." In another narration: "better than it and softer."[^1][^2]

Sa'd ibn Mu'adh was a Companion from the Ansar. A handkerchief is a small piece of cloth for wiping your hands; nobody wears one to look good.

The scholars said that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, chose the handkerchief because it is the humblest of garments. If the humblest thing Sa'd has in Jannah is better than the softest garment the Companions had ever seen in this world, then what about his finest clothes? And the account also carries good news: Sa'd is in Jannah.[^10][^11]

And notice how the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, handled the moment. He didn't make fun of the garment, and he didn't get angry because his Companions admired it. Instead, he moved their eyes from the garment in their hands to the Jannah that lay ahead of them.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Yusuf has just finished middle school, and one of his relatives gives him a gold chain. His friends tell him, "Wear it. Everybody wears one. It's a look." That same week, his sister Maryam notices that her photos in new outfits get more likes than anything else she posts. One question ties the two together: who decides what our clothes are worth, and where is the person wearing them really looking?

The ruling in this world is clear: silk and gold are unlawful for men and lawful for women.[^6] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, gave the reason for forbidding silk and vessels of gold and silver: "For they are theirs in this world and ours in the Hereafter."[^7] And a warning came: "Whoever wears silk in this world will not wear it in the Hereafter." The scholars have understood it in several ways: as referring to someone who claims it is lawful, as a stern threat meant to deter, or as referring to a period before entering Jannah.[^8][^17] In Jannah, though, its people, men and women, will wear garments of *sundus* and *istabraq*, and will be adorned with bracelets of gold, silver and pearls.[^3][^4][^5][^15]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-250" -->

- **`Adornment (hilyah)`** — something that beautifies; in the hadith of wudu, Ibn Kathir and Ibn 'Uthaymin understood it as the jewelry of Jannah, and al-Mubarakfuri understood it as the light on the parts washed in wudu.[^14][^19]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-5834" -->

- **`Interpolated (mudraj)`** — words that found their way into a hadith but are not the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, such as the addition "he will not enter Jannah," which is Ibn az-Zubayr's own statement.[^18]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A True Account: A King's Robe and Sa'd's Handkerchief

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3802" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by al-Bara' ibn 'Azib and Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with them, in the two Sahihs. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3802" audience="13+" -->

A gift arrived in Madinah from Ukaydir, the ruler of Dumah: a garment of silk, or in Anas's narration, a cloak of *sundus*. A gift from a king, and among the finest things anyone could wear in that age.

The people gathered around it were not people of luxury. They were Companions who knew that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, forbade silk. Even so, they reached out to touch it, and were overcome with wonder at how soft and beautiful it was. A perfectly natural, human reaction.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not rebuke them, and he did not say, "This is worthless." He asked them, "Are you amazed at how soft this is?" Then he swore: "By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, the handkerchiefs of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh in Jannah are finer than this." In another narration: "better than it and softer."[^1][^2]

Look closely at the word he chose. He didn't say "Sa'd's clothes" or "Sa'd's robe." He said "his handkerchiefs." An-Nawawi explained that the handkerchief is the humblest of garments, because it is meant for dirt and rough use; the meaning is that the humblest of Sa'd's garments in Jannah is better than this, and the hadith affirms that Sa'd is in Jannah.[^10] Al-Qari quotes al-Khattabi: "If the humblest of them is like this, what do you suppose the finest of them is like?"[^11]

In a single moment, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, reset the scale. The robe that dazzled everyone present does not measure up to a handkerchief in Jannah, and Sa'd's rank did not come from the clothes he wore in this world, but from his faith and his deeds.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the account of the silk garment and Sa'd's handkerchiefs from al-Bukhari 3802 and 2615 and Muslim 2468, and explains it in an-Nawawi's words; describes the clothing and jewelry of Jannah from al-Kahf 18:31, al-Hajj 22:23 and al-Insan 76:21, including the difference between Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on *sundus* and *istabraq*; sets out the ruling on silk and gold in this world from at-Tirmidhi 1720 and al-Bukhari 5426; presents the ways in which al-Bukhari 5834 has been understood; states the two views on "adornment" in Muslim 250; and plans a week of thorough wudu.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board divided into three columns headed "What the people of Jannah are promised," "The ruling in this world" and "The way there"; printed copies of the activity chart; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 3802, 3249, 5836 and 2615, Muslim 2468, al-Bukhari 5426 and 5834, at-Tirmidhi 1720 and Muslim 250; and reviews an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 2468 and 250, Ibn Hajar's discussion in *Fath al-Bari* of al-Bukhari 5834, and the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on the three ayat. The teacher also prepares a calm answer for anyone who asks about points of fiqh (such as small amounts of silk, or watches and eyeglasses): refer them to the books of fiqh and to those qualified to give fatwas.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What is the most beautiful fabric you have ever touched? Why does it stay in your memory?" Then: "What if someone told you that a handkerchief in Jannah is better than it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the account of the handkerchiefs in both narrations, with the words of an-Nawawi and al-Khattabi (6 minutes); then the three ayat with their tafsir, filling in the first column (6 minutes); then the three hadiths on the ruling, in order: at-Tirmidhi 1720 for the ruling, al-Bukhari 5426 for its reason, and al-Bukhari 5834 for the warning and the ways it has been understood (5 minutes); then the hadith of adornment with its two views (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 3 and 4. The teacher stresses three guardrails: the prohibition applies to men, and to wearing; silk and gold are not evil in themselves; and the warning is not applied to any particular person. The teacher also explains the difference between the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the addition that is Ibn az-Zubayr's own statement.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses the daily prayer for which they will make a thorough wudu, identifies the spot they usually neglect, and sets up their chart (8 minutes). Then the learners review the obligatory acts and the sunnahs of wudu from a reliable fiqh source, and each one decides whom they will teach on the seventh day (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "State the ruling on silk and gold for men and for women in this world, and what Allah has promised the people of Jannah of them," and "Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, choose the handkerchief?" Then read the lesson's du'a, and agree to review the charts in a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the account of the handkerchiefs, the ayah of al-Hajj and at-Tirmidhi 1720 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the different words used for the gift in the account of the handkerchiefs (*hullah*, *thawb*, *jubbah*) along with al-Qadi 'Iyad's explanation of the difference as an-Nawawi relates it, and to review what Ibn Hajar says about the interpolated addition.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The lesson is not to be used to shame men who wear gold or silk, or to declare anyone bound for the Fire or barred from Jannah. Ibn Kathir's interpretation of silver and gold is not to be presented as a definitive text. Do not rely on unsound reports describing the jewelry of Jannah, such as the report of the angel who crafts the jewelry of the people of Jannah, which Ibn Kathir relates from Ka'b al-Ahbar: those are Ka'b's own words, not a hadith. And do not teach that extending the washing up to the armpit is a binding Sunnah; an-Nawawi relates from al-Qadi 'Iyad what shows that Abu Hurayrah disliked people thinking it was an obligation.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence: "What is the difference between an adornment for show and an adornment in private?" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question about how it went, without looking at anyone's log.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may keep a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the merits of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih al-Bukhari.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not embarrass a student who wears something of gold or silk; deal with it through private advice. Do not discuss female students' bodies or clothing in front of the group, and discuss the issue of photos in general terms without looking into anyone's accounts. Be mindful of any student under financial pressure or anxious about their appearance, and refer them to a counselor when needed.
