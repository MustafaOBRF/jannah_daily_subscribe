# The Degrees of Jannah and the Ranks of Its People

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Draw on the ayat of Surah al-Isra' (17:18-21) to show that Jannah is made up of degrees, and that the distinctions among its people in the Hereafter are greater than any distinction between people in this world.
- Explain the hadith of the hundred degrees (al-Bukhari 2790), showing that entry into Jannah is promised to whoever believes and keeps the obligations, that those who enter are then distinguished from one another in the degrees above that, and that the distance between two degrees is like the distance between heaven and earth.
- Interpret the hadith of the people of the lofty chambers (al-Bukhari 3256), showing that the high degrees are not reserved for the prophets alone but are reached by "men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."
- Relate the account of the poor among the Emigrants who said, "The people of wealth have carried off the highest degrees" (Muslim 595), and draw from it the etiquette of competing for the degrees with a sound heart: seeking a deed within one's reach instead of wishing another person's blessing away, and accepting the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him: "That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills."
- Distinguish praiseworthy aspiration (*ghibtah*) from blameworthy envy (*hasad*) when comparing oneself with others.
- Carry out the "A Door Within My Reach" activity, which turns comparison with someone who has gone ahead through means the learner does not have into a good deed within the learner's own capacity.
- Remember the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to keep the heart free of envy, to help us compete in doing good, and to bring us to the highest degrees.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah is not a single station in which everyone who enters stands on equal footing. It is a structure of degrees, one above another, and its people differ in rank there more than people ever differ in this world. This lesson establishes that truth from the texts, shows that the high degrees are open to more than the prophets, and sets out the etiquette of competing for them with a sound heart. The highest and lowest stations, and al-Firdaws, each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-17-18-21" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And the Hereafter Is Greater in Degrees and Greater in Distinction"

> **{مَنْ كَانَ يُرِيدُ الْعَاجِلَةَ عَجَّلْنَا لَهُ فِيهَا مَا نَشَاءُ لِمَنْ نُرِيدُ ثُمَّ جَعَلْنَا لَهُ جَهَنَّمَ يَصْلَاهَا مَذْمُومًا مَدْحُورًا * وَمَنْ أَرَادَ الْآخِرَةَ وَسَعَىٰ لَهَا سَعْيَهَا وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ فَأُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ سَعْيُهُمْ مَشْكُورًا * كُلًّا نُمِدُّ هَٰؤُلَاءِ وَهَٰؤُلَاءِ مِنْ عَطَاءِ رَبِّكَ ۚ وَمَا كَانَ عَطَاءُ رَبِّكَ مَحْظُورًا * انْظُرْ كَيْفَ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَىٰ بَعْضٍ ۚ وَلَلْآخِرَةُ أَكْبَرُ دَرَجَاتٍ وَأَكْبَرُ تَفْضِيلًا}** [الإسراء: ١٨-٢١][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Whoever desires the fleeting world, We hasten for him in it whatever We will, for whomever We please; then We appoint Hell for him, where he will burn, condemned and cast out. And whoever desires the Hereafter, and strives for it with the striving it deserves, while he is a believer, those are the ones whose striving is gratefully acknowledged. To each, both these and those, We extend from the bounty of your Lord, and the bounty of your Lord is never withheld. See how We have favored some of them over others; and the Hereafter is greater in degrees and greater in distinction."** (al-Isra' 17:18-21)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Taymiyyah, may Allah have mercy on him, established that Allah sustains from His bounty both the one who seeks this world and the one who seeks the Hereafter, and that the people of the Hereafter differ from one another there more than people differ in this world. Jannah, then, consists of degrees that differ immensely, according to faith and God-consciousness (*taqwa*).[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The gaps of this world are small when measured against the gaps of the Hereafter, and the striving that Allah acknowledges brings together three things: the will, the deed, and faith.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "In Jannah There Are a Hundred Degrees"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«مَنْ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَبِرَسُولِهِ، وَأَقَامَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَصَامَ رَمَضَانَ، كَانَ حَقًّا عَلَى اللَّهِ أَنْ يُدْخِلَهُ الْجَنَّةَ، جَاهَدَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، أَوْ جَلَسَ فِي أَرْضِهِ الَّتِي وُلِدَ فِيهَا»**. فَقَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، أَفَلَا نُبَشِّرُ النَّاسَ؟ قَالَ: **«إِنَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مِائَةَ دَرَجَةٍ أَعَدَّهَا اللَّهُ لِلْمُجَاهِدِينَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ، مَا بَيْنَ الدَّرَجَتَيْنِ كَمَا بَيْنَ السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ ...»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever believes in Allah and His Messenger, establishes the prayer, and fasts Ramadan, it is a right upon Allah to admit him into Jannah, whether he strives in the path of Allah or stays in the land where he was born."** They said, "O Messenger of Allah, shall we not give the people this good news?" He said: **"In Jannah there are a hundred degrees that Allah has prepared for those who strive in His path (*al-mujahidun*); the distance between two degrees is like the distance between heaven and earth ..."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The hadith distinguishes between the basic entry into Jannah, a right that Allah, out of His grace, has made due to whoever believes and keeps the obligations even without going out in jihad, and the degrees in which those who enter are then distinguished from one another. The words omitted from the end (...) are the Prophet's instruction, peace and blessings be upon him, to ask Allah for al-Firdaws, together with his description of it. That passage belongs to the lesson on al-Firdaws al-A'la, and leaving it out does not alter the meaning of what is quoted here.

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith guards against two errors: the despair of someone who thinks Jannah is reached only through the deeds of the spiritual giants, and the complacency of someone who thinks that merely getting in is the whole goal of striving.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3256" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The People of the Lofty Chambers Are Like a Brilliant Star on the Horizon"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«إِنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَتَرَاءَيُونَ أَهْلَ الْغُرَفِ مِنْ فَوْقِهِمْ، كَمَا يَتَرَاءَيُونَ الْكَوْكَبَ الدُّرِّيَّ الْغَابِرَ فِي الْأُفُقِ مِنَ الْمَشْرِقِ أَوِ الْمَغْرِبِ، لِتَفَاضُلِ مَا بَيْنَهُمْ»**. قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، تِلْكَ مَنَازِلُ الْأَنْبِيَاءِ لَا يَبْلُغُهَا غَيْرُهُمْ؟ قَالَ: **«بَلَى وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، رِجَالٌ آمَنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَصَدَّقُوا الْمُرْسَلِينَ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The people of Jannah will gaze up at the people of the lofty chambers above them as you gaze at a brilliant star lingering far off on the horizon, in the east or the west, because of how they differ in rank."** They said, "O Messenger of Allah, those are the stations of the prophets, which no one else will reach?" He said: **"Not so! By the One in whose hand my soul rests, they are men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-ghabir* means a star that is far off on the horizon as it rises or sets. Al-Qurtubi, may Allah have mercy on him, mentioned that the chambers differ in height according to the deeds of those who dwell in them, and that what is meant is faith brought to its fullness and a wholehearted affirmation of the messengers without hesitation, not merely the basic core of faith.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Companions assumed these stations belonged to the prophets alone, so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore an oath that others would reach them too. No one is shut out of them because of lineage or poverty.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-595a" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The People of Wealth Have Carried Off the Highest Degrees"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه: أَنَّ فُقَرَاءَ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ أَتَوْا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَقَالُوا: ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ بِالدَّرَجَاتِ الْعُلَى وَالنَّعِيمِ الْمُقِيمِ. فَقَالَ: **«وَمَا ذَاكَ؟»**. قَالُوا: يُصَلُّونَ كَمَا نُصَلِّي، وَيَصُومُونَ كَمَا نَصُومُ، وَيَتَصَدَّقُونَ وَلَا نَتَصَدَّقُ، وَيُعْتِقُونَ وَلَا نُعْتِقُ. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«أَفَلَا أُعَلِّمُكُمْ شَيْئًا تُدْرِكُونَ بِهِ مَنْ سَبَقَكُمْ، وَتَسْبِقُونَ بِهِ مَنْ بَعْدَكُمْ، وَلَا يَكُونُ أَحَدٌ أَفْضَلَ مِنْكُمْ إِلَّا مَنْ صَنَعَ مِثْلَ مَا صَنَعْتُمْ؟»**. قَالُوا: بَلَى يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ. قَالَ: **«تُسَبِّحُونَ وَتُكَبِّرُونَ وَتَحْمَدُونَ دُبُرَ كُلِّ صَلَاةٍ ثَلَاثًا وَثَلَاثِينَ مَرَّةً»**. قَالَ أَبُو صَالِحٍ: فَرَجَعَ فُقَرَاءُ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَقَالُوا: سَمِعَ إِخْوَانُنَا أَهْلُ الْأَمْوَالِ بِمَا فَعَلْنَا فَفَعَلُوا مِثْلَهُ. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«ذَلِكَ فَضْلُ اللَّهِ يُؤْتِيهِ مَنْ يَشَاءُ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him: The poor among the Emigrants came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "The people of great wealth have carried off the highest degrees and the lasting bliss." He said: **"And how is that?"** They said, "They pray as we pray and fast as we fast, but they give in charity and we do not, and they free slaves and we do not." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Shall I not teach you something by which you will catch up with those who have gone ahead of you and stay ahead of those who come after you, and no one will be better than you except someone who does what you have done?"** They said, "Yes indeed, O Messenger of Allah." He said: **"Glorify Allah, declare His greatness, and praise Him after every prayer, thirty-three times."** Abu Salih said: Then the poor among the Emigrants came back to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "Our brothers, the people of wealth, heard what we were doing and did the same." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Ad-duthur* means great wealth. Most scholars understood the hadith to mean thirty-three repetitions of each of the three phrases of remembrance. An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, mentioned that some scholars cited this hadith to argue that the grateful rich person is better than the patient poor person, and that the question is a matter of scholarly disagreement.[^8] The account of the poor Emigrants' return comes here from the words of Abu Salih; in another narration recorded by Muslim, Suhayl incorporated it into the hadith of Abu Hurayrah.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, affirmed their ambition for the highest degrees. They did not ask for their brothers' blessings to be taken away; they asked for a door within their own reach. Then his words, "That is the bounty of Allah," shut the door on envy.

All of Jannah is beautiful, and everyone who goes there is very, very happy. But Allah made Jannah with levels, some higher than others. Our Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah see the people above them far away and shining, like a bright star at the edge of the sky.[^4] Whoever believes in Allah and does good as best they can, Allah lifts them up, level after level.

### A True Account: The Poor Friends Who Wanted the High Levels

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-595a" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened in the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-595a" audience="4-7" -->

In the city of Madinah, some of the Prophet's friends, peace and blessings be upon him, were poor. They had no money. Some of his other friends were rich, with lots and lots of money. The poor friends came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, the rich people have gotten ahead of us to the high levels of Jannah! They pray like we pray, and they fast like we fast, but they give their money in charity, and we can't."

The poor friends did not say, "We want the rich people to lose their money." No! They wanted to do good, just like them.

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to them: "Shall I teach you something that will help you catch up with the people who got ahead of you?" They said, "Yes, please, O Messenger of Allah!" And he taught them to say after every prayer: *Subhan Allah* (Glory be to Allah), *Alhamdulillah* (All praise belongs to Allah), and *Allahu Akbar* (Allah is the Greatest), thirty-three times each. That is something a poor person and a rich person can both do.

So they did it. Then the rich people heard about it, and they did it too! The poor friends went back and told the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and he said: "That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills." That means every good thing is a gift from Allah, and He gives it to whomever He chooses.[^5]

Here is what it all means, in a few simple words: if you see someone else doing a good thing that you can't do, don't be sad, and don't wish they would lose what they have. Instead, look for a good thing that *you* can do, and go and do it.[^5]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

You might think that everyone who enters Jannah ends up in the same place. But the Qur'an tells us that Jannah has degrees, and that the difference between its people in the Hereafter is bigger than the difference between people in this world.[^1] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Jannah has a hundred degrees that Allah has prepared for those who strive in His path, and that the distance between any two degrees is like the distance between heaven and earth.[^3] He also told us that the people of Jannah see the owners of the lofty chambers above them the way we see a bright star shining far off on the horizon. And here is the best part: these high stations are not for the prophets alone. They are reached by men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers.[^4] So how do we race toward them? And what should we do when we see someone getting ahead of us?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-595a" -->

- **`The people of wealth (ahl ad-duthur)`** — people who own a great deal of money; *duthur* is the plural of *dathr*, which means great wealth.
- **`The highest degrees (ad-darajat al-'ula)`** — the high stations in Jannah to which Allah raises people of faith and good deeds.
- **`After every prayer (dubura kulli salah)`** — right after finishing each of the obligatory prayers.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Poor Emigrants and the Highest Degrees

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-595a" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated in Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-595a" audience="8-12" -->

In Madinah, among the Prophet's Companions, peace and blessings be upon him, were some of the Emigrants (*al-Muhajirun*) who were poor and owned next to nothing. Among the Muslims there were also rich people with plenty of money. The poor Companions noticed something that worried them: the rich prayed just as they prayed and fasted just as they fasted, but then went further, giving charity and freeing slaves with their wealth, something the poor simply could not do.

So they came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "The people of wealth have carried off the highest degrees and the lasting bliss!" In other words: the people with money have beaten us to the high stations of Jannah. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked them, "And how is that?" So they explained.

Notice what they asked for. They did not ask for the rich to lose their money, and they did not say anything bad about them. They wanted a road that would let them catch up.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to them: **"Shall I not teach you something by which you will catch up with those who have gone ahead of you and stay ahead of those who come after you, and no one will be better than you except someone who does what you have done?"** They said, "Yes indeed, O Messenger of Allah." So he taught them to glorify Allah, declare His greatness, and praise Him after every prayer, thirty-three times: a deed that needs no money, one that anyone can do.

The poor Companions started doing it. But the rich heard about it and started doing it too! So the poor came back to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "Our brothers, the people of wealth, heard what we were doing and did the same." Notice that they called them "our brothers." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills."**[^5]

This account teaches us that racing toward the highest degrees is a beautiful thing, and that when a Muslim sees someone getting ahead of him through something he doesn't have, he looks for a good deed he *can* do, he is happy for his brother, and he knows that Allah gives His bounty to whom He wills.[^5]

### Activity: The "Within My Reach" Chart

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.022.activity.within-my-means-door" -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in an age of nonstop comparison: grades, followers, and achievements put on display across our screens every single day. The Qur'an does not deny that people differ in rank. It tells us, rather, that the ranking of the Hereafter is far greater: "And the Hereafter is greater in degrees and greater in distinction."[^1] In Jannah there are degrees, and between any two of them lies a distance like that between heaven and earth,[^3] and its people look up at the owners of the lofty chambers above them like a star far off on the horizon. When the Companions assumed those stations belonged to the prophets alone, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, swore that they belong to "men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers."[^4]

So the question is not whether competition exists. The question is: what are we competing for, and with what kind of heart? Comparison can lead to envy, which wishes another person's blessing gone, or to aspiration, which pushes you to act. The account of the poor Emigrants is a living model of the second path.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-595a" -->

- **`Aspiration (al-ghibtah)`** — wishing for the same good that someone else has without wishing it taken away from them; it is praiseworthy in matters of the Hereafter.[^9]
- **`Envy (al-hasad)`** — wishing for a blessing to be taken away from the one who has it; a blameworthy trait that corrupts the heart.[^9]
- **`The people of wealth (ahl ad-duthur)`** — people who own a great deal of money.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: "The People of Wealth Have Carried Off the Highest Degrees"

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-595a" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated in Sahih Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-595a" audience="13+" -->

In Madinah, the poor among the Emigrants did everything others did in prayer and fasting, yet they watched their wealthier brothers move ahead of them through a door they had no way of entering: charity and the freeing of slaves. So they came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with a sentence that laid bare what was really on their minds: "The people of wealth have carried off the highest degrees and the lasting bliss." What they were worried about was not a bigger house or nicer clothes. It was a station in Jannah.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked them, "And how is that?" They said, "They pray as we pray and fast as we fast, but they give in charity and we do not, and they free slaves and we do not." This was the moment of decision. That comparison could easily have curdled into bitterness or gossip about the rich. But the poor did not ask for anything to be taken from their brothers. Instead, they laid the matter before the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, so that he could show them a way forward.

He said to them: **"Shall I not teach you something by which you will catch up with those who have gone ahead of you and stay ahead of those who come after you, and no one will be better than you except someone who does what you have done?"** Then he taught them to glorify Allah, declare His greatness, and praise Him after every prayer, thirty-three times: a deed that takes no money, only consistency.

Then came a twist they had not seen coming: the rich heard about it and did the same. So the poor came back and said, "Our brothers, the people of wealth, heard what we were doing and did the same." The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, replied: **"That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills."** He did not tell the rich to stop, and he did not promise the poor that they would come out on top. He returned the whole matter to the bounty of Allah.[^5]

The lesson here runs deep. Competing for the degrees is legitimate, and comparison can fuel action instead of envy. When someone else gets ahead of you, two tasks remain in front of you: do what is within your reach, and be content with what Allah has apportioned, without wishing anyone's blessing away.[^5]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What does the poor Companions' choice of the phrase "the highest degrees" reveal about their ambition?
2. Why was the answer "That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills" not unfair to the poor?
3. How can you tell, inside yourself, the difference between a moment of aspiration and a moment of envy while scrolling through other people's accounts?
4. What does it mean that the lofty chambers belong to "men who believed in Allah and affirmed the truth of the messengers," and not to the prophets alone?
5. What is a good deed in which someone is ahead of you because of means you don't have, and what is the alternative within your reach?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The "From Comparison to Action" Journal

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.022.activity.within-my-means-door" -->

Set aside one page for a week. Every time you catch yourself comparing yourself with someone else in some area of goodness (memorization, volunteering, kindness to parents, or giving), write three lines: What does this person have that I don't have right now? What lawful deed is within my reach today and serves the same purpose? Did I do it? At the end of the week, write a short paragraph: How many comparisons turned into action? And is there any trace of envy left in your heart that you need to treat by making du'a for the person who has the blessing?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner uses the ayat of al-Isra' to show that Jannah consists of degrees and that the ranking of the Hereafter is greater than the ranking of this world; uses the hadith of the hundred degrees to distinguish basic entry from differences in rank; explains how the hadith of the lofty chambers opened the high degrees to those other than the prophets; draws three points of etiquette for competing in goodness from the account of the poor Emigrants; and writes a one-week action plan within the learner's own reach.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for consulting Surah al-Isra'; a whiteboard; an "A Door Within My Reach" activity sheet with two columns and a line for the end-of-week review; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the four texts and their references, as well as the complete text of al-Bukhari 2790 including the part omitted from its end, and reviews an-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim 595 in order to present the disagreement over the grateful rich person and the patient poor person in a balanced way, without rushing to a verdict. The teacher also notes that the account of the poor Emigrants' return comes from the words of Abu Salih in the narration of Qutaybah, and prepares a neutral example of a door of goodness that requires means (such as an endowment, *waqf*) together with an alternative within reach.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Do you picture Jannah as a single station where everyone is equal? And when have you felt that someone got ahead of you in doing good through means you don't have?" The teacher writes two answers on the board without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Isra' 17:18-21 aloud together, and ask learners to identify the conditions of the striving that Allah acknowledges (5 minutes). Then read the hadith of the hundred degrees with the question: "What did the Companions want to announce as good news, and what did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, add?" (5 minutes). Then read the hadith of the lofty chambers, pausing on "Not so! By the One in whose hand my soul rests" (5 minutes). Then read the account of the poor Emigrants in full, dividing it into four moments: the complaint, the question, the teaching, and the return (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 12 minutes:** The teacher builds a chain on the board: "Differences in rank are established → their cause is faith and deeds → the distances are immense → the door is open to those other than the prophets → the etiquette of competing." The teacher asks: "How does the request of the poor differ from envy?" and "Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, close with the words, 'That is the bounty of Allah'?" The teacher briefly presents the disagreement over the grateful rich person and the patient poor person.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 13 minutes:** Learners complete the "A Door Within My Reach" activity individually (8 minutes). Then pairs exchange the alternative each has chosen and check together that it is lawful, realistic, and fixed to a specific time (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** An exit card with two questions: "Give one piece of evidence that Jannah consists of degrees," and "Write one sentence that distinguishes aspiration (*ghibtah*) from envy." The teacher closes by reminding learners of the date for the weekly review of the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, it is enough to cover al-Isra' 17:21 and the account of the poor Emigrants. Advanced learners are asked to consult the narration in al-Bukhari 843, compare its wording with Muslim's narration, and discuss the meaning of "by which you will catch up with those who have gone ahead of you" alongside "except someone who does what you have done."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No specific individual should be declared certain of any particular degree in Jannah. The lesson must not be used to disparage the wealthy, to glorify poverty for its own sake, or to belittle anyone whose deeds are few; even the lowest station of Jannah is immense bliss. Take care that comparison never turns into a public ranking of learners by their deeds, and keep participation in the activity optional.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says in a simple sentence that Jannah has levels, some higher than others; retells what the poor Companions did when they had no money; names one good deed they can do themselves; and says *Subhan Allah* after one prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Large sheets of paper; child-safe coloring pencils or crayons; a picture or drawing of a sky with stars at different heights; the du'a card in large, clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account in a calm voice, prepares two examples (a good deed that grown-ups do, and a good deed that little ones can do), and chooses an upcoming prayer time for practicing the *tasbih*.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver shows the picture of the sky and asks: "Which stars are the highest? Are they all in the same place?" Then the caregiver says: "Jannah has levels too, and all of them are beautiful."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the short explanation, then tells the account of the poor Companions as given in the story unit, pausing to ask, "What do you think the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to them?" before continuing.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver repeats two ideas: "We do the good that we can do," and "We are happy when other people do good." Then the caregiver asks the three short questions.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The children carry out the "My Little Hand Can Do It" activity by tracing their two hands, then say *Subhan Allah* with the caregiver, counting on the fingers of the second hand, after the prayer or at an agreed time.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Observe whether the child can point to the hand showing a good deed they can do and name it; then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, the caregiver traces the hands and the child chooses a ready-made picture to glue on. Older children are asked to count thirty-three *tasbihat* with help.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Avoid sorting children into "higher" and "lower," and never link the levels to how much their families own; stress that everyone in Jannah is happy. Take care that no child from a low-income family feels embarrassed when the poor and the rich are discussed.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="35" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 35 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains that Jannah consists of degrees set far apart, defines the three terms, retells the account of the poor Emigrants in sequence, turns three comparisons from their own life into deeds within their reach, and practices one of them for a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a "Within My Reach" chart sheet with three columns; pens or pencils; a weekly follow-up card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the terms, the account in Muslim 595, and the hadith of the hundred degrees, and prepares a worked example of one row of the chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Imagine your friend donated a large amount of money to a charity project, and you don't have anything like that. How do you feel? And what do you do?" The teacher listens to two answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** The students read the explanation, then the teacher explains the three terms. Then the story is read aloud in turns, pausing after the poor Companions say, "The people of wealth have carried off...," and again after they report what the rich did.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher discusses: "Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, choose remembrance of Allah for them in particular?" and "Why did the poor call the rich 'our brothers'?" The teacher highlights that envy wishes a blessing away, and that the poor Companions did not do that.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** Students fill in three rows of the "Within My Reach" chart, and each student chooses one row to put into practice.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Students answer the fourth understanding question aloud; then read the du'a together, and agree to review the follow-up card after a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, a single row of the chart is enough, with ready-made examples to choose from. Advanced students are asked to read the hadith of the lofty chambers and write a sentence explaining "Not so! By the One in whose hand my soul rests."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask students to name, in front of the class, a classmate they compare themselves with, and do not compare students' deeds publicly. Avoid any suggestion that a family's wealth or poverty determines a child's standing with Allah.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the account of the poor Emigrants as a model for turning comparison into action; distinguishes aspiration (*ghibtah*) from envy (*hasad*); explains how the hadith of the lofty chambers shows that the high degrees are open to those other than the prophets; and starts a weekly journal for turning comparisons into deeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; the activity journal page; term cards for aspiration and envy; a whiteboard.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral, contemporary examples of comparison (a post about a volunteering achievement, Qur'an memorization, a scholarship) and reviews al-Isra' 17:21 and the hadith of the lofty chambers in order to present them briefly.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "When did the last comparison you made push you to work harder? And when did it leave you feeling bitter?" The teacher writes the two words "fuel" and "bitterness" on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read al-Isra' 17:21 and the sentence from the hadith of the lofty chambers quoted in the explanation. Then students read the story in small groups and identify its "moment of decision" and the phrases that show the soundness of the poor Companions' hearts.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher discusses the difference between aspiration and envy, why "That is the bounty of Allah, which He gives to whom He wills" was not unfair, and how students can apply all this to social media.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students begin the "From Comparison to Action" journal by writing their first entry in class, and commit to completing it over a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes one line: "One comparison I will turn into action is...". After a week, the teacher follows up with a general question about how many comparisons turned into action, without looking at any private details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing about themselves may analyze a hypothetical example instead. Advanced students are asked to compare the narration in al-Bukhari 843 with Muslim's narration and write a paragraph on the meaning of "by which you will catch up with those who have gone ahead of you."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The activity journal stays private to the student. Take care that talk of the degrees does not turn into anxiety or a sense of inferiority; the aim is striving together with contentment. Point out that envy is a passing thought to be treated through du'a and action, not through self-punishment, and direct any student who shows serious distress over comparison toward a supportive one-to-one conversation.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
