# The Location of Jannah and Its Vastness

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite Allah's statement, `We said, "O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Jannah and eat freely from it wherever you wish"` (al-Baqarah 2:35), and explain that the first human dwelling in existence was a garden of vast provision, restricted only by a single prohibition.
- Narrate the Qur'anic story of Adam, peace be upon him, being settled in Jannah, then removed from it because of one slip, and connect that to the vastness of that abode and the gravity of what was lost through disobedience.
- Recite the two ayat, `and a Jannah as wide as the heavens and earth` (Aal 'Imran 3:133) and `a Jannah whose width is like the width of the heaven and earth` (al-Hadid 57:21), and explain that this description invites awe at the promised Jannah, not a geometric calculation of it.
- Narrate the hadith, "The space of a whip in Jannah is better than this world and all it contains," and explain that Jannah's vastness is a vastness of value and bliss, not merely of area.
- Distinguish between believing in Jannah's vastness and greatness as stated in the text, and attempting to determine its exact nature or location by speculation.
- Carry out the "Scale of the Small Place" activity, weighing the value of the smallest part of Jannah against the greatest thing a person owns in this world, and applying that to real-life situations.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

When the Qur'an describes Jannah as `عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ` ("its width is that of the heavens and earth"), it opens the heart to a vastness beyond worldly measure, not a door to geometric calculation. The earliest witness to this vastness in the Qur'an is not a general verse but a specific event: the first human, Adam, peace be upon him, was settled with his wife in Jannah itself, according to the majority of scholars,[^3] free to eat from any place he wished, forbidden only one tree. That Jannah was so spacious that nothing in it called for more than a single prohibition.

Then came a single slip, through Satan's whispering, and Adam and his wife were removed from what they had been in and sent down to earth. This event shows the heart two linked truths: the Jannah Adam lost was a tremendous, vast bliss, and entering or leaving it depends not on its size but on obedience to Allah within it. The ayat of Aal 'Imran and al-Hadid tell us this same vastness is promised to every believer who hastens to his Lord's forgiveness, and the Sunnah adds another measure: not vastness of place alone but vastness of value, such that its smallest spot is better than this entire world and all it contains.

This corrects two opposite deviations:

1. **Turning vastness into cosmic speculation:** busying oneself with calculating Jannah's dimensions or its exact location, an unseen matter that can be established only by revealed text.
2. **Underestimating Jannah's value despite its vastness:** supposing worldly bliss, however small, approaches Jannah's bliss, when its smallest spot is better than this world and all it contains.

True vastness is a vastness of provision and value, not one measured in meters, and the first to make it his actual home was the father of humankind, Adam, peace be upon him.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-35" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### Adam's First Dwelling: A Jannah of Vast Provision

> **وَقُلْنَا يَا آدَمُ اسْكُنْ أَنْتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ وَكُلَا مِنْهَا رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا وَلَا تَقْرَبَا هَٰذِهِ الشَّجَرَةَ فَتَكُونَا مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ.** [البقرة: ٣٥][^1]

وفي سورة الأعراف: `وَيَا آدَمُ اسْكُنْ أَنْتَ وَزَوْجُكَ الْجَنَّةَ فَكُلَا مِنْ حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا وَلَا تَقْرَبَا هَٰذِهِ الشَّجَرَةَ فَتَكُونَا مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ.`[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We said, 'O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Jannah and eat freely from it wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers.'"** (al-Baqarah 2:35)[^1]

And in Surah al-A'raf: `"O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Jannah and eat from wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers."`[^2]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Ibn Kathir notes that scholars differed over the Jannah in which Adam, peace be upon him, was settled: was it in the heavens or on earth? Most held that it was in the heavens, and al-Qurtubi attributed the view that it was on earth to the Mu'tazilah and the Qadariyyah.[^3] `رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا` ("freely, wherever you wish") conveys broad, unrestricted permission: abundant, pleasant eating from any place they wished, with no limit of place or amount, and only one particular tree excepted.

#### Lesson Explanation

This is the first practical description of Jannah's vastness: a place where Allah allows its two inhabitants to eat from any spot they wish, forbidding them only one specific thing. The breadth of what was permitted is itself evidence of the place's vastness and bliss, even before any direct description of its size appears.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-36" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### One Slip Removes Them From That Bliss

> **فَأَزَلَّهُمَا الشَّيْطَانُ عَنْهَا فَأَخْرَجَهُمَا مِمَّا كَانَا فِيهِ وَقُلْنَا اهْبِطُوا بَعْضُكُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ وَلَكُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ مُسْتَقَرٌّ وَمَتَاعٌ إِلَىٰ حِينٍ.** [البقرة: ٣٦][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"But Satan caused them to slip out of it and removed them from what they had been in, and We said, 'Go down, some of you enemies to others, and you have on earth a settlement and provision for a time.'"** (al-Baqarah 2:36)[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

Adam and his wife were not removed for want of space, but by one slip, prompted by Satan's whispering, in taking the single thing forbidden to them amid all that vastness. Allah later accepted their repentance,[^9] but the lesson remains: the wider the bliss, the greater the loss of one who neglects the single command set for him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-3-133" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### A Jannah as Wide as the Heavens and Earth

> **وَسَارِعُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ أُعِدَّتْ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ.** [آل عمران: ١٣٣][^5]

> **سَابِقُوا إِلَىٰ مَغْفِرَةٍ مِنْ رَبِّكُمْ وَجَنَّةٍ عَرْضُهَا كَعَرْضِ السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ أُعِدَّتْ لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَرُسُلِهِ.** [الحديد: ٢١][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And hasten to forgiveness from your Lord and a Jannah as wide as the heavens and earth, prepared for the righteous."** (Aal 'Imran 3:133)[^5]

> **"Race toward forgiveness from your Lord and a Jannah whose width is like the width of the heaven and earth, prepared for those who believe in Allah and His messengers."** (al-Hadid 57:21)[^6]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Commentators note that describing Jannah's `عَرْضُهَا` ("width") as like the heavens and earth magnifies its vastness beyond human comprehension; it is not a geometric measurement of its area. Width is usually shorter than length, so what of its length?[^8] This description therefore must not be turned into cosmic speculation; it is left to what it indicates: the abode of the Hereafter is vaster than anything all the people of this world together could imagine.

#### Lesson Explanation

This vastness, according to the majority of scholars, extends the Jannah Adam, peace be upon him, first lived in; it is today promised to every believer who hastens to his Lord's forgiveness — Jannah is not confined to a historical event that has ended, but is an abode that exists now, promised and worth hastening toward.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3250" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Space of a Whip in Jannah Is Better Than This World

> عَنْ سَهْلِ بْنِ سَعْدٍ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«مَوْضِعُ سَوْطٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ خَيْرٌ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا وَمَا فِيهَا».**[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Sahl ibn Sa'd, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"The space of a whip in Jannah is better than this world and all it contains."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Bukhari narrated it from Sahl ibn Sa'd, and similarly from Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with them: "A single evening or morning outing in the path of Allah is better than this world and all it contains, and the length of a bow of one of you in Jannah, or the space of a qid — meaning his whip — is better than this world and all it contains."[^7] Muslim's version contains only the virtue of the morning and evening outing. `مَوْضِعُ سَوْطٍ` ("the space of a whip") means a very small area: the space a rider's whip would take up if laid on the ground.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith adds another measure of vastness besides area: vastness of value. The smallest conceivable spot in it is better than the whole world, its earth and sky and all its dominion and adornment. Jannah's vastness is thus two vastnesses together: a vastness of place that no description can encompass, and a vastness of value that nothing of this world's goods, however great, can equal.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What does the phrase `رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا` indicate about the vastness of the Jannah Adam, peace be upon him, was settled in?
2. Why was a single slip enough to remove Adam and his wife from that vast bliss?
3. Why do commentators warn against turning the description `عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ` into a geometric calculation?
4. How does the hadith of "the space of a whip" add new meaning to Jannah's vastness beyond area?
5. Where in your life do you find yourself valuing a small worldly possession more than it deserves, against what Allah has promised in the Hereafter?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

Jannah is very, very big — bigger than any place we have ever seen in our lives. Even a tiny piece of it is better than all this world's toys, houses, and gardens put together. The first person to live in Jannah was our father Adam, peace be upon him, and we will learn his story.

<!-- unit:end -->

### A Story of the Prophets: Adam's First, Spacious Home

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-35" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-35" audience="4-7" -->

Allah created the first human, named Adam, peace be upon him, and settled him in Jannah with his wife. Allah told them: `كُلَا مِنْهَا رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا` — meaning: eat from any place in this vast Jannah you wish, and never deny yourselves anything, except one single tree Allah told them not to go near.[^1] Jannah was so vast, full of every beautiful thing, yet Allah forbade them only one thing, so they would see that obedience matters more than all that vastness.

Satan came and tried to make them forget Allah's command, so they ate from that one tree. So Allah sent them out of that beautiful, spacious place, and they came down to earth.[^4] Then Adam, peace be upon him, prayed to his Lord and was truly sorry, so Allah forgave him and had mercy on him.[^9]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

When Allah says Jannah's width is like the width of the heavens and earth, He is teaching us it is too vast for our imagination to grasp. The first to truly live this vastness was Adam, peace be upon him, whom Allah settled in Jannah with his wife, permitting them to eat from every place in it except one tree. But the vastness of the place did not stop one slip from removing them from it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that even the smallest spot in Jannah is better than this whole world, so its vastness is both a vastness of place and a vastness of value.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story of the Prophets: The Scope of Obedience Within a Vast Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-35" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-35" audience="8-12" -->

Allah said: `"We said, 'O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Jannah and eat freely from it wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers.'"`[^1] After Allah created Adam, peace be upon him, taught him the names of things, and commanded the angels to prostrate to him in honor, He settled him in Jannah with his wife, permitting them to eat from any place they wished, without limit or restriction, except for one particular tree.

This enormous scope of permission points to the vastness of that place itself: a place that needed only one prohibition to set the bounds of obedience. But Satan whispered to them until they ate from the tree they had been forbidden, and Allah removed them from what they had been in and commanded them to descend to earth.[^4] They were not sent out because the place was too small, but because they disobeyed one command amid tremendous vastness; Allah later accepted the repentance of Adam, peace be upon him, because his remorse was sincere.[^9]

- **`اهْبِطُوا`** ("go down") — descend from a higher place to a lower one; here, descending from Jannah to earth.
- **`مَوْضِعُ سَوْطٍ`** ("the space of a whip") — a very small area, the size a whip would occupy if laid on the ground.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

Much of what we feel as narrowness at this stage of life — limited room for success, endless comparisons, choices that feel constrained — is met by a Qur'anic description of another abode: `عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ` ("its width is that of the heavens and earth"). The first to live this real vastness was Adam, peace be upon him, who at the very start of his existence was given a vast place where almost everything was permitted except one boundary. Yet the same story reminds us that vastness does not excuse abandoning that one boundary, and that a small slip can remove a person from the widest bliss.

Then comes the hadith, "The space of a whip in Jannah is better than this world and all it contains," confronting the temptation of our age to measure success by what can be seen and compared, while the smallest unseen part of the Hereafter outweighs everything people compete for in this world.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story of the Prophets: One Boundary Amid Limitless Vastness

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-35" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-35" audience="13+" -->

Allah said to Adam, peace be upon him, and his wife: `"Dwell, you and your wife, in Jannah and eat freely from it wherever you wish, but do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers."`[^1] This Jannah was no temporary place or small test; it was the first human dwelling in all of existence, and it was so vast that organizing it required no more than a single boundary amid immeasurable expanse.

This reveals an important truth: tremendous vastness does not mean the absence of limits; it may make the one remaining limit even clearer and more significant. Satan whispered to Adam and his wife until they crossed that single limit and ate from the tree, and they were removed from what they had been in and commanded to descend to earth, `"and We said, 'Go down, some of you enemies to others, and you have on earth a settlement and provision for a time.'"`[^4] The narrowness of that place was never the reason for leaving it, but crossing one boundary amid vast abundance; Allah then accepted the repentance of Adam, peace be upon him, after his sincere remorse.[^9]

- **`رَغَدًا`** ("freely") — a pleasant, ample life without restriction, describing the state of Adam, peace be upon him, in Jannah before the slip.
- **`مَوْضِعُ سَوْطٍ`** ("the space of a whip") — the smallest imaginable area, used to show that value is not measured by size.

**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the story of how Adam, peace be upon him, was settled in Jannah and removed from it, connects it to the ayat `عَرْضُهَا السَّمَاوَاتُ وَالْأَرْضُ`, explains the hadith of "the space of a whip," distinguishes vastness of place from vastness of value, and carries out the Scale of the Small Place activity on a real situation.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the four ayat and the hadith; a drawn or real two-pan scale model; paper and pens; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the scholarly disagreement over the identity of the Jannah of Adam, peace be upon him (most scholars hold that it was the Garden of Eternity), and prepares neutral contemporary examples comparing something large and visible to something small and hidden.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Is a thing's value always measured by its size?" Gather answers before presenting the evidence.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 18 minutes:** Groups read the four ayat and the hadith in full, and each group extracts: the meaning of `رَغَدًا حَيْثُ شِئْتُمَا`, the reason Adam, peace be upon him, was removed from Jannah, and the meaning of `مَوْضِعُ سَوْطٍ`.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 12 minutes:** The teacher explains that Jannah's vastness is twofold — vastness of place and vastness of value — citing that one slip removed Adam, peace be upon him, from the widest bliss, and that its smallest spot outweighs this whole world.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners carry out the Scale of the Small Place activity on something they wish for in this world, writing a justification paragraph, then volunteers share their examples with the group.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "State the one boundary set for Adam, peace be upon him, amid Jannah's vastness, explain the meaning of 'the space of a whip,' and write one situation where you will review your decision." The teacher closes by reading the du'a, noting that it is a lesson-authored teaching du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A beginner is given a written summary of the story of Adam, peace be upon him, before the discussion, and an advanced learner is tasked with discussing the difference between the majority position and the opposing view on the identity of Adam's Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The lesson is not used to open speculative discussion about Jannah's geographic location or precise dimensions; any such question is redirected to the fact that these details are unseen matters established only by text. The story of Adam, peace be upon him, is narrated respectfully, without dwelling on details of the slip beyond the text.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="30" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child states that Adam, peace be upon him, was the first to live in Jannah, understands in one simple sentence that a small piece of Jannah is better than all their toys, and participates in the scale activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A hanger or simple scale; printed pictures of toys; a small card reading "a piece of Jannah"; a du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares the simple scale and pictures, and practices narrating the story of Adam, peace be upon him, in short, clear sentences with no frightening detail about Satan.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What's the biggest place you've ever seen?" Listen to the children's answers, then say: "Jannah is far bigger than all of that."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The teacher narrates the story of Adam, peace be upon him, simply, then the hadith of "the space of a whip," pausing to ask: "Do you think Jannah is big or small?"

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 6 minutes:** The teacher explains that Allah permitted Adam, peace be upon him, everything except one tree, and that a small piece of Jannah is better than all this world.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 9 minutes:** Each child takes part in the small-piece scale activity, comparing a picture of "a piece of Jannah" with pictures of their favorite toys, without comparing children to each other.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Who was the first to live in Jannah?" Then read the du'a slowly so the children can repeat what they can.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Allow answering by pointing to the pictures, and give an advanced child a chance to explain why Allah forbade Adam, peace be upon him, only one tree.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Satan is mentioned briefly and respectfully, with no frightening detail, and the removal of Adam, peace be upon him, from Jannah is never used to frighten the child about Allah, but to gently teach the meaning of obedience.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="45" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student summarizes the story of how Adam, peace be upon him, was settled in Jannah and removed from it, explains the three terms, distinguishes vastness of place from vastness of value, and carries out the Scale of the Small Place activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The full text of the ayat and hadith; term cards; a Scale of the Small Place template; pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prints the ayat, hadith, and terms, and prepares a completed example of the scale activity to display before students begin.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher presents two phrases: "a very big place" and "a small thing worth more than anything big," and asks: "Can both be true of the same description?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Students read the ayat and hadith in full, underlining the sentence describing Jannah's vastness and circling the sentence describing its value.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher explains the three terms, then builds the class sentence: "Jannah is vast in place, vaster in value, and one slip can remove a person from the widest bliss."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students carry out the Scale of the Small Place activity, each writing one real-life situation they will review, then sharing a general example with a partner without sensitive details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences: "The difference between vastness of place and vastness of value is..." and "The situation I will change is...". The teacher closes by reading the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A ready-made list of examples is provided for struggling students, and advanced students are tasked with comparing the ayat of Aal 'Imran and al-Hadid in greater detail.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The lesson is not used to speculate about Jannah's geographic location or precise dimensions; any such question is redirected to the fact that these details are unseen. The story of Adam, peace be upon him, is narrated respectfully without detail beyond the text.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="55" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 55 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the relationship between vast bliss and a narrow margin for disobedience in the story of Adam, peace be upon him, explains the significance of the hadith of "the space of a whip" against social standards of value, and carries out a personal activity linking this to a real-life situation.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A file of the four pieces of evidence and the hadith; term cards; a Scale of the Small Place template; exit slips.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the story of Adam, peace be upon him, and the hadith, and prepares a neutral example comparing visible value to real value without singling out any specific student.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher poses a question: "Is the bigger or more visible thing always the better one?" and opens a short discussion before reading the story of Adam, peace be upon him.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 14 minutes:** Three groups read: the two ayat of al-Baqarah on Adam's settling and removal, the two ayat of vastness in Aal 'Imran and al-Hadid, and the hadith of "the space of a whip." Each group extracts the relationship between vastness and value in its text.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 12 minutes:** The teacher explains the lesson's terms, and discusses how the hadith of "the space of a whip" challenges a standard of value based on visibility and social comparison.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 14 minutes:** Students complete the Scale of the Small Place activity in writing for three social standards they feel pressured by, then a paragraph describing one situation they will change.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** The student writes a three-line response to the opening question, grounded in the evidence, then one private step they need not announce.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** A struggling student is given three ready-made social standards to choose from, and an advanced student is tasked with discussing the scholarly dispute over the identity of the Jannah of Adam, peace be upon him, and its effect on understanding the text.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The lesson is not used to judge those who publicly share their achievements or possessions, nor to turn the activity into hurtful comparison between students. The story of Adam, peace be upon him, is narrated respectfully without detail beyond the text, and no speculative discussion of Jannah's geographic location is opened.
