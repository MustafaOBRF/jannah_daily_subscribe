# The Angels' Welcome and Their Greeting of Peace

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the Qur'anic account in Surah an-Nahl: the question "What has your Lord sent down?", the two answers given by the deniers and by the mindful (an-Nahl 16:24, 30), and then the outcome for the mindful, when the angels say to them: `Peace be upon you. Enter Jannah because of what you used to do` (an-Nahl 16:32), while keeping the text of the ayat distinct from the background the commentators give.
- List three moments in the Qur'an when the angels greet the people of Jannah with peace: at death (an-Nahl 16:32), at the gates of Jannah, from the lips of its keepers (az-Zumar 39:73-74), and after they have settled in, when the angels come in to them from every gate (ar-Ra'd 13:23-24); and explain the reason each greeting names: purity, deeds, and patience.
- Explain the meaning of `good and pure` (*tayyibin*) and `you have been good and pure` (*tibtum*) from the words of the commentators, and show that deeds are a means of entering Jannah by Allah's mercy, not the price paid for it.
- Use the hadith of Adam and the angels (al-Bukhari 3326) to show that *salam* has been the greeting of Adam and his descendants since the very beginning of creation, and the hadith "Spread salam among yourselves" (Muslim 54) to show that spreading it is a road to the love by which entry into Jannah is hoped for.
- Carry out the "First and Fuller Salam" activity: being the first to greet, and answering every greeting with something fuller than what was heard, in real situations and in one chosen relationship, with a short log and a reflection.
- Recall the lesson's du'a asking Allah to make our hearts, tongues, and deeds good and pure, and to make us among those whom the angels receive with peace.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

No believer walks into Jannah as a stranger whom nobody knows. The Qur'an tells us that the angels receive the believer with a greeting of peace at more than one moment: when his appointed time comes to an end, at the gates of Jannah, and after he has settled within it. And at every one of these moments, the word on the angels' lips is the same: *salam*, "peace."

This lesson presents a Qur'anic scene from Surah an-Nahl: a question asked about revelation, "What has your Lord sent down?"; two answers set against each other; and then the outcome for those who answered "Good": angels who say to them, "Peace be upon you." So what is the link between a good word a believer speaks today and the greeting he will hear tomorrow?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-16-30-32" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Peace Be upon You. Enter Jannah"

> **{وَقِيلَ لِلَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا مَاذَا أَنْزَلَ رَبُّكُمْ ۚ قَالُوا خَيْرًا ۗ لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا فِي هَٰذِهِ الدُّنْيَا حَسَنَةٌ ۚ وَلَدَارُ الْآخِرَةِ خَيْرٌ ۚ وَلَنِعْمَ دَارُ الْمُتَّقِينَ ۝ جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ لَهُمْ فِيهَا مَا يَشَاءُونَ ۚ كَذَٰلِكَ يَجْزِي اللَّهُ الْمُتَّقِينَ ۝ الَّذِينَ تَتَوَفَّاهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ طَيِّبِينَ ۙ يَقُولُونَ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ}** [النحل: ٣٠-٣٢][^1]
>
> وقبلها بآيات، في جواب المكذبين عن السؤال نفسه: **{وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَهُمْ مَاذَا أَنْزَلَ رَبُّكُمْ ۙ قَالُوا أَسَاطِيرُ الْأَوَّلِينَ}** [النحل: ٢٤][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And it is said to those who are mindful of Allah, 'What has your Lord sent down?' They say, 'Good.' For those who do good in this world there is good, and the home of the Hereafter is better still; and how excellent is the home of the mindful! Gardens of Eden, which they will enter, with rivers flowing beneath them; there they will have whatever they wish. Thus does Allah reward the mindful: those whom the angels take in death while they are good and pure, saying, 'Peace be upon you. Enter Jannah because of what you used to do.'"** (an-Nahl 16:30-32)[^1]
>
> A few ayat earlier, the deniers answer the very same question: **"And when it is said to them, 'What has your Lord sent down?' they say, 'Legends of the ancients.'"** (an-Nahl 16:24)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The hafiz Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: This is news about the fortunate, in contrast to what Allah reported about the wretched. Those others turned away from answering and said, "Legends of the ancients," while these `say, "Good"`: that is, He sent down good, a mercy, a blessing, and a beautiful thing for whoever follows it. And in ayah 32 Allah tells of their state at the approach of death: that they are *tayyibin*, good and pure, meaning free of shirk, of defilement, and of every evil, and that the angels greet them with peace and give them the glad tidings of Jannah.[^1]

Al-Baghawi, may Allah have mercy on him, mentions a background to this question: during the pilgrimage season, the Arab tribes would send someone to bring them news of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. On the roads, that envoy would be met by people describing him as a sorcerer, a soothsayer, a poet, a liar, or a madman. And the envoy would say, "I would be the worst envoy ever sent if I went back to my people without entering Makkah and meeting him." So he would enter it, meet the Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and they would tell him that he was truthful and a Prophet sent by Allah. This is a report the commentators mention as context for the ayah; it is not a hadith with a chain of narration. Al-Baghawi also cites Mujahid on `good and pure` (*tayyibin*): their deeds and their words were pure.[^3] And as-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, said: `Peace be upon you` means the complete greeting and safety from every harm; deeds are the means of entering Jannah, and this came to them through Allah's mercy and generosity, not through their own power and strength.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

One question, two answers, two very different endings. Whoever saw revelation as "good" and lived by it hears, at the final moment of his worldly life, the word "peace." The greeting is not a reward for a word on the tongue alone, but for a whole life made good and pure in heart, in word, and in deed.

### "Peace Be upon You; You Have Been Good and Pure"

> **{وَسِيقَ الَّذِينَ اتَّقَوْا رَبَّهُمْ إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ زُمَرًا ۖ حَتَّىٰ إِذَا جَاءُوهَا وَفُتِحَتْ أَبْوَابُهَا وَقَالَ لَهُمْ خَزَنَتُهَا سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ طِبْتُمْ فَادْخُلُوهَا خَالِدِينَ ۝ وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي صَدَقَنَا وَعْدَهُ وَأَوْرَثَنَا الْأَرْضَ نَتَبَوَّأُ مِنَ الْجَنَّةِ حَيْثُ نَشَاءُ ۖ فَنِعْمَ أَجْرُ الْعَامِلِينَ}** [الزمر: ٧٣-٧٤][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And those who were mindful of their Lord will be led to Jannah in throngs, until, when they reach it, and its gates are opened, and its keepers say to them, 'Peace be upon you; you have been good and pure, so enter it, to abide forever' — and they will say, 'All praise belongs to Allah, who has kept His promise to us and given us the land as our inheritance, so that we may settle in Jannah wherever we wish.' How excellent is the reward of those who worked!"** (az-Zumar 39:73-74)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: The gates were opened for them as an honor and a mark of esteem, and the angels who keep Jannah met them with glad tidings, greetings of peace, and praise. The completion of the sentence "until, when..." is not stated here; and when it is left unsaid, the mind ranges freely through every hope and longing.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

At the gate, the very same word comes again, "peace," and with it a testimony: "you have been good and pure." And the people of Jannah return the greeting with praise of Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-13-23-24" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And the Angels Will Come In to Them from Every Gate"

> **{جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا وَمَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّاتِهِمْ ۖ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ كُلِّ بَابٍ ۝ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ بِمَا صَبَرْتُمْ ۚ فَنِعْمَ عُقْبَى الدَّارِ}** [الرعد: ٢٣-٢٤][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Gardens of Eden, which they will enter, together with those who were righteous among their fathers, their spouses, and their offspring; and the angels will come in to them from every gate: 'Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured. How excellent is the final home!'"** (ar-Ra'd 13:23-24)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: The angels come in to them from this side and that to congratulate them on entering Jannah. They arrive greeting them with peace and congratulating them on what Allah has granted them: nearness to Him, His favor, and a dwelling in the Abode of Peace.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The welcome does not end at the gate. The angels visit them after they have settled in, and they name the reason for this honor: "for what you patiently endured."

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3326" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Your Greeting and the Greeting of Your Descendants"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«خَلَقَ اللَّهُ آدَمَ وَطُولُهُ سِتُّونَ ذِرَاعًا، ثُمَّ قَالَ: اذْهَبْ فَسَلِّمْ عَلَى أُولَئِكَ مِنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ، فَاسْتَمِعْ مَا يُحَيُّونَكَ، تَحِيَّتُكَ وَتَحِيَّةُ ذُرِّيَّتِكَ. فَقَالَ: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ. فَقَالُوا: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكَ وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ. فَزَادُوهُ: وَرَحْمَةُ اللَّهِ. فَكُلُّ مَنْ يَدْخُلُ الْجَنَّةَ عَلَى صُورَةِ آدَمَ، فَلَمْ يَزَلِ الْخَلْقُ يَنْقُصُ حَتَّى الْآنَ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Allah created Adam, and his height was sixty cubits. Then He said: 'Go and greet that group of angels over there with salam, and listen to how they greet you in return, for it will be your greeting and the greeting of your descendants.' So he said: '*As-salamu 'alaykum*' (Peace be upon you). They said: '*As-salamu 'alayka wa rahmatullah*' (Peace be upon you, and the mercy of Allah). So they added for him: 'and the mercy of Allah.' Everyone who enters Jannah will be in the form of Adam, and human stature has kept on diminishing until now."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Bukhari recorded it in the chapter on the creation of Adam and his descendants, and recorded a similar version in the Book of Asking Permission under the chapter "The Beginning of Salam," thereby treating it as the foundation for the legitimacy of the greeting of salam.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The first greeting Adam ever heard came from the angels, and it was a reply fuller than what he had said. The greeting we exchange today is the same greeting with which the people of Jannah will be received, as they enter it in the form of their father Adam.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-54" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Spread Salam Among Yourselves"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«لَا تَدْخُلُونَ الْجَنَّةَ حَتَّى تُؤْمِنُوا، وَلَا تُؤْمِنُوا حَتَّى تَحَابُّوا، أَوَلَا أَدُلُّكُمْ عَلَى شَيْءٍ إِذَا فَعَلْتُمُوهُ تَحَابَبْتُمْ؟ أَفْشُوا السَّلَامَ بَيْنَكُمْ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"You will not enter Jannah until you believe, and you will not believe until you love one another. Shall I not point you to something that, if you do it, will make you love one another? Spread salam among yourselves."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "You will not believe until you love one another" means that your faith will not be complete without mutual love. The hadith contains a powerful encouragement to spread salam and to offer it to all Muslims, those you know and those you do not. Salam is the first of the means of drawing hearts together and the key to winning affection, and through it estrangement and the cutting of ties are removed.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world, salam is a deed by which the road is built; in Jannah, salam is the greeting with which the destination welcomes you.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat trace one continuous journey of welcome: peace at death, then at the gate, then in the dwellings of Jannah. And each greeting names a reason: purity, deeds, and patience. The two hadiths tie this greeting to our own lives: it is the greeting of Adam and his descendants, and spreading it today builds the love that is part of complete faith. So whoever hopes to be welcomed with peace should become one of its people now.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between the answer of the deniers and the answer of the mindful to the question "What has your Lord sent down?", and how was that difference reflected in the outcome for each group?
2. Name the three moments when the angels greet the believers with peace, and the reason each greeting mentions.
3. How do you reconcile the words `because of what you used to do` with the fact that Jannah comes through Allah's mercy, not as the price of deeds?
4. What does the angels' addition of "and the mercy of Allah" in their reply to Adam add to your understanding of the etiquette of answering a greeting?
5. Which relationship in your life needs you, today, to be the one who offers salam first?

### A Story from the Qur'an: A Good Answer and a Good Greeting

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-16-30-32" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

Long, long ago, in Makkah, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to recite to people the Qur'an that Allah had sent down.

And some people would ask, "What has your Lord sent down?"

They asked some people who did not love the truth, and those people said something that was not true: "That's just old stories from long ago!"[^2]

Then they asked the believers, the ones who are mindful of Allah and obey Him: "What has your Lord sent down?" And the believers answered with one beautiful word: "Good!" They meant: Allah has sent down good things, mercy, and blessings.[^1]

Allah kept their answer safe in the Qur'an, and He promised them wonderful things: good in this life, and gardens with rivers flowing through them, where they will have everything they love.

And Allah told us that when the lives of these good people in this world come to an end, the angels come to them and say: «سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ», "Peace be upon you. Come into Jannah because of the good you used to do."[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-16-30-32" audience="4-7" -->

Put simply: the people who said the good word and did good things are welcomed by the angels with the most beautiful greeting, "Peace," and the angels give them the happy news of Jannah. A good heart and good words lead to a good greeting.[^1]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Qur'an tells us that the angels welcome the people of Jannah with a greeting of peace at three different moments: when their lives in this world come to an end;[^1] at the gates of Jannah, when its keepers say to them, "Peace be upon you; you have been good and pure, so enter it, to stay forever";[^5] and after they have moved into their homes, when the angels come in to them through every gate, saying, "Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured."[^6]

This greeting is very old. Allah taught it to Adam, peace be upon him: Adam greeted the angels with salam, they answered him with even more than he had said, and Allah told him that this would be his greeting and the greeting of his descendants.[^7] So who are the people who earn a welcome like this? A story from Surah an-Nahl gives us the answer.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-16-30-32" -->

- **`Good and pure (tayyibin)`** — clean of shirk and of wrongdoing, with words and deeds that have grown pure, as Mujahid explained it.[^3]
- **`Peace be upon you (salamun 'alaykum)`** — a complete greeting meaning that safety from every harm is now yours, for good.[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: "What Has Your Lord Sent Down?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-16-30-32" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. The part about the pilgrimage season comes from the commentators.**

Some commentators mention that during the pilgrimage season, the Arab tribes used to send a man to Makkah to find out about the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. When the man arrived, he would find people waiting along the roads who told him: "He's a sorcerer, a soothsayer, a poet, a liar, a madman. You're better off not meeting him." And some of these messengers would say, "I'd be the worst messenger my people ever sent if I went back without entering Makkah and meeting him myself." So he would go in, meet the Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and they would tell him that he was truthful and a Prophet sent by Allah.[^3]

The Qur'an preserves the question for us, along with two answers to it. When the deniers were asked, "What has your Lord sent down?" they said, "Legends of the ancients," meaning old stories passed down from long ago.[^2] But when the mindful were asked the very same question, they said, "Good": Allah has sent down good, mercy, and blessing for whoever follows it.[^1]

Two answers, each taking up a single line, yet the outcomes could not be more different. Allah promised those who do good a good reward in this world, and a home in the Hereafter that is better still: Gardens of Eden with rivers flowing beneath them, where they will have whatever they wish. Then He described the moment they meet the angels: {الَّذِينَ تَتَوَفَّاهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ طَيِّبِينَ ۙ يَقُولُونَ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ}, "those whom the angels take in death while they are good and pure, saying, 'Peace be upon you. Enter Jannah because of what you used to do.'"[^1]

Notice this: the word "Good" was not just a polite reply. It came from a heart that saw revelation as a blessing and acted on it, so the person who said it became one of the "good and pure," and the angels welcomed him with peace.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-16-30-32" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: people were asked about the Qur'an. Some denied it and called it old stories, while the mindful said, "Good," and lived by it. So Allah promised them Jannah, and told us that the angels come to them while they are good and pure and say: "Peace be upon you. Enter Jannah because of what you used to do."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

### Questions to Understand and Think About

1. What question was put to the two groups, and how did each group answer?
2. According to the commentators, why did the man who came to Makkah insist on meeting the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, for himself?
3. What are the three moments when the angels greet the people of Jannah with peace?
4. What did the angels do when Adam greeted them with salam, and what does that teach us about answering a greeting?

### Activity: The Salam Map

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.030.activity.first-and-fuller-salam" -->

Draw a map with five places on it: home, your classroom, the masjid, the playground or the bus, and a neighbor's or relative's house. For one week, be the first to say salam to someone you know in each place, and once a day answer a greeting with something fuller than what you heard. Put a mark on the place each time you do it. At the end of the week, write two sentences: What changed when you were the one to go first? And who was the hardest person to say salam to first, and why? Only greet people you don't know when your mom, your dad, or your teacher is with you.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every day, one way or another, a Muslim gets asked: "So what is this religion you follow?" The question might come from a curious classmate, from a sarcastic comment, or from a video that paints Islam in a distorted light. And your answer, in what you say and in how you act, says something about you.

The Qur'an tells of a similar question asked fourteen centuries ago, and it ties an honest answer to that question to the end of the road: angels who welcome the people who gave it with a greeting of peace. The Qur'an mentions this welcome at three moments: at death;[^1] at the gates of Jannah, when its keepers say, "Peace be upon you; you have been good and pure";[^5] and in the dwellings of Jannah, when the angels come in from every gate: "Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured."[^6] It is the very same greeting Adam received from the angels when he was first created.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-39-73-74" -->

- **`Its keepers (khazanatuha)`** — the angels entrusted with Jannah and its gates, who welcome its people with glad tidings, greetings of peace, and praise.[^5]
- **`You have been good and pure (tibtum)`** — a testimony to their purity, the very same description found in Surah an-Nahl, `good and pure` (*tayyibin*): they were free of shirk and defilement, and their words and deeds had grown pure.[^1][^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: One Answer Amid the Noise

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-16-30-32" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. The part about the pilgrimage season comes from the commentators.**

Picture Makkah in the pilgrimage season: tribes from every corner of Arabia, and each tribe has sent someone to bring back news of this man who says he is a Prophet. Al-Baghawi mentions that a newcomer would find people posted along the roads, ready to greet him with ready-made verdicts: "Sorcerer. Soothsayer. Poet. Liar. Madman. You're better off not meeting him." Full-on pressure, before he had heard a single ayah.

But some of them refused to carry home a borrowed verdict. One would say, "I'd be the worst envoy ever sent if I went back to my people without entering Makkah and meeting him." So he went in, met the Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and they told him that he was truthful and a Prophet sent by Allah.[^3]

The Qur'an preserves both answers. The deniers were asked, "What has your Lord sent down?" and they said, "Legends of the ancients"; Ibn Kathir says they were dodging the question.[^2] The mindful were asked, and they answered with a single word: "Good," meaning mercy, blessing, and a beautiful thing for whoever follows it.[^1] Their answer was not a speech or an argument. It was honest clarity, in a setting that was not on their side.

Then the Qur'an reveals where each road ends. For those who do good there is good in this world; the Hereafter is better still; and Gardens of Eden await them, where they will have whatever they wish. And when the final moment comes: {الَّذِينَ تَتَوَفَّاهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ طَيِّبِينَ ۙ يَقُولُونَ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ}, "those whom the angels take in death while they are good and pure, saying, 'Peace be upon you. Enter Jannah because of what you used to do.'"[^1]

Notice how the two fit together: whoever said of revelation "Good" and lived by it hears, at the end, "Peace." As-Sa'di says that deeds are the means of entry, yet it came to them through Allah's mercy, not through their own power and strength.[^4]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-16-30-32" audience="13+" -->

To put it another way: surrounded by ready-made verdicts that labeled the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with the worst names imaginable, the mindful answered the question "What has your Lord sent down?" with one honest word, "Good," and they lived by what they said. So Allah told us that the angels take them in death while they are good and pure, and say to them: "Peace be upon you. Enter Jannah because of what you used to do."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why were the ready-made verdicts along the roads such strong pressure on the newcomer? Where do you see something like them today?
2. What made the word "Good" more than just a verbal answer?
3. Look at the reasons named in the three greetings: purity, deeds, and patience. Which of them do you need most right now?
4. How can offering salam be a stand that expresses who you are, and not just a social habit?
5. Why might it be harder to be the first to say salam to someone things have grown cool with?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The First Salam

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.030.activity.first-and-fuller-salam" -->

Privately, choose someone with whom things have gone cool or awkward: a classmate, a relative, a brother or sister. Just make sure it is not someone who has hurt you or with whom you don't feel safe. For seven days, be the first to offer the full salam, face to face, whenever you see them, and follow it with a short kind word. And in your messages, write the salam out in full instead of abbreviating it. In a private notebook, log the day, how they responded, and how you felt. At the end, write a paragraph: How does the salam you give today connect to the salam you hope to hear from the angels?

<!-- activity:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.030.dua.tayyib-hearts-greeted-with-salam" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ طَيِّبْ قُلُوبَنَا وَأَلْسِنَتَنَا وَأَعْمَالَنَا، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ تَتَلَقَّاهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ بِالسَّلَامِ عِنْدَ دُخُولِ الْجَنَّةِ.
>
> *"O Allah, make our hearts pure, our words true, and our deeds sincere, and make us among those whom the angels welcome with peace as they enter Jannah, so that the last word we hear from this world and the first we hear in the next is 'peace.'"*

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene in Surah an-Nahl (16:24, 30-32), distinguishing the text of the ayat from the pilgrimage-season background given by the commentators; lists the three moments when the angels give their greeting of peace and names the reason in each greeting; explains `good and pure` (*tayyibin*) and `you have been good and pure` (*tibtum*) from the words of the commentators; reconciles `because of what you used to do` with the fact that Jannah comes through Allah's mercy; uses al-Bukhari 3326 and Muslim 54 to establish the origin of salam and its fruit; and draws up a weekly plan for the first and fuller salam.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for looking up Surahs an-Nahl, az-Zumar, and ar-Ra'd; a board with a three-column table ("The Moment," "The Words of the Greeting," "The Reason Named"); an activity sheet with a seven-day table; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the five pieces of evidence and their references, reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, and as-Sa'di on the ayat of an-Nahl, and keeps in mind that the pilgrimage-season report in al-Baghawi is unchained exegetical context and must not be presented as a hadith. The teacher also notes that the keepers of Jannah and the hadith about its gates being opened were covered in Lesson 7, and that ar-Ra'd 13:22-24 appeared in Lesson 27, so here the focus stays on the angle of the greeting, without repeating that material.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Think of the most beautiful welcome you have ever received after a journey or a long absence. What was the first word you heard?" Then: "If you were asked today, in a single sentence, 'What has your Lord sent down?', how would you answer?" The teacher writes down the answers without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read an-Nahl 16:24, then ayat 30-32 in full, and present al-Baghawi's background account, noting its status (7 minutes). Then az-Zumar 39:73-74 and ar-Ra'd 13:23-24, as the learners fill in the table on the board (7 minutes). Then the hadiths of al-Bukhari 3326 and Muslim 54, pausing at "so they added for him" and at an-Nawawi's commentary (6 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion of three questions: Why is the answer "Good" paired with the greeting "Peace"? How do we understand the particle *bi-* ("because of," "for") in `because of what you used to do` and `for what you patiently endured` as indicating a cause, not a payment in exchange, as as-Sa'di established? And what is the connection between spreading salam, love, and faith?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner fills in a "First and Fuller Salam" plan: identifying the moments of entering the home, the relationship in which they will offer salam first, and the wording of their fuller reply. Then anyone who wishes shares with a partner the part they find hardest, without mentioning names.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Name the three moments of the greeting and the reason for each" and "In what situation will you first put the activity into practice?" Then the teacher returns to the answers from the opening and asks: Is an answer with the tongue enough? Close by reading the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayat of an-Nahl and the hadith of Adam are enough. Advanced learners are asked to draw up a comparison table of the three greetings by moment, speaker, and reason, referring to Ibn Kathir's tafsir of each ayah, with a note that Allah's own greeting of peace to the people of Jannah is the subject of a later lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not delve into the attributes and appearance of the angels without evidence. Do not declare with certainty of any specific person that the angels have welcomed him with peace. Present the pilgrimage-season report as exegetical context, not as an established text. And do not use the activity to pressure anyone who is in a conflict involving harm or danger; being the first to offer salam does not mean submitting to someone who does harm.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the angels welcome the people of Jannah by saying "Peace be upon you"; retells the believers' answer, "Good"; says that Adam was the first to say salam to the angels; and says the full greeting and answers with a fuller one.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A drawing sheet for each child with a large door on it; child-safe coloring pencils or crayons; a puppet or two cards for practicing the greeting; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the story in a calm voice, and prepares a gentle answer in case the child asks about death: "Everyone's life in this world comes to an end one day, and the angels give the good believer the happy news of Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver knocks on the door and comes in saying, "*As-salamu 'alaykum*!" Then asks, "What do we say when we hear salam?" and listens to the children.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the story, inviting the children to repeat the word "Good!" in a happy voice, and then the angels' words: «سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمُ ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ».

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What did the believers say? What do the angels say? What extra words did the angels give Adam?" Then teach that the fuller reply is "*Wa 'alaykum as-salam wa rahmatullah*."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The children practice in pairs being first to say salam and giving the fuller reply. Then they draw the door and color in their first heart, as in the activity unit, and take the sheet home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver notices whether the child can say the full greeting and the fuller reply, and whether the child remembers the answer "Good." Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, "*As-salamu 'alaykum*" and "*Wa 'alaykum as-salam*" are enough. Ask older children to tell their family the story of Adam and the angels.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the angels or describe what they look like. Mention the end of life in calm, general words, without details. Remind parents that the child says salam to family and to people they know, and only greets strangers first when the parents are present.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the story with its two answers, distinguishing the text of the Qur'an from what the commentators say about the pilgrimage season; lists the three moments of the greeting; defines `good and pure` (*tayyibin*) and "Peace be upon you"; and draws the salam map and completes its week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards for the two terms; a large sheet for each student for the salam map; pens and markers; a mushaf; a board with three cards for the moments: "Death," "The Gate," "The Dwellings."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes ayat 24 and 30 of an-Nahl side by side on the board to bring out the contrast, and prepares cards with the words of the three greetings for the students to arrange on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If everyone around you said bad things about someone, would you believe them, or would you go and get to know that person yourself? Why?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the two terms (4 minutes), then the whole story, pausing at the newcomer's words, "I'd be the worst messenger my people ever sent...", and at the two answers, and pointing out that the pilgrimage-season report comes from the commentators (8 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The students arrange the cards of the three greetings on the board and write under each one its reason: purity, deeds, and patience. Then the teacher asks: "What did the angels add for Adam? And how can we answer with something better?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The students draw the salam map as in the activity unit. Next to each place, each student writes the name of the person they will greet first there, and agrees with the teacher on a day to check in.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence "The mindful said 'Good,' so the angels said to them..." with the words of the ayah. Then read the du'a. A week later, anyone who wishes shares their two reflection sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, present the story as four pictures in sequence (the road, the question, the two answers, the greeting of peace). Ask advanced students to memorize an-Nahl 16:32 and to explain the word *tayyibin* in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the angels. Stress safety: greeting strangers happens only when a parent or the teacher is present. Do not compare students publicly by the number of marks on their maps, and do not blame anyone who forgot.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the pressure of ready-made verdicts in the pilgrimage-season account and compares it with their own world; explains why the answer "Good" was a stand and not merely a word; connects the reasons named in the three greetings to their own life; commits to seven days of offering salam first to a chosen person; and writes a closing reflection.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a private notebook that is not collected; a mushaf; small cards for anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares two neutral, current examples of "ready-made verdicts" (a misleading headline, a sarcastic comment about religion), reviews al-Baghawi's tafsir so as to distinguish clearly between the Qur'anic text and the pilgrimage-season report, and keeps in mind the safety limits on choosing a person.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When have you judged a person or an idea before getting to know it for yourself?" The students write anonymous answers on the cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction, the two terms, and then the story, with three pauses: the verdicts along the roads, the newcomer's decision, and the two answers in an-Nahl 16:24 and 16:30; then the ayah about death, 16:32.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Discussion in small groups of questions 1, 2, and 4, followed by brief reports. The teacher guides the students to see that standing firm in one's identity does not mean heated argument, and that salam itself is a hallmark that distinguishes Muslims, as an-Nawawi said.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student silently chooses the person they will greet first, and writes the seven-day plan in their notebook as in the activity unit, without sharing the name.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: the reason for the greeting they need most, what makes being first to offer salam difficult, and what they hope it will bring. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question about the experience, without asking for details. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to trace the question "What has your Lord sent down?" through the full context of an-Nahl 16:22-32 and to write a comparison of the outcomes of the two groups.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** State clearly that a student must not choose someone who has hurt them or with whom they feel unsafe, and that they should observe the religious and cultural etiquette of greeting between the sexes. No one is asked to disclose their conflicts. Do not describe the angels, and do not declare with certainty that any specific person will have a good end.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
