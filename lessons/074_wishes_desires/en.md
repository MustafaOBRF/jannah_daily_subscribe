# The Wishes of the People of Jannah and All That Souls Desire

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate, in order, the hadith of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, about the old woman of the Children of Israel (Sahih Ibn Hibban 723): the Bedouin who asked for a she-camel and some goats; the question of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, "Could you not be like the old woman of the Children of Israel?"; the old woman's request to Musa, peace be upon him, that she be with him in Jannah; and Allah's command to him to grant her request.

- Explain the Qur'an's principle concerning the wishes of the people of Jannah, "In it you will have whatever your souls desire, and in it you will have whatever you ask for" (Fussilat 41:31), and "In it is whatever souls desire and eyes delight in" (az-Zukhruf 43:71), in light of the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di.

- Distinguish between a permissible worldly need, which there is nothing wrong with asking for, and the high aspiration that makes what lasts the highest thing one asks for, and connect this to the request of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b to be the companion of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Jannah (Sahih Muslim 489).

- Carry out the "Wish Elevator" activity: taking a real personal wish and raising it floor by floor with the question "And then what?" until it reaches something that can only be fulfilled in Jannah, then writing that highest request on a card and asking for it in sujud for a set number of days.

- Recite the lesson's bedtime du'a, asking Allah to raise our aspirations to what lasts, to give us in Jannah whatever our souls desire, and to gather us there with the prophets and the righteous.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Everyone carries a list of wishes, whether spoken aloud or kept quiet. When the Qur'an describes Jannah, it does not simply list its delights; it lays down a sweeping principle: its people will have there whatever their souls desire and whatever they ask for. If every wish will be granted there, the question left for us here is this: what do we wish for? And how high are we willing to let our wishes rise?

This lesson answers that question through a story the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told about Musa, peace be upon him, and an old woman of the Children of Israel. He told it in response to the request of a Bedouin whom he had honored. The wish of the last person to enter Jannah, and that of the man who asks his Lord's permission to farm, were covered in two earlier lessons, so we will not repeat them here.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="ibnhibban-723" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Could You Not Be Like the Old Woman of the Children of Israel?"

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **أَتَى النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم أَعْرَابِيًّا فَأَكْرَمَهُ، فَقَالَ لَهُ: «ائْتِنَا». فَأَتَاهُ، فَقَالَ لَهُ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «سَلْ حَاجَتَكَ». قَالَ: نَاقَةٌ نَرْكَبُهَا، وَأَعْنُزٌ يَحْلِبُهَا أَهْلِي. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «أَعَجَزْتُمْ أَنْ تَكُونُوا مِثْلَ عَجُوزِ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ؟». قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، وَمَا عَجُوزُ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ؟ قَالَ: «إِنَّ مُوسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ لَمَّا سَارَ بِبَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ مِنْ مِصْرَ ضَلُّوا الطَّرِيقَ، فَقَالَ: مَا هَذَا؟ فَقَالَ عُلَمَاؤُهُمْ: إِنَّ يُوسُفَ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ لَمَّا حَضَرَهُ الْمَوْتُ أَخَذَ عَلَيْنَا مَوْثِقًا مِنَ اللَّهِ أَنْ لَا نَخْرُجَ مِنْ مِصْرَ حَتَّى نَنْقُلَ عِظَامَهُ مَعَنَا. قَالَ: فَمَنْ يَعْلَمُ مَوْضِعَ قَبْرِهِ؟ قَالَ: عَجُوزٌ مِنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ. فَبَعَثَ إِلَيْهَا فَأَتَتْهُ، فَقَالَ: دُلِّينِي عَلَى قَبْرِ يُوسُفَ. قَالَتْ: حَتَّى تُعْطِيَنِي حُكْمِي. قَالَ: وَمَا حُكْمُكِ؟ قَالَتْ: أَكُونُ مَعَكَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ. فَكَرِهَ أَنْ يُعْطِيَهَا ذَلِكَ، فَأَوْحَى اللَّهُ إِلَيْهِ: أَنْ أَعْطِهَا حُكْمَهَا. فَانْطَلَقَتْ بِهِمْ إِلَى بُحَيْرَةٍ، مَوْضِعِ مُسْتَنْقَعِ مَاءٍ، فَقَالَتْ: أَنْضِبُوا هَذَا الْمَاءَ، فَأَنْضَبُوهُ، فَقَالَتِ: احْتَفِرُوا، فَاحْتَفَرُوا، فَاسْتَخْرَجُوا عِظَامَ يُوسُفَ، فَلَمَّا أَقَلُّوهَا إِلَى الْأَرْضِ وَإِذَا الطَّرِيقُ مِثْلُ ضَوْءِ النَّهَارِ».**[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, said: **The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came to a Bedouin, who treated him generously. So he said to him, "Come and visit us." The man came to him, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him, "Ask for what you need." He said, "A she-camel for us to ride, and some goats for my family to milk." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Could you not be like the old woman of the Children of Israel?" They said, "O Messenger of Allah, who was the old woman of the Children of Israel?" He said: "When Musa, peace be upon him, set out from Egypt with the Children of Israel, they lost their way. He said, 'What is this?' Their scholars said, 'When death came to Yusuf, peace be upon him, he took from us a solemn pledge before Allah that we would not leave Egypt until we carried his bones with us.' He said, 'Then who knows where his grave is?' He was told, 'An old woman of the Children of Israel.' So he sent for her, and she came to him. He said, 'Show me the grave of Yusuf.' She said, 'Not until you grant me my request.' He said, 'And what is your request?' She said, 'That I be with you in Jannah.' He was reluctant to grant her that, so Allah revealed to him: 'Grant her her request.' So she led them to a small lake, a place where water had pooled, and said, 'Drain this water.' They drained it. Then she said, 'Dig,' and they dug, and they brought out the bones of Yusuf. And when they had lifted them up onto the ground, there was the road, as clear as the light of day."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Hibban placed this hadith in his Sahih in the Book of Heart-Softening Reports (*ar-Raqa'iq*), under the Chapter on Scrupulousness and Reliance on Allah, with the heading: "Mention of the report showing that a person should not, when an occasion arises for him, trade away the means to the Hereafter for any of the debris of this perishing, passing world." The imam, in other words, understood it to mean that when a chance to ask presents itself, it should not be spent on what perishes alone.[^1] In the context of the hadith, *hukmi* ("my request") means what she stipulated and asked for as the price of showing them the way.

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith sets two scenes side by side. In the first, a generous Bedouin has honored the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and when he is told, "Ask for what you need," he asks for what his family needs: a she-camel and some goats. This is a permissible request, and the Prophet did not rebuke him for it. In the second, an old woman holds a piece of knowledge that no one else has, and she does not sell it for money or goods. Instead she asks for the highest thing anyone could ask for: to be with Allah's Prophet in Jannah. The hadith does not say why Musa, peace be upon him, was reluctant to grant her request, so we will not speculate. What is established is that Allah revealed to him to grant it, and that the road was then laid open, as clear as the light of day.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "In It You Will Have Whatever Your Souls Desire"

> **{إِنَّ الَّذِينَ قَالُوا رَبُّنَا اللَّهُ ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا وَأَبْشِرُوا بِالْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ * نَحْنُ أَوْلِيَاؤُكُمْ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ ۖ وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِي أَنفُسُكُمْ وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا مَا تَدَّعُونَ * نُزُلًا مِّنْ غَفُورٍ رَّحِيمٍ}** [فصلت: ٣٠-٣٢][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, those who say, 'Our Lord is Allah,' and then remain steadfast, the angels descend upon them: 'Do not fear and do not grieve, but rejoice in the glad tidings of the Jannah you were promised. We are your allies in the life of this world and in the Hereafter. In it you will have whatever your souls desire, and in it you will have whatever you ask for: a welcome from One who is All-Forgiving, Most Merciful.'"** (Fussilat 41:30-32)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On "whatever your souls desire," Ibn Kathir said: "That is, in Jannah you will have everything you choose of what souls desire and what delights the eyes"; and on "whatever you ask for": "That is, whatever you ask for, you will find, and it will be brought before you, just as you chose." As-Sa'di said that "whatever your souls desire" "has been prepared and made ready"; that "whatever you ask for" means "you ask for anything your will reaches toward, of every kind of pleasure and delight, of what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and has never occurred to any human heart"; and that "a welcome" (*nuzulan*) means "a hosting and hospitality."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah holds two degrees of fulfillment: what the soul desires, which it finds already prepared before it even asks; and what it asks for in words, which appears exactly as it chose. These glad tidings are for those who said "Our Lord is Allah" and then remained steadfast. The road to having our wishes fulfilled there is steadfastness here.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-43-71" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "In It Is Whatever Souls Desire and Eyes Delight In"

> **{يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِم بِصِحَافٍ مِّن ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ ۖ وَفِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِيهِ الْأَنفُسُ وَتَلَذُّ الْأَعْيُنُ ۖ وَأَنتُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [الزخرف: ٧١][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Platters of gold and cups will be passed around among them, and in it is whatever souls desire and eyes delight in, and in it you will abide forever."** (az-Zukhruf 43:71)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said of "whatever souls desire and eyes delight in": "This is an all-embracing expression that takes in every delight and joy, every comfort of the eyes and gladness of the heart." Of "and in it you will abide forever," he said: "This is the completion of the bliss of the people of Jannah: abiding in it forever, which includes the permanence of its bliss, its increase, and its never being cut off." And Ibn Kathir said of "and eyes delight in": "That is, fine taste, fine fragrance and a beautiful sight."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world, wishes are only ever granted incompletely: some come true, some are delayed, and those that do come true pass away. In Jannah, desire, delight and eternity come together, so the soul never fears that what it has been given will be taken away.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-489" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Ask You for Your Companionship in Jannah"

> عَنْ رَبِيعَةَ بْنِ كَعْبٍ الْأَسْلَمِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **كُنْتُ أَبِيتُ مَعَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَأَتَيْتُهُ بِوَضُوئِهِ وَحَاجَتِهِ، فَقَالَ لِي: «سَلْ». فَقُلْتُ: أَسْأَلُكَ مُرَافَقَتَكَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ. قَالَ: «أَوَغَيْرَ ذَلِكَ؟». قُلْتُ: هُوَ ذَاكَ. قَالَ: «فَأَعِنِّي عَلَى نَفْسِكَ بِكَثْرَةِ السُّجُودِ».**[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b al-Aslami, may Allah be pleased with him, said: **I used to spend the night with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and I brought him his water for wudu' and whatever else he needed. He said to me, "Ask." I said, "I ask you for your companionship in Jannah." He said, "Or anything other than that?" I said, "That is it." He said, "Then help me to help you, by making many prostrations."**[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is the authenticated account of the lesson "The Names of Jannah and Its Reality," and we take it here only as supporting evidence, because it is the mirror image of the old woman's request. Rabi'ah was told "Ask," and he asked for the companionship of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Jannah; then he was shown the deed that would help make his wish come true: many prostrations.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The two ayat establish that the wishes of the people of Jannah will all be fulfilled, both what they desire and what they ask for. The two hadiths teach us where to place our highest wishes now: in the companionship of the prophets and the ranks of the Hereafter. In the hadith of the old woman, the Bedouin was not faulted for asking for what he needed; rather, those present were alerted to the fact that the request could have been higher. So the lesson is not contempt for the needs of this world. It is a matter of putting them in order: they are asked for in their proper place, and they must not take the place of the greatest wish.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between "whatever your souls desire" and "whatever you ask for," as it emerges from the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di?
2. Why was the Bedouin's request not a mistake, and yet the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "Could you not be like the old woman of the Children of Israel?"
3. What does Ibn Hibban's chapter heading for this hadith teach about the opportunities a person is given to ask?
4. What is the connection between "and then remain steadfast" in Fussilat 41:30 and the fulfillment of wishes in Jannah?
5. If someone said to you today, "Ask," what would be the first thing that came to mind? And what would you want to be the first thing that came to mind?

### Activity: The Wish Elevator

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.074.activity.wish-elevator-ask-the-highest" -->

At the bottom of a sheet of paper, write a real wish that has been on your mind lately, exactly as it is, without dressing it up: a house, a job, a recovery from illness, or a child's success.

Above it, draw four floors. On each floor, ask yourself: "If this came true, what would I want after that?" and write the answer on the floor above, until you reach a wish that only Jannah can fulfill completely, such as a peace that never fades, a reunion with no parting, or the companionship of the righteous.

Copy the top floor onto a small card, phrased as a du'a, and beneath it write one deed that will help you toward it, just as Rabi'ah was directed to many prostrations. Ask for what is on the card in your sujud for seven days, alongside your du'a for your permissible worldly need in its proper place. Then write three lines: how has the order of your wishes changed?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="4.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Do you wish for lots of things? A new toy, maybe, or a trip, or for your dad to stay home with you all day long? Everybody makes wishes.

And Allah has told us that the people of Jannah will find everything they wish for there. Allah says: "In it you will have whatever your souls desire." That means: in Jannah you will have everything you love and long for.[^2] So what is the most wonderful thing we could wish for? Listen to this story.

### A True Account: The Grandma Who Asked for the Best Thing of All

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="ibnhibban-723" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story told by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="ibnhibban-723" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, once visited a man who lived out in the desert, and the man was very kind to him. So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him, "Come and visit us." When the man came, the Prophet said to him, "Ask for what you need," which means: ask for whatever you want. The man asked for a camel to ride, and some goats so his family could drink their milk.

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told a story about an old grandma from the Children of Israel. The Prophet Musa, peace be upon him, was traveling with his people, and they got lost and couldn't find the way. The wise men told him that the Prophet Yusuf, peace be upon him, had asked them before he died to take his remains with them when they left Egypt. But nobody knew where his grave was, except this grandma.

Musa said to her, "Show us the place." She said, "Not until you give me what I ask for." He said, "And what do you ask for?" She said, "To be with you in Jannah!"

Allah told Musa to give her what she asked for. So she led them to a pond. They took all the water out, and they dug, and they found what they were looking for. And right then, the road became as clear as daylight.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

The grandma could have asked for gold or a house. But she asked for the best thing of all: Jannah, with Allah's Prophet. Small wishes are not a bad thing, but the most wonderful wishes are the ones that last forever.

### Activity: My Wish Elevator

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.074.activity.wish-elevator-ask-the-highest" -->

On the first floor, draw something you wish for right now, like a toy or a bike.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Imagine your teacher says at the end of the school year, "You can ask for one prize." What would you choose? Lots of people blurt out the first thing that pops into their heads, and then wish they had thought about it a little longer.

In Jannah, no one is ever denied a wish. Allah says: "In it you will have whatever your souls desire, and in it you will have whatever you ask for." Ibn Kathir explained: "Whatever you ask for, you will find."[^2] And Allah says: "In it is whatever souls desire and eyes delight in, and in it you will abide forever." That means everything the heart loves and the eyes enjoy, and it lasts without ever ending.[^3]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-41-30-32" -->

- **`Whatever you ask for (ma tadda'un)`** — what you ask for out loud, which then appears just as you chose it.[^2]
- **`A welcome (nuzulan)`** — the hospitality and honor Allah gives the people of Jannah, like a host welcoming a guest.[^2]
- **`My request (hukmi)`** — in the story of the old woman: the reward I name as my condition.[^1]
- **`Drain (andibu)`** — take the water out until the place is dry.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A True Account: The Old Woman of the Children of Israel

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="ibnhibban-723" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story told by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="ibnhibban-723" audience="8-12" -->

Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, stopped at the home of a Bedouin, who treated him generously. The Prophet invited him to come and visit, and when he came, the Prophet said to him, "Ask for what you need." The Bedouin said, "A she-camel for us to ride, and some goats for my family to milk."

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to the people around him, "Could you not be like the old woman of the Children of Israel?" They asked him who she was, and he told them. When Musa, peace be upon him, led the Children of Israel out of Egypt, they lost their way. Their scholars told him that Yusuf, peace be upon him, had made them promise before he died that they would not leave Egypt without carrying his bones with them. Musa asked, "Who knows where his grave is?" They said, "An old woman of the Children of Israel."

The old woman came, and Musa said to her, "Show me the grave of Yusuf." She said, "Not until you grant me my request." He said, "And what is your request?" She said, "That I be with you in Jannah." Musa was reluctant to grant her that, so Allah revealed to him that he should grant her request. She led them to a small lake. They drained its water, then dug and brought out the bones of Yusuf, and when they lifted them up, there was the road, as clear as the light of day.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

The old woman had something everyone needed, and she could have asked for a fortune. But she thought before she asked, and she asked for the highest thing there is. The Bedouin's request was not wrong; he asked for what his family needed. But the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was teaching his Companions that a wish can reach higher. In Jannah, every wish comes true, so let's make the highest of our wishes Jannah itself.

<!-- story:end -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. What did the Bedouin ask for? And why was his request not a mistake?
2. What was the one thing only the old woman knew?
3. Why is her request called "the highest request"?
4. What does "and in it you will have whatever you ask for" mean?
5. Your grandpa gives you some money and says, "Buy whatever you like." How would you divide it between what you need and something whose reward lasts?

### Activity: The Wish Elevator

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.074.activity.wish-elevator-ask-the-highest" -->

Draw an elevator with four floors. On the ground floor, write a real wish you have right now.

Ask yourself: "If I got it, what would I want after that?" and write the answer on the second floor. Keep asking the same question until you reach the top floor, where you write a wish that can only come true in Jannah.

Write the top-floor wish on a card, phrased as a du'a, and underneath it write one deed that will bring you closer to it.

Make that du'a in your sujud for five days, then write two sentences: has the thing you wish for first changed?

> اللَّهُمَّ ارْفَعْ هِمَمَنَا إِلَى مَا يَبْقَى، وَأَعْطِنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مَا تَشْتَهِي أَنْفُسُنَا، وَاجْمَعْنَا فِيهَا بِأَنْبِيَائِكَ وَالصَّالِحِينَ.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Our wishes aren't born in a vacuum. A lot of them are manufactured by ads and by the slices of life other people put on display: a phone, a car, followers, travel. The question isn't whether these wishes are haram; most of them are perfectly permissible. The question is: is this the highest thing I want?

The Qur'an promises the people of Jannah a principle with no exceptions: "In it you will have whatever your souls desire, and in it you will have whatever you ask for." As-Sa'di explains: "You ask for anything your will reaches toward... of what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and has never occurred to any human heart."[^2] But the same passage opens with those who "say, 'Our Lord is Allah,' and then remain steadfast." Over there, wishes are granted in full. What gets shaped here is their direction.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="ibnhibban-723" -->

- **`Ask for what you need (sal hajatak)`** — a generous invitation from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to someone who had treated him generously.[^1]
- **`A solemn pledge before Allah (mawthiqan min Allah)`** — a binding promise sworn in Allah's name.[^1]
- **`My request (hukmi)`** — what I stipulate as my reward for what I'm offering.[^1]
- **`And then remain steadfast (thumma istaqamu)`** — hold firm in obeying Allah, in knowledge and in action, as as-Sa'di explained it.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Woman Who Held a Single Secret

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="ibnhibban-723" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story told by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="ibnhibban-723" audience="13+" -->

The story opens with an act of generosity. A Bedouin honors the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the Prophet returns the kindness by inviting him over, then says to him, "Ask for what you need." It's a once-in-a-lifetime opening. And the man asks for what he knows and needs: a she-camel to ride, and some goats for his family to milk.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, turns to everyone present: "Could you not be like the old woman of the Children of Israel?" Then he tells the story. Musa, peace be upon him, led the Children of Israel out of Egypt, and they lost their way. Their scholars told him that Yusuf, peace be upon him, had taken a solemn pledge from them before Allah that they would not leave Egypt until they carried his bones with them. And no one knew where the grave was except one old woman.

An entire nation and its prophet are waiting on a piece of information held by an elderly woman. Had she wanted to, she could have named any worldly price. Musa said to her, "Show me the grave of Yusuf." She said, "Not until you grant me my request." He said, "And what is your request?" She said, "That I be with you in Jannah." Musa was reluctant to grant her that, so Allah revealed to him: "Grant her her request." She led them to a lake; they drained its water, dug, and brought out the bones, and when they lifted them up, there was the road, as clear as the light of day.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice that the hadith does not say why Musa, peace be upon him, was reluctant, so we don't invent a reason for him; what it does say is that Allah commanded him to grant the request. Notice too that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, neither scolded the Bedouin nor made fun of him; he put the question to everyone. Ibn Hibban gave the hadith a chapter heading saying that a person should not "trade away the means to the Hereafter for any of the debris of this perishing world."[^1]

The same picture appears in the request of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b, may Allah be pleased with him. He was told, "Ask," and he said, "I ask you for your companionship in Jannah." And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, pointed him to the work: "Then help me to help you, by making many prostrations."[^4] A high wish needs deeds to carry it.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, put his question to everyone present instead of scolding the Bedouin?
2. What made the old woman's request possible? And what "piece of information" or talent do you have that could be spent on a high request?
3. Where do most of your wishes come from these days? And how do you know they are really your own?
4. How does a Muslim combine asking for permissible worldly needs with making Jannah the highest of their wishes?
5. What is your own version of "many prostrations," the deed that will carry your highest wish?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Wish Elevator

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.074.activity.wish-elevator-ask-the-highest" -->

In a private note, write a real wish that's on your mind right now, even if it seems small or worldly to you.

Build four floors above it with a single repeated question: "If this came true, what would I want after that?" until you reach a wish that can only be fulfilled in Jannah.

Copy the top floor onto a card, phrased as a du'a, and add beneath it one deed to carry it, such as two rak'ahs at night or an act of kindness to your parents that you aren't used to doing. Make the du'a on the card in your sujud for seven days.

On the eighth day, write a private paragraph: has the order of your wishes changed? Which wish moved down a floor, and which one moved up?

<!-- activity:end -->

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.074.dua.raise-our-wishes-gather-us-with-prophets" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ ارْفَعْ هِمَمَنَا إِلَى مَا يَبْقَى، وَأَعْطِنَا فِي جَنَّتِكَ مَا تَشْتَهِي أَنْفُسُنَا، وَاجْمَعْنَا فِيهَا بِأَنْبِيَائِكَ وَالصَّالِحِينَ.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of the old woman of the Children of Israel in order and states its grading; explains the difference between "whatever your souls desire" and "whatever you ask for" using the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; weighs permissible worldly needs against high aspiration without looking down on the former; and builds their own wish elevator, leaving with a card bearing a high request and a deed to carry it.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a handout with the four texts; a large sheet with a five-floor elevator for the activity; small cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Fussilat 41:30-32 and az-Zukhruf 43:71, reviews the first footnote on the sourcing of the hadith and the differing numbering of al-Mustadrak, and prepares a disciplined answer to the question "Why was Musa, peace be upon him, reluctant?": the hadith does not give the reason, so we do not invent one.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "If you were told right now, 'Ask for what you need, and it will be given to you,' what would you say?" Each learner writes down their first answer within a few seconds and keeps it without sharing.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read Ibn Hibban 723 in full, divided into two scenes: the Bedouin, then the old woman (8 minutes); then Fussilat 41:30-32 and az-Zukhruf 43:71 with the two tafsirs (8 minutes); then Muslim 489 as supporting evidence (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Ask: "What shows that the Bedouin's request was not blameworthy?" "What does Ibn Hibban's chapter heading add?" and "Why are the glad tidings of fulfilled wishes in Fussilat tied to steadfastness?" Then read the paragraph "How Do These Texts Fit Together?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner builds their own wish elevator as described in the activity unit (10 minutes); then anyone who wishes names the kind of wish on their top floor, without private details (5 minutes). Agree on a check-in after one week.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner goes back to the first answer they wrote in the opening and compares it with their top floor. Exit card: "Give one proof that the wishes of the people of Jannah are all fulfilled, and one proof that the greatest wish is pursued now, through deeds."

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, Fussilat 41:31 and the second scene of the hadith are enough. Advanced learners gather the wordings of the hadith in Ibn Hibban, al-Hakim and Abu Ya'la from the Dorar website and compare the hadith scholars' gradings of it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not depict Musa or Yusuf, peace be upon them. Do not delve into the reason for Musa's reluctance, and do not add to the story anything not in it from the Isra'iliyyat (such as the old woman's name or a description of the grave). Do not use the lesson to belittle the worldly needs of the poor, and do not ask anyone to reveal their private wishes.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child tells, in three sentences, what the man asked for and what the grandma asked for; says that Allah gives the people of Jannah whatever they wish for; draws their wish elevator; and asks for Jannah in their sujud.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet of paper and crayons; two simple pictures of a camel and a goat; a small bowl of water and a spoon to act out "taking the water out"; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator learns the simplified story by heart and draws the outline of a three-floor elevator in advance.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** "If I told you, 'Ask for one thing,' what would you ask for?" Listen kindly to the answers without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Story — 6 minutes:** Read the story calmly and reverently. Show the pictures of the camel and the goat when the man's request comes up, and let the child empty the bowl of water with the spoon when the pond is mentioned.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 5 minutes:** "What did the grandma ask for?" "Why was her request so beautiful?" "Did the man do something wrong? No, but the grandma asked for something that lasts." Then read the ayah with its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child draws the three floors of their elevator as described in the activity unit, and is reminded to make the du'a in sujud at the next prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** "What do the people of Jannah find there?" "What is on the top floor of your elevator?" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, two floors are enough: "Something I love" and "Jannah." Older children are asked to tell the story to a brother or sister.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw Musa or Yusuf, peace be upon them, or the grandma. Mention the grave and the remains calmly and without detail, and do not dwell on death. Do not tell the child that their toys and small wishes are bad, and do not promise that every worldly wish will come true.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the four terms; puts the events of the story in order; cites two ayat about the wishes of the people of Jannah; distinguishes between a permissible need and the highest wish; and builds their own wish elevator.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; six story-event cards (the Bedouin's request, the Prophet's question, losing the way, the pledge, the old woman's request, the road made clear); the elevator sheet; the request card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares a worked example of a wish elevator that starts with a simple wish (such as a new bike) and ends with a wish for the Hereafter.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The one-prize question from the explanation unit; each student writes down their answer.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Explain the terms, then read the story; the students put the event cards in order; then read the ayat from Fussilat and az-Zukhruf.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discussion: "What could the old woman have asked for?" "Why wasn't the Bedouin wrong?" Then show the worked example of the elevator.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student builds their elevator and writes their request card as described in the activity unit, and takes it home for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each student writes the old woman's request from memory and the meaning of "whatever you ask for"; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, three floors are enough. Advanced students compare the old woman's request with that of Rabi'ah ibn Ka'b.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not act out the characters of Musa or Yusuf, peace be upon them. Respect students whose wishes are family needs (such as a job for their father or healing for someone who is sick), and do not ask for wishes to be shared with the class.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the positions of the Bedouin and the old woman without looking down on either; connects the fulfillment of wishes in Jannah with steadfastness; examines where their own wishes come from; and builds their wish elevator, linking its top floor to a deed.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private activity sheet; three case cards: "A student who wishes for a huge number of followers," "A student who wishes to get into medical school," "A student who wishes his parents would stop fighting."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the first and fourth footnotes and prepares a balanced answer to the question "Do worldly wishes weaken faith?": asking for what is permissible is legitimate; what matters is that it does not take the place of the greatest wish.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "Quickly write down three wishes, then next to each one write: where did it come from?" Silent reflection.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the hadith in full from the academic section, then Fussilat 41:30-32, then the hadith of Rabi'ah.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the three cases in groups: "And then what? Where does this wish lead?" Then ask: "Why was the information the old woman held an opportunity? And what is yours?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student completes the wish elevator individually as described in the activity unit, and keeps the sheet.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** A short paragraph: "What deed will carry my highest wish?" After one week, ask a general question, "Did you complete the seven days?", without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Give the worked example to anyone who finds it hard to identify their wishes. Advanced students trace the tafsir of "whatever you ask for" in at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi and compare it with that of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask anyone to share private wishes; they may be painful (as in the case of the parents who fight), so treat them gently and point the student toward someone who can help. Do not act out the character of any prophet. Do not present the religion as though it forbids permissible worldly ambition.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
