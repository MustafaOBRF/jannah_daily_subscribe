# The Strength of Jannah's People and the Purity of Their Bodies

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the scene of Surah Fatir (35:32-35) in its order: the heirs of the Book in their three categories; then their entry into the Gardens of Eden, with their adornment and their clothing; then the first thing they say, "All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken all sorrow away from us"; and its closing words, "no toil touches us here, and no weariness touches us here."
- Explain the meaning of *nasab* (toil) and *lughub* (weariness) from the words of at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir, and as-Sa'di's statement that Allah makes the bodies of the people of Jannah "in a perfect form of creation," with no fatigue upon body, heart, or faculties.
- Use the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam in the Musnad of Ahmad (19269) to show that each of the people of Jannah is given the strength of a hundred men, and that their bodies are pure, free of anything unclean: their food leaves them as sweat that smells like musk.
- Connect Allah's name *Shakur*, the Most Appreciative, with the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, when his feet had swollen from standing in prayer: "Should I not be a grateful servant?" (al-Bukhari 4836), and with Ibn Kathir's remark that they used to wear themselves out in worship and were then brought into lasting rest.
- Carry out the "Where Did My Strength Go?" activity: logging the efforts that tired the learner each day for a set period, sorting them, and then directing one deliberate effort the next day toward worship or service, with an intention set in advance.
- Learn by heart a lesson-authored bedtime du'a asking Allah for strength to obey Him, and to settle us in the Abode of Permanence, where there is neither toil nor weariness.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Fatigue is our constant companion in this world. The body tires from work and the heart from worry; the body needs sleep to recover its strength, and it weakens with illness and age. An earlier lesson dealt with the call announcing to the people of Jannah that they will be healthy and never fall ill, and another lesson deals with their food, which brings no impurity with it. This lesson has a narrower focus: the strength of the people of Jannah, their freedom from toil and exhaustion, the purity of their bodies, and what all this means for us as we spend our strength today.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-35-32-35" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "No Toil Touches Us Here, and No Weariness Touches Us Here"

> **{ثُمَّ أَوْرَثْنَا الْكِتَابَ الَّذِينَ اصْطَفَيْنَا مِنْ عِبَادِنَا ۖ فَمِنْهُمْ ظَالِمٌ لِنَفْسِهِ وَمِنْهُمْ مُقْتَصِدٌ وَمِنْهُمْ سَابِقٌ بِالْخَيْرَاتِ بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَضْلُ الْكَبِيرُ ۝ جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا يُحَلَّوْنَ فِيهَا مِنْ أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَلُؤْلُؤًا ۖ وَلِبَاسُهُمْ فِيهَا حَرِيرٌ ۝ وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَذْهَبَ عَنَّا الْحَزَنَ ۖ إِنَّ رَبَّنَا لَغَفُورٌ شَكُورٌ ۝ الَّذِي أَحَلَّنَا دَارَ الْمُقَامَةِ مِنْ فَضْلِهِ لَا يَمَسُّنَا فِيهَا نَصَبٌ وَلَا يَمَسُّنَا فِيهَا لُغُوبٌ}** [فاطر: ٣٢-٣٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Then We gave the Book as an inheritance to those of Our servants whom We chose. Among them are some who wrong themselves, some who keep to a middle course, and some who, by Allah's permission, race ahead in good deeds. That is the great bounty. Gardens of Eden, which they will enter; there they will be adorned with bracelets of gold and with pearls, and their clothing there will be silk. And they will say, 'All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken all sorrow away from us. Our Lord is indeed All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative: He who, out of His bounty, has settled us in the Abode of Permanence, where no toil touches us, and no weariness touches us.'"** (Fatir 35:32-35)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir defines the one who wrongs himself as "the one who falls short in some of the obligations and commits some of the prohibited things"; the one who keeps to a middle course as "the one who fulfills the obligations and leaves the prohibited things"; and the one who races ahead in good deeds as "the one who does both the obligatory and the recommended." He sets out the disagreement among the early generations about the one who wrongs himself, then concludes that he is "of this Ummah." On "All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative" he cites Ibn 'Abbas and others: "He forgave them a great many evil deeds and appreciated the few good deeds they did." On "out of His bounty" he writes: "Our deeds were not worth that much." On *nasab* and *lughub* he says, "each of them is used for fatigue," and that the meaning is "that there is no fatigue upon their bodies or their souls." Then he adds: "They used to wear themselves out in worship in this world, so once they entered it, the burden of religious obligation was lifted from them, and they came to lasting, unending rest."[^1]

At-Tabari explains "no toil touches us there" as "no fatigue and no pain afflicts us there," *lughub* as "strain and exhaustion," and the Abode of Permanence as "the abode of residence from which there is no moving away and no change."[^3]

As-Sa'di explains "has settled us" as "brought us down to settle and stay, not to stop on the way through," and says of the end of the ayah: "There is no fatigue in the bodies, nor in the heart and the faculties, nor from the abundance of enjoyment; and this shows that Allah, exalted is He, makes their bodies in a perfect form of creation." He also takes it as evidence that they do not sleep, "because the benefit of sleep is the removal of fatigue," and because sleep is "the lesser death, and the people of Jannah do not die"; this is his own inference. Of "by Allah's permission" he says it was added so that the one who races ahead would not be deluded by his own deeds.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The scene opens with the Book and closes with rest. The heirs differ in rank, yet the first words they speak in Jannah are praise for the passing of sorrow, then thanks for a dwelling they will never be moved out of, and then the denial of fatigue in both its forms: the toil of the body and the weariness of the soul.

### The Strength of a Hundred Men

> عَنْ زَيْدِ بْنِ أَرْقَمَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: أَتَى النَّبِيَّ ﷺ رَجُلٌ مِنَ الْيَهُودِ فَقَالَ: يَا أَبَا الْقَاسِمِ، أَلَسْتَ تَزْعُمُ أَنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَأْكُلُونَ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ؟ وَقَالَ لِأَصْحَابِهِ: إِنْ أَقَرَّ لِي بِهَذِهِ خَصَمْتُهُ. قَالَ: فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«بَلَى وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، إِنَّ أَحَدَهُمْ لَيُعْطَى قُوَّةَ مِائَةِ رَجُلٍ فِي الْمَطْعَمِ وَالْمَشْرَبِ وَالشَّهْوَةِ وَالْجِمَاعِ»**. قَالَ: فَقَالَ لَهُ الْيَهُودِيُّ: فَإِنَّ الَّذِي يَأْكُلُ وَيَشْرَبُ تَكُونُ لَهُ الْحَاجَةُ. قَالَ: فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«حَاجَةُ أَحَدِهِمْ عَرَقٌ يَفِيضُ مِنْ جُلُودِهِمْ مِثْلُ رِيحِ الْمِسْكِ، فَإِذَا الْبَطْنُ قَدْ ضَمُرَ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Zayd ibn Arqam, may Allah be pleased with him, said: A man from among the Jews came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, "O Abu al-Qasim, do you not claim that the people of Jannah eat and drink there?" He had said to his companions, "If he concedes this to me, I will have won the argument against him." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Yes indeed, by the One in whose hand is my soul! Each one of them will be given the strength of a hundred men in eating, drinking, desire, and conjugal intimacy."** The Jewish man said to him, "But whoever eats and drinks will need to relieve himself." The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The need of each of them is a sweat that flows from their skin, like the scent of musk, and then the stomach has become lean again."**[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The questioner assumed that granting the eating would force one to grant the uncleanliness that follows it. The answer gave him two things instead: multiplied strength for enjoyment, and complete purity with no waste at all. The strength here is strength for bliss, not for hard labor, and the body is pure and fragrant. The details concerning married life in Jannah belong to a later lesson.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-4836" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Should I Not Be a Grateful Servant?"

> عَنِ الْمُغِيرَةِ بْنِ شُعْبَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَامَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ حَتَّى تَوَرَّمَتْ قَدَمَاهُ، فَقِيلَ لَهُ: غَفَرَ اللَّهُ لَكَ مَا تَقَدَّمَ مِنْ ذَنْبِكَ وَمَا تَأَخَّرَ، قَالَ: **«أَفَلَا أَكُونُ عَبْدًا شَكُورًا»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah, may Allah be pleased with him, said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, stood in prayer until his feet swelled. It was said to him, "Allah has forgiven you your past and future sins." He said: **"Should I not be a grateful servant?"**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Mubarakfuri explains "stood" as "that is, in the night prayer," and "swelled" as "that is, became puffed up from standing so long."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

In Arabic the same word links the two texts: Allah is *Shakur*, the Most Appreciative, who rewards even a small deed, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to be a servant who is *shakur*, deeply grateful, so he spent his strength standing in prayer. This is the toil that ends with the words: "no toil touches us here."

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of Fatir describe an abode with no fatigue of body or of soul, and as-Sa'di explains this by the perfect form of creation in which Allah fashions the bodies of its people. The hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam adds that these bodies are many times stronger than the bodies of this world, and purer too, with no impurity and no waste. The hadith of al-Mughirah reminds us that the strength we have in this world is a trust, to be spent in gratitude. So the practical question is this: where is my strength going today, and what will remain of my toil on the day it is said, "No more toil after today"?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. How do at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir distinguish *nasab* from *lughub*? And why are both denied?
2. How did as-Sa'di use the ayah to argue that the bodies of the people of Jannah are "in a perfect form of creation"? And what are the limits of his inference about sleep?
3. Why does the denial of fatigue come right after "out of His bounty," and how does that fit with Ibn Kathir's words, "Our deeds were not worth that much"?
4. What difficulty was the questioner in the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam trying to force on the Prophet, and how did the Prophetic answer dismantle it?
5. What links Allah's name *Shakur* with the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him, "Should I not be a grateful servant?" And where do you spend most of your own strength?

### Activity: Where Did My Strength Go?

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.063.activity.where-did-my-strength-go" -->

For seven days, write down at the end of each day the three efforts that tired you most, whether physical, mental, or emotional. Estimate how much time each one took, then sort it into one of three columns: "For Allah or in service of others," "A permissible need," and "Wasted to no purpose." Then choose one effort for the next day in which you will deliberately tire yourself for Allah, such as two rak'ahs at night, visiting someone who is sick, or helping a relative. Write down your intention before you begin, and when you finish, say: "Al-hamdu lillah." On the seventh day, add up the columns and write a paragraph: which wasted effort will I replace with a steady act of obedience, and how can I be a grateful servant with my strength?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

After you play outside for a long, long time, what happens to your legs? They get tired, and you want to sit down. When your dad comes home from work, sometimes he is tired and needs to rest. And at night we sleep, because our bodies are tired.

But Jannah is different. The people of Jannah are very, very strong. They never get tired. Their legs never hurt, and their backs never hurt, and their bodies are always clean and smell lovely. This is a gift Allah gives them.

### From the Qur'an: "No Tiredness Here!"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-35-32-35" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In a surah called "Fatir," Allah tells us about the people He gave His Book, the Qur'an.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-35-32-35" audience="4-7" -->

Some of them hurried fast to do good things. Some of them did what they were supposed to do. And some of them made lots of mistakes. And Allah, because He is so generous, lets them into beautiful gardens called "the Gardens of Eden." There they wear bracelets of gold, and pearls, and clothes of soft silk.

Then they say: "All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken all sorrow away from us." That means: nothing makes us sad anymore. And they say: our Lord is the One who brought us to this home where we will stay forever, because He is so kind and generous. Then they say: "No toil touches us here, and no weariness touches us here." That means: we never get tired here, and nothing ever hurts.[^1][^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Look: the very first thing the people of Jannah said was "Al-hamdu lillah," all praise belongs to Allah. When we get tired doing something good, like helping Mom or carrying something for Dad, we say "Al-hamdu lillah" too, and we remember that in Jannah nobody ever gets tired.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On school sports day, the students ran until they were completely out of breath, then flopped down on the grass to gulp some water. That evening, some of them were so worn out that they went to bed early, and some forgot to go over their lessons. In this world our strength runs out, and afterward we need rest and sleep.

This lesson is about the strength of the people of Jannah: their bodies are strong and complete, no fatigue or exhaustion ever touches them, and they are pure, with nothing unclean about them. And it asks an important question: if our strength here is limited, what are we going to spend it on?

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to stand in prayer at night until his feet swelled, puffing up from standing so long. Someone said to him, "Allah has forgiven you your past and future sins." And he said: "Should I not be a grateful servant?"[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-35-32-35" -->

- **`Toil (nasab)`** — tiredness and aching, as at-Tabari explained.[^3]
- **`Weariness (lughub)`** — strain and exhaustion, the kind of tiredness that drains you and weighs you down, as at-Tabari explained.[^3]
- **`The Abode of Permanence (dar al-muqamah)`** — the home where you stay for good, never moving out and never changing, as at-Tabari explained.[^3]
- **`Most Appreciative (Shakur)`** — the One who accepts good deeds and multiplies them, and out of His bounty gives more than our deeds could ever reach, as as-Sa'di explained.[^2]

### From the Qur'an: What the People of Jannah Say When They Enter

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-35-32-35" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah Fatir talks about the people of this Ummah to whom Allah gave the Qur'an. But they are not all the same.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-35-32-35" audience="8-12" -->

Allah says: "Among them are some who wrong themselves, some who keep to a middle course, and some who, by Allah's permission, race ahead in good deeds." Ibn Kathir explained that the one who wrongs himself fell short in some of his duties and did some forbidden things; the one on the middle course did his duties and stayed away from the forbidden; and the one who races ahead did all that and added the recommended deeds too.

Then Allah tells us their reward: "Gardens of Eden, which they will enter." There they are adorned with bracelets of gold and with pearls, and their clothes are silk. And what do they say? "All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken all sorrow away from us. Our Lord is indeed All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative: He who, out of His bounty, has settled us in the Abode of Permanence, where no toil touches us, and no weariness touches us." As-Sa'di explained that there is no tiredness in their bodies or in their hearts, because Allah makes their bodies "in a perfect form of creation."[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice two things. First, they said "out of His bounty," so they did not claim that their deeds had paid for Jannah. Second, Allah is *Shakur*, Most Appreciative: He appreciates even a small deed. That is why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tired himself out in prayer, so that he could be a grateful servant. Getting tired today in obeying Allah is never wasted, and at the end of it comes a rest that no tiredness will ever follow.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

"I'm exhausted" is something we say all the time. But look at where the exhaustion sometimes comes from: a late night on a screen, endless scrolling, then a Fajr that feels like lifting a boulder, and a school day with no focus. Meanwhile, a classmate might be wiped out from sports practice, from helping at home, or from memorizing the Qur'an, and that kind of tired is a completely different thing. Strength in this world is limited, and the real question is: what are you spending it on?

In the Hereafter, everything changes. In an authentic hadith, a Jewish man asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about the eating and drinking of the people of Jannah, thinking this would force him to admit they must also have bodily waste. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him that each of them is given the strength of a hundred men to enjoy what they are given, and said of their need after eating: "a sweat that flows from their skin, like the scent of musk, and then the stomach has become lean again." Their bodies are strong, and pure, with nothing unclean about them.[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-35-32-35" -->

- **`Keeping to a middle course (muqtasid)`** — the one who fulfills the obligations and avoids the forbidden, though he may leave some of the recommended deeds, as Ibn Kathir explained.[^1]
- **`Has settled us (ahallana)`** — has brought us down to settle and stay, not to stop on the way through, as as-Sa'di explained.[^2]
- **`Weariness (lughub)`** — strain and exhaustion, as at-Tabari explained; *nasab* and *lughub* are both kinds of fatigue, so fatigue is denied for both bodies and souls, as Ibn Kathir explained.[^3][^1]

### From the Qur'an: From the Book to the Abode of Permanence

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-35-32-35" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true scene from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

The story begins with an inheritance: Allah made this Ummah the heir of His Book. As-Sa'di said that inheriting the Book means "inheriting its knowledge and its practice, studying its words, and drawing out its meanings."[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-35-32-35" audience="13+" -->

"Then We gave the Book as an inheritance to those of Our servants whom We chose. Among them are some who wrong themselves, some who keep to a middle course, and some who, by Allah's permission, race ahead in good deeds." Ibn Kathir held that all three groups belong to this Ummah, and as-Sa'di said that "by Allah's permission" is there so that the one who races ahead will not be deluded by his own deeds.[^1][^2]

Then comes the reward: "Gardens of Eden, which they will enter; there they will be adorned with bracelets of gold and with pearls, and their clothing there will be silk." Then their first words: "All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken all sorrow away from us. Our Lord is indeed All-Forgiving, Most Appreciative: He who, out of His bounty, has settled us in the Abode of Permanence, where no toil touches us, and no weariness touches us." Ibn Kathir said that they "used to wear themselves out in worship in this world," and so they came to "lasting, unending rest."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice that the rest came after effort, not after laziness. And notice that Allah is *Shakur*, Most Appreciative: as Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn 'Abbas and others, "He appreciated the few good deeds they did."[^1] So never look down on a small effort for Allah that no one else sees: two rak'ahs at night, or patience with a heavy duty. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wore himself out standing in prayer until his feet swelled, and said: "Should I not be a grateful servant?"[^5]

### Discussion Questions

1. Why does the scene begin with inheriting the Book and end with the denial of fatigue? What connects the two?
2. What is the difference between tiredness that drains your strength and leaves nothing behind, and tiredness you spend for Allah? Give an example from your week.
3. The people of Jannah said, "out of His bounty." How does this keep you from arrogance if you work hard, and from despair if you fall short?
4. What does it mean to you that the body in Jannah is strong and pure, while today you live with your body's tiredness and weakness?
5. What is the one effort you want to choose this week so that you can be a "grateful servant"?

### Activity: Energy Log

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.063.activity.where-did-my-strength-go" -->

For seven days, keep a private note that no one else sees, and record the three things that drained your energy most each day, including your screen time if it was one of them. Sort each one: "For Allah or in service of others," "A need," or "A drain with nothing to show for it." Then choose one deliberate effort for Allah for the next day, write down your intention before you begin, and close it with "Al-hamdu lillah." On the seventh day, read back through your log and write a paragraph: if I heard today the words "no toil touches us here," which of my efforts would I want to have gone on ahead of me?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.063.dua.strength-in-obedience-rest-in-the-abode" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ قَوِّنَا عَلَى طَاعَتِكَ، وَاجْعَلْ تَعَبَنَا فِيمَا يُرْضِيكَ، وَأَحِلَّنَا دَارَ الْمُقَامَةِ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ، لَا يَمَسُّنَا فِيهَا نَصَبٌ وَلَا يَمَسُّنَا فِيهَا لُغُوبٌ.
>
> What it means: O Allah, help us with strength from You to obey You. Turn our tiredness toward what pleases You, not toward what drains us and leaves nothing behind. And out of Your bounty, not because of our deeds, settle us in the Abode of Permanence, where no toil touches our bodies and no weariness touches our souls.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene of Fatir 35:32-35 in order; defines the three categories in Ibn Kathir's words; explains *nasab*, *lughub*, and the Abode of Permanence in at-Tabari's words; distinguishes as-Sa'di's tafsir from his inference about sleep; uses the hadith of Ahmad 19269 to show the strength of the people of Jannah and the purity of their bodies; connects Allah's name *Shakur* with the hadith of al-Bukhari 4836; and begins the seven-day "Where Did My Strength Go?" log.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board with three columns: "Fatigue of the body," "Fatigue of the soul," and "What answers them in Jannah"; a three-column table sheet for the activity; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Fatir 35:32-35, at-Tabari's tafsir of ayah 35, the sourcing of the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam in *Hadi al-Arwah* (chapter 48), and al-Mubarakfuri's commentary on the hadith of al-Mughirah. The teacher also reviews the two lessons on the call to lasting health and on the food that brings no impurity, so as not to repeat their topics.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What tired you out most this week? Was it the kind of tired you are glad of, or the kind you regret?" Write the answers in the body and soul columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of Fatir in full, then Ibn Kathir's words on the three categories and on "out of His bounty," then at-Tabari and as-Sa'di on *nasab* and *lughub*, and fill in the third column (10 minutes). Then read the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam, setting out the difficulty raised and its answer (5 minutes). Then read the hadith of al-Mughirah and its commentary (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the second and third questions. The teacher makes clear the difference between tafsir and inference in as-Sa'di's remarks on sleep, and stresses that Jannah comes by Allah's bounty and is not the price of our deeds, and that Allah is Most Appreciative and multiplies the little we do.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner fills in today's row of their table, sorts the three efforts, then writes down tomorrow's deliberate effort for Allah together with its intention. Anyone who wishes may share their choice, without private details.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What do *nasab* and *lughub* mean?", "How did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, answer the man who asked about the needs of the people of Jannah?", and "What links *Shakur* with the hadith of al-Mughirah?" Then read the bedtime du'a, noting that it is lesson-authored.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, ayat 34-35 and the hadith of al-Mughirah are enough. Ask advanced learners to trace the disagreement among the early generations about the one who wrongs himself, as Ibn Kathir presents it, and to explain why he preferred the view he chose.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Read the hadith's wording on intimacy as it is narrated, without digression, and leave its details to their own lesson. Do not treat as-Sa'di's remark about sleep as a definitive text; it is an inference. Be mindful of those with chronic illness, disability, illness-related fatigue, or depression: never present tiredness as a sign of falling short, and do not impose physical tasks on them; a deliberate effort may be a du'a, dhikr, or a kind word. Warn against reading the hadith as a call to push oneself beyond one's capacity; the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught moderation and consistency.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah never get tired and that the first thing they said was "Al-hamdu lillah"; names a good thing they used their strength for; begins the "My Strong Hands" drawing; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet of paper for each child to trace their hands; crayons; a light bag filled with soft toys for acting out carrying; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the scene in a calm voice, traces their own hands on a sheet as an example, and sends families a short note explaining the three-day activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The children jump in place ten times, then the caregiver asks: "Are you tired? Where do you feel tired? What do you need right now?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the scene from the Qur'an. The children repeat: "All praise belongs to Allah, who has taken all sorrow away from us." Then the caregiver says, "No toil touches us here," and explains: we never get tired there.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask the unit's questions. Then each child carries the light bag a few steps to put it in its place and says: "Al-hamdu lillah, who gave me strength."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** With the caregiver's help, each child traces their hands and draws in one finger the good thing they did with their strength today, then takes the sheet home to finish over two more days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Notice whether the child can name a good thing they used their strength for. Then read the du'a one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one hand and one good deed are enough. Ask older children to retell in their own words what the people of Jannah said.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not mention to the children any details of married life in Jannah, or of bodily needs and waste. Be mindful of a child who is ill or has a physical disability, and welcome any gentle act from them, such as a word of thanks, a smile, or tidying a small thing. Never call a tired child lazy.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the scene with its three groups and the words of the people of Jannah; defines the four terms; retells the hadith of al-Mughirah and explains its link to *Shakur*; and begins the five-day "My Strength Receipt."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; three cards for the groups: "Wrongs himself," "Keeps to a middle course," and "Races ahead in good deeds"; a receipt table for each student; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Fatir 35:32-35, and prepares a written sample receipt for one day of their own life.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the sports-day story and asks: "After the race, what did each student spend the rest of their strength on?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the scene from the Qur'an while arranging the group cards and explaining them in Ibn Kathir's words, pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of the scholars of tafsir (6 minutes), then the hadith of al-Mughirah (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss the second and fourth questions. The teacher makes clear that Jannah comes by Allah's bounty, that Allah is Most Appreciative and accepts even a little, and that effort spent in obeying Him is never wasted.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The teacher shows the sample receipt, then each student fills in yesterday's row of their own receipt, chooses tomorrow's deliberate effort for Allah, and writes down their intention.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "The people of Jannah never get tired because..." and "Tomorrow I will spend my strength on...". Then read the du'a. After five days, anyone who wishes may share their overall result.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, three days and two columns are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize Fatir 35:34-35 and explain "the Abode of Permanence" in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Receipts are never shown against a student's will, and no student is mocked for having a lot under "Wasted." No student, and no member of a student's family, is placed in any of the three groups. Be mindful of a student who is ill or who does more work at home than they can manage; the teacher should listen to them and inform the person responsible when needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the scene of Fatir from the inheritance of the Book to the Abode of Permanence; explains *ahallana*, *muqtasid*, and *lughub*; states what the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam says about the strength of the people of Jannah and the purity of their bodies; distinguishes fruitful effort from mere drain; and begins the seven-day "Energy Log."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; an anonymized screenshot of a "Screen Time" report from the teacher's phone, or a printed sample; cards for anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di, and at-Tabari on Fatir 35:32-35, and the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam with its sourcing, and decides how to mention the hadith briefly without going into anything related to married life.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the screen-time sample and asks: "When did you feel exhausted this week? And where did it come from?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the scene from the Qur'an, with three pauses: the inheritance of the Book and the three groups; then "out of His bounty"; then "no toil touches us here," together with Ibn Kathir's words on rest after effort.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 2, and 3, followed by brief presentations. The teacher draws out that lasting rest follows the effort of obedience, that the bounty belongs to Allah, and that Allah is Most Appreciative.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student sets up their private log, writes today's entry, and chooses tomorrow's deliberate effort for Allah along with its intention.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: the meaning of *lughub*, what "out of His bounty" means, and the effort they chose for Allah. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking to see anyone's log. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may keep private voice notes instead. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on Ibn Kathir's words: "They used to wear themselves out in worship in this world... and they came to lasting, unending rest."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Present the hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam to teens only in the brief form given in the lesson, and do not go into anything related to married life. Be mindful of anyone living with chronic fatigue, or with difficulties in sleep, mood, or body image: never link tiredness to sin, and encourage them to seek help from their family or a professional. Never encourage staying up late or going without sleep in the name of worship.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
