# The Light of Jannah, Its Color and Its Radiant Whiteness

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the two ayat of al-Hadid (57:12-13) and explain the scene they describe: the believers' light running before them and on their right; the glad tidings of gardens given to them; the hypocrites asking to borrow from their light and being turned away; and then the wall whose inner side holds mercy.
- Show that this light is given in proportion to a person's faith and deeds, as established by as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir, and that it cannot be borrowed on the Day of Resurrection.
- Explain the words of Allah, exalted is He, `they will see there neither sun nor bitter cold`, as meaning that Jannah holds no harmful heat and no painful cold, and that denying the sun there does not mean denying light.
- Narrate the hadith of al-Bukhari (3327) on the radiance of the faces of the people of Jannah, like the moon on the night it is full and then like the most brilliant shining star, and understand that the light differs as the ranks differ.
- Distinguish the whiteness with which the texts describe the drink and delights of Jannah, a whiteness of radiance and purity, from any ranking of human beings by the color of their skin.
- Connect all of this to a daily deed: the glad tidings of complete light on the Day of Resurrection given to those who walk through the darkness to the mosques (Abu Dawud 561).
- Carry out the activity "Borrowed Light or Carried Light?": sorting the sources of light in one's life, then doing a deed in the hours of darkness and writing a note at dawn.
- Learn by heart the du'a for light that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to make: "O Allah, place light in my heart..."

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live by borrowed light. Our days are lit by a sun that sets, and our nights by lamps that go dark the moment the power fails. Even the faith of many of us can be borrowed: lent by a setting, a circle of friends, or a special season, it shines while its source is near and then fades. The texts that describe the light of the Hereafter and of Jannah speak of a different kind of light altogether: a light given to the believer in proportion to his faith and deeds, by which he walks when the whole world has gone dark, and which travels with him to a home where no sun scorches and no cold bites.

This lesson brings together five texts that complete one another in three truths:

1. **Light there is the fruit of faith and deeds:** it runs before the believers on the Day of Resurrection, and no one can take a flame of it from its owner.
2. **Jannah is a home of light and clarity:** it has neither sun nor bitter cold, the faces of its people shine, and the whiteness of its delights is a radiant whiteness with nothing murky in it.
3. **Light has a path in this world:** whoever walks through the darkness to the mosques is given the glad tidings of complete light.

The alternation of morning and evening in Jannah is the subject of a later lesson.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-57-12-13" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Their Light Running Before Them"

> **{يَوْمَ تَرَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ يَسْعَىٰ نُورُهُمْ بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَبِأَيْمَانِهِمْ بُشْرَاكُمُ الْيَوْمَ جَنَّاتٌ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ * يَوْمَ يَقُولُ الْمُنَافِقُونَ وَالْمُنَافِقَاتُ لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا انْظُرُونَا نَقْتَبِسْ مِنْ نُورِكُمْ قِيلَ ارْجِعُوا وَرَاءَكُمْ فَالْتَمِسُوا نُورًا فَضُرِبَ بَيْنَهُمْ بِسُورٍ لَهُ بَابٌ بَاطِنُهُ فِيهِ الرَّحْمَةُ وَظَاهِرُهُ مِنْ قِبَلِهِ الْعَذَابُ}** [الحديد: ١٢-١٣][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"On the Day you see the believing men and the believing women, their light running before them and on their right: 'Your glad tidings today: gardens beneath which rivers flow, where you will abide forever.' That is the supreme triumph. On the Day the hypocrite men and the hypocrite women will say to those who believed, 'Wait for us, so that we may take a flame from your light!' It will be said, 'Go back behind you and seek out a light!' Then a wall will be raised between them, with a gate in it: its inner side holds mercy, and its outer side faces the punishment."** (al-Hadid 57:12-13)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says that on the Day of Resurrection, when "the sun is wrapped up, the moon is eclipsed, people are left in darkness, and the Bridge is set up," you will see the believers walking by their light in that terrifying station, "each according to the measure of his faith," and then they are given the glad tidings of gardens. He explains `Wait for us` as "Give us time, so that we may get some of your light to walk by," and says of `Go back behind you and seek out a light`: that is, if such a thing were possible, "when in fact it is not possible."[^3] Ibn Kathir says their light runs before them "according to their deeds," and reports from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him: "They cross the Bridge according to their deeds. Some have light like a mountain, some have light like a palm tree, some have light like a man standing upright, and the least of them in light is one whose light is on his big toe, flaring up one moment and going out the next." This is the statement of a Companion, narrated by Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir. Ibn Kathir also reports from al-Hasan and Qatadah that the wall is "a wall between Jannah and the Fire," and he explains its inner side, which holds mercy, as "Jannah and all that is in it."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

There are two lights in this scene: a light that carries its owner toward the glad tidings, and a light that others beg to borrow and are refused. Light on the Day of Resurrection is not something that can be bought or lent; it is the sum of the faith and deeds a servant has carried with him. That is why the believers pray, in Surah at-Tahrim, `Our Lord, perfect our light for us`.[^1]

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-13" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will See There Neither Sun nor Bitter Cold"

> **{مُتَّكِئِينَ فِيهَا عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ ۖ لَا يَرَوْنَ فِيهَا شَمْسًا وَلَا زَمْهَرِيرًا}** [الإنسان: ١٣][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Reclining there on raised couches, they will see there neither sun nor bitter cold."** (al-Insan 76:13)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "They have no troubling heat and no painful cold; rather, it is a single, constant, everlasting climate." As-Sa'di says they see there no sun "whose heat would harm them," and no *zamharir*, "that is, severe cold"; "rather, all their hours are spent in deep, abiding shade." Al-Ashqar quotes Ibn Taymiyyah as saying: "Jannah has neither sun nor moon, neither night nor day; but morning and evening are known there by a light that appears from the direction of the Throne."[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah denies the sun; it does not deny light. The light of Jannah is not the light of a sun that burns and then sets, but a light that neither harms nor fades. Anything beyond this in describing it belongs to the unseen, and there we stop at what has been established.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3327" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "In the Form of the Moon on the Night It Is Full"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«إِنَّ أَوَّلَ زُمْرَةٍ يَدْخُلُونَ الْجَنَّةَ عَلَى صُورَةِ الْقَمَرِ لَيْلَةَ الْبَدْرِ، ثُمَّ الَّذِينَ يَلُونَهُمْ عَلَى أَشَدِّ كَوْكَبٍ دُرِّيٍّ فِي السَّمَاءِ إِضَاءَةً، [...]»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The first group to enter Jannah will be in the form of the moon on the night it is full; then those who come after them will be like the most brilliantly shining star in the sky, [...]"**[^6]

The rest of the hadith describes the purity of their bodies, the fragrance of their scent, their spouses, and that they will have the stature of their father Adam, sixty cubits tall; it is omitted here because it does not bear on the subject of light.

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Zumrah* means a group or company. *Al-kawkab ad-durri* is an intensely brilliant star, named, it seems, after the pearl (*durr*) for its clarity. The hadith is agreed upon; Muslim also narrates it with similar wording.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Light in Jannah has another dwelling besides the place itself: the faces of its people. And it is a light of many degrees. The forerunners shine like the moon at its fullest, and those after them like the brightest of stars, which agrees with the ayah of al-Hadid that light is in proportion to faith and deeds. This radiance is promised to believers of every color and every nation; the color of a person's skin has no part in it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-37-45-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "White, a Delight to Those Who Drink"

> **{يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ * بَيْضَاءَ لَذَّةٍ لِلشَّارِبِينَ * لَا فِيهَا غَوْلٌ وَلَا هُمْ عَنْهَا يُنْزَفُونَ}** [الصافات: ٤٥-٤٧][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"A cup from a flowing spring will be passed around among them, white, a delight to those who drink, with no harm lurking in it, nor will they be robbed of their senses by it."** (as-Saffat 37:45-47)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir reports from Zayd ibn Aslam that it is "a flowing white wine," and then says: "that is, its color is radiant, lovely and splendid," unlike the drinks of this world, "with their redness, blackness, yellowness or cloudiness"; and on `a delight to those who drink` he says: "its taste is as fine as its color." As-Sa'di says that "in its color it is white, the most beautiful of colors."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

Whiteness in the description of Jannah's delights is a whiteness of radiance, clarity and freedom from anything murky. The lesson on the ground of Jannah has already described its soil as white too. This whiteness has nothing to do with the skin colors of human beings; honor with Allah lies in faith and deeds.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="abudawud-561" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Give Glad Tidings to Those Who Walk Through the Darkness"

> عَنْ بُرَيْدَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«بَشِّرِ الْمَشَّائِينَ فِي الظُّلَمِ إِلَى الْمَسَاجِدِ بِالنُّورِ التَّامِّ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Buraydah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Give those who walk often through the darkness to the mosques the glad tidings of complete light on the Day of Resurrection."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-mashsha'un* are those who walk often and habitually, and *az-zulam* is the plural of *zulmah*, darkness, which is deepest at the times of the 'Isha' and Fajr prayers. The reward matches the deed: they walked through the darkness of this world, so they are given complete light in the darkness of the Day of Resurrection.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is the lesson's practical bridge: steps taken in the dark on the way to prayer become light carried on the Day when light is sought and cannot be borrowed.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of al-Hadid shows that light on the Day of Resurrection is in proportion to faith and deeds; the ayah of al-Insan describes a home with neither sun nor bitter cold; the hadith of al-Bukhari places radiance in the faces of its people, differing with their ranks; the ayah of as-Saffat describes the whiteness of its delights; and the hadith of Abu Dawud points to a daily deed whose doer is given the glad tidings of complete light. The lesson does not rely on the hadith "Allah created Jannah white," because it is very weak, nor on the hadith of "a sparkling light" in Ibn Majah, because it is weak.[^10]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why was the hypocrites' request, `Wait for us, so that we may take a flame from your light`, refused? And what does that tell us about the nature of light on the Day of Resurrection?
2. How do you bring together Ibn Mas'ud's statement on the differing degrees of light and the hadith of the moon and the brilliant star?
3. What does the ayah of al-Insan deny, and what does it leave undenied?
4. How would you explain the whiteness in the description of Jannah's delights so that no one takes it to mean that one skin color is favored over another?
5. Which part of your faith do you feel is borrowed from your surroundings? And what step would turn it into a light you carry yourself?

### Activity: Borrowed Light or Carried Light?

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.038.activity.borrowed-or-carried-light" -->

Draw two columns: "Borrowed light" and "Carried light." In the first, write what lights up your faith from outside you: friends, a nearby mosque, classes, special seasons. In the second, write what you carry yourself even if all of that disappeared: prayer on time, a steady daily portion of worship, honesty that no one sees. Mark one item in the first column that you fear would go out if its source were gone. Then choose one deed in the hours of darkness to keep up for seven days: walking to 'Isha' or Fajr at the mosque if you can, or two rak'ahs before Fajr. Each morning, write a single line: What color was the sky as it whitened at dawn? And what did you carry with you out of the night?

### An Imaginative Teaching Story: Sarah and the Dark Street

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.038.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imaginative teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

Early one winter morning, on a day with no school, Sarah's dad tapped softly on her door. "Sarah, I'm walking to the mosque for Fajr. Do you want to come with me?"

Sarah looked out the window. The street was dark, and the trees looked like big black shadows. She pulled her blanket up tight. "I'm scared of the dark, Dad."

Her dad gave her a little flashlight. "We'll walk together, and I'll hold your hand the whole way." Sarah thought about it for a moment. Then she put on her red coat and went outside.

Her steps were small, and the flashlight drew a circle of light on the sidewalk. Whenever she felt scared, she squeezed her dad's hand, and he squeezed hers back. Then she saw the mosque far away, with its windows glowing. Sarah prayed right next to her dad, and she felt warm inside.

On the way home she looked up, and the sky was changing! It turned light blue, and then white over on the side where the sun comes up. Sarah switched off her flashlight. "Look, Dad! We don't need it anymore!"

Her dad smiled. "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that people who walk to the mosque in the dark will get happy news: a full, complete light on the Day of Resurrection."[^8]

"Is Jannah like this sky?" Sarah asked. "The light of Jannah is more beautiful than anything we've ever seen," her dad said, "and there's no sun there to burn us. We don't know exactly what it looks like, but Allah has promised it to His servants."

"Then I want to have a light too," said Sarah. And the next week, she woke up before her dad even knocked on the door.

### From the Noble Qur'an: The Believers' Light Walks Ahead of Them

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="quranic" source_id="quran-57-12-13" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-57-12-13" audience="4-7" -->

**This is true. It comes from the Noble Qur'an, and it is not a made-up story.**

In Surah al-Hadid, Allah tells us that on the Day of Resurrection, the believing men and women will have their light walking ahead of them and on their right side. And they will be told: "Here is happy news for you: gardens with rivers flowing beneath them!"

Some people only pretended to believe, but they didn't believe in their hearts. They will say to the believers, "Wait for us! Let us take some of your light!" And they will be told, "Go back behind you and look for a light." Then a wall with a gate will be put between them, and inside it is Allah's mercy.[^1]

The scholars say that everyone's light will be as big as their faith and their good deeds, so nobody can take someone else's light.[^3]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every light we see in this world comes from a source that can run out: the sun sets, and batteries die. But about Jannah, Allah says: `they will see there neither sun nor bitter cold`,[^4] which means no heat that hurts and no cold that stings. Even so, it is a home full of light, and the faces of the very first people to enter it are like the moon on the night it is full.[^6] Most amazing of all, on the Day of Resurrection every believer will have a light of his or her own, as bright as their faith and deeds, and nobody can lend it to anyone else.

### An Imaginative Teaching Story: Yaseen and Omar's Lamp

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.038.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imaginative teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

It was Science Week, and Ms. Huda asked each pair of students to build a small lamp: a battery, some wires, a switch and a little bulb. "On presentation day," she said, "I'm going to ask each of you to explain the circuit on your own."

Yaseen, a fifth grader, loved electricity and picked it up fast. His partner Omar got flustered every time he touched a wire. On the first day, Yaseen thought, "I'll just build it myself in fifteen minutes, and we'll both get a perfect score."

But then he remembered what Ms. Huda had said: "Each of you explains it on your own." He pictured Omar standing in front of the class in silence, the lamp glowing in his hands, with no idea why it was glowing.

So he decided to slow down. He handed Omar the wires. "You put it together, and I'll explain as we go." Omar got it wrong twice, and they ran out of time. A classmate laughed at them: "Every team's done except you two!" On the second day the bulb flickered and went out. Omar searched all by himself, found a loose wire, tightened it, and the lamp lit up. "I found it!" Omar shouted.

On presentation day, Omar explained the circuit with confidence, and he didn't need Yaseen's help at all.

That night, Yaseen's father recited from Surah al-Hadid: `Wait for us, so that we may take a flame from your light`,[^1] and explained that on the Day of Resurrection, light can't be borrowed. "So the best thing I can do for my friend," said Yaseen, "is help him make his own light, not lend him mine!"

Then his father said, "And you need your own light too. Will you walk with me to 'Isha' prayer?" On the dark road to the mosque, Yaseen remembered the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: **"Give those who walk often through the darkness to the mosques the glad tidings of complete light on the Day of Resurrection."**[^8]

### From the Noble Qur'an: A Light That Can't Be Borrowed

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="quranic" source_id="quran-57-12-13" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-57-12-13" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an, in Surah al-Hadid. It is not a made-up story.**

Allah describes a scene from the Day of Resurrection: the believing men and women, their light running before them and on their right, and they are told: `Your glad tidings today: gardens beneath which rivers flow, where you will abide forever`.

Then the hypocrites come, the people who showed faith on the outside and hid disbelief inside, and they say to the believers: "Wait for us, so we can take some of your light." They are told: "Go back behind you and look for a light." Then a wall with a gate is raised between them: on the believers' side is mercy, and on the other side is punishment.[^1]

As-Sa'di said that the believers walk by their light, "each according to the measure of his faith," and that `Go back behind you` means that getting light at that point is impossible.[^3] Light is made in this world, through faith and deeds, and it shows itself there.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-57-12-13" -->

- **`Running (yas'a)`** — moving swiftly ahead of them and lighting up the way.
- **`Take a flame (naqtabis)`** — to take a piece of the light, the way a flame is lit from a fire.
- **`Bitter cold (zamharir)`** — severe, painful cold.[^5]
- **`The darkness (az-zulam)`** — the plural of *zulmah*, darkness; here it means the dark road at night and before dawn.

### The Du'a for Light

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.038.dua.light-in-heart-and-all-sides" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-6316" attribution="prophetic" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to make this du'a when he got up at night and when he went out to prayer, and you can make it before you sleep:

> **اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ فِي قَلْبِي نُورًا، وَفِي بَصَرِي نُورًا، وَفِي سَمْعِي نُورًا، وَعَنْ يَمِينِي نُورًا، وَعَنْ يَسَارِي نُورًا، وَفَوْقِي نُورًا، وَتَحْتِي نُورًا، وَأَمَامِي نُورًا، وَخَلْفِي نُورًا، وَاجْعَلْ لِي نُورًا.**[^9]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a world of images, you can "glow up" with one tap: a filter that lightens your skin, smooths your face and turns up the brightness. But that is borrowed light, and it vanishes the moment the screen goes dark. The texts speak of a real radiance: the faces of the first to enter Jannah, "in the form of the moon on the night it is full,"[^6] and a light running before the believers on the Day of Resurrection in proportion to their faith and deeds.[^3] And when the texts describe the delights of Jannah as white, that is a whiteness of purity and radiance, not a ranking of human beings by the color of their skin.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imaginative Teaching Story: The Glow Filter

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.038.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imaginative teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

After the class field trip, the group chat filled up with photos. Noor, fifteen, posted hers with the "Glow" filter everyone was using: lighter skin, a smoother face, lighting straight out of a magazine. The likes poured in, and something warm spread through her.

Then someone started a "Glow Ranking," dropping a number under every photo. Under the photo of Habiba, a classmate with dark skin who had posted hers without a filter, someone wrote: "Try the white filter, maybe you'll make the ranking." A few people laughed with emojis. Everyone else went quiet.

Noor looked at her own photo. It didn't look like the face she saw in the mirror. She remembered yesterday's class at the mosque, when the teacher read the hadith of the full moon and said, "This radiance is promised to the people of Jannah of every color, and what it's measured by is faith and deeds." Then the teacher recited the ayah of al-Hadid: `their light running before them and on their right`.[^1]

Noor typed something and deleted it three times. She knew that whatever she said would make her "the too-religious one." In the end she deleted her filtered photo, posted an ordinary one from the trip, and wrote a single line under it: "Our faces aren't a competition. And Habiba is prettier than all of us when she laughs." Then she sent Habiba a private message: "Sorry I stayed quiet the first time."

Her friend Reem replied, "You're overreacting, it was a joke." Two people left the group. But the ranking stopped, and Habiba wrote to her privately: "Thank you. I thought nobody even noticed."

That night, Noor didn't post a word about what she'd done anywhere. She opened her journal and wrote: "Every light a screen gives you is borrowed light. I want the light no one can take a flame from." Then she set her alarm for the Fajr adhan.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.authenticated-story" kind="authenticated_story" -->

### From the Noble Qur'an: The Day Light Is Begged For and Never Lent

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="quranic" source_id="quran-57-12-13" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-57-12-13" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an, in Surah al-Hadid (57:12-13). It is not an imagined scene.**

Allah describes the believing men and women on the Day of Resurrection, their light running before them and on their right, and then the glad tidings reach them: `Your glad tidings today: gardens beneath which rivers flow, where you will abide forever. That is the supreme triumph.` And the hypocrite men and women, who had lived among the believers by their outward appearance, say, `Wait for us, so that we may take a flame from your light`. They are told, `Go back behind you and seek out a light`. Then a wall is raised between them with a gate in it: its inner side holds mercy, and its outer side faces the punishment.[^1]

As-Sa'di said that the meaning of this reply is that obtaining light at that point is "not possible."[^3] Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, that people cross the Bridge according to their deeds: some have light like a mountain, some have light like a palm tree, and the least of them is one whose light is on his big toe, flaring up one moment and going out the next.[^2]

Think about it: the hypocrites were close to the light. They saw it and walked right beside it, but it was never theirs. Being close to good people is no substitute for having a light of your own to carry.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-57-12-13" -->

- **`Take a flame (naqtabis)`** — to take part of someone else's light to walk by; the request was refused because on that Day light cannot be lent.
- **`The wall (sur)`** — a wall raised on the Day of Resurrection; al-Hasan and Qatadah said it is a wall between Jannah and the Fire.[^2]
- **`A brilliant star (kawkab durri)`** — an intensely shining star, named, it seems, after the pearl (*durr*) for its clarity.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the likes first make Noor feel warm, and then why did her own photo start to bother her?
2. Was everyone else's silence really neutral? And what did speaking up cost Noor?
3. Why didn't Noor post anything about the stand she took? How does that connect to the light no one can take a flame from?
4. What is the difference between the whiteness of Jannah's delights, as the commentators explained it, and a "white filter"?
5. What in your religious life might be "being close to the light" rather than "a light you carry"?

### Activity: Borrowed Light or Carried Light?

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.038.activity.borrowed-or-carried-light" -->

Under "Borrowed light," list everything that makes you look or feel bright because of someone or something else: likes, religious friends, a good photo, a religious occasion. Under "Carried light," list what you do even when no one sees it. Pick one item from the first column that you fear would go out if it disappeared. ### The Du'a for Light

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.038.dua.light-in-heart-and-all-sides" provenance="sunnah" source_id="bukhari-6316" attribution="prophetic" -->

Ibn Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, narrated this du'a after spending a night in the house of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and hearing him make it; in one narration in Muslim, he said it as he was going out to prayer:

> **اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ فِي قَلْبِي نُورًا، وَفِي بَصَرِي نُورًا، وَفِي سَمْعِي نُورًا، وَعَنْ يَمِينِي نُورًا، وَعَنْ يَسَارِي نُورًا، وَفَوْقِي نُورًا، وَتَحْتِي نُورًا، وَأَمَامِي نُورًا، وَخَلْفِي نُورًا، وَاجْعَلْ لِي نُورًا.**[^9]
>
> What it means: O Allah, place light in my heart, my sight and my hearing; let light surround me on my right and my left, above me and below me, before me and behind me; and grant me a light that is mine alone.[^9]

Notice that the du'a begins with the heart before the eyes: the light you are asking for is an inner light before it is ever a glow in a photo.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites the two ayat of al-Hadid (57:12-13) and explains their scene through the words of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir; shows that the ayah of al-Insan (76:13) denies the sun, not light; narrates al-Bukhari 3327 and connects the differing degrees of radiance to the differing ranks; explains the meaning of whiteness in the ayah of as-Saffat (37:46) without projecting it onto human skin colors; and identifies a deed in the hours of darkness to keep up for seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a flashlight and a switch to turn off the room lights; a board divided into two columns, "Borrowed light" and "Carried light"; a seven-day tracking card for each learner.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on al-Hadid (57:12-13), al-Insan (76:13) and as-Saffat (37:46); al-Bukhari 3327 together with Muslim 2834; and Abu Dawud 561 together with at-Tirmidhi's comment on it (223); and reads chapter thirty-five of Ibn al-Qayyim's *Hadi al-Arwah* to become familiar with the weak reports it contains.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher switches off the lights for a moment and turns on the flashlight, then asks: "If this flashlight went out, what light would still be with you?" Listen to three answers without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the two ayat of al-Hadid in full, then as-Sa'di's words on the darkness and the Bridge and his explanation of `Wait for us`, then Ibn Mas'ud's statement, pointing out that it is the statement of a Companion reported by Ibn Kathir. Then read the ayah of al-Insan with the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di and that of Ibn Taymiyyah; then al-Bukhari 3327, explaining the reason for the marked omission; then the ayah of as-Saffat with Zayd ibn Aslam's statement and Ibn Kathir's explanation; then the hadith of Abu Dawud. Learners record on the board what each text shows.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion of two questions: Why can light not be lent on the Day of Resurrection? And how do we speak about the whiteness of Jannah's delights in a way that wounds no one because of the color of their skin? Then read the closing paragraph, "How Do These Texts Fit Together?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners carry out the activity "Borrowed Light or Carried Light?" as described in the activity unit, and each writes down the chosen deed and its time on the tracking card.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes three sentences: as-Sa'di's explanation of `Go back behind you and seek out a light`; what the ayah of al-Insan denies and what it does not; and the hadith of Abu Dawud from memory. The teacher closes with the du'a for light and invites anyone who wishes to share their dawn lines after a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the two ayat of al-Hadid and the hadith of Abu Dawud are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare the narration of al-Bukhari 3327 with narration 3254 and with Muslim 2834, and to sort what is established and what is not in chapter thirty-five of *Hadi al-Arwah*.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the light of Jannah beyond what has been established, and do not liken it definitively to any particular light. Do not rely on the hadith "Allah created Jannah white, and the dress Allah loves most is white," which is very weak because it hinges on Hisham ibn Ziyad, who is abandoned (*matruk*) as a narrator; nor on the hadith of Usamah, "It is, by the Lord of the Ka'bah, a sparkling light," in Ibn Majah (4332), which is weak; nor on the report from Ibn Abbas likening its light to the hour before sunrise; nor on the long hadith of Laqit ibn Amir.[^10] Rule out any link between the whiteness of Jannah's delights and a person's skin color. Leave the alternation of morning and evening for the lesson on daytime in Jannah, and do not go into detail about the terrors of the Bridge.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Jannah has no sun that burns and no cold that hurts, and that it is full of beautiful light; that on the Day of Resurrection the believer's light walks ahead of him; names a deed that brings light (praying on time); and repeats part of the du'a for light.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small flashlight; two boxes labeled "Light for my eyes" and "Light for my heart" with clear drawings; picture cards (a lamp, a candle, the sun, a person praying, a mushaf, a helping hand); paper and blue and white crayons.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling Sarah's story in a calm voice and sets up the room so the light can be dimmed without making it completely dark.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver dims the light, turns on the flashlight, and asks: "When do we need a flashlight? And when don't we need it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell Sarah's story, then say, "And this next one is true, from the Qur'an," and calmly retell the scene from Surah al-Hadid. Read the ayah `they will see there neither sun nor bitter cold` and explain it with gestures: no heat, no cold.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver repeats with the child: "Light in the heart comes from prayer and telling the truth," "On the Day of Resurrection, everyone has their own light," "The light of Jannah is beautiful and never goes away."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The child does the two-box activity as described in the activity unit, agrees with the caregiver to pray 'Isha' or Fajr with the family, and draws the dawn sky the next morning.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "Is there a sun in Jannah that burns?" and "What do we do so that we have a light?" Then read the du'a for light, and the child repeats its opening: «اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ فِي قَلْبِي نُورًا» ("O Allah, place light in my heart").

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, use only three cards and repeat just the first line of the du'a. Older children point in each direction during the du'a: right, left, up, down, front, back.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never turn the lights off completely with a child who is afraid of the dark. Do not go into detail about punishment or the terrors of the Day of Resurrection; mention the wall and the mercy calmly. Do not force the child to wake up for Fajr; praying 'Isha' on time is enough. Never, in any way, link the color of a face or of skin with good or bad.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the scene from Surah al-Hadid and explains why light cannot be borrowed on the Day of Resurrection; explains the four terms; recalls the hadith of Abu Dawud; distinguishes between lending a friend your light and helping him make his own; and does a deed in the dark three times in one week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a simple electric circuit (battery, two wires, small bulb, switch) for each group; the two-column sheet; term cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher tests the circuits in advance and deliberately loosens a wire in one of them for the students to discover.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Give each group a circuit and ask them to light the bulb without help. Then ask: "Who lit their bulb on their own? And who had someone else help them?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Insan (76:13) and explain *zamharir*; then retell the scene from al-Hadid while students read `Wait for us, so that we may take a flame from your light`; then as-Sa'di's statement; then the hadith of Abu Dawud.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Yaseen's story. The teacher pauses at his decision to slow down: "What would have happened if he had built it by himself?" Then ask: "How can you help a friend make his own light in prayer or in his studies?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students do the two-column activity as described in the activity unit, and each writes down the deed chosen and the name of the adult who will go with them if they walk to the mosque.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student explains in one sentence why the hypocrites' request was refused and recalls the hadith of Abu Dawud from memory; then read the du'a for light. After a week, students share their dawn sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, give the two columns with ready-made examples to choose from. Advanced students write a paragraph comparing a lamp's light with the light of the Day of Resurrection in three differences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Walking to the mosque at night or at dawn must be with a parent or with their permission, and praying on time at home also completes the activity. No classmate is ever to be called a "hypocrite" in the discussion; judging hearts belongs to Allah. Do not go into detail about the terrors of the Bridge.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the difference between the manufactured glow of photos and the light promised in the texts; explains the two ayat of al-Hadid and Ibn Mas'ud's statement on the differing degrees of light; explains the whiteness of Jannah's delights as the commentators understood it while rejecting any ranking by skin color; and privately keeps up a deed in the hours of darkness for seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; two photos of the same landscape, one of them edited with an exaggerated filter (no human faces); the two-column sheet; a private tracking card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares the two photos, reviews as-Sa'di's explanation of `Go back behind you` and Zayd ibn Aslam's statement with Ibn Kathir's explanation of the whiteness, and is ready for the possibility that someone in the group has been on the receiving end of comments about their skin color.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the two photos and ask: "Which one is more beautiful? Which one is real? And why are we sometimes drawn to the edited one?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the whole scene from al-Hadid, then as-Sa'di's statement and Ibn Mas'ud's statement, then the hadith of the moon and the brilliant star, then the ayah of as-Saffat with Zayd ibn Aslam's statement and Ibn Kathir's explanation, and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Noor's story, and have groups discuss: "What if Noor had stayed silent?" and "Why did she write one line instead of a speech?" Then ask: "What does it mean that the hypocrites were close to the light but had no light of their own?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students do the two-column activity in silence as described in the activity unit, and each chooses a night-time deed and writes it on their private card.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student completes, on an unnamed card: "The difference between a filter's glow and the light no one can take a flame from is..." Then read the du'a for light, pointing out that it begins with the heart.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to compare Ibn Mas'ud's statement with the ayah of at-Tahrim, `Our Lord, perfect our light for us`.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not display students' photos, and do not comment on anyone's features or skin color. Anyone showing signs of emotional harm from bullying or body-image struggles should be gently drawn into a one-on-one conversation and referred to a qualified professional. Explicitly reject any reading that turns the whiteness in the texts into a racial privilege. Do not judge any specific person to be a hypocrite.
