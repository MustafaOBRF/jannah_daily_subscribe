# Believing Spouses and Believing Women in Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the question of Umm Salamah, may Allah be pleased with her, "Messenger of Allah, I do not hear Allah mention women in connection with the hijrah," and the revelation of the ayah that answered it: "I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female; you are of one another" (Al 'Imran 3:195).

- Use the ayah of ar-Ra'd (13:23-24) to show that Allah reunites the believer with those of his spouses, parents and descendants who were righteous, that righteousness is the condition of this reunion, and that, as as-Sa'di explains, "spouses" here includes "the husband or the wife."

- Explain from the ayah of al-Hijr (15:47) that the hearts of the people of Jannah are cleansed of rancor and envy, and show that "in it you will have whatever your souls desire" (Fussilat 41:31) is addressed to men and women alike.

- Distinguish what is established by text from what is scholarly reasoning: the statement of Hudhayfah, attributed to him rather than to the Prophet, that a woman belongs to the last of her husbands; the disagreement over the authenticity of the version attributed to the Prophet; and Ibn 'Uthaymin's answer about the woman who never married, or whose husband does not enter Jannah.

- Carry out the "Pact of Help" activity: an agreement between two family members on one good deed that each will help the other with for a set period, kept in a shared log and sealed with a du'a each makes for the other.

- Learn by heart a lesson-authored bedtime du'a asking Allah not to let our deeds be lost, to set right our hearts and our families, and to gather us in the Gardens of 'Adn with the righteous of our family.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Believing women ask honest questions about Jannah. Will I be with my husband? What if he was not a righteous man, or I never married, or I married more than once? Is the bliss of Jannah richer for men? These are legitimate questions, and the first person to ask one of their kind was a woman from among the finest of the female Companions; the answer came to her as Qur'an that is recited to this day. This lesson gathers the established texts on believing spouses and on the women of Jannah, and separates what revelation states from what the scholars have reasoned their way to. The detailed treatment of the hur al-'ayn and of marriage in Jannah belongs to two later lessons.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-3-195" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Will Not Let the Deed of Any Doer Among You Be Lost, Whether Male or Female"

> **{فَاسْتَجَابَ لَهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ أَنِّي لَا أُضِيعُ عَمَلَ عَامِلٍ مِنْكُمْ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنْثَى ۖ بَعْضُكُمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ ۖ فَالَّذِينَ هَاجَرُوا وَأُخْرِجُوا مِنْ دِيَارِهِمْ وَأُوذُوا فِي سَبِيلِي وَقَاتَلُوا وَقُتِلُوا لَأُكَفِّرَنَّ عَنْهُمْ سَيِّئَاتِهِمْ وَلَأُدْخِلَنَّهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ثَوَابًا مِنْ عِنْدِ اللَّهِ ۗ وَاللَّهُ عِنْدَهُ حُسْنُ الثَّوَابِ}** [آل عمران: ١٩٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And their Lord answered them: 'I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female; you are of one another. So those who emigrated, and were driven out of their homes, and were harmed in My way, and fought and were killed, I will surely wipe away their misdeeds and admit them into gardens beneath which rivers flow, as a reward from Allah. And with Allah is the finest reward.'"** (Al 'Imran 3:195)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On "I will not let the deed of any doer be lost," Ibn Kathir says: "No doer's deed is lost with Him; rather, He repays every doer in full measure for his deed, whether male or female." And on "you are of one another": "All of you are equal in My reward." As-Sa'di says: "All of them will meet the reward of their deeds, complete and in full," and explains "you are of one another" as: "You are all on an equal footing in reward and punishment."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah comes at the close of the supplication of the people of understanding, and the very first part of the answer is that men and women are equal in having their deeds preserved. Only then does it name the reward of those who emigrated and were harmed in Allah's way: their misdeeds wiped away, and entry into the gardens.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-3023" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### Umm Salamah's Question

> عَنْ أُمِّ سَلَمَةَ رضي الله عنها قَالَتْ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، لَا أَسْمَعُ اللَّهَ ذَكَرَ النِّسَاءَ فِي الْهِجْرَةِ. فَأَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى: {أَنِّي لَا أُضِيعُ عَمَلَ عَامِلٍ مِنْكُمْ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنْثَى ۖ بَعْضُكُمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ}.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Umm Salamah, may Allah be pleased with her, said: "Messenger of Allah, I do not hear Allah mention women in connection with the hijrah." So Allah the Exalted revealed: **"I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female; you are of one another."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites the report through Sa'id ibn Mansur with the wording: "We do not hear Allah mention women in connection with the hijrah at all." He also records what the Ansar said of Umm Salamah, "She was the first woman traveler to arrive among us," and that al-Hakim graded the report authentic "according to the criteria of al-Bukhari."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

Umm Salamah asked about the place of women in a momentous act of faith. She was not rebuked for asking; the answer came down as revelation. An honest question about justice and reward is a door to knowledge, not a sign of doubt.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-13-23-24" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Whoever Was Righteous Among Their Fathers, Their Spouses and Their Descendants"

> **{جَنَّاتُ عَدْنٍ يَدْخُلُونَهَا وَمَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ آبَائِهِمْ وَأَزْوَاجِهِمْ وَذُرِّيَّاتِهِمْ ۖ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنْ كُلِّ بَابٍ ۝ سَلَامٌ عَلَيْكُمْ بِمَا صَبَرْتُمْ ۚ فَنِعْمَ عُقْبَى الدَّارِ}** [الرعد: ٢٣-٢٤][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Gardens of 'Adn, which they will enter, together with whoever was righteous among their fathers, their spouses and their descendants. And the angels will come in to them from every gate: 'Peace be upon you for what you patiently endured. How excellent is the final home!'"** (ar-Ra'd 13:23-24)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "He brings them together there with their loved ones among their fathers, their families and their children, those of the believers who are fit to enter Jannah, so that their eyes may be gladdened by them." As-Sa'di says that "part of the completeness of their bliss and the delight of their eyes" is that they enter it together with those who were righteous among their fathers, "male and female," and that "their spouses" means "the husband or the wife," and likewise their peers and companions.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The reunion is a generous promise, and its condition is "whoever was righteous." Believing spouses will be together, but kinship alone does not avail anyone who was not fit to enter Jannah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-15-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And We Will Have Removed Whatever Rancor Is in Their Breasts"

> **{وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَانًا عَلَى سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ}** [الحجر: ٤٧][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We will have removed whatever rancor is in their breasts: brothers, on couches, facing one another."** (al-Hijr 15:47)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says: "So their hearts remain sound, free of all malice and envy, clear toward one another and full of love," and that their facing one another points to "their visiting each other, their gathering together and their fine manners toward one another."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

What some people dread, whether jealousy, a sense of being crowded, or an old grievance between family members, does not enter Jannah. Hearts there are clear toward one another, and nothing clouds them.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-41-31-32" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "In It You Will Have Whatever Your Souls Desire"

> **{نَحْنُ أَوْلِيَاؤُكُمْ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ ۖ وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِي أَنْفُسُكُمْ وَلَكُمْ فِيهَا مَا تَدَّعُونَ ۝ نُزُلًا مِنْ غَفُورٍ رَحِيمٍ}** [فصلت: ٣١-٣٢][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"We are your protecting friends in the life of this world and in the Hereafter. In it you will have whatever your souls desire, and in it you will have whatever you ask for: a welcome from One who is Ever-Forgiving, Most Merciful."** (Fussilat 41:31-32)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explains "whatever you ask for" as: "Whatever you request, you will find, and it will be set before you." As-Sa'di says: "That is, whatever you ask for of all that your will reaches toward."[^5] Ibn 'Uthaymin was asked about a woman of the people of Jannah who never married, or whose husband does not enter Jannah. He said: "The answer is taken from the general meaning of His words, 'In it you will have whatever your souls desire'...," and then: "If she desires to marry, she must have what she desires, because of the general meaning of these ayat," noting that he knew of "no specific text on this question." He was also asked why wives are mentioned for men while nothing is said of husbands for women. He replied that it is the man who seeks and desires, "but that does not mean they have no husbands."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

The address "you will have" is to men and women alike. In Jannah, a believing woman is not asked what she is willing to settle for; she is given what she desires.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="athar-hudhayfah-last-husband" kind="athar" mode="canonical" -->

### The Statement of Hudhayfah: A Woman Belongs to the Last of Her Husbands

> قَالَ حُذَيْفَةُ رضي الله عنه لِامْرَأَتِهِ: «إِنْ أَرَدْتِ أَنْ تَكُونِي زَوْجَتِي فِي الْجَنَّةِ فَلَا تَزَوَّجِي بَعْدِي، فَإِنَّ الْمَرْأَةَ لِآخِرِ أَزْوَاجِهَا»، وَلِذَلِكَ حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ تَعَالَى عَلَى أَزْوَاجِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ أَنْ يَتَزَوَّجْنَ بَعْدَهُ.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Hudhayfah, may Allah be pleased with him, said to his wife: "If you want to be my wife in Jannah, do not marry after me, for a woman belongs to the last of her husbands." And that is why Allah the Exalted forbade the wives of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, to marry after him.[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

These are the words of a Companion, a *mawquf* report, not a hadith traced back to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. A version attributed to the Prophet has been narrated from Abu ad-Darda' and from Aishah with the wording "A woman belongs to the last of her husbands," and the hadith scholars disagree about it: al-Albani judged it strong through the combination of its chains, while as-Suyuti and al-'Iraqi judged it weak.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

We give this statement exactly the weight it carries: it is the strongest thing said on the question, but it is not a definitive text. A woman's deepest reassurance comes from the definitive texts that precede it: her deeds are not lost, no rancor remains in her heart, and she will have whatever she desires.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

Three things are established by text: a woman's deeds are preserved just as a man's are; righteous spouses are brought together in the Gardens of 'Adn; and hearts are cleansed of rancor, so there is no hurtful jealousy and no envy. A fourth is general: every believing man and woman will have whatever they desire. Then come questions of scholarly reasoning, which are referred back to these foundations. For a woman who married more than once, the strongest evidence is Hudhayfah's statement; for a woman who never married, or whose husband does not enter Jannah, the answer lies in the general promise "whatever your souls desire." And the practical question for each of us is this: how can I help my family today toward the righteousness that Allah has made the condition of being together?

### Activity: The Pact of Help

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.066.activity.help-each-other-to-the-garden" -->

Choose one partner from your family: your husband or wife, one of your parents, or a brother or sister. Agree together on one good deed for which each of you needs the other's help, such as waking each other for Fajr, reviewing a surah, or a shared weekly act of charity. Write your "Pact of Help" in three lines: the deed, what each of you will do to help the other, and the length of the pact (ten days). Each day, record in a shared log "Help I gave" and "Help I received," with no reproach for the days that fall short. On the tenth day, each of you writes a du'a for the other, by name, and the other reads aloud the ayah: "and whoever was righteous among their fathers, their spouses and their descendants."

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="3.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

At home, we love each other: Mom and Dad, Grandma and Grandpa, our brothers and sisters. Sometimes we get a little cross or squabble a bit, and then we make up.

In Jannah, Allah brings the good people of a family together. And their hearts there are perfectly clean: no anger, no jealousy, and no hurt feelings, ever. Allah gives everyone in Jannah, man or woman, boy or girl, everything that makes them completely happy.[^3][^4][^5]

### From the Qur'an: Umm Salamah's Question

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-3-195" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story about an ayah of the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

Umm Salamah, may Allah be pleased with her, was a believing woman. She left Makkah and traveled all the way to Madinah to live with the Muslims. And the people of Madinah used to say about her: "She was the very first woman traveler to reach us!"[^1]

Umm Salamah listened to the Qur'an. One day she came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and asked him politely: "Messenger of Allah, I do not hear Allah mention women in the hijrah." She meant: I don't hear the Qur'an talk about the women who traveled for the sake of Allah.[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-3-195" audience="4-7" -->

So Allah sent down a beautiful ayah to answer her. In it, Allah says: "I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female." That means: every good thing a man or a woman, a boy or a girl does is never, ever lost with Allah. And at the end of the ayah, Allah says He will bring the believers into gardens with rivers flowing beneath them.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Umm Salamah asked a good question, and the answer came from Allah. And it's the same for you: when you help your mom, or pray next to your dad, Allah sees what you do and never forgets it.

### Activity: Two Seats Under the Tree

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.066.activity.help-each-other-to-the-garden" -->

With your dad, your mom, or your brother or sister, draw a big tree, and underneath it two seats side by side: one for you and one for them. Together, choose a good deed you will help each other with for three days, like lining up the shoes before prayer, or saying the morning remembrances together. Each day that you help each other, stick a green leaf on the tree and say to them, "Jazak Allahu khayran" (may Allah reward you with good). On the third day, say together: "O Allah, bring us together in Jannah."

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

After a lesson about Jannah, Sarah asked her teacher, "Will my family be with me there?" And her brother asked, "Is Jannah more for boys than for girls?" Two big questions, and the Qur'an answers both.

The first answer: in Jannah, Allah brings a believer together with those of his parents, spouses and children who were righteous, so the righteous members of a family meet again.[^3] The second answer: a girl's deeds and a boy's deeds are equal before Allah, and not one bit of them is lost. And on top of that, Allah takes rancor out of people's hearts, so there is no envy, no jealousy and no quarreling among the people of Jannah, and He gives every believing man and woman whatever they desire.[^4][^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-3-195" -->

- **`The hijrah (al-hijrah)`** — leaving Makkah for Madinah for the sake of Allah in the time of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; both men and women made the hijrah.
- **`I will not let be lost (la udi')`** — I will not let a person's deed go by without reward; I will repay it in full, as Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di explain.[^1]
- **`Rancor (ghill)`** — resentment and bitterness hidden in the heart; as-Sa'di says the hearts of the people of Jannah stay "free of all malice and envy."[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### From the Qur'an: A Woman Asked, and Allah Answered

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-3-195" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account of how an ayah of the Noble Qur'an was revealed; any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

Umm Salamah, may Allah be pleased with her, made the hijrah from Makkah to Madinah, and the Ansar said of her, "She was the first woman traveler to arrive among us," meaning the first woman emigrant to reach them.[^1] She listened as ayat of the Qur'an were revealed, and she noticed something: the ayat mentioned "those who emigrated," but never mentioned women by name.

So Umm Salamah took her question to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "Messenger of Allah, I do not hear Allah mention women in connection with the hijrah."[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-3-195" audience="8-12" -->

Then Allah revealed: "And their Lord answered them: 'I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female; you are of one another.'" After that, the ayah speaks of those who emigrated and were harmed in Allah's way, and says that Allah will wipe away their misdeeds and admit them into gardens beneath which rivers flow. Ibn Kathir explains "you are of one another" this way: "All of you are equal in My reward."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

One woman's question became the reason for an ayah that Muslims still recite today. The ayah holds two lessons. First, the deeds of girls and boys alike are kept safe with Allah. Second, men and women are "of one another": they help each other toward good. And it is exactly this kind of help that brings a righteous family together in Jannah.

<!-- story:end -->

### Activity: The Pact of Help

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.066.activity.help-each-other-to-the-garden" -->

### Bedtime Du'a

> اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ لَا تُضِيعُ عَمَلَ عَامِلٍ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنْثَى، فَتَقَبَّلْ مِنَّا، وَأَصْلِحْ قُلُوبَنَا وَأَهْلَنَا، وَاجْمَعْنَا فِي جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ مَعَ مَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ أَهْلِنَا.
>
> *"O Allah, You never let the deed of any man or woman go to waste. So accept our deeds, set our hearts right and set our family right, and bring us together in the Gardens of 'Adn with the righteous of our family."*

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A girl might scroll past a post, or sit through a debate, claiming that "Jannah was described for men, and women are just there as extras." And some young people keep their questions to themselves, afraid they will be taken for doubt. But the Qur'an itself gives the answer: a woman's deeds are preserved just as a man's are; a righteous family is brought together in the Gardens of 'Adn; jealousy and resentment are taken out of hearts; and every believing man and woman will have "whatever your souls desire."[^3][^4][^5]

Some questions have no definitive text, such as that of a woman who married more than once. On that one, there is a statement from the Companion Hudhayfah, may Allah be pleased with him, that a woman belongs to the last of her husbands; but it is the word of a Companion, not a definitive hadith traced back to the Prophet.[^7] The principle that ties it all together: every believing woman in Jannah will be completely content with what Allah has given her.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-3-195" -->

- **`You are of one another (ba'dukum min ba'd)`** — men and women, you are equal in reward; as Ibn Kathir puts it: "All of you are equal in My reward."[^1]
- **`Whoever was righteous (man salah)`** — the condition for being together in Jannah; kinship on its own is not enough, as is understood from Ibn Kathir's explanation of the ayah of ar-Ra'd.[^3]
- **`Mawquf`** — a statement or action attributed to a Companion rather than to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; it is taken into account as supporting evidence, but it is not treated like a definitive text.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### From the Qur'an: The Question That Became an Ayah

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-3-195" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account of how an ayah of the Noble Qur'an was revealed; any explanation in it comes from the scholars of tafsir.**

Madinah, after the hijrah. Umm Salamah, may Allah be pleased with her, was one of the very first women to emigrate, so much so that the Ansar said, "She was the first woman traveler to arrive among us."[^1] She hears the Qur'an mention "those who emigrated," and she takes her question straight to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: "Messenger of Allah, I do not hear Allah mention women in connection with the hijrah."[^2]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-3-195" audience="13+" -->

And the answer comes down: "And their Lord answered them: 'I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female; you are of one another. So those who emigrated, and were driven out of their homes, and were harmed in My way, and fought and were killed, I will surely wipe away their misdeeds and admit them into gardens beneath which rivers flow, as a reward from Allah. And with Allah is the finest reward.'" As-Sa'di says: "All of them will meet the reward of their deeds, complete and in full," and explains "you are of one another" as: "You are all on an equal footing in reward and punishment."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Notice three things. First, the question was not brushed aside; it became the occasion for an ayah that is recited to this day. Second, the answer did not stop at the hijrah; it came as a general rule covering every deed. Third, "you are of one another" makes men and women one team, not rivals. So when you hear talk that belittles believing women's place in the Hereafter, go to the text before the opinion, and ask someone who knows, just as Umm Salamah did.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What makes Umm Salamah's question a model of an honest question? And where did she ask it?

2. How would you use the ayah to respond to someone who says Jannah is "more for men"?

3. Why did Allah make righteousness the condition for being together in Jannah? And what does that mean for your relationship with your family today?

4. What is the difference between a definitive ayah and the *mawquf* statement of a Companion? Why does that difference matter?

5. What question about the Hereafter are you carrying that you haven't asked yet? And who would be the right person to ask?

### Activity: The Pact of Help

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.066.activity.help-each-other-to-the-garden" -->

Choose someone in your family you want to be with in Jannah: one of your parents, or a brother or sister. Suggest one good deed the two of you will help each other with for ten days, such as praying two rak'ahs before bed, memorizing some ayat, or giving a small shared charity. Write a short pact: the deed, each person's role in helping the other, and one rule: "We remind gently, and we don't reproach." Every day, write in a private note one way you helped and one way you were helped. On the tenth day, write your partner a message with a du'a for them by name, and write yourself a paragraph: How did this agreement change our relationship?

> اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّكَ لَا تُضِيعُ عَمَلَ عَامِلٍ مِنْ ذَكَرٍ أَوْ أُنْثَى، فَتَقَبَّلْ مِنَّا، وَأَصْلِحْ قُلُوبَنَا وَأَهْلَنَا، وَاجْمَعْنَا فِي جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ مَعَ مَنْ صَلَحَ مِنْ أَهْلِنَا.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the occasion of revelation of Al 'Imran 3:195 from the hadith of Umm Salamah; explains "you are of one another" in the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; uses ar-Ra'd 13:23-24 to show that righteous spouses are reunited, and names the condition; explains al-Hijr 15:47 and Fussilat 41:31; distinguishes the definitive text from Hudhayfah's *mawquf* statement, the disagreement over the version attributed to the Prophet, and Ibn 'Uthaymin's answer; and begins a ten-day "Pact of Help."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf; a board divided into two columns headed "Established by text" and "Scholarly reasoning"; small cards for anonymous questions; a printed "Pact of Help" template with three lines and a ten-day chart.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Al 'Imran 3:195, ar-Ra'd 13:23, al-Hijr 15:47 and Fussilat 41:31; the grading of the hadith in at-Tirmidhi 3023; the gradings of Hudhayfah's statement and of the hadith "A woman belongs to the last of her husbands" on Dorar.net; and Ibn 'Uthaymin's two answers. The teacher decides in advance how to refer questions about the hur al-'ayn and marriage in Jannah to the two later lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher hands out the cards and says: "Write down, without your name, a question about spouses or women in Jannah that you have heard or have carried yourself." The cards are collected, and some are read later.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Umm Salamah, then the full ayah of Al 'Imran with the commentators' words (7 minutes); then ar-Ra'd 13:23-24, focusing on "whoever was righteous" and as-Sa'di's phrase "the husband or the wife" (5 minutes); then al-Hijr 15:47 and Fussilat 41:31-32 (4 minutes); then Hudhayfah's statement and an explanation of its grade (4 minutes). Learners fill in the two columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher reads two or three of the cards and answers them by referring back to the two columns. Then discuss questions three and four, stressing that scholarly reasoning is presented with its proper grade, and that the definitive text is the source of reassurance.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner drafts a pact: the partner, the deed, each person's role, and the length. Then, in pairs, learners discuss how to propose the pact to the partner gently and without pressure.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What did Umm Salamah ask about, and how was she answered?" "What is the condition of the reunion in ar-Ra'd 13:23?" and "What is the grade of the statement 'A woman belongs to the last of her husbands'?" Then read the bedtime du'a, noting that it is lesson-authored.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only Al 'Imran 3:195, ar-Ra'd 13:23 and Fussilat 41:31. Ask advanced learners to trace the gradings of the hadith "A woman belongs to the last of her husbands" on Dorar.net and write a fair summary of the case for and against it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Be mindful of the divorced, the widowed, the never-married, and those whose husbands are not practicing: give no examples involving real people, and pass no judgment of Jannah or the Fire on anyone. Do not present Hudhayfah's statement as a definitive text, and never use it to pressure a widow not to remarry; marriage after widowhood or divorce is permissible. Refer the details of the hur al-'ayn and of marriage in Jannah to their own lessons. If the questions reveal signs of harm within a family, gently direct the person to a qualified professional.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Umm Salamah asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, a question and Allah sent down an ayah; says that the deeds of girls and boys are never lost with Allah; says that hearts in Jannah are clean, with no anger in them; begins the "Two Seats Under the Tree" activity; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet of paper for each child; crayons; green sticky-note leaves; a picture of a peaceful garden with no people in it; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the story calmly, draws a sample tree with two seats, and sends families a note explaining the three-day activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The caregiver asks: "Who lives in your home? What do you do together?" And then: "What do you do after you've had a squabble?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** The caregiver reads the introduction and then the story, and the children repeat: "I will not let the deed of any doer among you be lost, whether male or female." The boys raise their hands at the word "male" and the girls at "female," so they can see that everyone is included.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver asks the unit's questions, shows the garden picture and says: Allah brings the good people of a family together in Jannah, and their hearts there are clean.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child draws the tree and the two seats; the caregiver writes the child's name and their partner's name beside the seats; and the child sticks on the first green leaf for a good deed they did with someone today.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The caregiver notices whether the child can say: "A girl's good deeds and a boy's good deeds are never lost." Then the du'a is read one phrase at a time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, a tree, two seats and one leaf are enough. Ask older children to tell the story in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Say nothing to the children about the hur al-'ayn or about marriage in Jannah. Be mindful of any child who has lost a parent or lives in a separated family: do not ask "Where is your dad (or mom)?", accept whatever partner the child chooses, and reassure them that Allah is Merciful and brings good people together. Never tell a child that someone in their family will not enter Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the story of Umm Salamah's question and the revelation of the ayah; explains the three terms; names whom Allah brings together in Jannah and the condition for it; explains why there is no envy or jealousy in Jannah; and begins a seven-day "Pact of Help."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a mushaf; a pact card and an "I helped / I was helped" chart for each student; a small question box.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Al 'Imran 3:195 and ar-Ra'd 13:23, and prepares a completed sample pact between the teacher and a member of the teacher's own family.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the two questions from Sarah and her brother in the introduction and asks: "Have you ever heard a question like this? Where can we find the answer?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the story, pausing at Umm Salamah's question and at the words of the ayah, and distinguishing the ayah from the commentator's explanation (7 minutes); then the teacher reads ar-Ra'd 13:23 from the mushaf (2 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss questions three and four. The teacher explains that the reunion is for the righteous, that Allah takes rancor out of hearts, and that Jannah is not more for boys than for girls.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** The teacher shows the sample pact; then each student fills in a pact card, naming the partner, the deed and each person's role.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes: "With Allah, a girl's deeds and a boy's deeds are..." and "In Jannah, Allah brings together...". Anyone who wishes may put a question in the question box for the teacher to answer next session. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, three days and a single column are enough. Ask advanced students to memorize Al 'Imran 3:195 from its beginning up to "you are of one another."

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not discuss marriage in Jannah or the hur al-'ayn with this age group. If a student asks about them, say gently that Allah gives every believing man and woman whatever makes them completely happy, and that the details are learned at an older age. Be mindful of any student from a separated family or one that has lost a member. Do not allow comments that favor boys over girls, or girls over boys.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the account of Umm Salamah and the occasion of revelation of Al 'Imran 3:195; explains "you are of one another," "whoever was righteous," and the meaning of *mawquf*; uses the text to answer the claim that Jannah is more for men; distinguishes a definitive text from the statement of a Companion; and begins a ten-day "Pact of Help."

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; a printed, unattributed statement that belittles believing women's place in the Hereafter, for analysis; anonymous question cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on the ayat, and the gradings of at-Tirmidhi 3023 and of Hudhayfah's statement, and decides on the limits of answering questions about the hur al-'ayn and marriage in Jannah: briefly and modestly, with a referral to their own lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher shows the printed statement and asks: "Have you heard anything like this? How did it make you feel? How do we check it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the story, with three pauses: how the question was worded and where it was asked; the full text of the ayah; and as-Sa'di's words on equality in reward.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Small groups write a three-sentence response to the printed statement, based on Al 'Imran 3:195, ar-Ra'd 13:23 and Fussilat 41:31. Then discuss question four, on the difference between a definitive text and a *mawquf* report.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student drafts a pact with its rule, "We remind gently, and we don't reproach," and practices with a classmate how to propose it to a family member.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: what Umm Salamah asked, the condition for being together in Jannah, and the grade of the statement "A woman belongs to the last of her husbands." The teacher collects the anonymous question cards to answer later. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may record a private voice note instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the meaning of "occasion of revelation" (*sabab an-nuzul*) and find another example of an ayah revealed in answer to a question.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Answer questions about the hur al-'ayn and marriage in Jannah briefly and modestly, without detail, and refer them to their own lessons. Respect the feelings of girls when questions about fairness between the sexes come up: never mock a girl who asks, and never treat her question as doubt. Be mindful of anyone whose family has been through divorce, conflict or loss, and do not require them to choose any particular partner.
