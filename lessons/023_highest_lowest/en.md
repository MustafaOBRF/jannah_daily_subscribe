# The Highest and the Lowest in Rank Among the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Relate the hadith in which Musa, peace be upon him, asked his Lord about the lowest and the highest in rank among the people of Jannah (Muslim 189), and describe both ends of the scale: the gift of the lowest, which reaches the like of the kingdom of a king of this world and ten times as much, and the hidden honor of the highest, which no eye has seen, linking it to the ayah of as-Sajdah 32:17.
- Explain that the highest station in Jannah is *al-Wasilah*, which befits only one servant of Allah and which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped would be his (Muslim 384), and that asking for it on his behalf after the call to prayer is a daily deed that connects every Muslim to that station.
- Narrate the Qur'anic story of the wife of Pharaoh: how she saved the infant Musa with a word of mercy (al-Qasas 28:7-9), and then her prayer, `My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah` (at-Tahrim 66:11), and explain the scholars' saying: "She chose the neighbor before the house."
- Draw on the hadith "Many men attained perfection" (al-Bukhari 3411) to show that the ranks of this world are not the scale by which stations in the Hereafter are measured.
- Carry out the "Re-ranking What Is High" activity, in which the learner ranks what they consider lofty twice, before and after the evidence, gives a reason for every shift, and then chooses one deed that will bring them closer to Allah.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to make nearness to Him our highest wish, to raise our rank, and to grant us contentment.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah is a single abode, yet the stations of its people differ immensely. Musa, peace be upon him, once asked his Lord about the two ends of that range: the lowest of them and the highest. And at the very summit stands a single station called *al-Wasilah*, which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped would be his.

The Qur'an offers a living example of this scale of values: the wife of Pharaoh, a queen who did not ask her Lord for a grander palace, but for a house near Him in Jannah. Why, then, are the stations of the Hereafter not measured by the ranks of this world?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-189b" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Musa Asked His Lord: Who Is the Lowest in Rank Among the People of Jannah?"

> عَنِ الْمُغِيرَةِ بْنِ شُعْبَةَ رضي الله عنه، يَرْفَعُهُ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ قَالَ: **«سَأَلَ مُوسَى رَبَّهُ: مَا أَدْنَى أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ مَنْزِلَةً؟ قَالَ: هُوَ رَجُلٌ يَجِيءُ بَعْدَ مَا أُدْخِلَ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ الْجَنَّةَ، فَيُقَالُ لَهُ: ادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ. فَيَقُولُ: أَيْ رَبِّ، كَيْفَ وَقَدْ نَزَلَ النَّاسُ مَنَازِلَهُمْ وَأَخَذُوا أَخَذَاتِهِمْ؟ فَيُقَالُ لَهُ: أَتَرْضَى أَنْ يَكُونَ لَكَ مِثْلُ مُلْكِ مَلِكٍ مِنْ مُلُوكِ الدُّنْيَا؟ فَيَقُولُ: رَضِيتُ رَبِّ. فَيَقُولُ: لَكَ ذَلِكَ وَمِثْلُهُ وَمِثْلُهُ وَمِثْلُهُ وَمِثْلُهُ. فَقَالَ فِي الْخَامِسَةِ: رَضِيتُ رَبِّ. فَيَقُولُ: هَذَا لَكَ وَعَشَرَةُ أَمْثَالِهِ، وَلَكَ مَا اشْتَهَتْ نَفْسُكَ وَلَذَّتْ عَيْنُكَ. فَيَقُولُ: رَضِيتُ رَبِّ. قَالَ: رَبِّ، فَأَعْلَاهُمْ مَنْزِلَةً؟ قَالَ: أُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ أَرَدْتُ، غَرَسْتُ كَرَامَتَهُمْ بِيَدِي، وَخَتَمْتُ عَلَيْهَا، فَلَمْ تَرَ عَيْنٌ، وَلَمْ تَسْمَعْ أُذُنٌ، وَلَمْ يَخْطُرْ عَلَى قَلْبِ بَشَرٍ»**. قَالَ: وَمِصْدَاقُهُ فِي كِتَابِ اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: {فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَا أُخْفِيَ لَهُمْ مِنْ قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ} الْآيَةَ.[^1]
>
> وَتَمَامُ الْآيَةِ: **{فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَّا أُخْفِيَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ جَزَاءً بِمَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ}** [السجدة: ١٧][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah, may Allah be pleased with him, tracing it back to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"Musa asked his Lord: 'Who is the lowest in rank among the people of Jannah?' He said: 'He is a man who comes after the people of Jannah have been admitted into Jannah. It is said to him, "Enter Jannah." He says, "My Lord, how, when the people have already settled into their places and taken what they have taken?" It is said to him, "Would you be content to have the like of the kingdom of one of the kings of this world?" He says, "I am content, my Lord." He says, "You shall have that, and the like of it, and the like of it, and the like of it, and the like of it." At the fifth, he says, "I am content, my Lord." He says, "This is yours, and ten times as much, and you shall have whatever your soul desires and whatever delights your eye." He says, "I am content, my Lord."' Musa said: 'My Lord, and the highest of them in rank?' He said: 'Those are the ones I chose. I planted their honor with My own Hand and set a seal upon it, so no eye has seen it, no ear has heard of it, and it has never crossed the heart of any human being.'"** He said: And its confirmation in the Book of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, is: `No soul knows what delight of the eyes has been hidden away for them` (the ayah).[^1]
>
> The ayah in full: **`No soul knows what delight of the eyes has been hidden away for them, as a reward for what they used to do.`** (as-Sajdah 32:17)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Imam an-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, cited al-Qadi 'Iyad as explaining "what they have taken" (*akhadhatihim*) as the honor they have received from their Master and secured for themselves; he explained "the ones I chose" (*aradtu*) as those whom He selected and singled out, and said that "I planted their honor with My own Hand and set a seal upon it" means: I selected them and took charge of them, so no change can reach their honor.[^3] And the words "you shall have whatever your soul desires" show that the gift of the lowest does not stop at the numbers mentioned.

#### Lesson Explanation

The lowest station is no deprivation: five times the kingdom of a king, then ten times as much, along with everything the soul desires, while its owner says again and again, "I am content, my Lord." The highest, by contrast, is not described by any quantity at all, but by concealment, because what lies beyond the imagination cannot be counted in numbers.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-384" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### *Al-Wasilah*: A Station That Befits Only One Servant

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَمْرِو بْنِ الْعَاصِ رضي الله عنهما، أَنَّهُ سَمِعَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ يَقُولُ: **«إِذَا سَمِعْتُمُ الْمُؤَذِّنَ فَقُولُوا مِثْلَ مَا يَقُولُ، ثُمَّ صَلُّوا عَلَيَّ، فَإِنَّهُ مَنْ صَلَّى عَلَيَّ صَلَاةً صَلَّى اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ بِهَا عَشْرًا، ثُمَّ سَلُوا اللَّهَ لِيَ الْوَسِيلَةَ، فَإِنَّهَا مَنْزِلَةٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ لَا تَنْبَغِي إِلَّا لِعَبْدٍ مِنْ عِبَادِ اللَّهِ، وَأَرْجُو أَنْ أَكُونَ أَنَا هُوَ، فَمَنْ سَأَلَ لِيَ الْوَسِيلَةَ حَلَّتْ لَهُ الشَّفَاعَةُ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, that he heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"When you hear the mu'adhdhin, say what he says. Then invoke blessings upon me, for whoever invokes one blessing upon me, Allah blesses him ten times for it. Then ask Allah to grant me *al-Wasilah*, for it is a station in Jannah that befits only one of the servants of Allah, and I hope that I will be the one. Whoever asks for *al-Wasilah* for me, intercession becomes his due."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, cited this hadith in the chapter "On the Highest and the Lowest in Rank Among the People of Jannah" in his book *Hadi al-Arwah*, and he titled that chapter with the statement that the highest of them in rank is the master of the children of Adam, peace and blessings be upon him.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

At the summit of Jannah there is a station for one servant alone, and every Muslim connects to it by asking for it on behalf of his Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, after every call to prayer; and with that, intercession becomes his due.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-66-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "My Lord, Build for Me Near You a House in Jannah"

> **{وَضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا لِّلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا امْرَأَتَ فِرْعَوْنَ إِذْ قَالَتْ رَبِّ ابْنِ لِي عِندَكَ بَيْتًا فِي الْجَنَّةِ وَنَجِّنِي مِن فِرْعَوْنَ وَعَمَلِهِ وَنَجِّنِي مِنَ الْقَوْمِ الظَّالِمِينَ}** [التحريم: ١١][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And Allah sets forth an example for those who believe: the wife of Pharaoh, when she said: 'My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah, and save me from Pharaoh and his deeds, and save me from the wrongdoing people.'"** (at-Tahrim 66:11)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, reported the scholars' comment on her words `My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah`: "She chose the neighbor before the house" (in Arabic a rhyming phrase, *ikhtarat al-jar qabla ad-dar*). He explained `and save me from Pharaoh and his deeds` as meaning: deliver me from him, for I disown his deeds before You. He reported from Qatadah that Pharaoh was the most tyrannical of all the people on earth, and yet his wife was not harmed by her husband's disbelief once she had obeyed her Lord. He also mentioned reports about her being tortured, some of which stop at the Companion Salman, may Allah be pleased with him, and others at certain of the Successors; these are reports that stop at a Companion (*mawquf*) or at a Successor (*maqtu'*), and this lesson builds nothing on them.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

She occupied one of the highest ranks this world has to offer, and yet in her prayer she placed "near You" before "a house." Nearness to Allah is the real aim; the house simply follows from it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-28-7-9" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "A Delight of the Eye for Me and for You"

> **{وَأَوْحَيْنَا إِلَىٰ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ أَنْ أَرْضِعِيهِ ۖ فَإِذَا خِفْتِ عَلَيْهِ فَأَلْقِيهِ فِي الْيَمِّ وَلَا تَخَافِي وَلَا تَحْزَنِي ۖ إِنَّا رَادُّوهُ إِلَيْكِ وَجَاعِلُوهُ مِنَ الْمُرْسَلِينَ ۝ فَالْتَقَطَهُ آلُ فِرْعَوْنَ لِيَكُونَ لَهُمْ عَدُوًّا وَحَزَنًا ۗ إِنَّ فِرْعَوْنَ وَهَامَانَ وَجُنُودَهُمَا كَانُوا خَاطِئِينَ ۝ وَقَالَتِ امْرَأَتُ فِرْعَوْنَ قُرَّتُ عَيْنٍ لِّي وَلَكَ ۖ لَا تَقْتُلُوهُ عَسَىٰ أَن يَنفَعَنَا أَوْ نَتَّخِذَهُ وَلَدًا وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ}** [القصص: ٧-٩][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And We inspired the mother of Musa: 'Nurse him; then, when you fear for him, cast him into the river, and do not fear and do not grieve. We will surely return him to you and make him one of the messengers.' Then the household of Pharaoh picked him up, so that he would become for them an enemy and a cause of grief. Indeed, Pharaoh and Haman and their soldiers were wrongdoers. And the wife of Pharaoh said: 'A delight of the eye for me and for you. Do not kill him; perhaps he will be of benefit to us, or we may take him as a son.' And they did not perceive."** (al-Qasas 28:7-9)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators mention that Pharaoh had been slaughtering the sons of the Children of Israel, as the opening of the surah states. Ibn Kathir reports from Muhammad ibn Ishaq and others that the *lam* in `so that he would become` is the *lam* of outcome (*lam al-'aqibah*): they did not pick him up in order to make him their enemy, but that is how their deed ended. Ibn Kathir adds that, seen in light of Allah's decree, it remains a *lam* of purpose: Allah caused them to pick him up so that He would make him an enemy and a cause of grief for them.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Qur'an does not say when the wife of Pharaoh came to believe, but it preserves two of her stands: a word of mercy that saved a child who would become a prophet, and a prayer that made her an example for the believers.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3411" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Many Men Attained Perfection"

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«كَمَلَ مِنَ الرِّجَالِ كَثِيرٌ، وَلَمْ يَكْمُلْ مِنَ النِّسَاءِ إِلَّا آسِيَةُ امْرَأَةُ فِرْعَوْنَ، وَمَرْيَمُ بِنْتُ عِمْرَانَ، وَإِنَّ فَضْلَ عَائِشَةَ عَلَى النِّسَاءِ كَفَضْلِ الثَّرِيدِ عَلَى سَائِرِ الطَّعَامِ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Many men attained perfection, but among women none attained perfection except Asiyah, the wife of Pharaoh, and Maryam, the daughter of Imran. And the excellence of Aishah over other women is like the excellence of *tharid* over all other food."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar, may Allah have mercy on him, cited al-Kirmani as saying that perfection is used for a thing being complete and reaching its utmost in its kind, so what is meant is reaching the utmost in all the virtues proper to women, and that the word "perfection" does not by itself establish prophethood. He also mentioned the scholars' disagreement over whether Maryam was a prophet, and cited al-Qurtubi as saying that nothing has been transmitted indicating that Asiyah was a prophet.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, bore witness to the perfection of Pharaoh's wife, while kingship did nothing to raise Pharaoh himself. The scale of the Hereafter is not the scale of this world.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The hadith of Musa sketches the two ends of the range; the hadith of *al-Wasilah* reveals the summit and opens a daily door toward it; and the story of Pharaoh's wife shows the scale by which choices are weighed: she put nearness to Allah ahead of worldly rank, and Allah raised her. So the believer holds together the contentment of the one who said, "I am content, my Lord," and the high aspiration of the one who said, "near You."

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why is the gift of the lowest in Jannah described in numbers, while the honor of the highest is described by its concealment?
2. What does the repeated "I am content, my Lord" teach us about a servant's proper manners toward what his Lord gives?
3. How does the hadith of *al-Wasilah* connect the highest station in Jannah to a simple daily deed?
4. How does the meaning of "She chose the neighbor before the house" show itself in the order of the words in the prayer of Pharaoh's wife?
5. Which worldly rank occupies you today more than it should?

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Queen Who Asked for a House Near Allah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-66-11" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

Pharaoh was a harsh and unfair king, and he hurt the little boys of the Children of Israel. His wife was a queen who lived with him in his palace.

One day, Pharaoh's family picked a tiny baby up out of the water. The queen said gently, "A delight of the eye for me and for you. Do not kill him." That means: "This baby makes our hearts happy, so don't hurt him." And so the baby lived. Do you know who that baby was? He was Musa, peace be upon him, who grew up to be a great prophet![^7]

This queen loved Allah. She had a palace in this world, but she didn't ask Allah for a bigger palace. Instead, she prayed to her Lord and said, "My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah." First she asked to be close to Allah, and then she asked for the house.[^6]

So Allah told us about her in the Qur'an as a beautiful example for all the believers, and our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that she was one of the most perfect women of all.[^8]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-66-11" audience="4-7" -->

Here it is in simple words: a kind queen saved baby Musa. She didn't care very much about her palace. She asked Allah for a house close to Him in Jannah, because being close to Allah is higher than any palace.[^6]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Musa, peace be upon him, once asked his Lord: who has the lowest rank of all the people of Jannah? Allah told him it is a man who enters after everyone else. He is given the like of the kingdom of one of this world's kings, then that much four more times, then ten times all of that, plus everything he could wish for, and he says, "I am content, my Lord!" Then Musa asked about the highest of them, and Allah told him they are people He chose, and that He has prepared for them an honor no eye has seen and no ear has ever heard of.[^1] And the very highest station in all of Jannah has a name, *al-Wasilah*; the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught us to ask for it for him after every call to prayer.[^4]

So if the lowest person in Jannah gets more than kings, is a king in this world really the highest of people? That is the question the story of the queen answers.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-189b" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: A Queen in the Palace, Her Heart with Her Lord

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-66-11" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

Pharaoh was the most powerful king of his time, and he was killing the sons of the Children of Israel. So, by Allah's command, the mother of Musa placed her baby in the river, and Pharaoh's household picked him up.

Right then, Pharaoh's wife took a brave stand. She said to her husband, "A delight of the eye for me and for you. Do not kill him; perhaps he will be of benefit to us, or we may take him as a son." Her words saved the baby's life. The Qur'an tells us that Pharaoh's household picked him up "so that he would become for them an enemy and a cause of grief," and they had no idea. Musa, peace be upon him, grew up in their own palace and became a prophet calling people to the truth.[^7]

The queen had what so many people dream of: a title, a palace, and a special place beside the most powerful man on earth. But she believed in Allah, and she refused to go along with her husband's cruelty. So she prayed to her Lord with three requests: "My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah, and save me from Pharaoh and his deeds, and save me from the wrongdoing people."

Notice the order of her words: she said "near You" before "a house." That is why the scholars said, "She chose the neighbor before the house." In other words, she asked to be close to Allah before she asked for the house itself.[^6]

And what came of it? Allah mentioned her in the Qur'an as an example for those who believe, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us she was one of the women who reached perfection.[^8] As for Pharaoh's kingdom, it vanished, and it did nothing at all to raise him in Allah's sight.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-66-11" audience="8-12" -->

Put another way: a queen saved baby Musa with a word of mercy. Then she believed in Allah, rejected Pharaoh's cruelty, and asked her Lord for a house near Him in Jannah, putting closeness to Him ahead of the house, so Allah made her an example for the believers.[^6][^7]

<!-- retelling:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live surrounded by rankings that never end: who has the highest grades, who has the most followers, whose clothes cost the most. Islam doesn't deny that people differ in rank; in fact, it tells us the differences in Jannah are far greater. In an authentic hadith, Musa, peace be upon him, asked his Lord about the lowest in rank among the people of Jannah. The answer: a man given the like of the kingdom of one of this world's kings five times over, and then ten times as much, who responds, "I am content, my Lord." Then Musa asked about the highest of them, and Allah told him they are people He chose, for whom He has hidden away an honor no eye has seen and that has never crossed the heart of any human being.[^1] And at the very top is *al-Wasilah*, a station for a single servant, which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, hoped would be his, and which he taught us to ask for on his behalf after the call to prayer.[^4]

So here's the question: if the lowest person in Jannah ranks above kings, what scale are we using to measure what counts as "high" today?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-66-11" -->

- **`Allah sets forth an example (daraba Allahu mathalan)`** — Allah made her situation a model to follow and a standard to measure by, not just a piece of history.
- **`Perfection (al-kamal)`** — in the hadith "Many men attained perfection": reaching the utmost in all virtues, as Ibn Hajar cites from al-Kirmani.[^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: Integrity Inside the Most Powerful Palace on Earth

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-66-11" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

Imagine living at the very center of power: your husband is the mightiest king of his age, and the most brutal oppressor of the Children of Israel. That was the position of Pharaoh's wife.

It was inside this palace that she took the first stand the Qur'an preserves for her. By revelation from Allah, the mother of Musa had set her infant adrift on the river, and Pharaoh's household picked him up. Then Pharaoh's wife spoke: "A delight of the eye for me and for you. Do not kill him; perhaps he will be of benefit to us, or we may take him as a son." One word, spoken in the right place, saved Musa, peace be upon him, while the whole palace "did not perceive" what Allah was bringing about.[^7]

Then the Qur'an preserves her second stand, her prayer: "My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah, and save me from Pharaoh and his deeds, and save me from the wrongdoing people." Here was a woman who held the highest title and the finest palace this world could offer, yet she put "near You" ahead of "a house." The scholars said: "She chose the neighbor before the house." She also disowned "his deeds" while living under his roof; being close to him did not make her a partner in his oppression. As Qatadah said, her husband's disbelief did her no harm once she had obeyed her Lord.[^6]

The outcome? Allah made her "an example for those who believe," men and women alike, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, testified that she was among those who reached perfection.[^8] As for Pharaoh, the man with the highest rank in everyone's eyes, his kingdom did him no good at all.

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-66-11" audience="13+" -->

In other words: a woman at the very top of this world's ranks saved a prophet with a single word, disowned her husband's oppression, and asked for nearness to Allah before the house. So Allah raised her as an example for the believers until the Day of Resurrection, while the man who held the kingdom fell.[^6][^7]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What is the difference between describing the gift of the lowest in Jannah in numbers and describing the honor of the highest as something that has never crossed the heart of any human being?
2. How was Pharaoh's wife able to keep her integrity while living inside an oppressive system?
3. What would it mean in your own life to put "near You" ahead of "a house"?
4. How does a believer combine the contentment of the one who said "I am content, my Lord" with the ambition of one who asks for the highest stations?
5. Which "ranking" around you puts the most pressure on you, and how does this lesson change how much weight you give it?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner describes the two ends of the range of stations in the hadith of Muslim 189 in the hadith's own words; explains why the highest is described by concealment; connects the hadith of *al-Wasilah* to a specific daily deed; draws from the ayat of at-Tahrim and al-Qasas and the hadith of al-Bukhari 3411 the conclusion that the ranks of this world are not the scale of the Hereafter; and writes two rankings of their own priorities, with reasons and a weekly deed.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for referring to Surat at-Tahrim and Surat al-Qasas; a board divided into two columns ("The Lowest Station" and "The Highest"); an activity sheet with two side-by-side tables for the first and second rankings; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the five pieces of evidence and their references, reviews an-Nawawi's commentary on the hadith of Muslim 189 and Ibn Kathir's tafsir of at-Tahrim 66:11, and notes that the reports narrated about the torture of Pharaoh's wife are *mawquf* and *maqtu'* reports on which nothing is built. The teacher also bears in mind that an earlier lesson in the series (Lesson 19, "The Last to Enter Jannah") presented a hadith close to the "lowest" end of the range, and so simply refers to it without repeating it.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "If you were asked to list the ten people with the highest standing in your city, what criteria would you use to choose them?" The teacher writes the criteria on the board without comment, to return to them at the close.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Musa in full and fill in the two columns on the board with what it says about each end of the range (7 minutes). Then the hadith of *al-Wasilah*, with the question: "What makes a station meant for one servant alone close to us?" (4 minutes). Then the ayat of al-Qasas 28:7-9, followed by at-Tahrim 66:11, pausing at the order "near You... a house" and at the words "and his deeds" (6 minutes). Then the hadith of al-Bukhari 3411 (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion around three contrasts: the numbers of the lowest versus the concealment of the highest; the contentment of "I am content, my Lord" versus the aspiration of "near You"; Pharaoh's kingdom versus his wife's perfection. The teacher asks: "Does contentment conflict with seeking the highest?" and guides learners to see that contentment with what Allah has allotted does not stop striving, and that striving does not spoil contentment.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners carry out the "Re-ranking What Is High" activity individually (10 minutes); then whoever wishes shares with a partner one item that moved and the reason it moved (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** An exit card with two questions: "Describe the lowest and the highest of the people of Jannah in the words of the hadith" and "What deed did you commit to, and when will you review it?" The teacher then returns to the criteria from the opening and asks: which of them still holds up as a scale for the Hereafter? The teacher closes by reminding learners to ask for *al-Wasilah* after the next call to prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith of Musa and the ayah of at-Tahrim are enough. Advanced learners are asked to consult the chapter "On the Highest and the Lowest in Rank Among the People of Jannah" in *Hadi al-Arwah* and to compare the hadith of Muslim 189 with the hadith of Abu Hurayrah in Ahmad's *Musnad* stating that the lowest in rank has seven levels and three hundred servants, which Ibn al-Qayyim judged in that same chapter to be *munkar*, contradicting the authentic hadiths, so as to learn to distinguish the authentic from the *munkar* in reports about the stations of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The attributes of Allah mentioned in the hadith are affirmed in the manner befitting His majesty, without likening them to creation and without asking how, and their details are not delved into. No specific individual is declared with certainty to hold any particular station in Jannah. The *mawquf* and *maqtu'* reports about the torture of Pharaoh's wife are not presented as established facts. And the lesson is never used to look down on anyone whose deeds are few, for the lowest station is itself immense bliss.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Jannah has homes, some higher than others; retells the queen's two stands: saving Musa, and praying for a house near Allah; sorts three cards twice and explains one move in a sentence; and prays one prayer on time.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Three blank cards per child; child-safe coloring pencils; star stickers; a picture of a calm river and a small basket; the du'a card in large, clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the story in a calm voice, and prepares a gentle answer in case a child asks why Pharaoh hurt children: "He was unfair and cruel, but Allah kept Musa safe."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The educator asks: "If I offered you a big palace, or being close to someone you love, which would you choose?" and listens without correcting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The educator reads the short explanation and then the story, shows the river picture at the moment Musa is saved, and asks the child to repeat after them: "رَبِّ ابْنِ لِي عِنْدَكَ بَيْتًا فِي الْجَنَّةِ" ("My Lord, build for me, near You, a house in Jannah").

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator asks: "What did the queen ask for first: to be close to Allah, or the house?" and explains that the person with the smallest place in Jannah gets more than kings do, so imagine what those higher up receive!

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child draws the three cards and sorts them twice as described in the activity unit, then puts the star on the praying card.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The educator notices whether the child can say what the queen asked for and why the child moved a card. Then everyone reads the du'a together and agrees on which prayer the child will pray on time.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, two cards are enough (a palace and a child praying), and they may move the cards instead of explaining in words. Older children are asked to draw a fourth card for a word of mercy they said today.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Pharaoh's cruelty is mentioned only in general terms, without details of killing or torture. Neither Allah nor Musa, peace be upon him, is depicted in any drawing. A child is never told that their house in Jannah will be "smaller" because of some mistake; the focus stays on love, closeness, and hope.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains Allah's answer to Musa about the lowest and the highest; defines *al-manzilah* and *al-Wasilah*; narrates the story of Pharaoh's wife with both of her stands; explains "She chose the neighbor before the house"; completes two rankings of six cards with reasons; and chooses a deed for one week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards for the two terms; six blank cards per student; pens; a large sheet of poster paper to display the two rankings; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares a simple drawing of a staircase, writing at the bottom "ten times more than a worldly king's kingdom" and at the top "what no eye has seen," and writes out the du'a said in response to the call to prayer, which includes asking for *al-Wasilah*, for the students to read.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Who is the 'highest' at your school? Why?" and then: "Could the person who is highest in people's eyes be different from the one who is highest with Allah?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Students read the explanation and fill in the staircase with the lowest and the highest stations (4 minutes); then the whole story is read, pausing at the words "Do not kill him" and at the order "near You... a house" (8 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What did the queen own? What did she ask for? What happened to Pharaoh's kingdom?" and connects the answers to the hadith "Many men attained perfection." The teacher then teaches students how to ask for *al-Wasilah* for the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, after the call to prayer.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students carry out the re-ranking activity as described in the activity unit, and anyone who wishes may post their sheet on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence explaining "She chose the neighbor before the house," assessed on how accurately it links the saying to nearness to Allah. Then the du'a is read.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who are struggling, the story is presented as four pictures in sequence (the river, the word, the prayer, the example). Advanced students are asked to memorize at-Tahrim 66:11 and explain the three requests in it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The activity is never used to rank the students themselves or to compare them publicly. Pharaoh's killing of the sons is mentioned briefly, without description. Students are reminded that seeking nearness to Allah does not mean taking school or excellence lightly; it means having a good intention in both.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the difference between describing the lowest in numbers and describing the highest through concealment; explains how Pharaoh's wife kept her integrity inside an oppressive system; balances contentment and ambition; completes two rankings of eight cards with reasons; and commits to one item for seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; eight cards per student; a personal sheet that is not collected; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares neutral contemporary examples of the pressure of social ranking (grades, followers, brands) and reviews Ibn Kathir's tafsir of the ayah of at-Tahrim and the saying of Qatadah, in order to clarify that what she disowned was the deed, not the person.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Which ranking made you feel less than others this week?" Answers are written anonymously on small slips of paper.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** The explanation is read and then the story, pausing at three moments: the word that saved Musa, the order "near You... a house," and her words "and his deeds."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Small-group discussion of questions 2 and 4, followed by brief presentations. The teacher guides students to see that integrity does not mean confrontation or disrespect toward parents, but refusing to take part in wrongdoing while keeping good character, and that contentment with what Allah has allotted does not cancel ambition.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students carry out the re-ranking activity individually and in silence, and each student keeps their own sheet.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a short paragraph: "How does a believer combine 'I am content, my Lord' with 'near You'?" After a week, the teacher follows up with a general question about the commitment, without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private audio recording instead. Advanced students are asked to trace the story of Pharaoh's wife through Surat al-Qasas and Surat at-Tahrim and to write about what can be learned from the Qur'an's silence on the details of her life.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Pharaoh's situation is never projected onto any father, family, or specific person, and the lesson is not to be understood as a call to rebel against parents. No one is asked to disclose personal pressures. Students are reminded that talk of followers and brands is not a condemnation of the people who have them, but a recalibration of the heart's scale.
