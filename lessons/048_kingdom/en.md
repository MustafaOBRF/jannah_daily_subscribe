# The Kingdom and Dominion of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the ayah of al-Insan (76:20) and explain the meaning of `a great kingdom` through Ibn Kathir's words, "a vast realm and a dazzling dominion," together with as-Sa'di's account of what this bliss contains and his statement that its greatest part is Allah's good pleasure, the sight of Him, and everlasting life.
- Narrate the account of Sulayman, peace be upon him, from the Qur'an (Sad 38:35-40, an-Naml 27:38-40, and Saba' 34:14) in its proper order: the plea for forgiveness and then the request for a kingdom; the gift; the gratitude when the throne appeared; the end of his worldly kingdom in death; and the promise of `nearness and a good place of return`.
- Show that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, honored the du'a of his brother Sulayman in the hadith of the 'ifrit (al-Bukhari 3423); that the space of a whip in Jannah is better than this world and all it contains (al-Bukhari 3250); and that the lowest of the people of Jannah is given the like of the kingdom of one of the kings of this world, and ten times as much (Muslim 189).
- Distinguish the kingdom of this world, a test that comes to an end, from the kingdom of Jannah, lasting bliss in which there is no test.
- Carry out the "My Small Kingdom and the Gratitude Decree" activity: taking stock of what Allah has placed in the learner's hands, choosing one area to govern by a decree of gratitude for a set period, and asking Sulayman's question each night: `Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?`
- Recite the lesson's bedtime du'a, asking Allah for forgiveness, for gratitude, and for a great kingdom in Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

When people imagine a kingdom, they picture it in scattered pieces: broad lands, commands that are obeyed, servants, and a security that nothing can take away. The Qur'an gathers all of this for the people of Jannah into two words: `bliss and a great kingdom`. Each of them is an honored owner in his own home, not a subordinate and not a passing guest. Ibn al-Qayyim devoted a whole chapter to the theme: "On the Kingdom of Jannah, and That All Its People Are Kings in It."[^1]

This lesson measures the kingdom of Jannah against the greatest kingdom the earth has ever known: the kingdom of Sulayman, peace be upon him, who asked his Lord, `and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me`. Three questions guide the reading. How did a prophet who was also a king handle his kingdom? Where did that kingdom end? And what in Jannah surpasses it? The hadith of the last person to enter Jannah and the hadith of the lowest in rank among its people were studied in two earlier lessons; here we take only one piece of evidence from them.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-20" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Bliss and a Great Kingdom"

> **{وَإِذَا رَأَيْتَ ثَمَّ رَأَيْتَ نَعِيمًا وَمُلْكًا كَبِيرًا}** [الإنسان: ٢٠][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And when you look there, you will see bliss and a great kingdom."** (al-Insan 76:20)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

`There` (*thamma*) means there in Jannah. Ibn Kathir said that `and a great kingdom` means a vast realm belonging to Allah there, and a dazzling dominion. As-Sa'di set out the details of this kingdom: palaces, dwellings and adorned upper rooms; gardens and rivers; spouses, youths and servants. Then he said: "And on top of all that, and greatest of it, is the triumph of seeing the Merciful Lord... and rejoicing in His good pleasure, and everlasting life." Al-Baghawi recorded several views on it, among them: "a kingdom that never passes away."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah speaks to whoever looks: if you look there, you will see a kingdom. In Jannah, kingship is not a title handed to a select few; it describes what the onlooker sees in the home of every one of the righteous.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-38-35-40" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "This Is Our Gift"

> **{قَالَ رَبِّ اغْفِرْ لِي وَهَبْ لِي مُلْكًا لَا يَنْبَغِي لِأَحَدٍ مِنْ بَعْدِي ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْوَهَّابُ * فَسَخَّرْنَا لَهُ الرِّيحَ تَجْرِي بِأَمْرِهِ رُخَاءً حَيْثُ أَصَابَ * وَالشَّيَاطِينَ كُلَّ بَنَّاءٍ وَغَوَّاصٍ * وَآخَرِينَ مُقَرَّنِينَ فِي الْأَصْفَادِ * هَٰذَا عَطَاؤُنَا فَامْنُنْ أَوْ أَمْسِكْ بِغَيْرِ حِسَابٍ * وَإِنَّ لَهُ عِنْدَنَا لَزُلْفَىٰ وَحُسْنَ مَآبٍ}** [ص: ٣٥-٤٠][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"He said, 'My Lord, forgive me, and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me. You are indeed the Bestower.' So We made the wind subject to him, blowing gently at his command wherever he directed it; and the devils, every builder and diver, and others bound together in shackles. 'This is Our gift, so bestow or withhold, without account.' And indeed, he has with Us nearness and a good place of return."** (Sad 38:35-40)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that the correct view is that he asked for a kingdom whose like would belong to no human being after him, "and this is the apparent meaning of the context of the ayah, and the authentic hadiths have come with it." `Gently` (*rukha'an*) means softly, not as a storm, and `wherever he directed it` (*haythu asab*) means wherever he wished. On `without account`, as-Sa'di said that there is no blame on you in that; then he said: "And do not suppose that this was Sulayman's in this world and not in the Hereafter; rather, he has great good in the Hereafter." He explained `nearness` (*zulfa*) as meaning that he is among those brought near to Allah and honored by Him.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

Sulayman began with `forgive me` before `grant me`: for him, forgiveness came ahead of the kingdom. And the ayat close on something better than the whole kingdom: nearness to Allah and a good return.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-27-38-40" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "This Is from the Favor of My Lord, to Test Me"

> **{قَالَ يَا أَيُّهَا الْمَلَأُ أَيُّكُمْ يَأْتِينِي بِعَرْشِهَا قَبْلَ أَنْ يَأْتُونِي مُسْلِمِينَ * قَالَ عِفْرِيتٌ مِنَ الْجِنِّ أَنَا آتِيكَ بِهِ قَبْلَ أَنْ تَقُومَ مِنْ مَقَامِكَ ۖ وَإِنِّي عَلَيْهِ لَقَوِيٌّ أَمِينٌ * قَالَ الَّذِي عِنْدَهُ عِلْمٌ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ أَنَا آتِيكَ بِهِ قَبْلَ أَنْ يَرْتَدَّ إِلَيْكَ طَرْفُكَ ۚ فَلَمَّا رَآهُ مُسْتَقِرًّا عِنْدَهُ قَالَ هَٰذَا مِنْ فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ ۖ وَمَنْ شَكَرَ فَإِنَّمَا يَشْكُرُ لِنَفْسِهِ ۖ وَمَنْ كَفَرَ فَإِنَّ رَبِّي غَنِيٌّ كَرِيمٌ}** [النمل: ٣٨-٤٠][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"He said, 'O assembly, which of you will bring me her throne before they come to me in submission?' An 'ifrit from among the jinn said, 'I will bring it to you before you rise from your place, and I am strong enough for it, and trustworthy.' The one who had knowledge from the Book said, 'I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you.' And when he saw it set firmly before him, he said, 'This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me: will I be grateful, or ungrateful? Whoever is grateful is grateful only for his own good, and whoever is ungrateful, then my Lord is Free of all need, Most Generous.'"** (an-Naml 27:38-40)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that `to test me` (*li-yabluwani*) means to put me to the test. As-Sa'di said: "He, peace be upon him, was not deluded by his kingdom, his authority and his power, as is the habit of ignorant kings; rather, he knew that it was a test from his Lord." He added that gratitude for blessings invites more of them, while ingratitude invites their loss.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

At the very height of his power, when the throne arrived in the blink of an eye, Sulayman did not say, "Look at what I own." He said, "This is a test."

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-34-14" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And When We Decreed Death for Him"

> **{فَلَمَّا قَضَيْنَا عَلَيْهِ الْمَوْتَ مَا دَلَّهُمْ عَلَىٰ مَوْتِهِ إِلَّا دَابَّةُ الْأَرْضِ تَأْكُلُ مِنْسَأَتَهُ ۖ فَلَمَّا خَرَّ تَبَيَّنَتِ الْجِنُّ أَنْ لَوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ الْغَيْبَ مَا لَبِثُوا فِي الْعَذَابِ الْمُهِينِ}** [سبأ: ١٤][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And when We decreed death for him, nothing showed them his death but a creature of the earth eating away at his staff. And when he fell, the jinn saw plainly that, had they known the unseen, they would not have stayed on in humiliating torment."** (Saba' 34:14)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Minsa'ah* means his staff. Ibn Kathir said that he remained leaning on his staff for a long time, and when the creature of the earth (the termite) had eaten through it, he fell; and so it became clear that the jinn do not know the unseen.[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The greatest kingdom on earth ended the way every kingdom on earth ends, and what remained for its owner was what his Lord had promised him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3423" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then I Remembered the Du'a of My Brother Sulayman"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«إِنَّ عِفْرِيتًا مِنَ الْجِنِّ تَفَلَّتَ الْبَارِحَةَ لِيَقْطَعَ عَلَيَّ صَلَاتِي، فَأَمْكَنَنِي اللَّهُ مِنْهُ، فَأَخَذْتُهُ، فَأَرَدْتُ أَنْ أَرْبُطَهُ عَلَى سَارِيَةٍ مِنْ سَوَارِي الْمَسْجِدِ حَتَّى تَنْظُرُوا إِلَيْهِ كُلُّكُمْ، فَذَكَرْتُ دَعْوَةَ أَخِي سُلَيْمَانَ: رَبِّ هَبْ لِي مُلْكًا لَا يَنْبَغِي لِأَحَدٍ مِنْ بَعْدِي، فَرَدَدْتُهُ خَاسِئًا»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"An 'ifrit from among the jinn broke loose last night to cut off my prayer, and Allah gave me power over him. I seized him, and I wanted to tie him to one of the pillars of the mosque so that all of you could look at him. Then I remembered the du'a of my brother Sulayman: 'My Lord, grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me.' So I sent him back, driven off and disgraced."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir cites it in his tafsir of Sad 38:35 as evidence that Sulayman asked for a kingdom whose like would belong to no human being after him. *Khasi'an* means driven away and humbled.[^2][^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, held back from something within his power out of respect for a distinction Allah had given his brother. Sulayman's kingdom is a reality that even the Seal of the Prophets honors.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3250" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Space of a Whip in Jannah"

> عَنْ سَهْلِ بْنِ سَعْدٍ السَّاعِدِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«مَوْضِعُ سَوْطٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ خَيْرٌ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا وَمَا فِيهَا»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Sahl ibn Sa'd as-Sa'idi, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The space of a whip in Jannah is better than this world and all it contains."**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The space of a whip means the smallest and humblest of places. A rider would toss his whip down at the spot where he meant to dismount.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

`This world and all it contains` takes in every one of its kingdoms; so the smallest patch of Jannah is better than the greatest kingdom in this world.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-189b" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Like of the Kingdom of One of the Kings of This World"

> عَنِ الْمُغِيرَةِ بْنِ شُعْبَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«سَأَلَ مُوسَى رَبَّهُ: مَا أَدْنَى أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ مَنْزِلَةً؟ قَالَ: هُوَ رَجُلٌ يَجِيءُ بَعْدَ مَا أُدْخِلَ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ الْجَنَّةَ، فَيُقَالُ لَهُ: ادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ. فَيَقُولُ: أَيْ رَبِّ، كَيْفَ وَقَدْ نَزَلَ النَّاسُ مَنَازِلَهُمْ، وَأَخَذُوا أَخَذَاتِهِمْ؟ فَيُقَالُ لَهُ: أَتَرْضَى أَنْ يَكُونَ لَكَ مِثْلُ مُلْكِ مَلِكٍ مِنْ مُلُوكِ الدُّنْيَا؟ فَيَقُولُ: رَضِيتُ رَبِّ. فَيَقُولُ: لَكَ ذَلِكَ، وَمِثْلُهُ، وَمِثْلُهُ، وَمِثْلُهُ، وَمِثْلُهُ. فَقَالَ فِي الْخَامِسَةِ: رَضِيتُ رَبِّ. فَيَقُولُ: هَذَا لَكَ وَعَشَرَةُ أَمْثَالِهِ، وَلَكَ مَا اشْتَهَتْ نَفْسُكَ، وَلَذَّتْ عَيْنُكَ. فَيَقُولُ: رَضِيتُ رَبِّ»** [ثم سأل موسى عن أعلاهم منزلة، وهو تمام الحديث].[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: **"Musa asked his Lord: 'Who is the lowest in rank among the people of Jannah?' He said: 'He is a man who comes after the people of Jannah have been admitted into Jannah. It is said to him, "Enter Jannah." He says, "My Lord, how, when the people have already settled into their places and taken what they have taken?" It is said to him, "Would you be content to have the like of the kingdom of one of the kings of this world?" He says, "I am content, my Lord." He says, "You shall have that, and the like of it, and the like of it, and the like of it, and the like of it." At the fifth, he says, "I am content, my Lord." He says, "This is yours, and ten times as much, and you shall have whatever your soul desires and whatever delights your eye." He says, "I am content, my Lord."'"** [Musa then asked about the highest of them in rank; that is the rest of the hadith.][^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The hadith makes the kings of this world the unit of measure for the lowest rank, multiplies it, and then adds whatever the soul desires and whatever delights the eye.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

If this is the least of the kingdom, then the righteous of the ayah of al-Insan stand many degrees above it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of al-Insan establishes the great kingdom. The account of Sulayman shows the greatest of worldly kingdoms: it was asked for with a plea for forgiveness, received with gratitude, and ended in death, while its owner kept `nearness and a good place of return`. The two hadiths of al-Bukhari and Muslim weigh the whole world against the space of a whip, and make the kingdom of its kings the yardstick for the lowest rank. The kingdom of this world, then, is a gift with which its owner is tested and which then passes away; the kingdom of Jannah is bliss with no test in it and no end to it. We do not rely on the reports giving the numbers of servants and chamberlains, nor on the hadith "he looks over his kingdom across a distance of two thousand years."[^8]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why did Sulayman put `forgive me` before `grant me a kingdom`? What does that teach us about the order of our requests?
2. How does his phrase `to test me` change the way you see a position, wealth or influence that is in your hands right now?
3. What does it signify that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, let the 'ifrit go out of regard for the du'a of his brother Sulayman?
4. Compare the end of Sulayman's kingdom in Saba' 34:14 with what was said of `a great kingdom`: "a kingdom that never passes away."
5. In what area has Allah made you a "small king" today, and how can your gratitude for it be a deed and not only a word?

### Activity: My Small Kingdom and the Gratitude Decree

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.048.activity.my-small-kingdom-gratitude-decree" -->

Draw a table with three columns: "What Allah has placed in my hands" (time, money, my tongue, my home, my children, my work, a social media account), "How I show gratitude for it," and "How I show ingratitude for it." Fill in at least five rows.

Then choose one area and write a one-line "gratitude decree" that begins with "This is from the favor of my Lord" and names a measurable action, for example: "I switch off my phone for an hour every evening for my children."

Carry it out for seven days, and each night ask yourself Sulayman's question, "Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?", and put a mark. On the seventh day, write three lines: What changed? What was governing you instead of you governing it? And what will you keep doing?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

A king is someone who owns lots of land and lots of things, and the people around him do what he says. In Jannah, Allah makes each one of its people like a noble king in his own beautiful home. Allah says: `And when you look there, you will see bliss and a great kingdom`. That means: if you look there, in Jannah, you will see wonderful blessings and a great kingdom.[^1]

And our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that a teeny-tiny place in Jannah, as small as the space of a whip, is better than the whole world and everything in it. A whip is a thin stick that a horse rider carries.[^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: The King Who Said, "This Is from the Favor of My Lord"

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-38-35-40" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-38-35-40" audience="4-7" -->

Sulayman, peace be upon him, was a prophet and a king. He made du'a to his Lord. First he said, "O my Lord, forgive me." Then he said, "And give me a kingdom that no one after me will have." So Allah gave him an amazing kingdom. The wind blew wherever he told it to, soft and gentle, to the place he wanted. The jinn built houses for him and dived deep into the sea. And Allah said to him: "This is Our gift."[^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

One day, Sulayman wanted the throne of a faraway queen brought to him. A throne is a queen's big, grand chair. And the one who had knowledge from the Book brought it faster than the blink of an eye! When Sulayman saw it right there in front of him, he did not show off. He did not say, "Look how great I am!" Instead he said: `This is from the favor of my Lord`. That means: this is a gift Allah gave me, to test me: will I say thank you, or won't I?[^3]

Later, Sulayman's life in this world came to an end, the way every person's life does, and his kingdom in this world stayed behind. But Allah promised him something even more beautiful: `nearness and a good place of return`. That means being close to Allah, with a lovely place waiting for him there.[^2]

So here is what we learn: everything we have now is a present from Allah, and we thank Him for it. And the great kingdom that never ends is waiting for the people of Jannah.

### Activity: My Small Kingdom

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.048.activity.my-small-kingdom-gratitude-decree" -->

With your mom or dad, draw a big circle and name it "My Small Kingdom." Inside it, draw three things Allah has given you, like your hands, your favorite toy, and a plant or a little pet you take care of.

Pick one of them, and agree with your mom or dad on a thank-you action for it, like sharing your toy, watering the plant, or tidying up your toys.

Do it for three days. Each night, say, "This is from the favor of my Lord," and put a star in your circle.

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.048.dua.forgive-us-great-kingdom-good-return" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Ask your friends, "What would you do if you were made king or queen?" One might say, "Build a palace!" Another might say, "Own every game there is!" But the kings of this world get tired, they worry about losing their kingdoms, and one day they leave them behind.

The people of Jannah are different. Allah says about them: `And when you look there, you will see bliss and a great kingdom`.[^1] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the space of a whip in Jannah, a really tiny spot, is `better than this world and all it contains`.[^6] And he told us that the lowest in rank among the people of Jannah is given the like of the kingdom of one of the kings of this world, then several times that, then ten times as much.[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-38-35-40" -->

- **`A great kingdom (mulkan kabira)`** — a vast realm and wide dominion that Allah gives the people of Jannah.[^1]

- **`Gently (rukha'an)`** — soft and calm, not stormy.[^2]

- **`To test me (li-yabluwani)`** — to put me to the test: will I be grateful or not?[^3]

- **`Nearness and a good place of return (zulfa wa husna ma'ab)`** — closeness to Allah, and a good home to return to in the Hereafter.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Greatest Kingdom on Earth

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-38-35-40" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-38-35-40" audience="8-12" -->

Sulayman son of Dawud, peace be upon them both, was a prophet who ruled over people. He raised his hands in a du'a with a careful order to it. He began with `My Lord, forgive me`, and then he said, `and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me`. Allah answered him. He made the wind subject to him, blowing `gently` at his command wherever he wanted it to go, and He placed jinn under his command, some building and some diving into the sea, and any of them who disobeyed were bound in chains. And Allah said to him: `This is Our gift, so bestow or withhold, without account`.[^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Then came a test that would be hard for any king. Sulayman wanted the throne of the Queen of Sheba brought to him before her people came to him in submission. An 'ifrit from among the jinn said, "I'll bring it to you before you get up from your seat." And the one who had knowledge from the Book said, "I'll bring it to you before you can blink." And suddenly, there was the throne, right in front of him! A proud king would have bragged. But Sulayman said: `This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me: will I be grateful, or ungrateful?`[^3]

Yet even with such a kingdom, Sulayman's life came to an end, just as every person's life does. He died leaning on his staff, and the jinn did not know he had died until a tiny creature ate through his staff and he fell.[^4] As for what awaits him, Allah says: `And indeed, he has with Us nearness and a good place of return`.[^2]

Many centuries later, an 'ifrit of the jinn tried to disrupt the prayer of our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Allah gave the Prophet power over him, and he wanted to tie him to a pillar in the mosque so that the people could see him in the morning. Then he remembered the du'a of his brother Sulayman, and he let him go, driven off.[^5]

Think about it: if the greatest kingdom on earth came to an end, and the space of a whip in Jannah is better than the whole world, then what kind of kingdom is waiting for the people of Jannah?

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. Put Sulayman's du'a in order: what did he begin with, and what did he ask for next?
2. What did Sulayman say when he saw the throne in front of him? Why didn't he brag?
3. How did Sulayman's kingdom in this world come to an end? And what did Allah promise him after it?
4. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, let the 'ifrit go instead of tying him up?
5. You're the captain of your team at school. How can your actions say, "This is from the favor of my Lord"?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: My Kingdom Map and the Gratitude Decree

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.048.activity.my-small-kingdom-gratitude-decree" -->

Draw a map of "My Kingdom" with five regions: my time, my words, my room and my things, my allowance, and something I'm responsible for (a little brother or sister, a plant, a role on the team).

Next to each region, write one thank-you action.

Then pick one region, and on a card headed "Gratitude Decree" write: "This is from the favor of my Lord, so I will..." and finish it with a specific action.

Carry it out for five days. Each night, ask, "Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?" and put a mark. On the fifth day, write two sentences about what changed.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a world of followers and views, plenty of people rule over "small kingdoms": an account followed by hundreds, a standing in the group chat, a talent people applaud. And with them comes a question almost nobody asks: Am I the one ruling this kingdom, or is it ruling me?

The Qur'an promises the people of Jannah a real kingdom: `bliss and a great kingdom`. Ibn Kathir explained it as a vast realm and a dazzling dominion, and al-Baghawi recorded the view that it is "a kingdom that never passes away."[^1] To grasp the size of that promise, we measure it against the greatest kingdom the earth has ever known.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-38-35-40" -->

- **`Such as will not belong to anyone after me (la yanbaghi li-ahadin min ba'di)`** — a kingdom whose like no human being after him would have, according to the view Ibn Kathir held to be correct.[^2]

- **`Without account (bi-ghayri hisab)`** — no blame on you in what you give or hold back.[^2]

- **`To test me (li-yabluwani)`** — to put me to the test; a blessing is an exam, not a final reward.[^3]

- **`His staff (minsa'atahu)`** — the staff he was leaning on.[^4]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: A King Without Arrogance

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-38-35-40" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-38-35-40" audience="13+" -->

Sulayman son of Dawud, peace be upon them both, was a prophet and a king. When he called on his Lord, the order of his du'a was striking: `My Lord, forgive me` first, and only then `and grant me a kingdom such as will not belong to anyone after me`. What he received was a kingdom no human being has ever held: a wind that ran gently at his command wherever he wished, jinn who built and dived, the rebellious among them bound in shackles, and then complete authority over it all: `This is Our gift, so bestow or withhold, without account`.[^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Then comes the moment that lays the heart bare. Sulayman asks for the throne of the Queen of Sheba. An 'ifrit from among the jinn offers to bring it before he rises from his seat; the one who had knowledge from the Book offers to bring it before his glance returns to him. And the throne really does come to rest in front of him. If that happened today, it would be on every screen in the world. Yet the first words out of his mouth were: `This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me: will I be grateful, or ungrateful?` As-Sa'di said that he was not deluded by his kingdom, as is the habit of ignorant kings; he knew it was a test.[^3]

The Qur'an tells the ending quietly. Allah decreed death for him while he was leaning on his staff, and nothing showed the jinn that he had died except a creature of the earth eating away at it.[^4] The greatest kingdom on earth was over, and what remained for its owner was something better: `nearness and a good place of return`. As-Sa'di said: do not suppose that this was Sulayman's in this world and not in the Hereafter.[^2]

Centuries later, Allah gave our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, power over an 'ifrit who had broken loose to cut off his prayer. He wanted to tie it to a pillar of the mosque so the people could see it, but then he remembered the du'a of his brother Sulayman, and sent it back, driven off and disgraced.[^5]

The bottom line: the kingdom of this world, even at its very peak, is a gift, a test, and an ending. In Jannah, the space of a whip is `better than this world and all it contains`,[^6] and the kingdom there is bliss with no test after it and no end to it. And your small kingdom today, your phone, your time, your influence, will be held to the very same question: Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did Sulayman begin by asking forgiveness when he was asking for a kingdom without equal?
2. What is the difference between seeing your success as a "reward" and seeing it as a "test"? How does that change the way you act afterward?
3. What does it mean that the greatest kingdom on earth ended with a staff eaten through by a tiny creature?
4. What "kingdom" sometimes rules you instead of you ruling it?
5. How does the hadith of the space of a whip change the way you compare yourself with celebrities and the rich?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: Auditing My Kingdom and the Gratitude Decree

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.048.activity.my-small-kingdom-gratitude-decree" -->

In a private note, write a list called "My Kingdom": my phone and my accounts, my time after school, my talent, my standing among my friends, my money. Next to each item, write: Who rules whom? Me, or it?

Choose the item that rules you the most, and write a one-line "gratitude decree" that begins with "This is from the favor of my Lord, to test me," followed by a specific decision, for example: "No phone after dinner, and I'll use my talent to help a classmate once a week."

Stick to it for seven days, and each night ask, "Will I be grateful, or ungrateful?" and put a mark. Then write a private paragraph: What did you learn about a kingdom that doesn't rule you?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains `a great kingdom` through the words of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and al-Baghawi; narrates the account of Sulayman from Sad 38:35-40, an-Naml 27:38-40 and Saba' 34:14 in its proper order; uses al-Bukhari 3423 and 3250 and Muslim 189 as evidence in comparing the kingdom of this world with the kingdom of Jannah; and writes a gratitude decree for an area over which they have charge.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a handout with the seven texts printed on it; a board with two columns headed "Sulayman's kingdom in this world" and "The kingdom of the people of Jannah"; activity sheets.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on Sad 38:35-40, an-Naml 27:40, Saba' 34:14 and al-Insan 76:20, and al-Baghawi's tafsir of al-Insan 76:20. The teacher also reviews what the lesson does not rely on, as listed in the eighth footnote, and steers clear of the Isra'iliyyat (narratives taken from the People of the Book) about Sulayman's ring and the body cast upon his throne.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If you were given the kingdom of the whole earth, what would you be afraid of losing?" Collect the answers (health, safety, time, staying alive) and write them on the board, then say: "These are exactly what set the kingdom of Jannah apart."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Insan 76:20 with the three tafsirs (4 minutes); then Sad 38:35-40, pausing at the order of the du'a and at how the ayat close (6 minutes); then an-Naml 27:38-40 and Saba' 34:14 (4 minutes); then the three hadiths (6 minutes). Sort the texts into the two columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** Key questions: "What did Sulayman put before the kingdom?"; "How did the arrival of the throne become a test rather than a reward?"; "Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, let the 'ifrit go?"; "What makes the kingdom of Jannah greater: its size, its permanence, the absence of any test, or Allah's good pleasure?" Point out that preferring Jannah does not mean disparaging the blessing of a kingdom in this world, for Allah praised Sulayman in connection with it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each participant fills in the "My Small Kingdom" table and writes their decree in silence (10 minutes); then anyone who wishes may name the area they chose, without private details (2 minutes). Agree on a follow-up after seven days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two differences between Sulayman's kingdom in this world and the great kingdom in Jannah, with evidence for each." Close by reciting an-Naml 27:40 together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only al-Insan 76:20, Sad 38:35 and 40, and the hadith of the space of a whip. Ask advanced learners to gather what the commentators say about `a great kingdom` from the tafsirs of at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi, and to distinguish what is explanation by established transmitted reports from what is an opinion with no evidence behind it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not depict Sulayman, peace be upon him, or any other prophet in drawings or role-play. Do not relate the Isra'iliyyat about his ring or his trial. Do not present the numbers of servants and chamberlains found in weak reports as established. Take care that talk of kingship does not turn into contempt for the poor or the glorification of wealth.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Allah gives the people of Jannah `a great kingdom`; retells in two sentences that Sulayman said "This is from the favor of my Lord" and was not proud; and chooses something they have and thanks Allah for it with an action.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet of paper; crayons or colored pencils; star stickers; a light feather or a hand fan; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver learns the simplified story by heart and draws a "My Small Kingdom" circle on a large sheet of paper for each child.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The caregiver gently waves the fan toward the feather: "Look, the wind is moving the feather. Allah gave Sulayman a wind that blew wherever he told it to, soft and gentle."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** Read the story calmly and reverently, and repeat with the child `This is from the favor of my Lord`, then the ayah `bliss and a great kingdom`.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 5 minutes:** "What did Sulayman ask for first? What did he say when the throne came? Where did your toys and your hands come from?" Then: "When we say, 'This is from the favor of my Lord,' it reminds us to say thank you."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Draw the "My Small Kingdom" activity as described in the activity unit, agree on the thank-you action, and add the stars at home on the following nights.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask: "What did Sulayman say when he saw the throne?" and "What will you do to thank Allah for something in your kingdom?" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one thing in the circle and the sentence "This is from the favor of my Lord" are enough. Ask older children to tell the story in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw Sulayman, peace be upon him, or the jinn. Mention Sulayman's death in one calm sentence, without details. Do not compare a child with others in how many toys or things they have, and choose a thank-you action that costs the family no money.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the four terms; puts the scenes of the story in order, from the du'a to the promise of a good place of return; gives two pieces of evidence that the kingdom of Jannah is greater than the kingdom of this world; and completes their kingdom map and gratitude decree.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; five cards for the scenes of the story (the du'a, the gift, the throne, the end, the hadith of the 'ifrit); the map sheet; the decree card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares two school examples of a "small king": a team captain who looks down on teammates, and a captain who says "This is from the favor of my Lord" and serves the team.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "If you were made king or queen for one day, what would you do?" Then read al-Insan 76:20.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Explain the terms, then read the story; students put the five scene cards in order; then read the hadith of the space of a whip.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the two examples: "Which one is like Sulayman? Why?" Then: "If the space of a whip in Jannah is better than the whole world, how should we look at what we have now?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Carry out the "My Kingdom Map and the Gratitude Decree" activity as described in the activity unit; students keep their cards for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each student writes Sulayman's words from memory and one piece of evidence for the greatness of the kingdom of Jannah; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, cut the map down to three regions. Ask advanced students to explain why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, let the 'ifrit go, with reference to Sad 38:35.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not act out Sulayman or the jinn, and do not ask students to draw them. Present the hadith of the 'ifrit calmly, without frightening anyone. Do not ask students to reveal their allowance or their family's belongings in front of the class.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes Sulayman's stance toward blessing as a test; connects the end of his kingdom to the promise of `nearness and a good place of return` and to the great kingdom in Jannah; and identifies an area of their life that "rules" them and sets a seven-day gratitude decree for it.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private activity sheet; three written cases: "An account with thousands of followers that controls its owner's mood," "A top student who looks down on classmates," and "A talented athlete who coaches younger kids for free."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews as-Sa'di's tafsir of an-Naml 27:40 and of Sad 38:39-40, and prepares a balanced answer to the question "Is it wrong to aim for power and success in this world?": Sulayman asked for a kingdom and Allah praised him; the measure is gratitude and how it is used, not simply owning it.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "Who rules whom: you or your phone?" A quick show of hands, with no comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read Sad 38:35-40, an-Naml 27:38-40 and Saba' 34:14 from the adults' academic section, then the hadiths of the 'ifrit and of the space of a whip, focusing on `to test me`.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the three cases in groups: "Who treats their blessing the way Sulayman did? And who is ruled by their blessing?" Then ask: "Why do the ayat of Surah Sad close with nearness to Allah, and not with the kingdom?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students carry out the "Auditing My Kingdom and the Gratitude Decree" activity individually as described in the activity unit, and keep their sheets.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** A short paragraph: "How do I own my blessing without letting it own me?" A week later, ask the students a general question, "Did you finish the seven days?", without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For anyone who finds self-auditing difficult, a single item is enough. Ask advanced students to summarize what the commentators say about `a great kingdom` and to show which view has the strongest evidence.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to disclose their accounts, follower counts or money. Giving up the phone is not presented as a punishment, but as a free decision. Do not act out the prophets, do not dwell on details about the jinn, and do not relate the Isra'iliyyat.
