# The Poor and the Weak of the Ummah in the Sight of Allah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Cite three authentic hadiths showing that the weak of the ummah and its sincere poor hold a great station with Allah: the hadith `Every weak one, looked down upon by others: were he to swear by Allah, Allah would fulfill his oath`; the hadith of the poor among the Emigrants reaching Jannah ahead of the rich; and the hadith `I stood at the gate of Jannah, and most of those who entered it were the poor`.
- Show that this virtue does not make wealth blameworthy in itself, reasoning from two facts: "the people of fortune are held back" means held back for reckoning, not shut out of Jannah; and a number of senior Companions who were wealthy, such as Uthman ibn Affan and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with them both, were among those given the glad tidings of Jannah.
- Narrate the authenticated account from Sahih al-Bukhari of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the People of the Suffah, and the cup of milk, and draw out its moment of choice: between his own longing to drink first, with hunger gripping him hard, and his obedience to the Prophet's command, peace and blessings be upon him, to put his poor brothers ahead of himself.
- Explain the meaning of *ithar* (putting others before oneself) and connect it to what Abu Hurayrah did, showing that what raises a person in Allah's sight is patience in poverty joined with contentment, not poverty itself.
- Carry out the "Share Before Yourself" activity by practicing *ithar* in one real situation in the family, at school, or in the community, and recording how it felt.
- Recite a bedtime du'a that asks for humility and gratitude, for patience and contentment in poverty, and for protection from the trial of wealth, should wealth be given.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Nearness to Allah is not measured by the size of a fortune or the height of a social position. It is measured by a truthful heart, by patience in need and gratitude in plenty, and by humility before Allah and before His servants. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, singled out the weak of his ummah and its sincere poor for remarkable glad tidings. Some of them will reach Jannah long ages before the rich. Some are so dear to Allah that, were they to swear an oath by Him, He would fulfill it, though people think little of them. And, as the Prophet himself, peace and blessings be upon him, reported, they make up most of the people of Jannah.

For all that, wealth is not blameworthy in itself. A number of the senior Companions who were wealthy were given the glad tidings of Jannah, among them Uthman ibn Affan and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with them both. The true measure, then, is what the heart does with its circumstances, whether it is poor or rich. Does it bear patiently, give thanks, and put others first? Or does it fall apart when it is poor and grow insolent when it is rich? There is no truer witness to this than the life of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, among the People of the Suffah: real poverty, and with it *ithar* and obedience. That is what raised him, rather than bringing him low.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6071" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Shall I Not Tell You Who the People of Jannah Are? Every Weak One, Looked Down Upon by Others"

> عَنْ حَارِثَةَ بْنِ وَهْبٍ الْخُزَاعِيِّ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقُولُ: **«أَلَا أُخْبِرُكُمْ بِأَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ؟ كُلُّ ضَعِيفٍ مُتَضَعِّفٍ، لَوْ أَقْسَمَ عَلَى اللَّهِ لَأَبَرَّهُ، أَلَا أُخْبِرُكُمْ بِأَهْلِ النَّارِ؟ كُلُّ عُتُلٍّ جَوَّاظٍ مُسْتَكْبِرٍ»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Harithah ibn Wahb al-Khuza'i, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"Shall I not tell you who the people of Jannah are? Every weak one, looked down upon by others: were he to swear by Allah, Allah would fulfill his oath. Shall I not tell you who the people of the Fire are? Every harsh, hoarding, arrogant one."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar, may Allah have mercy on him, says in *Fath al-Bari* that "the weak one, looked down upon" (*ad-da'if al-mutada'af*) is the person whom people regard as weak and whose worth they dismiss in this world, because of his poverty or his quiet voice, though his standing with Allah is so great that, were he to swear an oath by Allah, Allah would fulfill it. Against him the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, set the description of the people of the Fire: *al-'utull*, the harsh and coarse; *al-jawwaz*, the one who amasses and withholds; and *al-mustakbir*, the one who is arrogant toward the truth and toward people. The fault the hadith points to, then, is not wealth itself, but the hardness and arrogance that can travel with wealth when it is not met with gratitude.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith sets the true scale: someone despised in this world may be among the greatest of people in Allah's sight. What counts is the state of the heart, not the state of the wallet.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2351" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "The Poor Among the Emigrants Will Enter Jannah Before Their Rich"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«فُقَرَاءُ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ يَدْخُلُونَ الْجَنَّةَ قَبْلَ أَغْنِيَائِهِمْ بِخَمْسِمِائَةِ سَنَةٍ»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The poor among the Emigrants will enter Jannah five hundred years before their rich."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The poor among the Emigrants (*al-Muhajirun*) were those who left their wealth, their families, and their homeland, emigrating to Allah and His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, until nothing of this world was left to them. In them the emigration of the heart met the emptiness of the hand. The scholars have noted that this precedence honors the sincerity of their poverty and their patience; it is not a blanket ruling that every rich person is denied this virtue. A rich person who is sincere and grateful may attain a like precedence, or something greater still, through his charity and his spending.

#### Lesson Explanation

The precedence here is a matter of time, not a verdict on final rank. The sincere poor person may enter first, while the grateful rich person who spends freely may hold a higher degree in Jannah, if his good deeds are many and his gratitude is true. This is no contest between rich and poor. It is a call to each of them to be true to Allah in whatever state he finds himself.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5196" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Stood at the Gate of Jannah, and Most of Those Who Entered It Were the Poor"

> عَنْ أُسَامَةَ بْنِ زَيْدٍ رضي الله عنهما قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«قُمْتُ عَلَى بَابِ الْجَنَّةِ، فَإِذَا عَامَّةُ مَنْ دَخَلَهَا الْمَسَاكِينُ، وَأَصْحَابُ الْجَدِّ مَحْبُوسُونَ، غَيْرَ أَصْحَابِ النَّارِ فَقَدْ أُمِرَ بِهِمْ إِلَى النَّارِ، وَقُمْتُ عَلَى بَابِ النَّارِ، فَإِذَا عَامَّةُ مَنْ دَخَلَهَا النِّسَاءُ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Usamah ibn Zayd, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"I stood at the gate of Jannah, and most of those who entered it were the poor, while the people of fortune were held back, except for the people of the Fire, who had already been ordered to the Fire. And I stood at the gate of the Fire, and most of those who entered it were women."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar, may Allah have mercy on him, explained that "the people of fortune" (*ashab al-jadd*) are those who enjoyed wealth, good fortune, and standing in this world, and that "held back" here does not mean they are barred from Jannah. It means they are detained for a long reckoning over the wealth and blessings they were given: how they earned them and on what they spent them. Once their reckoning is complete, those of them who are to enter Jannah will enter it; but the length of that reckoning delays them, while the poor go on ahead, since they are not questioned about what the rich are questioned about.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith closes the door on reading the virtue of the poor as a sentence of exclusion passed on the rich. The delay comes from a long reckoning, not a refusal of entry. It is a reminder to everyone on whom Allah has bestowed plenty that a blessing is a trust, and every trust will be asked about.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6452" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of Abu Hurayrah, May Allah Be Pleased with Him, on His Hunger and the People of the Suffah

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: أَصَابَنِي جَهْدٌ شَدِيدٌ، فَلَقِيتُ عُمَرَ بْنَ الْخَطَّابِ رضي الله عنه فَسَأَلْتُهُ عَنْ آيَةٍ مِنْ كِتَابِ اللَّهِ، مَا سَأَلْتُهُ إِلَّا لِيُشْبِعَنِي، فَمَرَّ وَلَمْ يَفْعَلْ، ثُمَّ لَقِيتُ أَبَا بَكْرٍ رضي الله عنه فَسَأَلْتُهُ عَنِ الْآيَةِ، مَا سَأَلْتُهُ إِلَّا لِيُشْبِعَنِي، فَمَرَّ وَلَمْ يَفْعَلْ، حَتَّى مَرَّ بِي أَبُو الْقَاسِمِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَتَبَسَّمَ حِينَ رَآنِي، وَعَرَفَ مَا فِي نَفْسِي وَمَا فِي وَجْهِي، ثُمَّ قَالَ: **«يَا أَبَا هِرٍّ»**، قُلْتُ: لَبَّيْكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، قَالَ: **«الْحَقْ»**، وَمَضَى فَاتَّبَعْتُهُ، فَدَخَلَ فَاسْتَأْذَنَ فَأُذِنَ لِي فَدَخَلْتُ، فَوَجَدَ لَبَنًا فِي قَدَحٍ، فَقَالَ: **«مِنْ أَيْنَ هَذَا اللَّبَنُ؟»** قَالُوا: أَهْدَاهُ لَكَ فُلَانٌ، قَالَ: **«يَا أَبَا هِرٍّ»**، قُلْتُ: لَبَّيْكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، قَالَ: **«الْحَقْ إِلَى أَهْلِ الصُّفَّةِ فَادْعُهُمْ لِي»**.
>
> قَالَ أَبُو هُرَيْرَةَ: وَأَهْلُ الصُّفَّةِ أَضْيَافُ الْإِسْلَامِ، لَا يَأْوُونَ إِلَى أَهْلٍ وَلَا مَالٍ وَلَا عَلَى أَحَدٍ، إِذَا أَتَتْهُ صَدَقَةٌ بَعَثَ بِهَا إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَمْ يَتَنَاوَلْ مِنْهَا شَيْئًا، وَإِذَا أَتَتْهُ هَدِيَّةٌ أَرْسَلَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَأَصَابَ مِنْهَا وَأَشْرَكَهُمْ فِيهَا. فَسَاءَنِي ذَلِكَ، فَقُلْتُ: وَمَا هَذَا اللَّبَنُ فِي أَهْلِ الصُّفَّةِ؟ كُنْتُ أَحَقُّ أَنَا أَنْ أُصِيبَ مِنْ هَذَا اللَّبَنِ شَرْبَةً أَتَقَوَّى بِهَا، فَإِذَا جَاءُوا أَمَرَنِي فَكُنْتُ أَنَا أُعْطِيهِمْ، وَمَا عَسَى أَنْ يَبْلُغَنِي مِنْ هَذَا اللَّبَنِ؟ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ مِنْ طَاعَةِ اللَّهِ وَطَاعَةِ رَسُولِهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم بُدٌّ، فَأَتَيْتُهُمْ فَدَعَوْتُهُمْ فَأَقْبَلُوا، فَاسْتَأْذَنُوا فَأُذِنَ لَهُمْ وَأَخَذُوا مَجَالِسَهُمْ مِنَ الْبَيْتِ، قَالَ: **«يَا أَبَا هِرٍّ»**، قُلْتُ: لَبَّيْكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، قَالَ: **«خُذْ فَأَعْطِهِمْ»**.
>
> قَالَ: فَأَخَذْتُ الْقَدَحَ فَجَعَلْتُ أُعْطِيهِ الرَّجُلَ فَيَشْرَبُ حَتَّى يَرْوَى، ثُمَّ يَرُدُّ عَلَيَّ الْقَدَحَ فَأُعْطِيهِ الرَّجُلَ الْآخَرَ حَتَّى يَرْوَى، ثُمَّ يَرُدُّ عَلَيَّ الْقَدَحَ، حَتَّى انْتَهَيْتُ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم وَقَدْ رَوِيَ الْقَوْمُ كُلُّهُمْ، فَأَخَذَ الْقَدَحَ فَوَضَعَهُ عَلَى يَدِهِ، فَنَظَرَ إِلَيَّ فَتَبَسَّمَ، فَقَالَ: **«يَا أَبَا هِرٍّ»**، قُلْتُ: لَبَّيْكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، قَالَ: **«بَقِيتُ أَنَا وَأَنْتَ»**، قُلْتُ: صَدَقْتَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، قَالَ: **«اقْعُدْ فَاشْرَبْ»**، فَقَعَدْتُ فَشَرِبْتُ، فَقَالَ: **«اشْرَبْ»**، فَشَرِبْتُ، فَمَا زَالَ يَقُولُ: **«اشْرَبْ»** حَتَّى قُلْتُ: لَا وَالَّذِي بَعَثَكَ بِالْحَقِّ، مَا أَجِدُ لَهُ مَسْلَكًا، قَالَ: **«فَأَرِنِي»**، فَأَعْطَيْتُهُ الْقَدَحَ فَحَمِدَ اللَّهَ وَسَمَّى وَشَرِبَ الْفَضْلَةَ.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I was worn down by severe hunger. I met Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, and asked him about an ayah from the Book of Allah, and I asked him for no other reason than that he might feed me. He walked on and did not do it. Then I met Abu Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him, and asked him about the ayah, and I asked him for no other reason than that he might feed me. He walked on and did not do it. Then Abu al-Qasim, peace and blessings be upon him, passed by me. He smiled when he saw me, for he knew what was in my heart and what showed on my face. Then he said: **"O Abu Hirr!"** [an affectionate form of Abu Hurayrah's name]. I said: "At your service, O Messenger of Allah!" He said: **"Come along."** He went on, and I followed him. He went in; I asked leave to enter, was given it, and went in. He found milk in a bowl and asked: **"Where has this milk come from?"** They said: "So-and-so sent it to you as a gift." He said: **"O Abu Hirr!"** I said: "At your service, O Messenger of Allah!" He said: **"Go to the People of the Suffah and call them to me."**
>
> Abu Hurayrah said: The People of the Suffah were the guests of Islam. They had no family, no wealth, and no one to fall back on. Whenever charity came to him, he would send it on to them and take none of it himself; and whenever a gift came to him, he would send for them, take some of it himself, and share it with them. That troubled me, and I said to myself: "What is this milk among all the People of the Suffah? I had more right to a drink of this milk to get my strength back. When they come, he will tell me to serve them, and I will be the one handing it out. How much of this milk is likely to reach me?" But there was no way around obeying Allah and obeying His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him. So I went to them and called them, and they came. They asked leave to enter, were given it, and took their places in the house. He said: **"O Abu Hirr!"** I said: "At your service, O Messenger of Allah!" He said: **"Take it and give it to them."**
>
> He said: So I took the bowl and began handing it to one man, who would drink until he had his fill and then hand the bowl back to me. Then I would give it to the next man, who would drink until he had his fill and hand the bowl back to me, until I came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and every one of them had drunk his fill. He took the bowl, set it on his hand, looked at me, and smiled. He said: **"O Abu Hirr!"** I said: "At your service, O Messenger of Allah!" He said: **"Only you and I are left."** I said: "You have spoken the truth, O Messenger of Allah." He said: **"Sit down and drink."** So I sat down and drank. He said: **"Drink."** So I drank. He kept on saying, **"Drink,"** until I said: "No, by the One who sent you with the truth, I can find no more room for it!" He said: **"Then show it to me."** I gave him the bowl, and he praised Allah, said His name, and drank what was left.[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The hadith shows how severe the poverty of the People of the Suffah was, and how the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, preferred them with whatever reached him, even from his own provision. It also shows that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, did not hide his honest wish to drink first, and he was not taken to task for that natural thought. His merit lay in refusing to put his own desire ahead of the command of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He obeyed, and served his brothers first until all of them had drunk their fill; then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, honored him by drinking with him at the very end.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is the authenticated account at the heart of this whole lesson. There is real poverty, harsh enough that he bound a stone against his stomach. There is a natural human urge to go ahead of the other hungry men. And, despite it, there is obedience and *ithar*, as Abu Hurayrah weighed his immediate craving against the command of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. The outcome: everyone drank their fill, and he too drank his fill at the end, alongside the Prophet himself, peace and blessings be upon him. He lost nothing. Instead, he was honored with the nearness and warm companionship of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. This is precisely what he meant, peace and blessings be upon him, by `every weak one, looked down upon by others`: a poor man no one notices, who in Allah's sight is among the people of truthfulness and *ithar*.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the difference between the poor reaching Jannah first in time and holding a higher rank within it? And why does this not conflict with the glad tidings given to some wealthy Companions, such as Uthman and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with them both?
2. How does explaining "the people of fortune are held back" as being held back for reckoning prevent the hadith from being read as a condemnation of wealth in itself?
3. Why was Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, not taken to task for his inner wish to drink first? And where did the real merit of his conduct lie?
4. Describe a real situation in your life in which your own desire pulled against putting someone else first. How did it end?

### Activity: "Share Before Yourself"

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.018.activity.share-before-self" -->

Choose a real day this week on which you deliberately put someone else ahead of yourself in a small, concrete situation: the best portion of food, your place in line or your turn to be served, or your time and attention for someone who genuinely needs it. Write down: whom did you put first, and what did you feel as you did it? Then connect your experience to Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who gave drink to every one of the People of the Suffah before drinking himself, though in his heart he wished he could drink first. Close with one sentence describing how humility and *ithar* can become a path to a higher rank with Allah, whatever your material circumstances, rich or poor.

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

Allah loves people who stay patient when they don't have very much, and He loves people who share with their friends instead of taking for themselves first. Our Prophet Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that his poor friends who stayed patient are very, very dear to Allah, and that one of them did something really beautiful, even though he was hungry.

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Event: Abu Hurayrah and the Cup of Milk

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="bukhari-6452" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened to a Companion of the Prophet named Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not a made-up story.**

Abu Hurayrah was a friend and Companion of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He was very poor and had no home. One day he got so hungry, so very hungry, that he tied a stone against his tummy to make the hurting feel smaller. He sat down by the path where the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would walk by. Along came the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and he could tell just from Abu Hurayrah's face that he was hungry. So he said to him, `Come with me`, and Abu Hurayrah went with him to his house.[^4]

There the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, found a cup of milk that someone had given him as a present. He said to Abu Hurayrah, `Go and call the People of the Suffah for me`. They were his poor friends who had no homes, so they slept in the mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Deep down inside, Abu Hurayrah wished he could drink first, because he was so hungry. But he obeyed the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and went and called all his friends.[^4]

Abu Hurayrah stood and gave each friend a drink until he had all he wanted, then passed the cup to the next one, one man after another, until everybody had a drink and only he and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, were left! Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, smiled and said to him, `Now it's just you and me`, and then, `Sit down and drink`. Abu Hurayrah drank until he was full, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, drank the very last of the milk in the cup.[^4]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6452" audience="4-7" -->

So here is what it all means, in simple words: Abu Hurayrah was very hungry and wanted to drink first, but he gave his friends the milk before himself, because the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him to. He didn't lose anything at all! He got to drink too in the end, and he was happy that he had shared.[^4]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the weak and the sincere poor of this ummah have a great place with Allah. Their place is so great that if one of them swore an oath by Allah, Allah would make it come true, and many of the people who enter Jannah are poor people who stayed patient. Even so, being rich is not bad in itself. Some of the wealthy Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, like Uthman ibn Affan and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, were also given the good news of Jannah. So what really matters is not how much or how little money someone has, but how they handle what Allah has given them. Are they patient? Are they thankful? Do they share with others?

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6452" -->

- **`Putting others first (al-ithar)`** — letting someone else go ahead of you in something you need too, not just handing over what you have left over.
- **`The weak one, looked down upon (ad-da'if al-mutada'af)`** — someone people think little of, or don't even notice, even though their worth with Allah is great.

### An Authenticated Account: Abu Hurayrah and the Night of the Milk with the People of the Suffah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="bukhari-6452" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, told in his own words, recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, was one of the Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He was poor, with no home and no regular meals, and one day hunger hit him so hard that he tied a stone against his stomach to bear it. He sat by the path where the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would pass, and asked some of the people walking by about an ayah of the Qur'an, hoping one of them would understand what he really needed and give him something to eat. Abu Bakr and Umar, may Allah be pleased with them, passed by without realizing he was hungry. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came by. He smiled when he saw him, understood his need from the look on his face, and said to him, `O Abu Hirr!` Abu Hurayrah answered, "At your service, O Messenger of Allah!" The Prophet said, `Come along`, and Abu Hurayrah followed him all the way into his house.[^4]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, found a bowl of milk that someone had given him as a gift, and said, `Go to the People of the Suffah and call them to me`. The People of the Suffah were poor Emigrants with no family and no money, who lived in the mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Because he was so hungry, Abu Hurayrah secretly wished the milk could be his before it was theirs. He said to himself, "I have more right to a drink that would give me my strength back." But he found no way around obeying Allah and His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, so he went and called them all.[^4]

Abu Hurayrah took the bowl and began handing it to one man, who drank until he had his fill and then passed the bowl back, and then he handed it to the next man, and so on, until he reached the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and every last one of them had drunk his fill! The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, took the bowl, looked at Abu Hurayrah, smiled, and said, `Only you and I are left`. Abu Hurayrah said, "You have spoken the truth, O Messenger of Allah." The Prophet told him, `Sit down and drink`, and he drank until he said, "By Allah, I have no room left for it!" Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, praised Allah and drank what was left.[^4]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6452" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: Abu Hurayrah was terribly hungry and wished he could drink the milk first, but he obeyed the command of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and served every man among the People of the Suffah until each had his fill, before he himself drank last, together with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. He lost nothing. Instead, Allah honored him with the nearness and warm company of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^4]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

It's easy to assume that a person's worth is measured by what they own: where they live, what they wear, what they can afford compared with their friends. But the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, turned that scale completely upside down when he said: `Every weak one, looked down upon by others: were he to swear by Allah, Allah would fulfill his oath`. The person who looks small in everyone's eyes may be the greatest of them in Allah's sight. This is not a call to scorn blessings or to make a show of being poor. Senior Companions who were wealthy, such as Uthman ibn Affan and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with them both, were given the glad tidings of Jannah. The measure is not money. It is what the heart does with it: patience when there is little, gratitude when there is much, and *ithar* either way.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6452" -->

- **`Putting others first (al-ithar)`** — preferring others over yourself when you have a real need of your own, not merely giving up whatever is surplus to your needs.
- **`The people of fortune (ashab al-jadd)`** — those blessed with wealth and good fortune in this world; in the hadith, they are held back for a long reckoning over their blessings, not shut out of Jannah.
- **`The guests of Islam (adyaf al-islam)`** — a description of the People of the Suffah, who had no shelter and no wealth, so the Muslim community, led by the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, took responsibility for them.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authenticated Account: The Choice of Abu Hurayrah, May Allah Be Pleased with Him, Between His Hunger and His Obedience

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="bukhari-6452" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, told in his own words, recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, describes in his own words one of the hardest moments of his poverty. Hunger had worn him down, so he went around the senior Companions asking them about an ayah of the Qur'an. He did not need the knowledge; he was hoping one of them would notice the state he was in and feed him. Abu Bakr and Umar, may Allah be pleased with them, came by, and he asked them, but they walked on without doing so. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came by. He read in Abu Hurayrah's face what was going on inside him, smiled, and said, `O Abu Hirr!` Then he took him home with him.[^4]

At home, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, found a bowl of milk that had been given to him as a gift, and told Abu Hurayrah to go and call the People of the Suffah to him. They were the "guests of Islam," with no family, no wealth, and no clan, who lived in the mosque of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Here Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, is strikingly honest about what went on inside him; he neither hides it nor dresses it up: `That troubled me, and I said: What is this milk among all the People of the Suffah? I had more right to a drink of this milk to get my strength back`. He really was hungry, and wanting to drink first was a natural human wish with nothing shameful about it. But he added straight away: `There was no way around obeying Allah and obeying His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him`. He placed obedience above his immediate craving, and went and called them all.[^4]

Abu Hurayrah came back and served the People of the Suffah one man at a time. Each drank until he had his fill, then handed back the bowl so it could go to the next, and the whole time Abu Hurayrah knew that his own share might shrink, or run out, before his turn ever came. When he reached the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with every one of them satisfied, the Prophet smiled, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: `Only you and I are left`. The milk had not run out as Abu Hurayrah had feared. Instead, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him it was his turn to drink now, and he drank until he said: `No, by the One who sent you with the truth, I can find no more room for it`. Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, drank what was left.[^4]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6452" audience="13+" -->

Put another way: at the height of his hunger, Abu Hurayrah faced a real choice. He could drink first, as everything in him longed to do, or he could obey the command of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to put his poor brothers ahead of himself, with no way of knowing whether anything would be left for him. Despite his very real need, he chose obedience and *ithar*. He served everyone first, and then Allah honored him: he too drank until he was satisfied, alongside the Prophet himself, peace and blessings be upon him.[^4]

<!-- retelling:end -->

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="50" -->

### Adults — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner distinguishes between the merit of "going first" in time and the merit of "rising higher" in rank, interprets "the people of fortune are held back" correctly, in a way that does not condemn wealth in itself, and applies *ithar* to a real situation in their own life.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a board for recording the differences between going first and rising higher; a sheet for the "Share Before Yourself" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** Before the session, the teacher reviews the four hadiths and their gradings, and prepares a neutral personal example of a time they put someone else before themselves, to share if needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask the learners: "Do you think wealth keeps a person out of Jannah? And why was Uthman ibn Affan given the glad tidings of Jannah when he was one of the wealthiest of the Companions?" Listen to their answers before presenting the evidence.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Read the four hadiths in order: the hadith of Harithah ibn Wahb (the weak one, looked down upon), then the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (the poor going first), then the hadith of Usamah ibn Zayd (the gate of Jannah and the gate of the Fire), then the hadith of Abu Hurayrah in full, pausing at the moment he admits his inner wish.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** The teacher discusses with the learners the difference between the merit of going first and the merit of rising higher, and explains that "the people of fortune are held back" means held back for reckoning, not shut out. The teacher then connects Abu Hurayrah's inner state (his wish to drink first) with his outward conduct (his obedience and *ithar*), making clear that the merit lay in what he did, not in erasing a natural human desire.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Explain the "Share Before Yourself" activity and fix a day during the week to carry it out, agreeing on a time to share the experience afterward.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Ask each learner to explain aloud the difference between going first and rising higher, and to name one wealthy Companion who was given the glad tidings of Jannah, as proof that wealth is not condemned in itself.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, limit the material to the hadith of "the weak one, looked down upon" and the story of Abu Hurayrah; for advanced learners, add a discussion of how to reconcile the hadith of the poor going first with the hadith of the people of fortune being held back.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Take the greatest care that no one leaves with the idea that wealth is blameworthy or that a family of comfortable means is any less near to Allah. Repeat throughout that the measure is patience, gratitude, and *ithar*, not money itself, and do not let the activity turn into a public material comparison between participants.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="18" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 18 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child can retell the main idea, that Abu Hurayrah gave his friends the milk before himself even though he was hungry, and tries out an act of sharing once on their own.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** An empty cup for acting out the story; paper and colors; a du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver prepares the simplified story and practices acting it out with an empty cup, pouring out pretend "milk" for dolls or for the child's friends.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "Have you ever given your friend something you love before taking it for yourself?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Story — 6 minutes:** Tell the story of Abu Hurayrah in a lively voice, acting it out with the empty cup when the milk is handed around to the friends.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 3 minutes:** In simple words, the caregiver explains that Allah loves those who give to their friends before themselves, and that Abu Hurayrah lost nothing when he did so.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 4 minutes:** The child carries out the "Share First" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 2 minutes:** Observe whether the child can say what Abu Hurayrah did before he drank himself; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, one example acted out with the cup is enough, without the drawing.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Avoid describing Abu Hurayrah's hunger in detail that could frighten the child; focus on the joy of sharing and on being close to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, at the end of the story. Make sure no child whose family has limited means is made to feel embarrassed.

### Children Ages 8-12 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the meaning of *ithar* and of the People of the Suffah, connects the story of Abu Hurayrah to a real situation in their own life, and understands that poverty is not a sign of being far from Allah's love.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher discusses with the students: What did Abu Hurayrah wish for? What did he do in spite of it? And why doesn't this lesson mean that being rich is a bad thing?

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 2 minutes:** Assess the students' answers by how accurately they explain the difference between what Abu Hurayrah wished for and what he actually did; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, cover only the hadith of "the weak one, looked down upon" and the story of the bowl of milk; ask advanced students to discuss the meaning of "the people of fortune are held back."

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the relationship between natural human desire and freely chosen obedience in Abu Hurayrah's conduct, connects the idea of "the weak one, looked down upon" to a present-day social pressure around appearances or possessions, and follows through on a practical commitment to *ithar*.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the four hadiths; the term cards; an individual commitment sheet.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares for a discussion of peer pressure tied to material appearances, as a present-day picture of what "the weak one, looked down upon" and "the people of fortune" mean.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask the teens: "Have you ever felt that your worth was being measured by what you own compared with your friends?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the four hadiths, with special focus on Abu Hurayrah's frank admission of his inner wish before he obeyed.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher discusses the difference between natural human desire (nothing blameworthy in it) and moral choice (where accountability and merit lie), and how this applies to a present-day social pressure involving money or appearances.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students carry out the "Put Others First" activity as described in the activity unit, and name someone who will follow up on the commitment after a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each teen writes two sentences connecting their own situation to Abu Hurayrah's.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Anyone hesitant to share their situation aloud may write it privately and hand it to the teacher alone.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The teacher must take care not to pressure any teen into disclosing sensitive financial or social details in front of the group, and must avoid any suggestion that poverty is something to seek for its own sake or that wealth is a fault.
