# The Poor, the Weak, and the Great Numbers of Muhammad's ﷺ Nation in Paradise

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.intro" kind="explanation" -->
The measure of closeness to Jannah is not wealth, status, or worldly power -- it is sincerity of heart with Allah, gratitude, patience, and humility. The Prophet ﷺ gave glad tidings to the sincere poor and the humble weak that many of them would precede the wealthy of this world into Jannah, and that they would make up the majority of its people. At the same time, the grateful wealthy person is not excluded from this favor at all -- many wealthy Companions were given the glad tidings of Jannah, such as Uthman ibn Affan and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with them. The true lesson is not about how much or how little money a person has, but about what the heart does with it: does it give thanks, or does it grow arrogant?
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="14" -->

## For Adults

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.adults.objectives" kind="explanation" -->
**Lesson Objectives:**
- Understand that the poor preceding the rich into Jannah, and the weak and poor forming the majority of its people, does not mean wealth is a fault or that the wealthy are excluded from Jannah.
- Distinguish between the "merit of precedence" (entering Jannah first) and the "merit of elevation" (a higher rank within it), and recognize that these may combine or occur separately.
- Recall that the nation of Muhammad ﷺ is hoped to be half of the people of Jannah or more, as a favor from Allah upon this nation.
- Connect this favor to gratitude, patience, and humility -- not to a person's outward condition of wealth or poverty.
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.adults.interpretation" kind="explanation" -->
#### Scholarly Interpretation

Hafiz Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, may Allah have mercy on him, explains in his commentary on the hadith of Haritha ibn Wahb that "the weak, deemed-lowly one" (*ad-da'if al-mutada'af*) refers to a person whom others consider insignificant and look down upon in this world -- because of his poverty or his quiet, unassuming voice -- even though his standing before Allah is so great that if he swore an oath upon Allah, Allah would fulfill it for him. The true scale in Allah's sight is taqwa and sincerity, not social appearance. Imam an-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, says something similar in his commentary on Sahih Muslim, clarifying that this does not mean the grateful wealthy person is blamed -- it means Allah elevates those to whom the world pays no attention.

As for reconciling the narration of "forty autumns" with the narration of "five hundred years," Imam al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on them, explained that this difference reflects the differing circumstances of the individuals themselves: some poor believers precede the wealthy by a longer span than others, according to their sincerity and patience. This is not a contradiction between the two narrations but a variation among the different people the promise describes.

The most precise principle in this whole topic -- and it is the essential key to this lesson -- is what Imam Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, established in *Hadi al-Arwah*, where he distinguished between two separate merits: the "merit of precedence" (entering first) and the "merit of elevation" (a higher rank and station). He wrote: "*So there are two merits: a merit of precedence, and a merit of elevation, and the two may combine or occur separately.*" A sincere poor person may enter before a wealthy one, yet a grateful, generous wealthy person may hold a higher rank in Jannah than that same poor person, if his good deeds are many and his gratitude is sincere. Precedence in time does not necessarily entail a higher station. With this, the apparent difficulty is resolved: all favor belongs to Allah, who grants it to whomever He wills according to the sincerity of the heart and its deeds -- not according to a person's outward appearance in this world, whether wealthy or poor.

A number of the greatest Companions were themselves wealthy and were given the glad tidings of Jannah -- Uthman ibn Affan, Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, and al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam, may Allah be pleased with them. This shows that wealth in itself is not blameworthy; what is blameworthy is wealth that distracts a person from gratitude, charity, and humility before Allah.

### Authenticated Account: The Patience of the People of Suffah and the Prophet's ﷺ Preference for Them

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6452" audience="adults" -->
Imam al-Bukhari records in his Sahih, on the authority of Abu Hurairah, may Allah be pleased with him, that on one occasion severe hunger overtook him -- so severe that he tied a stone against his stomach because of it -- and he sat along the Prophet's ﷺ path, asking about a verse of the Qur'an in the hope that a Companion would understand his need and feed him. Abu Bakr and Umar, may Allah be pleased with them, passed by and did not perceive his condition. Then the Prophet ﷺ passed by, smiled when he saw him, and recognized his need from the look on his face. He said, "O Abu Hirr [Abu Hurairah]," and Abu Hurairah answered, "At your service, O Messenger of Allah." The Prophet ﷺ told him to follow him, and they entered his house, where they found a bowl of milk. The Prophet ﷺ asked, "Where did this milk come from?" and was told it was a gift. He said, "O Abu Hirr, go to the people of Suffah and invite them to me." The people of Suffah were the poor guests of Islam who had no family, no money, and no clan to shelter them, and they lived in the Prophet's ﷺ mosque.

Abu Hurairah went to invite them, secretly hoping in his heart that the Prophet ﷺ would give him some of the milk before them -- yet he could do nothing but obey. When they had gathered, the Prophet ﷺ instructed Abu Hurairah to give the bowl to each of them in turn to drink his fill, then return it to him. Abu Hurairah did this, man after man, until every one of them had drunk and was satisfied, and finally there remained only himself and the Prophet ﷺ. The Prophet ﷺ took the bowl, placed it in his hand, looked at Abu Hurairah, smiled, and said, "There remain you and I." Abu Hurairah said, "You have spoken the truth, O Messenger of Allah." He said, "Sit and drink," so he drank, and the Prophet ﷺ kept saying "Drink" until Abu Hurairah said, "No, by the One who sent you with the truth, I find no more room for it." The Prophet ﷺ then took the bowl, praised Allah, mentioned His name, and drank what remained.

Despite this poverty and severe need, the people of Suffah did not complain in resentment, nor did they envy the wealthy; rather, they were patient and content, seeking their reward from Allah. The Prophet ﷺ honored them and gave them preference whenever a gift or charity reached him, sitting among them and teaching them -- until he said of them and others like them among the weak, as mentioned above: "Every person deemed weak and lowly, who, were he to swear an oath upon Allah, Allah would fulfill it for him." This is a living example of sincere poverty joined with patience and contentment -- a cause of their elevation before Allah, not of their disgrace.

### Questions for Reflection
1. What is the difference between the "merit of precedence" and the "merit of elevation," as Ibn al-Qayyim explained it? Why does this distinction matter so we do not misunderstand these hadiths?
2. How can we reconcile the poor preceding others into Jannah with the fact that some wealthy Companions, such as Uthman and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, were among those given glad tidings of Jannah?
3. What does the story of the people of Suffah teach us about the relationship between poverty, patience, and contentment?
4. If you are wealthy or materially comfortable, what turns a blessing into a trial, and what turns wealth into a cause of elevation before Allah?

### Activity: "Give Before Yourself"

Choose one day this week to deliberately place someone else ahead of yourself in something small: the best portion of food, the first turn in service, or your time and attention toward someone in need. Write in your journal: whom did you place first, and what did you feel? Then connect this to the Prophet's ﷺ own example of preferring the people of Suffah and Abu Hurairah over himself with the milk. Close with one sentence describing how humility and preferring others can be a path to a higher station in Jannah, regardless of your own material condition.

### Terminology
- **Merit of precedence:** Entering Jannah earlier in time than someone else.
- **Merit of elevation:** A higher rank and station within Jannah, which may be separate from precedence.
- **The people of Suffah:** Poor emigrants who had no family or wealth, and who lived in the Prophet's ﷺ mosque.
- **The weak, deemed-lowly one:** A person others consider insignificant in this world, despite his great standing before Allah.

#### Lesson Explanation
These hadiths do not mean wealth is a fault. Uthman ibn Affan and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf were among the wealthiest Companions and were both given the glad tidings of Jannah. The distinction -- as Ibn al-Qayyim explained -- is between "who enters first" and "whose rank is higher." A sincere poor person may enter earlier in time, while a grateful wealthy person may be raised to a higher rank through generous giving and sincere gratitude. The real measure, then, is sincerity of heart, not the size of a bank account.

### Authenticated Account: The Night of the Milk with the People of Suffah
<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6452" audience="13+" -->
One day Abu Hurairah, may Allah be pleased with him, was overtaken by severe hunger, so severe he tied a stone to his stomach, and he sat along the Prophet's ﷺ path asking about a verse of the Qur'an, hoping a Companion would notice his hunger and feed him. Abu Bakr and Umar passed by without noticing, until the Prophet ﷺ passed and recognized his need just from the look on his face, and took him home. There the Prophet ﷺ found a bowl of milk that had been given to him as a gift, and told Abu Hurairah to go call all the people of Suffah -- the poor emigrants who had no shelter but the Prophet's ﷺ mosque.

Abu Hurairah secretly hoped he would be given the milk before them, but he obeyed and called them all, serving each one in turn until everyone had drunk their fill. Only he and the Prophet ﷺ remained. The Prophet ﷺ said to him, "Sit and drink," and Abu Hurairah drank until he said, "By Allah, I find no more room left in me!" The Prophet ﷺ smiled and drank what remained. The people of Suffah never complained about their poverty; they were patient and content, and the Prophet ﷺ himself honored them.
<!-- retelling:end -->

## For Kids (8-12)

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.812.objectives" kind="explanation" -->
We will learn that someone who is poor but patient and thankful to Allah is loved by Him, and may even enter Jannah before many wealthy people! But this does not mean wealth is bad -- some of the Prophet's ﷺ wealthy Companions were also given the glad tidings of Jannah.
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.812.evidence" kind="evidence" -->
### A Hadith
<!-- evidence:start id="jannah.018.812.ev1" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->
The Prophet ﷺ said: «أَلَا أُخْبِرُكُمْ بِأَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ؟ كُلُّ ضَعِيفٍ مُتَضَعِّفٍ، لَوْ أَقْسَمَ عَلَى اللهِ لَأَبَرَّهُ» [Bukhari 6071].
<!-- evidence:translation -->
> "Shall I tell you about the people of Paradise? Every humble person whom others looked down on -- if he asked Allah for something, Allah would give it to him."
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.812.primary_story" kind="primary_story" -->
<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="bukhari-6452" authenticated="true" -->
### Story: The Cup of Milk

Abu Hurairah, may Allah be pleased with him, was one of the Prophet's ﷺ Companions, and he was very poor -- no house of his own, no steady food. He lived with a group of poor people called "the people of Suffah," who slept in the Prophet's ﷺ mosque because they had no other shelter.

One day, Abu Hurairah became terribly hungry. He sat by the road, hoping someone passing by would notice his condition. Abu Bakr and Umar, may Allah be pleased with them, passed by but did not notice. Then the Prophet ﷺ passed by, looked at Abu Hurairah's face, and understood from his eyes that he was hungry! He said to him, "Come with me."

They went into the Prophet's ﷺ house and found a gift: a cup of milk. The Prophet ﷺ told Abu Hurairah, "Go and call all the people of Suffah for me." Abu Hurairah secretly wished he could drink first, because he was so hungry -- but he obeyed the Prophet ﷺ and called all of them.

Abu Hurairah stood and gave each person a turn to drink until they were full, one after another, until no one was left except himself and the Prophet ﷺ! Then the Prophet ﷺ said to him with a smile, "Now you drink." Abu Hurairah drank until he said, "By Allah, there is no more room left even for one drop!" The Prophet ﷺ smiled and drank what remained.

Abu Hurairah was poor, but he was not sad or angry -- he was patient and happy to be near the Prophet ﷺ. And the Prophet ﷺ honored him and never forgot him, even though he was not wealthy or famous.
<!-- story:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.812.questions" kind="questions" -->
### Questions
1. What did Abu Hurairah do, even though he wanted to drink first?
2. Why did the Prophet ﷺ love the people of Suffah, even though they were poor?
3. Does being poor mean Allah does not love a person? Why or why not?
<!-- unit:end -->

## For Little Ones (4-7)

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.47.objectives" kind="explanation" -->
Allah loves those who are patient and thankful, whether they have a lot of money or a little.
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="jannah.018.47.primary_story" kind="primary_story" -->
<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="islamic_heritage" source_id="bukhari-6452" authenticated="true" -->
### Story: The Little Cup of Milk

Abu Hurairah was a friend of the Prophet ﷺ, and he was poor and hungry one day. The Prophet ﷺ saw him and knew he was hungry, so he took him home. There was only one small cup of milk there.

The Prophet ﷺ said, "Call our poor friends so they can drink first." Abu Hurairah wanted to drink first because he was very hungry, but he listened to the Prophet ﷺ and called his friends.

Each friend drank a little milk until everyone was full! At the end, the Prophet ﷺ gave the cup to Abu Hurairah and said, "Now you drink." Abu Hurairah drank and was happy, and the Prophet ﷺ smiled because he loved that Abu Hurairah shared with his friends first.
<!-- story:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->
