# The Fragrance of Jannah, Its Breeze, and Its Gentle Air

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the account of Anas ibn al-Nadr, may Allah be pleased with him (al-Bukhari 2805), in its proper sequence: his absence from Badr; then his pledge, "Allah will surely see what I do"; then his steadfastness on the day of Uhud and his words to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, "Jannah, by the Lord of al-Nadr! I can smell its fragrance from beyond Uhud"; then his martyrdom, and the revelation of al-Ahzab 33:23 concerning him and those like him.
- Explain that Jannah has a sweet fragrance that can be found from a distance of forty years' travel (al-Bukhari 3166), and that the same hadith forbids wronging anyone who holds a covenant and a guarantee of safety with the Muslims.
- Explain the gentle, temperate air of Jannah from the words of Allah, exalted is He, `where they will see neither sun nor bitter cold`, using the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and al-Sa'di: no oppressive heat and no painful cold.
- Distinguish, with Ibn al-Qayyim, between a fragrance of Jannah that souls perceive in this world and a fragrance perceived by the sense of smell, without claiming certainty about the details of what Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, experienced.
- Carry out the activity "Next Time, Allah Will See What I Do": naming a missed opportunity for good, pinpointing when it will come around again, keeping the pledge when it does, and then recording what happened.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for the fragrance of Jannah, for faithfulness to one's covenant, and for protection from wronging any of His creation.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The smell of fresh bread can reach you before the bakery comes into view, and the smell of rain before a single drop has touched you. A scent is a messenger that runs ahead of its sender. Through this everyday experience we can draw closer to a remarkable Prophetic description: Jannah has a sweet fragrance that its people can sense from a vast distance, and its air is perfectly temperate, never hurting with heat or cold. And on the day of Uhud a Companion said of Jannah, "I can smell its fragrance." How can a person in this world catch the scent of a home he has not yet entered?

This lesson pauses at three meanings: the fragrance of Jannah, what deprives a servant of it, and the gentle balance of its air. The light of Jannah and its daylight have two neighboring lessons of their own, and its trees and shade are left for a later lesson.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-2805" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of Anas ibn al-Nadr: "I Can Smell Its Fragrance from Beyond Uhud"

> عَنْ أَنَسٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: **غَابَ عَمِّي أَنَسُ بْنُ النَّضْرِ عَنْ قِتَالِ بَدْرٍ، فَقَالَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، غِبْتُ عَنْ أَوَّلِ قِتَالٍ قَاتَلْتَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ، لَئِنِ اللَّهُ أَشْهَدَنِي قِتَالَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ لَيَرَيَنَّ اللَّهُ مَا أَصْنَعُ. فَلَمَّا كَانَ يَوْمُ أُحُدٍ وَانْكَشَفَ الْمُسْلِمُونَ قَالَ: اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعْتَذِرُ إِلَيْكَ مِمَّا صَنَعَ هَؤُلَاءِ -يَعْنِي أَصْحَابَهُ- وَأَبْرَأُ إِلَيْكَ مِمَّا صَنَعَ هَؤُلَاءِ -يَعْنِي الْمُشْرِكِينَ- ثُمَّ تَقَدَّمَ، فَاسْتَقْبَلَهُ سَعْدُ بْنُ مُعَاذٍ، فَقَالَ: يَا سَعْدُ بْنَ مُعَاذٍ، الْجَنَّةَ وَرَبِّ النَّضْرِ، إِنِّي أَجِدُ رِيحَهَا مِنْ دُونِ أُحُدٍ. قَالَ سَعْدٌ: فَمَا اسْتَطَعْتُ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ مَا صَنَعَ. قَالَ أَنَسٌ: فَوَجَدْنَا بِهِ بِضْعًا وَثَمَانِينَ ضَرْبَةً بِالسَّيْفِ أَوْ طَعْنَةً بِرُمْحٍ أَوْ رَمْيَةً بِسَهْمٍ، وَوَجَدْنَاهُ قَدْ قُتِلَ وَقَدْ مَثَّلَ بِهِ الْمُشْرِكُونَ، فَمَا عَرَفَهُ أَحَدٌ إِلَّا أُخْتُهُ بِبَنَانِهِ. قَالَ أَنَسٌ: كُنَّا نُرَى -أَوْ نَظُنُّ- أَنَّ هَذِهِ الْآيَةَ نَزَلَتْ فِيهِ وَفِي أَشْبَاهِهِ: {مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ} إِلَى آخِرِ الْآيَةِ.** [ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ أَنَسٌ خَبَرًا آخَرَ عَنْ عَمَّتِهِ الرُّبَيِّعِ فِي الْقِصَاصِ، وَهُوَ خَارِجَ مَوْضُوعِ الدَّرْسِ][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, said: **My uncle Anas ibn al-Nadr was absent from the fighting at Badr. He said, "O Messenger of Allah, I was absent from the first battle you fought against the polytheists. If Allah lets me witness a battle against the polytheists, Allah will surely see what I do." When the day of Uhud came and the Muslims broke ranks, he said, "O Allah, I offer You my apology for what these have done" (meaning his companions), "and I declare myself innocent before You of what these have done" (meaning the polytheists). Then he advanced, and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh met him. He said, "O Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, Jannah! By the Lord of al-Nadr, I can smell its fragrance from beyond Uhud." Sa'd said, "O Messenger of Allah, I could not do what he did." Anas said: We found on him more than eighty wounds, from sword blows, spear thrusts, or arrows. We found that he had been killed and that the polytheists had mutilated his body, so that no one recognized him except his sister, who knew him by his fingertips. Anas said: We used to think (or: suppose) that this ayah was revealed about him and those like him: "Among the believers are men who have been true to the covenant they made with Allah," to the end of the ayah.** [Anas then mentions another report, about his aunt al-Rubayyi' and a case of legal retaliation, which lies outside the subject of this lesson.][^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "The fragrance of Jannah is of two kinds: a fragrance found in this world, which souls sometimes catch and which words cannot capture; and a fragrance perceived by the body's sense of smell, just as the scents of flowers are smelled." This second kind, he explains, is perceived by the people of Jannah in the Hereafter, from near and far, and may be perceived in this world by whomever Allah wills. He then said: "What Anas ibn al-Nadr found may belong to this second kind, and it may belong to the first."[^7] And in a commentary on *Riyad al-Salihin*: "It is possible that he truly breathed in the fragrance of Jannah, and it is possible that he called Jannah so vividly to mind that he pictured it right there in that place."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The account opens with a missed opportunity. Anas did not stay stuck in regret; he made a pledge about the next time: "Allah will surely see what I do." He did not say, "People will see." When the opportunity came, he kept his word. We do not claim to know exactly what he experienced, but this much is certain: his conviction had brought Jannah closer to him than the mountain standing in front of him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-33-23" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### True to the Covenant They Made with Allah

> **{مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ ۖ فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ قَضَىٰ نَحْبَهُ وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَنْتَظِرُ ۖ وَمَا بَدَّلُوا تَبْدِيلًا}** [الأحزاب: ٢٣][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Among the believers are men who have been true to the covenant they made with Allah. Some of them have fulfilled their vow, and some are still waiting; and they have not changed in the least."** (al-Ahzab 33:23)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, explained `have been true to the covenant they made with Allah` as "that is, they fulfilled it, completed it, and brought it to perfection"; `fulfilled their vow` (*qada nahbahu*) as "his aim and his goal, and the right he owed"; `some are still waiting` as waiting "to complete what is due from him... he is in hope of completing it and striving toward it"; and `they have not changed in the least` as "rather, they never stopped holding to the covenant."[^5] Ibn Kathir cites al-Bukhari's narration from Anas: "We think this ayah was revealed about Anas ibn al-Nadr."[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah is not reserved for martyrs, because it includes `some are still waiting`: those who are still on the road to fulfilling their pledge. Every believer who has pledged himself to Allah to do some good and then held firm to it shares in its meaning, in proportion to how faithfully he keeps it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3166" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Its Fragrance Can Be Found from a Distance of Forty Years' Travel"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَمْرٍو رضي الله عنهما، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«مَنْ قَتَلَ مُعَاهَدًا لَمْ يَرَحْ رَائِحَةَ الْجَنَّةِ، وَإِنَّ رِيحَهَا تُوجَدُ مِنْ مَسِيرَةِ أَرْبَعِينَ عَامًا»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abdullah ibn Amr, may Allah be pleased with them both, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever kills a *mu'ahad* will not smell the fragrance of Jannah, though its fragrance can be found from a distance of forty years' travel."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Lam yarah* means "will not smell." A *mu'ahad* is a non-Muslim who holds a covenant and a guarantee of safety with the Muslims. Other narrations give different figures for the distance; the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah* authenticated the narration of "forty years" in this hadith and graded as weak the narrations of "five hundred years" and "a thousand years."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith brings together a piece of news and a ruling. The news: the fragrance of Jannah can be sensed from a distance a traveler would need forty years to cover. The ruling: wronging someone to whom the Muslims have granted safety, even someone of another faith, can deprive the wrongdoer of that fragrance. Justice toward all people is part of the road to Jannah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-12-14" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### Neither Sun nor Bitter Cold

> **{وَجَزَاهُمْ بِمَا صَبَرُوا جَنَّةً وَحَرِيرًا ۝ مُتَّكِئِينَ فِيهَا عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ ۖ لَا يَرَوْنَ فِيهَا شَمْسًا وَلَا زَمْهَرِيرًا ۝ وَدَانِيَةً عَلَيْهِمْ ظِلَالُهَا وَذُلِّلَتْ قُطُوفُهَا تَذْلِيلًا}** [الإنسان: ١٢-١٤][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And He will reward them for their patience with a Garden and with silk, reclining there on raised couches, where they will see neither sun nor bitter cold; its shade will be close over them, and its clusters of fruit will hang low within easy reach."** (al-Insan 76:12-14)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, said: "That is, they have no oppressive heat and no painful cold; rather, it is a single climate, constant, everlasting, without end."[^6] And al-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, explained `sun` as a sun "whose heat would harm them," and `nor bitter cold` (*zamharir*) as "that is, severe cold; rather, all their time is spent in deep, spreading shade, neither hot nor cold, in which their bodies take delight."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

This reward is tied to the words `for their patience`. Whoever is patient in this world through the heat and the cold that obedience brings, such as wudu' on a winter morning or fasting through a summer day, will be rewarded with air in which no body ever grows weary.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why did Anas say, "Allah will surely see what I do," and not "People will see"?
2. What are the two kinds of the fragrance of Jannah that Ibn al-Qayyim mentioned? And why did he not commit to either one in the case of Anas?
3. What do the words `and some are still waiting` add to our understanding of the ayah?

### A True Account: The Companion Who Smelled Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2805" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. The Companion Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, told it about his uncle. It is not a made-up story.**

Anas ibn Malik had an uncle who was also called Anas. He was Anas ibn al-Nadr, may Allah be pleased with him.

One time, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the Muslims went out to a place called Badr. But Anas ibn al-Nadr was not with them.

So he came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, I missed the very first time you went out. If Allah gives me another chance, Allah will see what I do!"

He made a promise to Allah: next time, he would do something good and true.

Then another day came, near a big mountain called Uhud. On that day, many of the Muslims went back. But Anas did not go back. He went forward.

A Companion named Sa'd ibn Mu'adh met him. And Anas said to him: "Sa'd, Jannah! By the Lord of al-Nadr, I can smell it, right here by Mount Uhud!"

Anas stayed brave that whole day until he died as a martyr, may Allah be pleased with him. And Sa'd told the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "I could not do what he did."

The Companions believed that Allah sent down an ayah of the Qur'an about him and people like him. It praises men who kept their promise to Allah.[^1][^3]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2805" audience="4-7" -->

Here is what it means: Anas missed a chance, but he didn't just sit there feeling sad. He promised Allah he would do good next time, and then he kept his promise. He loved Jannah so much that he said he could smell it. We can do that too! When we miss something good, we can say: "Next time, I'll do it."[^1]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Omar missed the first day of the neighborhood park cleanup because he was sick. When his friends came back talking about everything they had done, he felt left behind. He had two choices. He could say, "Well, I missed it, and that's that." Or he could ask, "When is the next cleanup day, and what am I going to do there?"

In this lesson you will meet a Companion who missed a huge opportunity and chose the second path. His story contains an amazing sentence about the fragrance of Jannah. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the fragrance of Jannah can be sensed from a distance it would take a traveler forty years to cover. He said this while warning against killing a person who has a covenant and a promise of safety with the Muslims: whoever does that will not smell its fragrance.[^2] And Allah told us that the air of Jannah is perfectly mild: "They will see neither sun nor bitter cold," which means no heat that hurts and no harsh cold.[^4][^10]

- **`A protected non-Muslim (mu'ahad)`** — a person who is not Muslim but has a covenant and a promise of safety with the Muslims, so it is forbidden to wrong him.

### A True Account: "Jannah, by the Lord of al-Nadr!"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2805" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

Anas ibn al-Nadr, may Allah be pleased with him, was one of the Ansar, the Helpers of Madinah. He was the uncle of Anas ibn Malik, the young man who served the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.

At the Battle of Badr, the first battle the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, fought against the polytheists, Anas ibn al-Nadr was not there. So he came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: "O Messenger of Allah, I was absent from the first battle you fought against the polytheists. If Allah lets me witness a battle against the polytheists, Allah will surely see what I do."

Notice this: he didn't say, "People will see." He said, "Allah will see."

Time passed, and then came the day of Uhud. On that day the Muslims broke ranks: many of them fell back, and their lines were thrown into confusion. Anas said: "O Allah, I offer You my apology for what these have done" (meaning his own companions who had fallen back), "and I declare myself innocent before You of what these have done" (meaning the polytheists).

Then he moved forward. Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, may Allah be pleased with him, met him, and Anas said: "O Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, Jannah! By the Lord of al-Nadr, I can smell its fragrance from beyond Uhud," meaning from the direction of Mount Uhud.

Anas, may Allah be pleased with him, held his ground until he was martyred that day. Later Sa'd told the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "O Messenger of Allah, I could not do what he did."

Anas ibn Malik said: We used to think this ayah was revealed about him and those like him: "Among the believers are men who have been true to the covenant they made with Allah."[^1][^3]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2805" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: Anas missed his chance, so he turned his regret into a promise for next time, and when next time came, he kept it. As for his words "I can smell its fragrance," the scholars said it is possible that he truly smelled the fragrance of Jannah, and it is possible that his longing and certainty were so strong that Jannah felt as if it were right in front of him.[^7][^8] Either way, certainty brings Jannah close.[^1]

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.040.dua.fragrance-of-jannah-true-to-covenant" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ ارْزُقْنَا رِيحَ الْجَنَّةِ وَطِيبَ نَسِيمِهَا، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوكَ عَلَيْهِ، وَلَا تَحْرِمْنَا رِيحَهَا بِظُلْمِ أَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ.

It means: O Allah, grant us the fragrance of Jannah and the sweetness of its breeze. Make us among those who were true to their covenant with You, as Anas ibn al-Nadr was, and do not let any wrong we do to any of Your creation become a reason we are kept from its fragrance.

<!-- bedtime-dua:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

At break, a few students started mocking a new kid because of his accent. You were right there. You knew the right thing was to say something, but you stayed quiet. The moment passed, and a question stayed with you: did I miss my chance for good?

A lot of people handle a missed opportunity in one of two ways: they beat themselves up until they can't move, or they forget about it as if nothing happened. In this lesson you'll meet a Companion who chose a third way: a sincere pledge for next time, with no witness but Allah. His story contains a sentence about the fragrance of Jannah that makes us ask: how can certainty about the unseen be so strong that you can actually feel it close by?

Among the landmarks of that unseen world: the fragrance of Jannah can be sensed from a distance of forty years' travel, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us while forbidding the killing of a *mu'ahad*;[^2] and its air holds neither scorching heat nor bitter cold (*zamharir*).[^4][^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-33-23" -->

- **`Fulfilled his vow (qada nahbahu)`** — carried out to the end what he had pledged to Allah and stayed true to it until he met Him, like one who was martyred or who died having given what was due.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-3166" -->

- **`A protected non-Muslim (mu'ahad)`** — a non-Muslim who holds a covenant and a guarantee of safety with the Muslims; any attack on him is forbidden.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A True Account: "Allah Will Surely See What I Do"

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-2805" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, about his uncle, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

Anas ibn al-Nadr was a man of the Ansar. When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, set out for Badr, the first battle against the polytheists, Anas was not with him. He didn't make excuses, and he didn't pretend it didn't matter to him. Instead he said to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "I was absent from the first battle you fought against the polytheists. If Allah lets me witness a battle against the polytheists, Allah will surely see what I do."

Look closely at how he phrased that pledge. He didn't specify what he would do, and he didn't promise people anything they could praise him for. He made Allah alone his witness.

About a year later came the day of Uhud. At a hard moment, the Muslims broke ranks. This was where he could have said, "I just did what everyone else did." Instead he said: "O Allah, I offer You my apology for what these have done" (meaning his companions), "and I declare myself innocent before You of what these have done" (meaning the polytheists). He apologized to Allah for his brothers' retreat, disowned the aggression of the polytheists, and then moved forward.

Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, chief of the Aws, met him, and Anas said to him: "O Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, Jannah! By the Lord of al-Nadr, I can smell its fragrance from beyond Uhud." He held his ground until he was martyred. Sa'd told the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "O Messenger of Allah, I could not do what he did." That was a brave man's tribute to a man braver still.

Anas ibn Malik said: We used to think this ayah was revealed about him and those like him: "Among the believers are men who have been true to the covenant they made with Allah."[^1][^3]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-2805" audience="13+" -->

Put another way: a missed opportunity, then a private pledge with Allah, then faithfulness when the real test arrived. As for his words "I can smell its fragrance," Ibn al-Qayyim held that they could mean a real sense of smell with which Allah honored him, or a fragrance the soul perceives and words cannot capture; and we do not claim certainty about either.[^7][^1] The ayah goes on: `and some are still waiting`. So whoever is still living by his pledge is included in this praise, as long as he has not changed.[^5]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What is the difference between beating yourself up after a missed opportunity and Anas's pledge for next time?
2. Why might a pledge that only Allah witnesses be more sincere than a promise made in public?
3. Anas apologized to Allah for his brothers' retreat instead of insulting them. What can you learn from that?
4. What does it mean for certainty about Jannah to be so strong that you "can smell its fragrance"?
5. Why does the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, link the fragrance of Jannah to justice toward non-Muslims?
6. When did you stay silent and then regret it? And when might that moment come around again?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Next Time, Allah Will See What I Do

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.040.activity.next-time-allah-will-see" -->

On a private sheet of paper that you will not share or post, write down a moment when you missed a real chance to do good: you stayed silent in the face of wrong, you left a friend on his own, or you missed Fajr. Figure out when a moment like it might come around again, and write your pledge: "If Allah lets me witness it, He will see what I do." Then describe the action precisely. When the moment comes again, follow through without announcing it. Afterward, write a private paragraph: Did your intention turn into action? And what changed when your witness was Allah and not people?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.040.dua.fragrance-of-jannah-true-to-covenant" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ ارْزُقْنَا رِيحَ الْجَنَّةِ وَطِيبَ نَسِيمِهَا، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوكَ عَلَيْهِ، وَلَا تَحْرِمْنَا رِيحَهَا بِظُلْمِ أَحَدٍ مِنْ خَلْقِكَ.

It means: O Allah, grant us the fragrance of Jannah and the sweetness of its breeze. Make us among those who keep their covenant with You in private before they ever do in public, and do not let any wrong we do to any of Your creation, Muslim or not, become a reason we are kept from its fragrance.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the account of Anas ibn al-Nadr in sequence from al-Bukhari 2805; explains al-Ahzab 33:23 using al-Sa'di's tafsir; states the distance from which the fragrance of Jannah can be sensed and the ruling on the *mu'ahad* from al-Bukhari 3166; explains the temperate air of Jannah from al-Insan 76:13; presents Ibn al-Qayyim's view of the two kinds of the fragrance of Jannah without claiming certainty about Anas's experience; and writes a pledge for a specific missed opportunity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for Surat al-Ahzab and Surat al-Insan; a board with three columns headed "Its Fragrance," "What Keeps One from It," and "Its Air"; the activity sheet; exit cards.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads al-Bukhari 2805, 4048, and 4783; Muslim 1903 (which contains the words "Ah, the fragrance of Jannah! I find it this side of Uhud"); and al-Bukhari 3166 and 6914. The teacher reviews Chapter 42 of *Hadi al-Arwah* together with its editor's notes, and the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and al-Sa'di on al-Ahzab 33:23 and al-Insan 76:13. The teacher also decides how to read the details of the wounds in the full text calmly and without sensationalism.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "What scent instantly takes you back to a place or a person you love?" Then: "Can certainty about something unseen be as powerful as a scent?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the account of Anas in full, pausing at the pledge, at the apology and the disavowal, at the sentence about the fragrance, and at Sa'd's testimony (8 minutes). Then read the ayah of al-Ahzab with al-Sa'di's tafsir, focusing on `some are still waiting` (4 minutes). Then the hadith of the *mu'ahad* (4 minutes), then the ayat of al-Insan with Ibn Kathir and al-Sa'di (4 minutes). Learners fill in the three columns as they go.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 2, and 4. The teacher presents Ibn al-Qayyim's view of the two kinds of fragrance and stresses the method of staying with the text: we affirm what has come to us, and we do not claim certainty about how Anas experienced what he did. The teacher also shows that the hadith of the *mu'ahad* is a foundational text for justice toward non-Muslims.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes a specific missed opportunity, when it will recur, the pledge, and the specific action (8 minutes). Then pairs discuss the wording of their pledges so that the action is clear and doable, without anyone seeing private details they do not wish to share (7 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "From what distance can the fragrance of Jannah be sensed, and who, according to the hadith, will not smell it?" and "Explain `where they will see neither sun nor bitter cold` in one sentence." Then read al-Ahzab 33:23, make the lesson's du'a together, and agree to review the pledges in two weeks.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the account of Anas and al-Insan 76:13. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations of the account of Anas (al-Bukhari 2805 and 4048, and Muslim 1903) and compare their wording, and to review, from the notes to *Hadi al-Arwah*, how the narrations differ on the distance of the fragrance (forty years; seventy autumns in at-Tirmidhi 1403; and weak narrations of five hundred and a thousand years).

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not rely on what is not authentic, such as the hadith "Make it fragrant for your people... that is the coolness people feel in the hours before dawn" (its chain contains a narrator whose reports are abandoned, *matruk*), or the hadith "The fragrance of Jannah can be found from a distance of a thousand years" (very weak). Do not describe what Anas experienced with details that have not come down to us. Do not use the account to glorify violence: its context is defense in a battle fought alongside the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the hadith of the *mu'ahad* forbids attacking peaceful people. The north wind in the market of Jannah is left for its own lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Jannah has a very beautiful smell and that its air is lovely, neither hot nor cold; retells that Anas ibn al-Nadr made a promise to Allah for next time and then kept it; and chooses a good deed they missed to do next time.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Three small covered containers holding familiar smells (a piece of bread, a rose or mint leaves, orange peel); two sheets of drawing paper; crayons or markers; a bottle of perfume handled only by the adult; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver makes sure none of the smells contain allergens, and practices telling the account without any description of the fighting or of wounds, using only the word "martyred."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** With eyes closed, the child sniffs each container and guesses what is inside. The caregiver says: "You knew what it was from its smell before you even saw it!"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The caregiver reads the explanation and the ayah, then the account in a calm voice, holding up a finger at each stage: "He missed it," "He promised," "He kept his promise."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "What did Anas promise? Did he keep his promise? And what did he say about Jannah?" Stress that Allah sees what we do even when no one else does.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 6 minutes:** Together with the caregiver, the child remembers a good deed they missed, draws the two pictures, and agrees on the next time the moment will come. After the child does the good deed at home, a parent puts a little perfume on the second picture.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Notice whether the child says that Jannah smells beautiful, and whether they can repeat the three stages of the account. Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the two words "promised" and "kept it" are enough. Ask older children to tell the account to a brother or sister in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not represent the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions with dolls or characters. Do not mention the details of the wounds or scenes of fighting. Do not spray perfume directly on children. Do not make the child feel guilty about what they missed; instead, help them feel happy about next time.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the account of Anas in sequence; explains the words *zamharir* and *mu'ahad*; states the distance from which the fragrance of Jannah can be sensed and who will not smell it; names the two possibilities in his words "I can smell its fragrance"; and fills in the "I Missed It / When It Comes Again" card.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Five sequence cards without pictures ("Absent from Badr," "The Pledge," "The Day of Uhud," "I Can Smell Its Fragrance," "The Ayah"); term cards; activity cards; pens.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes al-Insan 76:13 on the board and prepares two examples: a vague pledge ("I'll be better") and a specific one ("I'll visit my grandpa on Friday and read to him").

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read Omar's cleanup situation and ask: "What do you do when you miss out on something great?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story while students put the sequence cards in order (7 minutes). Then explain the terms and read the hadith about the distance and the ayah (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Discuss questions 2, 3, and 4, making clear that justice toward all people, Muslims and non-Muslims alike, is part of the road to Jannah, and that the scholars did not claim certainty about how Anas experienced what he did.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student fills in his or her card, then compares it with the two examples to make sure the action has a clear time and a clear deed.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes two sentences: "What did I learn from Anas ibn al-Nadr?" and "What is the air of Jannah like?" Then read the du'a, and agree to share the cards in two weeks.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, offer a ready-made list of opportunities to choose from. Ask advanced students to read al-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Ahzab 33:23 and explain `and some are still waiting`.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the battle without the details of the wounds. Do not allow any hostile remarks about non-Muslims; the hadith itself forbids attacking a *mu'ahad*. Be sensitive to students who have missed things because of family or health circumstances, and do not ask them to disclose those circumstances.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes Anas's pledge ("Allah will surely see") against a public promise; explains `fulfilled their vow` and `some are still waiting`; connects the fragrance of Jannah with justice toward the *mu'ahad*; presents Ibn al-Qayyim's view without claiming certainty; and writes a private pledge for a situation that may come around again.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a personal sheet that will not be collected; three scenario cards (staying silent during mockery, abandoning a friend, putting off a prayer); a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews al-Sa'di's tafsir of al-Ahzab 33:23 and Ibn al-Qayyim's words in Chapter 42 of *Hadi al-Arwah*, and words the scenarios neutrally so that none of them points to a particular student.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the scene at break and ask: "What makes us stay silent when we know what's right?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the account, pausing at the wording of the pledge, at the apology and the disavowal, and at Sa'd's testimony; then read the ayah and its tafsir.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Small groups work through the scenario cards, write a specific pledge for next time for each one, and discuss questions 2 and 5.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** On a private sheet, each student writes the missed moment, when it might come around again, the pledge, and the precise action, and names what he or she will hold back from (announcing it and posting it).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence: "What is the difference between regret that paralyzes me and regret that moves me forward?" Then read the du'a. Two weeks later, the teacher follows up with a general question about how the experience went.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short research piece on the life of Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, may Allah be pleased with him, and his testimony about Anas.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask any student to take on dangerous confrontations or violent situations in the name of courage; a pledge is made for good that lies within one's ability and is carried out with wisdom. Do not use the account to glorify fighting outside its lawful context. Be sensitive to anyone living with heavy regret or anxiety, and refer them to a counselor when needed.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
