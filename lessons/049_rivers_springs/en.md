# The Rivers and Springs of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate, in sequence, the hadith of the revelation of Surat al-Kawthar (Muslim 400): the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, dozing briefly among his Companions in the mosque; raising his head with a smile; reciting the surah; asking, "Do you know what al-Kawthar is?"; answering that it is a river his Lord promised him, upon which is abundant good and a Pond to which his ummah will come; and then the news of the one who is turned away from it because he introduced something new after him.
- Affirm what the texts establish about the rivers and springs of Jannah: gardens beneath which rivers flow (al-Baqarah 2:25); a spring from which the servants of Allah drink, making it gush forth wherever they wish (al-Insan 76:5-6); and the river of al-Kawthar, whose banks are domes of hollowed pearl and whose mud is pungent musk (al-Bukhari 6581).
- Explain the hadith "Sayhan, Jayhan, the Euphrates and the Nile are all among the rivers of Jannah" (Muslim 2839) through the two views of the scholars as an-Nawawi relates them, without adding anything about how this is so.
- Draw two lessons of conduct from the hadith of al-Kawthar: meeting Allah's gifts with prayer and gratitude, as in His words "So pray to your Lord and sacrifice," and holding firm to the Sunnah of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, without altering it.
- Carry out the "Trace the Water, Then Give Others a Drink" activity: tracing the water they drink from their cup back to its source, then giving others water to drink for a set number of days and keeping a record of it.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to bring us to the Pond of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to keep us firm upon his Sunnah, and to admit us into the gardens beneath which rivers flow.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Few sights soothe the soul like running water. It cools the air, brings the land to life, and calms anyone who hears it murmur past. That is why the Qur'an so often describes Jannah together with its rivers: "gardens beneath which rivers flow." A river in this world depends on rain, a channel and dams; it runs dry and turns muddy. The rivers of Jannah are pure gift, with no toil in them at all.

This lesson stays with the broad picture of the rivers and springs of Jannah: the rivers flowing beneath its trees and lofty chambers; the spring its people direct wherever they wish; the river of al-Kawthar, which was given to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; and the link between the rivers of this world and Jannah. The four rivers named in Surat Muhammad (of water, milk, wine and honey) have a lesson of their own, as do Tasnim and Salsabil.

The lesson is built on an authentic hadith that preserves a living scene: a Prophet who lifts his head, smiling, because he has been given the glad tidings of a river in Jannah, and who then teaches his Companions that Allah's gifts are answered with prayer, and that nearness to his Pond on the Day of Resurrection comes from holding firm to his way.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-25" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Gardens Beneath Which Rivers Flow"

> **{وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ أَنَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ كُلَّمَا رُزِقُوا مِنْهَا مِنْ ثَمَرَةٍ رِزْقًا ۙ قَالُوا هَٰذَا الَّذِي رُزِقْنَا مِنْ قَبْلُ ۖ وَأُتُوا بِهِ مُتَشَابِهًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُطَهَّرَةٌ ۖ وَهُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [البقرة: ٢٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And give glad tidings to those who believe and do righteous deeds that theirs are gardens beneath which rivers flow. Whenever they are given fruit from them as provision, they will say, 'This is what we were provided with before,' for they will be given it in close resemblance. And in them they will have purified spouses, and in them they will abide forever."** (al-Baqarah 2:25)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, explains "beneath which rivers flow" as meaning "that is, beneath its trees and its lofty chambers."[^6] As-Sa'di, may Allah have mercy on him, says they are "rivers of water, milk, honey and wine, which they make gush forth however they wish and direct wherever they want, and from which those trees drink, so that they bring forth every kind of fruit."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah joins the glad tidings to their cause: faith and righteous deeds. Its rivers are not a separate ornament; they are the very life of Jannah. They water its trees and flow beneath its dwellings, so that its people see the water and hear it without ever laboring to fetch it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-5-6" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "A Spring They Make Gush Forth in Abundance"

> **{إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِنْ كَأْسٍ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا ۝ عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا عِبَادُ اللَّهِ يُفَجِّرُونَهَا تَفْجِيرًا}** [الإنسان: ٥-٦][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Indeed, the righteous will drink from a cup whose mixture is camphor: a spring from which the servants of Allah drink, making it gush forth in abundance."** (al-Insan 76:5-6)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: "The cooling quality and fine fragrance of camphor are well known, together with the added delight it will have in Jannah." On "making it gush forth in abundance" he says: "that is, they direct it as they wish and wherever they wish, from their palaces, their homes and their places of gathering," and he relates from Mujahid: "They lead it wherever they wish."[^6] As-Sa'di says it is "a spring ever overflowing and ever flowing, which the servants of Allah make gush forth abundantly, wherever they wish and however they want."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world, people search for a spring and dig their way down to it, and then they worry that it will run dry. The spring of Jannah its people send wherever they please, with no fear that it will ever give out. And describing those who drink as "the righteous" (*al-abrar*) and "the servants of Allah" is a reminder that the road to it is righteousness and servanthood.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-400" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Do You Know What al-Kawthar Is?"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: بَيْنَا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ ذَاتَ يَوْمٍ بَيْنَ أَظْهُرِنَا إِذْ أَغْفَى إِغْفَاءَةً، ثُمَّ رَفَعَ رَأْسَهُ مُتَبَسِّمًا، فَقُلْنَا: مَا أَضْحَكَكَ يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ؟ قَالَ: **«أُنْزِلَتْ عَلَيَّ آنِفًا سُورَةٌ»**، فَقَرَأَ: **«بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ، {إِنَّا أَعْطَيْنَاكَ الْكَوْثَرَ ۝ فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَانْحَرْ ۝ إِنَّ شَانِئَكَ هُوَ الْأَبْتَرُ}»**، ثُمَّ قَالَ: **«أَتَدْرُونَ مَا الْكَوْثَرُ؟»** فَقُلْنَا: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: **«فَإِنَّهُ نَهْرٌ وَعَدَنِيهِ رَبِّي عَزَّ وَجَلَّ، عَلَيْهِ خَيْرٌ كَثِيرٌ، هُوَ حَوْضٌ تَرِدُ عَلَيْهِ أُمَّتِي يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ، آنِيَتُهُ عَدَدُ النُّجُومِ، فَيُخْتَلَجُ الْعَبْدُ مِنْهُمْ، فَأَقُولُ: رَبِّ، إِنَّهُ مِنْ أُمَّتِي، فَيَقُولُ: مَا تَدْرِي مَا أَحْدَثَتْ بَعْدَكَ»**.[^3]
>
> وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ: «بَيْنَ أَظْهُرِنَا فِي الْمَسْجِدِ»، وَفِيهَا: **«مَا أَحْدَثَ بَعْدَكَ»**. وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ أُخْرَى: **«نَهْرٌ وَعَدَنِيهِ رَبِّي عَزَّ وَجَلَّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، عَلَيْهِ حَوْضٌ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: One day, while the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was there among us, he dozed off for a moment. Then he raised his head, smiling. We said, "What has made you smile, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: **"A surah has just now been revealed to me,"** and he recited: **"In the name of Allah, the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful. 'Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar. So pray to your Lord and sacrifice. Indeed, it is the one who hates you who is cut off.'"** Then he said: **"Do you know what al-Kawthar is?"** We said, "Allah and His Messenger know best." He said: **"It is a river that my Lord, the Mighty and Majestic, has promised me. Upon it is abundant good. It is a Pond to which my ummah will come on the Day of Resurrection, and its vessels are as many as the stars. Then a servant from among them will be pulled away, and I will say, 'My Lord, he is one of my ummah!' And He will say, 'You do not know what they introduced after you.'"**[^3]
>
> In one narration: "among us in the mosque," and in it: **"what he introduced after you."** And in another narration: **"a river that my Lord, the Mighty and Majestic, has promised me in Jannah, upon which is a Pond."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi, may Allah have mercy on him, says: "*Aghfa ighfa'ah*: that is, he slept"; "al-Kawthar here is a river in Jannah, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained it, and elsewhere the word denotes abundant good"; "*yukhtalaj*: that is, he is snatched away and cut off"; and "*ash-shani'*: the one who hates."[^8] The second narration makes clear that the river is in Jannah and that the Pond is upon it. Ibn Hajar says: "The Pond, which is outside Jannah, is supplied from the river, which is inside Jannah."[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith invites us to pause three times. First, glad tidings that bring joy: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, raised his head smiling. Second, a way of teaching through questions, "Do you know what al-Kawthar is?", met by the Companions' good manners: "Allah and His Messenger know best." Third, the surah itself pairs the gift with gratitude put into action: "So pray to your Lord and sacrifice." And the hadith closes with a warning: the one kept away from the Pond is the one who introduced something new into the religion after the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Nearness to him comes through following, not through mere belonging.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### "Its Banks Were Domes of Hollowed Pearl"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ رضي الله عنه عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: **«بَيْنَمَا أَنَا أَسِيرُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، إِذَا أَنَا بِنَهَرٍ حَافَتَاهُ قِبَابُ الدُّرِّ الْمُجَوَّفِ، قُلْتُ: مَا هَذَا يَا جِبْرِيلُ؟ قَالَ: هَذَا الْكَوْثَرُ الَّذِي أَعْطَاكَ رَبُّكَ، فَإِذَا طِينُهُ - أَوْ طِيبُهُ - مِسْكٌ أَذْفَرُ»**. شَكَّ أَحَدُ الرُّوَاةِ: طِينُهُ أَوْ طِيبُهُ.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"While I was walking in Jannah, I suddenly came upon a river whose two banks were domes of hollowed pearl. I said, 'What is this, O Jibril?' He said, 'This is al-Kawthar, which your Lord has given you.' And its mud (or: its scent) was pungent musk."** One of the narrators was unsure whether the word was *tinuhu*, "its mud," or *tibuhu*, "its scent."[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Hajar mentions that this took place on the night of the Isra', and that the reading *tinuhu*, "its mud," is the one relied upon.[^9] The two banks (*hafatan*) are the two sides of the river; *ad-durr* is pearl; and *adhfar* means intensely fragrant.

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith describes a river the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw in Jannah: along both its sides stand domes of hollowed pearl, and its mud is musk. What in this world is mud we step around is, in Jannah, a fragrance we breathe in.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2839" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Sayhan, Jayhan, the Euphrates and the Nile"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«سَيْحَانُ وَجَيْحَانُ وَالْفُرَاتُ وَالنِّيلُ كُلٌّ مِنْ أَنْهَارِ الْجَنَّةِ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Sayhan, Jayhan, the Euphrates and the Nile are all among the rivers of Jannah."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi explains that Sayhan and Jayhan are two great rivers in the land of the Armenians, near Greater Syria, and that they are not the same as the Sayhun and the Jayhun. On what it means for these rivers to be among the rivers of Jannah, he relates two interpretations from al-Qadi 'Iyad: "The first: that faith spread through their lands, or that the bodies nourished by their water are bound for Jannah. The second, which is the more correct: that the words are to be taken at face value, and that these rivers have a source from Jannah."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith brings Jannah close to our own world: rivers we know by name have a connection to it. We affirm what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, and we do not delve into how that connection works without knowledge.

<!-- evidence:end -->

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What does the ayah of al-Insan (76:5-6) add to the picture of the rivers in the ayah of al-Baqarah (2:25)?
2. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, smile, and how did he teach his Companions the meaning of al-Kawthar?
3. What is the relationship between the river and the Pond, as the narration "in Jannah, upon which is a Pond" makes clear?
4. What did the surah command after mentioning the gift? What does that look like in your own life?
5. Why are some people turned away from the Pond even though they belong to the ummah?
6. What is the right stance toward the hadith "Sayhan, Jayhan, the Euphrates and the Nile," between affirming it and delving into how it is so?

### Activity: Trace the Water, Then Give Others a Drink

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.049.activity.trace-the-water-then-give-drink" -->

Find out where the water that reaches your home comes from: a river, an underground aquifer, a desalination plant, or a dam. Draw a chain from your cup back to the source, with at least five stations, and at each station write down what people labor over to keep the water safe and bring it to you. Then write two lines comparing all this with the rivers of Jannah and its spring, which its people make gush forth wherever they wish. Next, choose one way of giving others water to drink and keep it up for seven days: water for worshippers at the mosque, for workers on the street, or a contribution to a water-giving project. Record each day what you did, and on the seventh day write three lines: What did tracing the water teach me about Allah's blessing? And how will I meet His gifts with what the surah commanded: "So pray to your Lord and sacrifice"?

Have you ever had a drink of cold water on a hot day? Have you ever heard the sound of water rushing along in a river? ### An Authentic Hadith: Why Did the Prophet Smile?

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-400" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. Imam Muslim recorded it in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-400" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was sitting with his Companions in the mosque. He fell asleep for a little moment, and then he lifted his head, smiling.[^3]

The Companions asked him, "What made you smile, O Messenger of Allah?" He said, "A surah has just been sent down to me." Then he recited: "Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar."[^3]

Then he asked them, "Do you know what al-Kawthar is?" And they answered politely, "Allah and His Messenger know best."[^3]

He told them it is a river his Lord promised him in Jannah, full of good things. On it there is a Pond that the Muslims will come to on the Day of Resurrection to drink, and its cups are as many as the stars in the sky.[^3] Another hadith tells us that along both sides of the river there are domes made of pearl, and its mud is musk that smells beautiful.[^4]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, also told us that some people will be kept away from the Pond, because they changed the religion after him.[^3] So we love to follow his Sunnah, just the way he taught us.

And what did the surah say after the gift? "So pray to your Lord."[^3] Allah gave us a blessing, so we thank Him and pray to Him.

### Activity: Trace the Water, Then Give Others a Drink

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.049.activity.trace-the-water-then-give-drink" -->

Fill a cup with water together with your mom or dad, and ask, "Where did this water come from?" Then draw the path backward together: the cup, then the faucet, then the pipe, then the river or the well, then the cloud and the rain. At each drawing, say, "Alhamdulillah." Then, for three days: with your mom or dad, put a small dish of clean water outside your home for the birds. Change the water every morning, and each time, color in a blue drop on your paper. On the third day, next to your drops, draw a beautiful river of Jannah.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Picture a trip on a scorching summer day. The road goes on and on, and the water in your bottle is almost gone. Then you reach a cool, flowing river. That moment helps you understand why Allah so often describes Jannah as "gardens beneath which rivers flow," that is, beneath its trees and its lofty chambers.[^1][^6] In it there is a spring the servants of Allah drink from, "making it gush forth in abundance": they send it wherever they want, and they never worry that it will run out.[^2][^7] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that four rivers in this world, among them the Nile and the Euphrates, are among the rivers of Jannah. The scholars said the more correct view is that these rivers have a source in Jannah, and Allah knows best how that is.[^5][^10] And Allah gave our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, a special river of his own in Jannah. So how did the Companions first hear about it?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-400" -->

- **`Al-Kawthar`** — a river in Jannah that Allah gave to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, as he himself explained; the word is also used to mean "abundant good."[^8]
- **`The Pond (al-hawd)`** — a vast pool of water that the believers will come to on the Day of Resurrection to drink from; its water comes from the river of al-Kawthar.[^3][^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-76-5-6" -->

- **`Making it gush forth in abundance (yufajjirunaha tafjira)`** — making the spring flow wherever they want, from their homes and their gathering places.[^6]

### An Authentic Hadith: "Do You Know What al-Kawthar Is?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-400" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account recorded by Imam Muslim in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-400" audience="8-12" -->

Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, tells us: One day the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was with us in the mosque, and he dozed off lightly. Then he raised his head, smiling.[^3]

The Companions noticed the smile and asked him, "What has made you smile, O Messenger of Allah?" He said, "A surah has been revealed to me *anifan*," which means "just a moment ago." Then he recited: "Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar. So pray to your Lord and sacrifice. Indeed, it is the one who hates you who is cut off."[^3]

He didn't explain it to them right away. Instead he asked, "Do you know what al-Kawthar is?" Someone could easily have taken a guess here, but they said, "Allah and His Messenger know best."[^3] They would not speak about the unseen without knowledge.

Then he, peace and blessings be upon him, told them that it is a river his Lord, the Mighty and Majestic, promised him in Jannah. Upon it is abundant good, and upon it is a Pond his ummah will come to on the Day of Resurrection, with vessels as many as the stars.[^3] In another hadith, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, describes seeing it in Jannah: along both its sides stand domes of hollowed pearl, and its mud is musk.[^4]

Then he mentioned something important: a man will be pulled away from the Pond, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, will say, "My Lord, he is one of my ummah!" And he will be told: you do not know what he introduced after you, meaning what he changed in the religion.[^3]

We learn three things. Allah's blessings fill the heart with joy. We answer them the way the surah commanded: "So pray to your Lord." And we reach the Pond of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, by following his Sunnah, not by changing it.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in an age that measures people in numbers: followers, likes, views. If your numbers are low, someone will find a way to say it: "Irrelevant. Nobody even knows who that is." A short surah answers this whole way of thinking: "Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar." *Al-Kawthar* means abundant good, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained it as a river in Jannah.[^3][^8] Then the surah declares that the one who hates him is "*al-abtar*," the one cut off: cut off from all good, according to one of the two views an-Nawawi mentions.[^8]

Before the story, keep the big picture in view. Jannah is "gardens beneath which rivers flow";[^1] in it is a spring its people direct wherever they wish;[^2][^6] and there is the river of al-Kawthar, whose mud is musk.[^4] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, also told us that Sayhan, Jayhan, the Euphrates and the Nile are among the rivers of Jannah, and the more correct view among the scholars is that they have a source there.[^5][^10]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-400" -->

- **`Al-Kawthar`** — in this hadith, a river in Jannah, as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, explained it; elsewhere the word refers to abundant good.[^8]
- **`Pulled away (fa-yukhtalaj)`** — snatched and cut off, that is, kept away from the Pond.[^8]
- **`What he introduced after you (ma ahdatha ba'dak)`** — the changes and innovations he brought into the religion after the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: A Smile in the Mosque and a River in Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-400" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account recorded by Imam Muslim in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-400" audience="13+" -->

The setting: the Prophet's mosque in Madinah. Anas ibn Malik and a number of Companions are sitting there, with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, among them. He drifts off for a brief moment, then lifts his head, smiling.[^3]

They ask him, "What has made you smile, O Messenger of Allah?" He says, "A surah has just now been revealed to me," and he recites: "Indeed, We have given you al-Kawthar. So pray to your Lord and sacrifice. Indeed, it is the one who hates you who is cut off."[^3]

Then he asks, "Do you know what al-Kawthar is?" They answer, "Allah and His Messenger know best."[^3] A short answer, but it is a stance: when it comes to the unseen, you don't offer your own opinion.

He, peace and blessings be upon him, tells them it is a river his Lord promised him in Jannah. Upon it is abundant good, and upon it is a Pond his ummah will come to on the Day of Resurrection, its vessels as many as the stars.[^3] He had seen it on the night of the Isra': along its banks, domes of hollowed pearl, and its mud pungent musk.[^4][^9]

Then comes the part we shouldn't skip over. A man from the ummah is snatched away from the Pond, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, says, "My Lord, he is one of my ummah!" And he is told, "You do not know what he introduced after you."[^3] The man was counted as part of the ummah, but he had altered the religion.

Now put yourself in the picture. There are people around you saying, "Religion has to keep up with what's trending," or bending what is firmly established to please their audience. The hadith says, quietly: nearness to the Pond of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, on the Day of Resurrection belongs to whoever holds firm to his way, even if he is the only one in his group who does. And the surah says: whoever has been given abundant good gives thanks through prayer and sacrifice, not through showing off.

<!-- retelling:end -->

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of al-Kawthar (Muslim 400) in both its narrations and explains the relationship between the river and the Pond; explains the ayat of al-Baqarah 2:25 and al-Insan 76:5-6 through the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; sets out the two views of the scholars on the hadith of "Sayhan and Jayhan" as an-Nawawi relates them; draws out the two lessons of conduct, gratitude through worship and holding firm to the Sunnah; and draws the chain of the water's source and commits to a week of giving others water to drink.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board divided into three columns headed "What is established about the rivers and springs," "What we say, and go no further," and "What the gift asks of us"; a map or picture of the city's water source; the activity sheet; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Muslim 400 in both its narrations with an-Nawawi's commentary; al-Bukhari 6581 and 4964 with Ibn Hajar's commentary and al-'Ayni's remark; Muslim 2839 with an-Nawawi's commentary; the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Baqarah 2:25 and al-Insan 76:5-6; and chapter forty-seven of Ibn al-Qayyim's *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher keeps in mind that the details of the four rivers in Surat Muhammad 47:15, Tasnim and Salsabil, and the rivers flowing without channels belong to other lessons, and does not expand on them. The teacher also prepares reliable information about the city's water source.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "When did you feel the value of water more than ever before?" and then: "How many steps does water pass through before it reaches your cup?" The answers are written on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Baqarah with Ibn Kathir's tafsir (3 minutes), then the ayah of al-Insan with the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on making the spring gush forth (4 minutes). Then read the hadith of al-Kawthar in full, pausing at the smile, the question, the Companions' answer, and the end of the hadith; then the second narration and Ibn Hajar's statement (8 minutes). Then read al-Bukhari 6581 and Muslim 2839 with the two views of the scholars (5 minutes). Fill in the first and second columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 3, 4 and 5. The teacher points out that the surah made prayer and sacrifice the response to the gift, and that the hadith made being kept away from the Pond the result of introducing new things into the religion, so belonging alone is not enough. Fill in the third column.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner draws the chain of water from their cup back to its source with the information available (7 minutes) and writes the two lines of comparison (3 minutes), then chooses their water-giving act, sets its place and time, and swaps it with a partner for follow-up (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "Give two pieces of evidence describing the rivers and springs of Jannah, and name the lesson of conduct you took from the end of the hadith of al-Kawthar." Then recite Surat al-Kawthar, and agree to review the water-giving logs in a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayah of al-Baqarah and the hadith of al-Kawthar are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations of the hadith of al-Kawthar in the two Sahihs and the Sunan and compare their wordings, and to review what Ibn Hajar and al-'Ayni say about the relationship between the Pond and the river.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not delve into how the rivers of this world come from Jannah without evidence, and do not present interpretive views as though they were the text itself. Do not name individuals or groups as being among those turned away from the Pond; keep to the general meaning: holding firm to the Sunnah. Do not build descriptions on reports in the books on the description of Jannah that are not established. Remind participants that giving water is done without photos or showing off, and with attention to safety when dealing with strangers.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that in Jannah there are rivers flowing under the trees and homes, and that al-Kawthar is a river Allah gave to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; retells that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, smiled when the surah was revealed, and that the Companions said, "Allah and His Messenger know best"; draws the path of water to their cup; and gives their family water before themselves for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A clear cup of water; a small dish; large paper and blue crayons; a picture of a beautiful river with no writing and no people; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account without adding anything that is not in it, and memorizes Surat al-Kawthar in order to recite it. The caregiver also prepares a simple drawing of the water's path (cup, faucet, pipe, river, cloud) for the child to use as a guide.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The caregiver pours water into the cup in front of the child and asks, "Can you hear the water? Where did it come from?" Then the caregiver shows the picture of the river.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation and then the account, pausing at the Prophet's smile, peace and blessings be upon him, to ask, "Why did he smile?" Then recite Surat al-Kawthar, and the child repeats it along with you.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 1, 2 and 3, and teach the child the Companions' sentence, "Allah and His Messenger know best," for things we do not know.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child and the caregiver draw the water's path backward, saying "Alhamdulillah" at each drawing; then together they choose a safe place for the birds' water dish. The child finishes coloring the drops over the following three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** The caregiver asks the child, "Who will you put water out for tomorrow?" and then, "What is the name of the Prophet's river, peace and blessings be upon him?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, three stations (cup, faucet, rain) and the sentence "Al-Kawthar is a river in Jannah" are enough. Ask older children to tell the story to a brother or sister in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, the Companions, or the angels, and do not act out the scene through role-play. Mention that some people will be kept away from the Pond in one calm sentence, without frightening the child. Do not describe the river with details that are not in the texts as though they were certain. The child places the water dish together with a parent, in a safe spot away from the road.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith of al-Kawthar in sequence; explains the terms al-Kawthar, the Pond, and "making it gush forth in abundance"; names three established features of the rivers and springs of Jannah; explains the Companions' answer, "Allah and His Messenger know best"; and draws a map of the water's source and gives others water to drink for five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; paper for the map; a five-day chart; a mushaf; a picture of a river and a picture of a faucet, with no writing.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the ayah of al-Baqarah 2:25 (its first part) and the ayat of al-Insan 76:5-6 on the board, and prepares information about the city's water source for students who cannot find an answer at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Imagine the water at your home were cut off for a whole day. What would you miss first?" and then: "How many people work hard so that water can reach you?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the ayat (3 minutes), then the story, pausing at the question "Do you know what al-Kawthar is?" before reading the answer (6 minutes), then explain the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 2, 4 and 5. Make clear that the Companions did not guess about the unseen, that the surah taught thanking Allah for a blessing through prayer, and that the way to the Pond is following the Sunnah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student starts their map with the stations they already know and leaves the rest to ask their parents about, writes the two sentences of comparison, prepares the five-day chart, and decides who beyond their family table they will give water to: a bird, an animal, or a worker.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes: "What is al-Kawthar? And what brings me closer to the Pond of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him?" Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide a ready-made map with blanks. Ask advanced students to memorize Surat al-Kawthar along with the meanings of its words, and to write a paragraph about the four rivers of this world mentioned in the hadith and where they are.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions. Never tell a student that he or she "will not reach the Pond," and do not use the hadith to frighten or accuse. Point out that water is offered to workers only with the parents' permission and in their presence, and that the dish for animals is placed somewhere safe and clean, without touching stray animals.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes Surat al-Kawthar in light of the hadith and distinguishes a person's worth before Allah from the measure of numbers; explains the meaning of "pulled away" and "what he introduced after you"; distinguishes belonging from following; identifies a situation in which they feel pressure to adjust their religion; and carries out the water chain and the week of giving water without posting about it.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; cards for anonymous questions; a mushaf; a map of the city's water source.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares three neutral scenarios: a post calling for an established ruling to be "updated" to suit the audience; a friend who mocks someone for keeping a visible Sunnah; and a measure of success based on follower counts. The teacher is also ready for the question "Who are the people turned away from the Pond?" with an answer that keeps to the wording of the hadith and names no one.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "When have you felt that your worth was being measured by a number?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the story, pausing at three moments: the smile, the Companions' answer, and the man who is snatched away from the Pond. Then read the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Groups discuss the three scenarios in light of questions 1 and 3, then answer question 4 in private writing. The teacher makes clear that holding firm to the Sunnah does not mean being harsh with people, and that true gratitude is worship, not a show.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student draws the water chain and writes at each station what would cut off the water if it stopped, then writes the comparison paragraph, and decides who outside their circle they will give water to and when.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "What does it mean to me that the answer to a gift is prayer?" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about the experience without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the two views of the scholars about the meaning of "*al-abtar*" and about what it means for the Nile and the Euphrates to be among the rivers of Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not name contemporary individuals or groups as being among those who "introduced new things," and do not use the hadith to declare anyone an unbeliever or to shame anyone. Discuss peer pressure without revealing names. Point out safety when dealing with strangers, and that the water-giving should take place in a public place with the family's knowledge.
