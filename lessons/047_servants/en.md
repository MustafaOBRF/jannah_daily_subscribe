# The Servants and Young Attendants of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the Qur'anic scene in Surah al-Insan (76:5-22): the truly righteous, who give food, despite their love for it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive for the sake of Allah, wanting neither reward nor thanks, out of fear of a grim Day; and so Allah protects them from the evil of that Day and rewards them with a Garden in which "there circulate among them immortal youths; when you see them, you would think them scattered pearls."
- Explain what the commentators say *mukhalladun* means: youths who stay forever at one age, never growing older and never changing; mention the other view, that they wear earrings; and explain why they are likened to scattered pearls and to hidden pearls.
- Present fairly the two views on who these youths are: that they are beings Allah has created in Jannah, the view Ibn al-Qayyim preferred and as-Sa'di held, and a view reported from Ali and al-Hasan; and make clear that the lesson does not rely on a weak hadith on this question.
- Draw on the hadith of Fatimah, may Allah be pleased with her, when she asked for a servant (al-Bukhari 3705), and the hadith of Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to be at the service of his household (al-Bukhari 676), to show that serving others in this world is worship, not a loss of dignity.
- Carry out the "Take Over the Service Shift" activity: predicting the hidden tasks behind a service someone does for you every day, then doing it yourself for a set period and recording what you actually found, then thanking that person in specific terms, and keeping one task as your own for good, for the sake of Allah.
- Learn by heart the bedtime du'a of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "O Allah, protect me from Your punishment on the Day You raise up Your servants" (Abu Dawud 5045), and connect it with what Allah says of the truly righteous: "So Allah protected them from the evil of that Day."

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every home and every institution has people we see without ever really noticing: the ones who cook, who clean, who set things in order before we arrive and after we leave. In most people's eyes, serving is a lowly station. Many would love to be served; few want to serve.

The Qur'an tells us that the people of Jannah will have servants moving among them who never grow older and never change, as though they were scattered pearls. Yet the passage in Surah al-Insan where this appears begins with people who, in this world, were the ones doing the serving: they fed the hungry and the vulnerable with food they themselves loved. This lesson reads the whole scene, pauses over what the scholars have said about who these youths are, and then asks: how do we serve here, while we hope for the honor of that abode?

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-76-5-22" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "We Feed You Only for the Sake of Allah"

> **{إِنَّ الْأَبْرَارَ يَشْرَبُونَ مِنْ كَأْسٍ كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا كَافُورًا ۝ عَيْنًا يَشْرَبُ بِهَا عِبَادُ اللَّهِ يُفَجِّرُونَهَا تَفْجِيرًا ۝ يُوفُونَ بِالنَّذْرِ وَيَخَافُونَ يَوْمًا كَانَ شَرُّهُ مُسْتَطِيرًا ۝ وَيُطْعِمُونَ الطَّعَامَ عَلَىٰ حُبِّهِ مِسْكِينًا وَيَتِيمًا وَأَسِيرًا ۝ إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ لَا نُرِيدُ مِنْكُمْ جَزَاءً وَلَا شُكُورًا ۝ إِنَّا نَخَافُ مِنْ رَبِّنَا يَوْمًا عَبُوسًا قَمْطَرِيرًا ۝ فَوَقَاهُمُ اللَّهُ شَرَّ ذَٰلِكَ الْيَوْمِ وَلَقَّاهُمْ نَضْرَةً وَسُرُورًا ۝ وَجَزَاهُمْ بِمَا صَبَرُوا جَنَّةً وَحَرِيرًا ۝ مُتَّكِئِينَ فِيهَا عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ ۖ لَا يَرَوْنَ فِيهَا شَمْسًا وَلَا زَمْهَرِيرًا ۝ وَدَانِيَةً عَلَيْهِمْ ظِلَالُهَا وَذُلِّلَتْ قُطُوفُهَا تَذْلِيلًا ۝ وَيُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِآنِيَةٍ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ كَانَتْ قَوَارِيرَا ۝ قَوَارِيرَ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ قَدَّرُوهَا تَقْدِيرًا ۝ وَيُسْقَوْنَ فِيهَا كَأْسًا كَانَ مِزَاجُهَا زَنْجَبِيلًا ۝ عَيْنًا فِيهَا تُسَمَّىٰ سَلْسَبِيلًا ۝ وَيَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ وِلْدَانٌ مُخَلَّدُونَ إِذَا رَأَيْتَهُمْ حَسِبْتَهُمْ لُؤْلُؤًا مَنْثُورًا ۝ وَإِذَا رَأَيْتَ ثَمَّ رَأَيْتَ نَعِيمًا وَمُلْكًا كَبِيرًا ۝ عَالِيَهُمْ ثِيَابُ سُنْدُسٍ خُضْرٌ وَإِسْتَبْرَقٌ ۖ وَحُلُّوا أَسَاوِرَ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَسَقَاهُمْ رَبُّهُمْ شَرَابًا طَهُورًا ۝ إِنَّ هَٰذَا كَانَ لَكُمْ جَزَاءً وَكَانَ سَعْيُكُمْ مَشْكُورًا}** [الإنسان: ٥-٢٢][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"The truly righteous will drink from a cup whose mixture is camphor: a spring from which the servants of Allah drink, making it gush forth in abundance. They fulfill their vows, and they fear a Day whose evil will spread far and wide. And they give food, despite their love for it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive: 'We feed you only for the sake of Allah. We want from you neither reward nor thanks. We fear from our Lord a Day, grim and distressful.' So Allah protected them from the evil of that Day, and granted them radiance and joy, and rewarded them for their patience with a Garden and silk. Reclining there on canopied couches, they will see there neither scorching sun nor bitter cold. Its shade will be close above them, and its clusters of fruit will hang low, within easy reach. Vessels of silver will be passed around among them, and goblets of crystal: crystal made of silver, measured out to the exact amount. And they will be given to drink there a cup whose mixture is ginger, from a spring there called Salsabil. And there will circulate among them immortal youths; when you see them, you would think them scattered pearls. And when you look there, you will see bliss and a great kingdom. Upon them will be garments of fine green silk and rich brocade, and they will be adorned with bracelets of silver, and their Lord will give them a pure drink. 'This is your reward, and your striving has been met with thanks.'"** (al-Insan 76:5-22)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On "despite their love for it" (*'ala hubbihi*), Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, says that the most evident reading takes the pronoun to refer to the food: they give it away while they love it and crave it themselves. This is the view of Mujahid and Muqatil, and the one Ibn Jarir chose. On the captive, he mentions that on the day of Badr the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, ordered his Companions to treat the prisoners with honor, and they would serve the prisoners first, before themselves, at the morning meal. On "We want from you neither reward nor thanks," he says: we seek from you no repayment, and no thanks offered before people; and he reports from Mujahid and Sa'id ibn Jubayr that they did not say this aloud, but Allah knew it was in their hearts and praised them for it. On "immortal youths" (*wildanun mukhalladun*), he says: youths from among the youths of Jannah circulate among its people to serve them, remaining in one state that never changes, their age never going beyond that point; and when you see them spread out on the errands of their masters, in their great numbers and with their bright, beautiful faces, you would think them scattered pearls, "and no comparison could be finer than this."[^1]

As-Sa'di says that they say "We feed you only..." through the language of their conduct, not their tongues, seeking "neither a material reward nor verbal praise." The youths "were created from Jannah to remain forever," and "this is part of the completeness of the delight of the people of Jannah": they enter their dwellings with the people of Jannah "free of any worry on their account," and bring them whatever they ask for. On "and your striving has been met with thanks," he says that for even a little of it, Allah grants you bliss beyond all counting.[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The scene is built on a fine correspondence. People served those in need in this world, with things they loved, and waited for no thanks; part of their reward is that in Jannah they are served, and then they are told: "your striving has been met with thanks." The thanks they never asked of people came to them from their Lord. This correspondence is the lesson's own educational reflection, not a quoted statement of the scholars.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-52-24" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "As Though They Were Hidden Pearls"

> **{وَيَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ غِلْمَانٌ لَهُمْ كَأَنَّهُمْ لُؤْلُؤٌ مَكْنُونٌ}** [الطور: ٢٤][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And there will circulate among them young servants of their own, as though they were hidden pearls."** (at-Tur 52:24)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says this tells us about their servants in Jannah: they are like fresh, hidden pearls in their beauty, their splendor, their cleanness and the fineness of their clothing. As-Sa'di says they are young servants who go around among them, serving them and seeing to whatever they need, and that this shows how abundant their bliss is and how complete their ease.[^4][^2]

The scholars differ over who these youths are. It is reported from Ali ibn Abi Talib, may Allah be pleased with him, and from al-Hasan al-Basri that they are the children of the Muslims who die young, and from Salman that they are the children of the polytheists.[^3] The other view is that they are beings Allah has brought into existence in Jannah. Ibn al-Qayyim says: "The likelier view is that these youths are created from Jannah, like the *hur al-'in*," and he takes as evidence the words "young servants of their own" (*ghilmanun lahum*), saying: "These are other than their own children, for part of the completeness of Allah's honoring of them is that He makes their children served along with them, not that He makes them their servants." Al-Ashqar quotes Ibn Taymiyyah as saying: "They are a creation from among the creation of Jannah; they are not from the children of this world."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

In Surah at-Tur, this ayah comes right after the ayah about the believers' offspring being joined with their parents (at-Tur 52:21), so the believers' children are honored alongside their parents. How all this will be remains part of the unseen, so the difference of opinion is mentioned with courtesy, and nothing is built on a weak report.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-17-18" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "With Goblets and Ewers"

> **{يَطُوفُ عَلَيْهِمْ وِلْدَانٌ مُخَلَّدُونَ ۝ بِأَكْوَابٍ وَأَبَارِيقَ وَكَأْسٍ مِنْ مَعِينٍ}** [الواقعة: ١٧-١٨][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"There will circulate among them immortal youths, with goblets and ewers, and a cup from a flowing spring."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:17-18)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says: *mukhalladun* means they remain in a single state, never growing out of it, never turning gray and never changing.[^5] Al-Qurtubi cites Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "*Mukhalladun* means wearing earrings (*muqarratun*)," that is, with earrings in their ears; and Ibn al-Qayyim brings the two views together: they are youths whom old age never touches, and in their ears are earrings.[^3][^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Goblets and ewers are serving vessels we know from our own homes, and anyone who has carried a pitcher of water to their family knows the worth of this picture.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3705" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Better for You Both Than a Servant"

> عَنْ عَلِيٍّ رضي الله عنه أَنَّ فَاطِمَةَ عَلَيْهَا السَّلَامُ شَكَتْ مَا تَلْقَى مِنْ أَثَرِ الرَّحَا، فَأَتَى النَّبِيَّ ﷺ سَبْيٌ، فَانْطَلَقَتْ فَلَمْ تَجِدْهُ، فَوَجَدَتْ عَائِشَةَ فَأَخْبَرَتْهَا، فَلَمَّا جَاءَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ أَخْبَرَتْهُ عَائِشَةُ بِمَجِيءِ فَاطِمَةَ، فَجَاءَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ إِلَيْنَا وَقَدْ أَخَذْنَا مَضَاجِعَنَا، فَذَهَبْتُ لِأَقُومَ، فَقَالَ: **«عَلَى مَكَانِكُمَا»**، فَقَعَدَ بَيْنَنَا حَتَّى وَجَدْتُ بَرْدَ قَدَمَيْهِ عَلَى صَدْرِي، وَقَالَ: **«أَلَا أُعَلِّمُكُمَا خَيْرًا مِمَّا سَأَلْتُمَانِي؟ إِذَا أَخَذْتُمَا مَضَاجِعَكُمَا تُكَبِّرَا أَرْبَعًا وَثَلَاثِينَ، وَتُسَبِّحَا ثَلَاثًا وَثَلَاثِينَ، وَتَحْمَدَا ثَلَاثَةً وَثَلَاثِينَ، فَهُوَ خَيْرٌ لَكُمَا مِنْ خَادِمٍ»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ali, may Allah be pleased with him: Fatimah, peace be upon her, complained of what the hand mill was doing to her. Then some captives were brought to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, so she went to him, but she did not find him. She found Aishah and told her. When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came, Aishah told him that Fatimah had come. So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, came to us after we had already gone to bed. I started to get up, but he said: **"Stay where you are."** He sat down between us, so close that I felt the coolness of his feet on my chest, and he said: **"Shall I not teach you both something better than what you asked me for? When you go to bed, say 'Allahu akbar' thirty-four times, 'Subhan Allah' thirty-three times, and 'Al-hamdu lillah' thirty-three times. That is better for you both than a servant."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Ar-raha* is the millstone turned by hand for grinding grain, and it had left its mark on her hand, as in al-Bukhari's narration 5361: "what she suffered in her hand from the mill." In narration 6318, she came "to ask him for a servant."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The Prophet's own daughter, peace and blessings be upon him, served her household with her own hands until she was worn out. He did not give her a servant; instead, he guided her to a remembrance at bedtime that is better than a servant. Serving in this world is no shame, and true help comes from Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-676" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "He Would Be at the Service of His Household"

> عَنِ الْأَسْوَدِ قَالَ: سَأَلْتُ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها: مَا كَانَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ يَصْنَعُ فِي بَيْتِهِ؟ قَالَتْ: **كَانَ يَكُونُ فِي مِهْنَةِ أَهْلِهِ، تَعْنِي خِدْمَةَ أَهْلِهِ، فَإِذَا حَضَرَتِ الصَّلَاةُ خَرَجَ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of al-Aswad, who said: I asked Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, "What did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, do at home?" She said: **"He would be busy with the work of his household (meaning, serving his household), and when the time for prayer came, he would go out to the prayer."**[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The best of creation, peace and blessings be upon him, served his family in his own home. Whoever serves others is following his example, not stepping down from their standing.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayat of al-Insan trace a path: service to those in need, given with love for what is given; a sincere intention; fear of a hard Day; and then protection, joy, and a Garden whose people are served. At-Tur and al-Waqi'ah describe these servants in their beauty and their permanence. The two hadiths show that the household of the Prophet was a household of service. So the practical question is: who serves you every day, and what service do you offer for the sake of Allah?

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What three deeds describe the truly righteous in al-Insan 76:7-10, and what motive did they state?
2. What does "despite their love for it" mean according to Ibn Kathir, and why does that raise the value of the giving?
3. What does *mukhalladun* mean, and what is the point of likening the youths to scattered pearls, and then to hidden pearls?
4. What are the two views on who the youths are, and what evidence did Ibn al-Qayyim use to support his view?
5. How do "We want from you neither reward nor thanks" and "your striving has been met with thanks" fit together?

### Activity: Take Over the Service Shift

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.047.activity.take-over-a-service-shift" -->

Choose a service someone regularly does for you: a meal a family member prepares, the laundry, cleaning an office, or setting up a meeting room. Before you begin, write a list of the tasks you expect it to involve. Then take that service over yourself for three days, record every task you actually found, and circle whatever was not on your list. At the end, thank the person who usually does it, in specific terms, naming two hidden tasks you discovered. Keep one task to do from then on, permanently, and write a paragraph: what has changed in the way you see the people who serve you, and in your intention when you serve?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="4.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Who puts your cup on the table? Who pours you water when you're thirsty? At home, there are people who love you and take care of you every single day.

In Jannah, Allah gives its people a great and special honor: beautiful boys go around among them, carrying cups and pitchers, and they never grow up and never change. Allah said about them: when you see them, you would think they were scattered pearls, like shiny pearls sprinkled here and there.[^1][^5] And many scholars said: they are special beings Allah created in Jannah to serve its people.[^2][^6]

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to help his family at home and serve them.[^8]

### A Story from the Qur'an: The Food They Loved

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-76-5-22" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

In Surah al-Insan, Allah tells us about some very good people. They are called "the truly righteous" (*al-abrar*).

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-76-5-22" audience="4-7" -->

The truly righteous had food that they loved and really wanted to eat. And near them were people in need: a poor person with no food, an orphan child whose father had died, and a captive who was locked up.

What did the truly righteous do? They gave them the food they loved! And their hearts were saying: "We're feeding you because Allah loves it. We don't want anything from you, not even a thank-you."[^1]

They were afraid of the Day of Judgment, and they hoped Allah would keep them safe on that Day. So Allah kept them safe from the bad things of that Day. He made their faces shine and their hearts happy, and He brought them into Jannah. There, beautiful boys go around among them, like scattered pearls, and they are told: "Your striving has been met with thanks." That means Allah thanked them for what they did.[^1][^2]

<!-- retelling:end -->

The truly righteous served people in need in this world, for Allah, and Allah honored them in Jannah. You can serve your family today for Allah, too.

### Activity: I'm the Table Helper

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.047.activity.take-over-a-service-shift" -->

Then be the "table helper" for three days: put out the cups, carry the little water pitcher, and set out the spoons. Every time you find a job you didn't draw, put a small pebble in a bowl. On the last day, count the pebbles with your family. Then draw a "thank-you pearl" for the person who gets the table ready every day and give it to them, and choose one job you will always keep doing, for Allah.

### The Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him, When We Go to Bed

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.047.dua.guard-me-on-the-day-you-raise" provenance="sunnah" source_id="abudawud-5045" attribution="prophetic" -->

When the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to go to sleep, he would put his right hand under his cheek and say:

> **اللَّهُمَّ قِنِي عَذَابَكَ يَوْمَ تَبْعَثُ عِبَادَكَ.**[^9]
>
> *"O Allah, protect me from Your punishment on the Day You raise up Your servants."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, keep me safe from Your punishment on the Day of Judgment, when You bring all the people back to life, just as You kept the truly righteous safe.[^9]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

At recess, some kids toss their empty juice pouches on the ground and walk off. After the bell rings, the custodian comes and quietly picks them all up. Hardly anyone knows his name, and nobody says "thank you" to him.

In this lesson we learn that Allah described the people of Jannah this way: "And there will circulate among them immortal youths," serving them with goblets and pitchers,[^1][^5] and that this came as a reward for people who, in this world, served those in need for the sake of Allah. Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, also told us that at home the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was "busy with the work of his household," which means serving them.[^8]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-76-5-22" -->

- **`The truly righteous (al-abrar)`** — the people of *birr*, deep goodness: obedient people who do a great deal of good, as the ayat right after describe them.[^1]
- **`Despite their love for it ('ala hubbihi)`** — while they themselves loved the food and wanted it, as Ibn Kathir explained it.[^1]
- **`Immortal youths (wildanun mukhalladun)`** — young servants who stay at one age forever, never growing older and never changing.[^1][^5]
- **`Scattered pearls (lu'lu'an manthuran)`** — pearls spread out here and there, because the youths are spread out everywhere, busy serving.[^1][^3]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: We Want No Thanks from You

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-76-5-22" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah al-Insan begins with the creation of the human being, and then speaks of two paths: being grateful or being ungrateful. Then it tells us about the grateful ones, and calls them "the truly righteous."[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-76-5-22" audience="8-12" -->

The truly righteous kept the promises they had made to Allah, and they feared a Day whose evil would spread far and wide. And "they give food, despite their love for it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive." Ibn Kathir said: they gave it while they loved it and wanted it themselves. And he mentioned that on the day of Badr, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told his Companions to treat the prisoners with honor, and they would serve the prisoners first, before themselves, at the morning meal.[^1]

And they would say: "We feed you only for the sake of Allah. We want from you neither reward nor thanks." Mujahid and Sa'id ibn Jubayr said: they did not say it out loud, but Allah knew it was in their hearts, and He praised them for it.[^1]

So what happened in the end? "So Allah protected them from the evil of that Day, and granted them radiance and joy." He rewarded them with a Garden and silk; they sit on canopied couches, with the shade close above them. And immortal youths go around among them: "when you see them, you would think them scattered pearls." Then they are told: "This is your reward, and your striving has been met with thanks."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

As-Sa'di said that part of the complete delight of the people of Jannah is that their servants are these immortal youths, who are a joy to look at.[^2] Notice this: the truly righteous did not ask people for thanks, so Allah thanked them for their striving. They served in this world, and they were honored by being served in Jannah.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. Who were the three kinds of people the truly righteous fed? And why did Allah mention that they fed them "despite their love for it"?
2. What did Mujahid say about their words "We want from you neither reward nor thanks"?
3. What does *mukhalladun* mean? And why are the youths compared to scattered pearls?
4. Who is the "custodian" in your life, the person who serves you and whom nobody thanks?
5. What service can you do this week without waiting for anyone to thank you?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: The Hidden Service Shift

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.047.activity.take-over-a-service-shift" -->

Choose a service someone does for you every day: packing your school lunch, folding the laundry, or washing the dishes. Draw a chart with two columns: "What I expect" and "What I actually found." Fill in the first column before you start. Then, with your family's permission, take that service over for three days, and in the second column write down every task you did, circling the ones you didn't expect. After that, write a thank-you card to the person who usually does it, naming two hidden tasks you discovered. Then choose one task to keep as yours for the whole week, and make your intention that you are doing it for the sake of Allah.

<!-- activity:end -->

### The Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him, When Going to Bed

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="8-12" id="lesson.047.dua.guard-me-on-the-day-you-raise" provenance="sunnah" source_id="abudawud-5045" attribution="prophetic" -->

Hafsah, the Mother of the Believers, may Allah be pleased with her, reported that whenever the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to lie down to sleep, he would put his right hand under his cheek and then say:

> **اللَّهُمَّ قِنِي عَذَابَكَ يَوْمَ تَبْعَثُ عِبَادَكَ.**[^9]
>
> *"O Allah, protect me from Your punishment on the Day You raise up Your servants."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, protect me from Your punishment on the Day of Judgment, when You raise Your servants from their graves. It is close to what Allah says about the truly righteous: "So Allah protected them from the evil of that Day."[^9][^1]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a world of posts, almost every act of service gets filmed: handing out food, visiting someone in need, helping with a campaign. Filming is not forbidden in itself, but here is the hard question: would you still serve if no one saw you and no one thanked you? And the other side of it: how many people serve you every day without your ever seeing them?

This lesson reads a scene from Surah al-Insan: people who served those in need and said in their hearts, "We want from you neither reward nor thanks," and part of whose reward is that in Jannah "there will circulate among them immortal youths."[^1] And in an authentic hadith, Fatimah, the daughter of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him for a servant because grinding grain had worn her out, and he taught her a remembrance for bedtime and said: "That is better for you both than a servant."[^7]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-76-5-22" -->

- **`For the sake of Allah (li-wajhillah)`** — seeking the pleasure of Allah alone, not something in return from people; as-Sa'di said: "neither a material reward nor verbal praise."[^2]
- **`A Day, grim and distressful ('abusan qamtarira)`** — a Day on which faces scowl in terror, intensely hard and long in its affliction, as Ibn Kathir reports.[^1]
- **`Hidden (maknun)`** — guarded and covered, like a pearl in its shell that no hand has touched.[^4][^6]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: Those Who Served Without Thanks

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="quranic" source_id="quran-76-5-22" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an; the explanations in it come from the scholars of tafsir.**

Surah al-Insan opens with the creation of the human being, then says that Allah showed him the way, to be either grateful or ungrateful, and then paints a portrait of the truly righteous, the ones who chose the path of gratitude.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-76-5-22" audience="13+" -->

Their life in this world: they fulfill their vows, and they fear a Day whose evil spreads far and wide, and "they give food, despite their love for it, to the poor, the orphan and the captive." Three people who have no way of paying back a kindness: someone poor, a child with no father, and a prisoner in chains. And the captives at that time were polytheists, as Ibn Abbas said in what Ibn Kathir reports.[^1]

Their intention: "We feed you only for the sake of Allah. We want from you neither reward nor thanks. We fear from our Lord a Day, grim and distressful." Ibn Kathir explains: we do not ask you to thank us in front of people; and he reports that they never said it aloud, but Allah knew it was in their hearts.[^1]

Then the reward: "So Allah protected them from the evil of that Day, and granted them radiance and joy": a Garden and silk, canopied couches, shade close above them, vessels of silver, and "there will circulate among them immortal youths; when you see them, you would think them scattered pearls." And at the close: "your striving has been met with thanks."[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

So who are these youths? Ibn al-Qayyim preferred the view, which as-Sa'di also held, that they are beings Allah has brought into existence in Jannah; and it is reported from Ali and al-Hasan that they are the children of the Muslims who died young. Ibn al-Qayyim said: part of the completeness of Allah's honoring of the believers is that He makes their children served along with them.[^6][^2][^3]

Notice the irony: those who refused to make people's thanks their goal were thanked by Allah. And those who served people who could never repay them were themselves served in the abode of honor.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the truly righteous choose to serve people who had no way of repaying them? What would that look like in your own life?
2. Does wanting thanks from people always spoil an act of service? What does Mujahid's statement tell us about where the intention lives?
3. How do you understand the fact that Fatimah, may Allah be pleased with her, was given not a servant but a remembrance "better than a servant"?
4. Why is it right to mention the difference of opinion about who the youths are with courtesy, without asserting as certain what has not been established?
5. Who serves you every day whom you have never once thanked in specific terms?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Silent Service Shift

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.047.activity.take-over-a-service-shift" -->

Choose a service someone regularly does for you: your mom or dad, a staff member at school or at your club, or an older brother or sister. In a private notebook, list what you expect that service involves. Then, with that person's permission, take it over yourself or share in it for seven days, and record every actual task and how many minutes it took, circling whatever you did not expect. Record, too, every moment you wished someone would notice you, and what you did with that moment. At the end, thank the person in specific terms, naming what you discovered. Keep one task for yourself that you tell no one about, and write a paragraph: what is the difference between being served and serving?

<!-- activity:end -->

### The Du'a of the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him, When Going to Bed

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.047.dua.guard-me-on-the-day-you-raise" provenance="sunnah" source_id="abudawud-5045" attribution="prophetic" -->

It is authentically reported that when the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, wanted to sleep, he would put his right hand under his cheek and then say:

> **اللَّهُمَّ قِنِي عَذَابَكَ يَوْمَ تَبْعَثُ عِبَادَكَ.**[^9]
>
> *"O Allah, protect me from Your punishment on the Day You raise up Your servants."*
>
> What it means: O Allah, protect me from Your punishment on the Day You raise up Your servants. Sleep resembles death, and waking up is a reminder of the Resurrection; so the believer ends the day with the same fear the truly righteous voiced, "We fear from our Lord a Day," hoping for what they were given: "So Allah protected them from the evil of that Day."[^9][^1]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the scene of al-Insan 76:5-22 in its stages: the deed, the intention and the fear, then the protection and the reward; explains "despite their love for it," *mukhalladun*, "scattered pearls" and *maknun* using the statements of Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and al-Qurtubi; presents the two views on who the youths are, with Ibn al-Qayyim's preferred view and his evidence; draws on al-Bukhari 3705 and 676; and plans a three-day service shift.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson for each learner; a mushaf for Surahs al-Insan, at-Tur and al-Waqi'ah; a board divided into two columns headed "In this world" and "In Jannah"; the activity sheet with two columns, "What I expect" and "What I actually found"; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Ibn Kathir's tafsir of al-Insan 76:7-12, 19 and 22; as-Sa'di on ayat 8, 9, 19 and 22; al-Qurtubi on al-Waqi'ah 56:17 and al-Insan 76:19; and chapter fifty-two of *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher also prepares a personal example of a service they receive without ever having noticed what goes into it.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Name someone who served you today before you got here. How many tasks do you think they did?" And then: "When was the last time you served someone without waiting to be thanked?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Insan 76:5-22 in full, while learners fill in the "In this world" and "In Jannah" columns (7 minutes). Then discuss the statements of Ibn Kathir, Mujahid and as-Sa'di on ayat 8-9 (4 minutes). Then at-Tur 52:24 and al-Waqi'ah 56:17-18, the meaning of *mukhalladun*, and the two views on who the youths are (5 minutes). Then the two hadiths (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher discusses question five: the truly righteous did not ask for thanks, and yet "your striving has been met with thanks." The teacher makes clear that the correspondence between their serving in this world and their being served in Jannah is the lesson's own educational reflection. Then the teacher asks: how does a believer combine not waiting for thanks when serving with thanking those who serve them?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner chooses a service they receive and writes the "What I expect" column, then swaps with a partner so that each can add a task the other may have missed. Each then sets the three days and names the person whose permission they will ask.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "What does 'despite their love for it' mean?", "What are the two views on who the youths are?" and "Which service will you take over?" Then read the bedtime du'a from Abu Dawud 5045, connecting it with al-Insan 76:11.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, limit the reading to al-Insan 76:8-11, 19 and 22, together with the hadith of Aishah. Ask advanced learners for a table comparing what Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di, al-Qurtubi and Ibn al-Qayyim say about *mukhalladun* and about who the youths are, with the source of each statement.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not assert anything about who the youths are beyond what the scholars have said, and do not present as established the hadith of Anas: "I asked my Lord that He not punish the heedless ones (*al-lahin*) among the descendants of mankind, and He granted them to me, so they are the servants of the people of Jannah." Ibn al-Qayyim mentions it, but the editors of his book cite Ibn al-Jawzi: "This hadith is not established," and it rests entirely on Yazid ar-Raqashi, who is weak; so the lesson does not rely on it.[^10] Nor does the lesson rely on stories about the occasion of revelation of the ayat of al-Insan whose authenticity has not been verified. Do not use the lesson in a way that belittles people who work in service jobs; its purpose is to honor them. Be gentle with anyone who has lost a young child: remind them that the children of the believers are joined with their parents, as in at-Tur 52:21, without bringing the scholarly disagreement up in front of them.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the truly righteous gave food they loved to people who needed it, for Allah; that boys as beautiful as pearls go around among the people of Jannah; and that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, used to serve his family; and the child plays the role of "table helper" for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Imitation pearls or shiny white beads; a dark piece of cloth; plastic cups and a small pitcher; paper and coloring pencils; a bowl and small pebbles; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the story calmly, sets up a small table for acting it out, and sends families a short note explaining the three-day activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The educator scatters the pearls across the dark cloth and asks: "Which is prettier: pearls in a bag, or pearls scattered and sparkling here and there?" Then says: "That is how Allah described the servants of the people of Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** The educator reads the introduction and then the story, and asks the children to repeat after them: «لُؤْلُؤًا مَنْثُورًا» (*lu'lu'an manthura*, "scattered pearls") and «وَكَانَ سَعْيُكُمْ مَشْكُورًا» (*wa kana sa'yukum mashkura*, "and your striving has been met with thanks").

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator asks the unit's questions and emphasizes that the truly righteous gave for Allah, not to hear a thank-you, and that Allah thanked them.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The children practice setting the small table with the cups and the pitcher. Before they begin, they draw what they think getting the table ready will need; then they put down a pebble for every new job they find, and they take the sheet home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** The educator notes whether the child can say whom the truly righteous gave food to, and what the boys in Jannah were compared to. Then the children are shown how to put their right hand under their cheek, and everyone says the bedtime du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest children, setting out the cups is enough. Older children can be asked to memorize: «إِنَّمَا نُطْعِمُكُمْ لِوَجْهِ اللَّهِ» (*innama nut'imukum li-wajhillah*, "We feed you only for the sake of Allah").

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not mention to the children the difference of opinion about who the youths are, or the weak hadith, and do not link the youths to the death of children. Do not frighten the child about the Day of Judgment; present the du'a as asking Allah to keep us safe. And do not give the child any task that involves danger, such as knives or hot water.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the stages of the scene in al-Insan 76:7-22; defines the four terms; explains what Mujahid said about intention; fills in the "What I expect" and "What I actually found" chart over three days; and writes a specific thank-you card.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; a chart sheet for each student; a mushaf; blank thank-you cards; four cards for the stages: "They gave," "Their intention," "They feared," "Their reward."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares a list of everyday services students receive at school and at home and, if possible, arranges for the school custodian to visit the class at the end of the week to be thanked.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher reads the custodian scene from the introduction and asks: "How many tasks does the custodian do after recess? Does anyone here know his name?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the terms (3 minutes), then the whole story, arranging the four stage cards on the board and pointing out the difference between the text of the ayah and the words of Ibn Kathir, Mujahid and as-Sa'di (9 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** In groups, each group picks a service from the list and writes the tasks they expect it involves; then the teacher compares this with what they know of its real tasks, to show how much stays hidden from us.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student sets up their chart as described in the activity unit, fills in the "What I expect" column, and agrees with the teacher on a review day three days later.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The truly righteous gave... because they... so Allah rewarded them with..." Then read the bedtime du'a. Three days later, anyone who wishes can share how many tasks they circled.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, one short service, such as setting the table, is enough. Ask advanced students to memorize al-Insan 76:8-11 and to explain "despite their love for it" in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not display students' charts for comparison. Be sensitive to a student who has no one at home who serves them, or who is living through hard circumstances, and allow them to choose a service at school instead. Do not mention the weak hadith about who the youths are; it is enough to say that many scholars said they are beings Allah created in Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the link between giving up on people's thanks and Allah's thanking of the striving; presents the two views on who the youths are, with courtesy; uses the hadith of Fatimah to show that serving does not lower a person's standing; and completes the seven-day silent service shift, with a log of the moments they wanted to be noticed.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a private notebook that is not collected; a mushaf; cards for anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews Ibn Kathir's tafsir of al-Insan 76:8-10 and what Ibn al-Qayyim says about who the youths are, and prepares two neutral examples: an act of service posted on social media, and an act of service no one sees.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "If every photo of charitable work were deleted from your accounts, what would be left?" Students write anonymous answers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the introduction and the terms, then the story, with three pauses: who was fed and why; then the intention and Mujahid's statement; then the reward and who the youths are.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Group discussion of questions 1, 2 and 3, followed by brief presentations. The teacher guides students to see that the intention lives in the heart, as Mujahid said, and that thanking those who serve us is a moral duty even if they never ask for it.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student chooses their service, writes their list of expectations, and decides how they will ask the person's permission, as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes three sentences: who serves them without being seen, what they understand from "We want from you neither reward nor thanks," and which task they will take over. A week later, the teacher follows up with a general question, without asking for the notebooks. Then read the bedtime du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find written logging difficult may keep private voice notes instead. Ask advanced students for a written reflection on the difference between likening the youths to scattered pearls and likening the young servants to hidden pearls.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask anyone to present their acts of service in front of the class. Do not shame those who post their charitable work, for judging intentions belongs to Allah. Point out that the weak hadith saying the children of the polytheists are the servants of the people of Jannah is not relied upon, and be gentle with anyone who has lost a young relative. And do not assign the service shift to a student who is already doing more at home than they can manage.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
