# The Fruits of Jannah and Its Fragrant Rayhan

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the words of Allah the Exalted, "Whenever they are given fruit from them as provision, they will say, 'This is what we were provided with before,' and they will be given it in resemblance" (al-Baqarah 2:25), and set out the commentators' views on the meaning of "in resemblance" (*mutashabihan*) as Ibn Kathir reports them and as-Sa'di weighs them, together with the statement of Ibn 'Abbas, which is his own and not a hadith: "Nothing in this world is like what is in Jannah except the names."
- Describe the fruits of Jannah as varied and within reach, from "In both of them are two kinds of every fruit" and "the fruit of the two gardens hangs low" (ar-Rahman 55:52-54), and as never running out, from the hadith of the cluster of grapes in the eclipse prayer (al-Bukhari 748).
- Explain the meaning and context of "then rest, and rayhan" (al-Waqi'ah 56:89), and the two views on *rayhan*: provision, and the well-known sweet-smelling plant; together with the courtesy of accepting rayhan in this world (Muslim 2253).
- Narrate how people used to bring the first fruit of the season to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and how he would pray for blessing and then give it to the youngest child present (Muslim 1373), and draw from it two courtesies: gratitude and putting others first.
- Carry out the "Predict, Taste, Give Thanks" activity: recording, over a set number of days, how a fruit is expected to taste from its looks and how it actually tastes, praising Allah after every tasting, and then offering the first of the fruit to the youngest person present and giving away a sprig of rayhan at the end.
- Close the day with a lesson-authored du'a that thanks Allah for the fruits of this world and asks Him for the fruits and rayhan of Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We buy fruit with our eyes before our tongues. We pick out the most beautiful one, bite into it, and sometimes it turns out sour. In this world, looks can promise what taste fails to deliver; the season comes to an end; and the high branch needs a ladder and some effort.

This lesson gathers the texts on the fruits of Jannah and its rayhan, and three truths emerge from them:

1. **Fruit with a familiar name and a reality beyond compare:** the people of Jannah are given a fruit and recognize it, and then find in it what they never knew.

2. **Varied, within reach, lasting and never exhausted:** two kinds of every fruit, the harvest hanging low, and a cluster that, had it been taken, would have fed people for as long as the world remains.

3. **The fruit of this world is a blessing to be thanked for and shared:** the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would pray for blessing over the first fruit of the season and then hand it to the youngest child present.

The trees of Jannah and their shade, and the food and drink of its people, each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-25" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "This Is What We Were Provided with Before"

> **{وَبَشِّرِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ أَنَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ كُلَّمَا رُزِقُوا مِنْهَا مِنْ ثَمَرَةٍ رِزْقًا ۙ قَالُوا هَٰذَا الَّذِي رُزِقْنَا مِنْ قَبْلُ ۖ وَأُتُوا بِهِ مُتَشَابِهًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ فِيهَا أَزْوَاجٌ مُطَهَّرَةٌ ۖ وَهُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ}** [البقرة: ٢٥][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And give glad tidings to those who believe and do righteous deeds that theirs are gardens beneath which rivers flow. Whenever they are given fruit from them as provision, they will say, 'This is what we were provided with before,' and they will be given it in resemblance. And they will have in them purified spouses, and they will abide in them forever."** (al-Baqarah 2:25)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On their words "This is what we were provided with before," Ibn Kathir reports two views. The first is that "they were brought the fruit in Jannah, and when they looked at it they said: This is what we were provided with before, in the world." This is narrated from Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and a number of the Companions, and Ibn Jarir argued for it. The second is that the meaning is: this is what we were given of the fruits of Jannah before this one, "because its parts resemble one another so closely." On "in resemblance" he says: "in color and appearance, but not in taste." 'Ikrimah said: "It resembles the fruit of this world, except that the fruit of Jannah is better." And it is narrated from Ibn 'Abbas, as his own statement: "Nothing in this world is like what is in Jannah except the names."[^2] As-Sa'di said: "All of it is alike in excellence and delight; no one fruit is set apart from the rest, and there is no moment for them that is empty of delight." He preferred the view that it "resembles itself, in excellence, in delight and in pleasure," and he said: "There is no way to reach these glad tidings except through these two," meaning faith and righteous deeds.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The views are close to one another: familiar in name and appearance, and immeasurably different in reality. And notice the word "whenever" (*kullama*): the provision is renewed and never stops, and every new fruit opens the door to a new joy.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-55-52-54" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And the Fruit of the Two Gardens Hangs Low"

> **{فِيهِمَا مِنْ كُلِّ فَاكِهَةٍ زَوْجَانِ ۝ فَبِأَيِّ آلَاءِ رَبِّكُمَا تُكَذِّبَانِ ۝ مُتَّكِئِينَ عَلَىٰ فُرُشٍ بَطَائِنُهَا مِنْ إِسْتَبْرَقٍ ۚ وَجَنَى الْجَنَّتَيْنِ دَانٍ}** [الرحمن: ٥٢-٥٤][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"In both of them are two kinds of every fruit. So which of the favors of your Lord will you both deny? Reclining on couches lined with rich brocade, and the fruit of the two gardens hangs low."** (ar-Rahman 55:52-54)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said that "of every fruit" means "of every kind of fruit: those they know, and better than those they know." He cited the statement of Ibn 'Abbas about the names and commented: "Meaning that there is a vast gulf between the two." On "and the fruit of the two gardens hangs low" he said: "Its fruit is close to them; whenever they wish, they take it, in whatever position they happen to be."[^5] As-Sa'di said that "two kinds" (*zawjan*) means "two varieties, each with a delight and a color that the other does not have," that "*al-jana* is ripe fruit," and that it is "within reach of the one standing, the one sitting and the one lying down."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

In this world, fruit is reached with a ladder and with waiting. In Jannah, it comes to its owner whatever state he is in.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-88-89" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then Rest, and Rayhan"

> **{فَأَمَّا إِنْ كَانَ مِنَ الْمُقَرَّبِينَ ۝ فَرَوْحٌ وَرَيْحَانٌ وَجَنَّتُ نَعِيمٍ}** [الواقعة: ٨٨-٨٩][^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Then if he is one of those brought near, then rest, and rayhan, and a garden of bliss."** (al-Waqi'ah 56:88-89)[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

These two ayat come in the context of the state of a person at the point of death. Ibn Kathir said: "For them is rest and rayhan, and the angels give them these glad tidings at death." He reported from Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid and Sa'id ibn Jubayr that *rayhan* means "provision," and then said: "All of these views are close to one another and correct." As-Sa'di said that *rayhan* "is a comprehensive name for every bodily pleasure, of all kinds of food, drink and other things; and it has been said that *rayhan* is the well-known fragrant plant."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

In the language, *rayhan* is a sweet-smelling plant; in the ayah, it is good provision, ease and bliss. The two meanings meet: everything that delights the soul, in scent and in taste.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-748" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Had I Taken It, You Would Have Eaten from It for as Long as the World Remains"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَبَّاسٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: **خَسَفَتِ الشَّمْسُ عَلَى عَهْدِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَصَلَّى، قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، رَأَيْنَاكَ تَنَاوَلُ شَيْئًا فِي مَقَامِكَ، ثُمَّ رَأَيْنَاكَ تَكَعْكَعْتَ، قَالَ: «إِنِّي أُرِيتُ الْجَنَّةَ، فَتَنَاوَلْتُ مِنْهَا عُنْقُودًا، وَلَوْ أَخَذْتُهُ لَأَكَلْتُمْ مِنْهُ مَا بَقِيَتِ الدُّنْيَا»**.[^9]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of 'Abdullah ibn 'Abbas, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, who said: **The sun was eclipsed in the time of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and he prayed. They said, "O Messenger of Allah, we saw you reaching for something where you stood, and then we saw you step back." He said: "I was shown Jannah, and I reached out for a cluster of grapes from it. Had I taken it, you would have eaten from it for as long as the world remains."**[^9]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Rajab reports from al-Khattabi that *taka'ku'* means stepping back. On the meaning of "I reached out" he said: "He stretched out his hand intending to take the cluster, but he did not take it." And on the words "you would have eaten from it for as long as the world remains" he said: "This indicates that what is in Jannah never runs out."[^10]

#### Lesson Explanation

A single cluster would have been enough for people for as long as the world lasts. And the unseen stays unseen: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not take it, and what remains for us is the true report, which we believe.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-1373" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then He Would Give It to the Youngest Child Present"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّهُ قَالَ: **كَانَ النَّاسُ إِذَا رَأَوْا أَوَّلَ الثَّمَرِ جَاءُوا بِهِ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم، فَإِذَا أَخَذَهُ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: «اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لَنَا فِي ثَمَرِنَا، وَبَارِكْ لَنَا فِي مَدِينَتِنَا، وَبَارِكْ لَنَا فِي صَاعِنَا، وَبَارِكْ لَنَا فِي مُدِّنَا، اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّ إِبْرَاهِيمَ عَبْدُكَ وَخَلِيلُكَ وَنَبِيُّكَ، وَإِنِّي عَبْدُكَ وَنَبِيُّكَ، وَإِنَّهُ دَعَاكَ لِمَكَّةَ، وَإِنِّي أَدْعُوكَ لِلْمَدِينَةِ بِمِثْلِ مَا دَعَاكَ لِمَكَّةَ وَمِثْلِهِ مَعَهُ»، قَالَ: ثُمَّ يَدْعُو أَصْغَرَ وَلِيدٍ لَهُ فَيُعْطِيهِ ذَلِكَ الثَّمَرَ**.[^11]
>
> وفي رواية أخرى عند مسلم: **أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم كَانَ يُؤْتَى بِأَوَّلِ الثَّمَرِ فَيَقُولُ: «اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لَنَا فِي مَدِينَتِنَا، وَفِي ثِمَارِنَا، وَفِي مُدِّنَا، وَفِي صَاعِنَا، بَرَكَةً مَعَ بَرَكَةٍ»، ثُمَّ يُعْطِيهِ أَصْغَرَ مَنْ يَحْضُرُهُ مِنَ الْوِلْدَانِ**.[^11]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: **Whenever people saw the first fruit of the season, they would bring it to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, took it, he would say: "O Allah, bless us in our fruit, bless us in our city, bless us in our sa', and bless us in our mudd. O Allah, Ibrahim was Your servant, Your intimate friend and Your prophet, and I am Your servant and Your prophet. He called on You for Makkah, and I call on You for Madinah with the like of what he called on You for Makkah, and as much again along with it." Then he would call the youngest little child he had near him and give him that fruit.**[^11]
>
> In another narration in Muslim: **The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to be brought the first fruit of the season, and he would say: "O Allah, bless us in our city, in our fruits, in our mudd and in our sa', blessing upon blessing." Then he would give it to the youngest of the children present.**[^11]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The first fruit of the season is called *al-bakurah*, "the first fruit to ripen." Al-Mubarakfuri reports al-Baji's comment on his giving it to the youngest: "the great reward in bringing happiness to one who, being so young, has no sin; for his delight in it is greater than an adult's delight." He also reports Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's comment: "Among the courtesies and fine character it contains is giving to the little one and treating him to something new."[^12] And ad-Dihlawi said: "In preferring someone else there is a reminder and a lesson for the Ummah to hold back greed and appetite, especially in what the soul craves most strongly and most often, such as the first fruit of the season."[^12]

#### Lesson Explanation

The first fruit of the season is what the soul craves most. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, began by praying for blessing, and then, instead of eating it, he gave it to the youngest child there.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2253" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whoever Is Offered Rayhan Should Not Refuse It"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«مَنْ عُرِضَ عَلَيْهِ رَيْحَانٌ فَلَا يَرُدُّهُ، فَإِنَّهُ خَفِيفُ الْمَحْمِلِ، طَيِّبُ الرِّيحِ»**.[^13]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Whoever is offered rayhan should not refuse it, for it is light to carry and sweet in scent."**[^13]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn 'Uthaymin said: "Rayhan is a kind of fragrance, and it is just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described it: light to carry and sweet in scent."[^14]

#### Lesson Explanation

It is a gift that weighs nothing on the giver or the receiver, and accepting it is a courtesy that keeps affection alive.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of al-Baqarah describes the nature of the fruit of Jannah; the ayat of ar-Rahman, its variety and nearness; the hadith of the cluster, its permanence; and the ayah of al-Waqi'ah adds rayhan. The two hadiths in Muslim, for their part, teach us the etiquette of this world's fruit and rayhan: we give thanks and ask for blessing, we put the youngest first, and we accept a fragrant gift.

### Activity: Predict, Taste, Give Thanks

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.056.activity.predict-taste-thank-then-offer-rayhan" -->

1. For five days, whenever fruit is served in your home, look at it before you eat and predict its taste on a scale of 1 to 5; then taste it and record three things in a table: the fruit, your prediction, and the reality. On the first day, choose two or three fruits of one kind that look alike, such as two apples of different varieties.

2. After each tasting, praise Allah aloud, and share with whoever is at the table one idea from this lesson about the fruit of Jannah.

3. On the fifth day, offer the first slice of fruit to the youngest person in the house, give a relative or neighbor a sprig of rayhan (sweet basil) or mint, and accept any that is offered to you. Then write three lines: How many times did the taste contradict the looks? What does "in resemblance" mean to you after the experiment? And what did you learn from putting the youngest first with the first fruit?

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We love fruit: grapes and apples, pomegranates and dates. In Jannah there are fruits more beautiful and more delicious than anything we have ever eaten. When the people of Jannah eat them, they say, "This is what we were given before!"[^1] That means, "This looks just like what we ate before!" But it tastes so much better.[^2] Every fruit there is delicious: none is sour or wilted.[^3] And it is close by: you can reach it sitting, standing, or lying down.[^6] Jannah also has rayhan, a plant that smells lovely.[^8]

### A Made-Up Teaching Story: Hamzah and the Two Bunches of Grapes

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.056.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a made-up teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

Hamzah went to visit his grandma with his dad and his little sister. On Grandma's balcony there was a pot with a green plant in it, and on the table there was a plate with two bunches of green grapes.

Hamzah looked at the two bunches. They looked exactly the same! He took a grape from the first bunch. It was so sweet! Then he took a grape from the second bunch... and his face scrunched up. "Ewww! Sour!" He picked up the second bunch to throw it away.

"Wait, Hamzah," said Grandma. "This bunch isn't ripe yet. We'll leave it in the sun for two days, and it will turn sweet." So Hamzah put the bunch back on the plate.

Then Grandma picked a little sprig from the green plant. "Here," she said. "This is rayhan, sweet basil." "I don't want leaves," said Hamzah. "I want candy!"

His dad said gently, "Hamzah, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that if someone offers you rayhan, you shouldn't say no, because it's light and it smells good.[^13] Smell it first."

Hamzah took the sprig and sniffed it... and he smiled. "It smells so nice! Thank you, Grandma." Then he looked at his little sister and gave her the sweetest grape from the sweet bunch.

His dad said, "Here in this world, two bunches can look the same, and one is sweet while the other is sour. But in Jannah, every fruit is delicious, and close by, and you never have to work hard to get it."

"How do we get there?" asked Hamzah. His dad said, "By believing in Allah and doing good things here."

### A True Report: The First Fruit for the Littlest Child

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-1373" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-1373" audience="4-7" -->

**This really happened. Imam Muslim reported it. It is not a made-up story.**

In the city of Madinah, people grew date palms and other trees. And when they saw the very first fruit of the season getting ripe, they would bring it to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^11]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, would take the fruit and pray to Allah: "Allahumma barik lana fi thamarina," which means, "O Allah, put lots of goodness in our fruit." He would also pray for their city, and for the measuring cups they used to measure out their food.[^11]

And then what did he do with the fruit? He didn't eat it! He would call the littlest child there and give that fruit to him.[^11]

A little child is happier with a brand-new fruit than a grown-up is,[^12] so the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, filled little children's hearts with joy.

We can do it too: we say "Alhamdulillah" for our fruit, and we give the youngest one the first piece.

### Activity: Predict, Taste, Give Thanks

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.056.activity.predict-taste-thank-then-offer-rayhan" -->

1. For three days, at fruit time, your mom or dad puts two pieces of fruit that look alike in front of you. Point to the one you think is sweeter, then taste both, and draw a smiley face or a frowny face for each one.

2. After you eat, say "Alhamdulillah," and tell someone in your family: "In Jannah, all the fruits are yummy."

3. On the third day, give the first piece of fruit to the youngest person in your home, and give your grandma or a neighbor a sprig of rayhan and say, "It smells lovely." And if someone gives you rayhan, take it and say "Thank you," just like Hamzah did.

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.056.dua.thankful-for-fruit-rayhan-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ كَمَا رَزَقْتَنَا مِنْ ثَمَرَاتِ الدُّنْيَا فَاجْعَلْنَا لَكَ مِنَ الشَّاكِرِينَ، وَبَارِكْ لَنَا فِيمَا رَزَقْتَنَا، وَارْزُقْنَا فِي الْجَنَّةِ رَوْحًا وَرَيْحَانًا، وَثِمَارًا دَانِيَةً لَا تَنْفَدُ.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Revelation describes Jannah's fruits in amazing ways. First, they are familiar and surprising at once: whenever the people of Jannah are given a fruit, they say, "This is what we were provided with before," and "they will be given it in resemblance."[^1] Some commentators said it shares the look and name of this world's fruit but tastes far better, and Ibn 'Abbas said: "Nothing in this world is like what is in Jannah except the names."[^2] Second, they are varied and within reach: "In both of them are two kinds of every fruit," and "the fruit of the two gardens hangs low."[^4] Third, they never run out. Shown Jannah during the eclipse prayer, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, reached toward a cluster of grapes, did not take it, and said: "Had I taken it, you would have eaten from it for as long as the world remains."[^9] Allah also promises those brought near "rest, and rayhan."[^7] And the ayah of al-Baqarah opens with glad tidings for "those who believe and do righteous deeds": that is the road there.[^3]

### A Made-Up Teaching Story: Salim's Basil

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.056.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a made-up teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

The science teacher announced the month's project: "Each of you will keep a sprig of basil in a cup of water until it grows roots, then plant it in a pot and keep a journal. At month's end, we'll show them at the school fair."

Eleven-year-old Salim was excited at first. Then soccer took over, and he forgot to change the water for a whole week. Three days before the fair, he looked at the cup: yellow, drooping leaves in cloudy water.

Walking home, he passed a nursery selling thick green basil cheap. His friend Faris whispered, "Buy it and say it's yours. It looks exactly the same. No one will know."

Salim stood there a long time. "If I buy it," he thought, "I'll get a perfect grade... but my journal would be full of things that never happened."

He remembered the ayah of al-Baqarah he had read with his dad: Jannah's fruits are "in resemblance," looking like what we know, yet real provision for "those who believe and do righteous deeds." He told himself, "This pot looks like my basil, but it isn't my work."

He didn't buy it. At home he snipped off the wilted leaves, changed the water, and found a thin root at the bottom of the stem, still alive. In his journal he wrote honestly: "I forgot the water for seven days, and the leaves wilted. This is what I learned."

At the fair, his basil was the smallest plant. Some students laughed. But the teacher read his journal and told the class, "This is the most honest journal I've read. I'll give you two more weeks to finish."

Two weeks later, the basil had six new leaves. Salim picked a sprig for his teacher. She smelled it and accepted it with a smile.

### A True Report: The First Fruit of the Season in Madinah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-1373" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-1373" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true report narrated by Muslim from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not a made-up story.**

Madinah was a land of palms and crops. When people saw the season's first fruit, *al-bakurah*, they brought it to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^12]

Taking it, he would pray: "O Allah, bless us in our fruit, bless us in our city, bless us in our sa', and bless us in our mudd." The *sa'* and *mudd* were measures for dates and grain.[^11]

Then he would call the youngest child present and give him that fruit. Another narration has: "Then he would give it to the youngest of the children present."[^11]

Why the youngest? Scholars explained that a little one is happier with a new fruit than a grown-up, and that treating a young child to something new is a beautiful courtesy.[^12]

Notice the three steps: thanking Allah and asking for blessing, giving up something you really want, and bringing joy to a young child.

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-2-25" -->

- **`The fruit of the two gardens hangs low (jana al-jannatayni dan)`** — their ripe fruit is close, within reach standing, sitting or lying down.[^6]
- **`The first fruit (al-bakurah)`** — the first fruit to ripen in its season.[^12]
- **`Rayhan`** — a sweet-smelling plant; in al-Waqi'ah also explained as good provision.[^8]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

On earth, looks can fool you: a glossy fruit ad, or a box with a perfect top layer and mushy berries hidden below. The Qur'an describes the fruit of Jannah as "in resemblance":[^1] each fruit resembles the others "in excellence and in delight," as as-Sa'di preferred,[^3] and it resembles this world's fruit in name and looks while being far better. That is why Ibn 'Abbas said, "Nothing in this world is like what is in Jannah except the names," meaning, as Ibn Kathir explained, that "there is a vast gulf between the two."[^5] There, the outside never lies about the inside, and the fruit "hangs low," reaching its owner however he is resting.[^4] The fruit of this world, though, is a small test: Will we be grateful? Will we put others first? And does our outside match our inside?

<!-- unit:end -->

### A Made-Up Teaching Story: Reem and the Strawberry Boxes

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.056.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is a made-up teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

Over summer break, sixteen-year-old Reem worked at her uncle's fruit stand in the market. She was saving for a laptop, and the pay was good.

On day one, her coworker Mazen showed her how to pack: "Big shiny berries on top, soft and squashed ones underneath. Everybody does it. The customer only sees the top."

That evening Reem packed twenty boxes his way. The next day an older woman bought two boxes for her grandchildren and smiled at Reem: "Your boxes are beautiful, dear." Something heavy settled in Reem's chest.

That night, in her daily portion of Qur'an, she read: "Whenever they are given fruit from them as provision, they will say, 'This is what we were provided with before.'" She remembered her teacher's words: the fruits of Jannah resemble one another in excellence; they hide nothing. Then she asked herself: "If I were the buyer, would I be okay with this box?"

In the morning she spoke to her uncle respectfully: "Uncle, what if we put the soft berries in boxes of their own, labeled 'For juice and jam,' at half price?"

"We'll lose money, Reem," her uncle said. And Mazen sneered, "What, are you the market's sheikh now?"

Reem was quiet for a moment, then said, "Just try it for one day. If we lose money, I'll cover the difference out of my pay."

That day the full boxes sold a little more slowly, but the "juice and jam" boxes sold out before noon. Two days later, the older woman came back and said, "Every berry was as good as the ones on top. I'm going to buy from you from now on."

Her uncle didn't change everything, but he kept the juice boxes. By the end of the summer, Reem hadn't saved enough for the whole laptop, but she wrote in her notebook: "A box that's the same inside as it is on top... that's who I want to be."

### A True Report: The First Fruit He Did Not Eat

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="prophetic_era" source_id="muslim-1373" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-1373" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report in Sahih Muslim from Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not an imagined scene.**

In Madinah, a city of palms, the season's first fruit was an event, and people would carry it to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Taking it, he would pray: "O Allah, bless us in our fruit, bless us in our city, bless us in our sa', and bless us in our mudd," and ask Allah to grant Madinah the like of what Ibrahim, peace be upon him, had prayed for Makkah, and as much again.[^11]

Then comes the moment worth pausing on: he did not eat it, but called the youngest child present and gave it to him. One narration says: "Then he would give it to the youngest of the children present."[^11]

The commentators explained that the joy of a little one "is greater than an adult's delight," as al-Mubarakfuri reports from al-Baji. And ad-Dihlawi said: "In preferring someone else there is a reminder and a lesson for the Ummah to hold back greed and appetite, especially in what the soul craves most strongly and most often, such as the first fruit of the season."[^12]

Look at the order: a blessing arrives; gratitude and a prayer for blessing follow; then what the soul craves goes to someone weaker, who will be happier to have it. For the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, this world's fruit was never the goal, but a chance for gratitude and generosity.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-2-25" -->

- **`In resemblance (mutashabihan)`** — each fruit resembles the others in excellence and delight, and resembles the fruit of this world in name and appearance, while the reality is vastly different.[^3]
- **`Greed (ash-sharah)`** — an intense craving for food and for what one desires; ad-Dihlawi saw giving away the first fruit as a lesson for the Ummah in holding it back.[^12]
- **`Rest and rayhan (rawhun wa rayhan)`** — ease and joy, and good provision or a sweet-smelling plant, according to two views that are close to each other.[^8]

### Bedtime Du'a

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites the ayah of al-Baqarah (2:25) and sets out the views on "This is what we were provided with before" and "in resemblance" as Ibn Kathir reports them and as-Sa'di weighs them; describes the variety and nearness of the fruits of Jannah from the ayat of ar-Rahman (55:52-54) and their permanence from the hadith in al-Bukhari (748); explains the two views on *rayhan* and the context of the ayah of al-Waqi'ah; narrates the hadith of the first fruit (Muslim 1373) and draws from it the two courtesies of gratitude and putting others first; and begins the "Predict, Taste, Give Thanks" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; three fruits of one kind that look alike, sliced onto small plates; fresh sprigs of rayhan (sweet basil); the activity table printed out (fruit, prediction, reality); a board with three columns: "Familiar and astonishing," "Varied, within reach, never exhausted," and "This world's fruit: a blessing to be thanked for and shared."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsirs of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Baqarah (2:25), ar-Rahman (55:52-54) and al-Waqi'ah (56:89); Ibn Rajab's commentary on al-Bukhari 748; and what al-Mubarakfuri and ad-Dihlawi wrote on Muslim 1373; and checks that no learner is allergic to the foods being used.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Display the fruits and ask, "Which one is the sweetest?" Each learner writes down a prediction, then everyone tastes and compares. The teacher says: "In this world, looks can promise what taste fails to deliver. So how does revelation describe the fruit of Jannah?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of al-Baqarah and the commentators' views, distinguishing between the ayah, its tafsir, and the statement of Ibn 'Abbas, which is his own and not a hadith. Then read the ayat of ar-Rahman; then the two ayat of al-Waqi'ah, pointing out that their context is the moment of death; then the hadith of the cluster with Ibn Rajab's comments; then the hadith of the first fruit in both its narrations with the commentators' remarks; then the hadith of rayhan. Learners fill in the three columns.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion: How do the views on "in resemblance" come together without contradicting one another? What does it mean that the unseen stays unseen in the hadith of the cluster? And how does the hadith of the first fruit combine gratitude, putting others first, and holding back greed?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** The teacher explains the stages of the activity as set out in the activity unit. Learners fill in the first row of the table from the opening fruits, exchange sprigs of rayhan, practicing both giving and accepting, and each decides whom they will give rayhan to on the fifth day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes two meanings of "in resemblance," two views on *rayhan*, and two reasons for giving the first fruit to the youngest. Agree that anyone who wishes may share their three lines after five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the ayah of al-Baqarah and the hadith of the first fruit are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare the narration in al-Bukhari 748 with the longer narration in al-Bukhari 1052, and to read what Ibn al-Qayyim wrote in *Hadi al-Arwah* in the chapter on the fruits of Jannah, distinguishing what is established from what is not.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the shape or size of the fruit of Jannah with details that have not been established. Under the ayah of al-Baqarah, Ibn Kathir cites reports from Yahya ibn Abi Kathir that "the color is one and the taste differs," and under the ayah of al-Waqi'ah the statement of Abu al-'Aliyah about a sprig of rayhan at death, and a long hadith from Tamim ad-Dari mentioning bundles of rayhan; the lesson relies on none of these.[^15] The hadith of the eclipse prayer is cited here only as supporting evidence; its full account has a lesson of its own. The du'a over the first fruit is a Prophetic du'a, narrated in its exact wording, and must not be confused with the lesson's own composed du'a. Be mindful of food allergies, and no one is pressured to taste anything.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Reem's story, and have groups discuss: "What was Mazen's argument? How did Reem talk to her uncle without accusing him? And why did she offer to cover the difference?" Then ask: "What did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, give up when he gave the first fruit to the little one? And what corresponds to that in our lives?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students taste the fruits and record the difference; then each starts a private notebook as described in the activity unit and writes a first line about an outside that didn't match the reality, without naming anyone.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes on an unnamed card: "The fruit of Jannah never has an outside that lies about its inside, and I want to...", and then the du'a is read. After five days, anyone who wishes may share their paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to compare the views on "in resemblance" and show how they can be reconciled.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not accuse shopkeepers or students' relatives of cheating, and do not ask anyone to talk about their family's business. The story teaches giving sincere advice with good manners, not confrontation. Students' private notebooks are not read. Do not go into details about the unseen beyond what has been established.
