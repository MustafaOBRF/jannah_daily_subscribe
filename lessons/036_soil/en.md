# The Ground of Jannah: Its Soil and Its Pebbles

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Recite the words of Allah the Exalted, `And He has made us inherit the land, so that we may settle in Jannah wherever we wish` (az-Zumar 39:74), and explain that "the land" here means the land of Jannah, as as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir state.
- Narrate the account of al-Isra' and al-Mi'raj from Sahih al-Bukhari (349) through its major events: the opening of the Prophet's chest, the ascent through the heavens, the prayer made obligatory as fifty and then lightened to five, the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, and finally his entry into Jannah, where he saw pearls and found that "its soil was musk."
- State the established descriptions of Jannah's soil: musk, and "fine white flour, pure musk" (Muslim 2928); explain how Ibn al-Qayyim reconciles these with the description of it as saffron; and know the status of the hadith describing its pebbles as pearls and rubies (at-Tirmidhi 2526) and why it is not used as primary evidence.
- Connect the ground we walk on and prostrate on today with the ground of Jannah, through the hadith of the man whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw roaming freely in Jannah because of a tree he had cut down from the middle of a road (Muslim 1914).
- Carry out the activity "From Roadside Dust to Soil of Musk": observing a sample of this world's soil with the senses and comparing it with the established descriptions, then removing a real hazard from a road or shared space and recording it.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah to make our steps on this earth steps of obedience to Him, and to let us inherit the land of Jannah whose soil is musk.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The earth of this world gives us a great deal: we walk on it, we eat from it, and we prostrate on it. But it is also an earth of dust, mud and thorns. People quarrel over every inch of it; they inherit it from one another, and then they leave it behind. Revelation has described another land, one the believers inherit and never leave, and its soil is musk.

This lesson gathers the established texts on the ground of Jannah and its soil, and draws three truths from them:

1. **The land of Jannah is the believers' inheritance:** the people of Jannah praise their Lord for having made them inherit its land, so that they settle in it wherever they wish.
2. **Its soil is fragrant, white and pure:** the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw it on the night of the Mi'raj, and its soil was musk; and he described it as fine white flour, pure musk.
3. **The road to it runs through the earth of this world:** a small deed done on a roadside can become a cause of bliss in Jannah.

The buildings of Jannah, their bricks and their walls, are the subject of the next lesson; its light and its colors belong to the lesson after that.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-74" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And He Has Made Us Inherit the Land"

> **{وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي صَدَقَنَا وَعْدَهُ وَأَوْرَثَنَا الْأَرْضَ نَتَبَوَّأُ مِنَ الْجَنَّةِ حَيْثُ نَشَاءُ ۖ فَنِعْمَ أَجْرُ الْعَامِلِينَ}** [الزمر: ٧٤][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And they will say, 'Praise be to Allah, who has fulfilled His promise to us and made us inherit the land, so that we may settle in Jannah wherever we wish.' How excellent is the reward of those who work!"** (az-Zumar 39:74)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di explains `and made us inherit the land` as "that is, the land of Jannah," and `so that we may settle in Jannah wherever we wish` as "we may stay in any place in it we choose, and take from it any delight we want."[^2] Ibn Kathir reports the view that it is the land of Jannah from Abu al-'Aliyah, Abu Salih, Qatadah, as-Suddi and Ibn Zayd, and then cites under this ayah the two hadiths of al-Bukhari and Muslim on the soil of Jannah that follow below.[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The people of Jannah say these words as they enter, praising their Lord. An inheritance in this world passes from hand to hand; this inheritance will never pass out of theirs. And the ayah's closing words, `How excellent is the reward of those who work`, tie this land to deeds.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-349" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Its Soil Was Musk"

> عَنْ أَنَسِ بْنِ مَالِكٍ قَالَ: كَانَ أَبُو ذَرٍّ يُحَدِّثُ أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«فُرِجَ عَنْ سَقْفِ بَيْتِي وَأَنَا بِمَكَّةَ، فَنَزَلَ جِبْرِيلُ فَفَرَجَ صَدْرِي، ثُمَّ غَسَلَهُ بِمَاءِ زَمْزَمَ، ثُمَّ جَاءَ بِطَسْتٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ مُمْتَلِئٍ حِكْمَةً وَإِيمَانًا، فَأَفْرَغَهُ فِي صَدْرِي ثُمَّ أَطْبَقَهُ، ثُمَّ أَخَذَ بِيَدِي فَعَرَجَ بِي إِلَى السَّمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا، فَلَمَّا جِئْتُ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا قَالَ جِبْرِيلُ لِخَازِنِ السَّمَاءِ: افْتَحْ. قَالَ: مَنْ هَذَا؟ قَالَ: هَذَا جِبْرِيلُ. قَالَ: هَلْ مَعَكَ أَحَدٌ؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ، مَعِي مُحَمَّدٌ صلى الله عليه وسلم. فَقَالَ: أُرْسِلَ إِلَيْهِ؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ»**. [ثم ذكر عروجه في السماوات ولقاءه آدم وإدريس وموسى وعيسى وإبراهيم عليهم السلام وترحيبهم به، ثم قال:] **«فَفَرَضَ اللَّهُ عَلَى أُمَّتِي خَمْسِينَ صَلَاةً، فَرَجَعْتُ بِذَلِكَ حَتَّى مَرَرْتُ عَلَى مُوسَى، فَقَالَ: مَا فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَكَ عَلَى أُمَّتِكَ؟ قُلْتُ: فَرَضَ خَمْسِينَ صَلَاةً. قَالَ: فَارْجِعْ إِلَى رَبِّكَ، فَإِنَّ أُمَّتَكَ لَا تُطِيقُ ذَلِكَ. فَرَاجَعْتُ فَوَضَعَ شَطْرَهَا، فَرَجَعْتُ إِلَى مُوسَى قُلْتُ: وَضَعَ شَطْرَهَا. فَقَالَ: رَاجِعْ رَبَّكَ، فَإِنَّ أُمَّتَكَ لَا تُطِيقُ، فَرَاجَعْتُ فَوَضَعَ شَطْرَهَا، فَرَجَعْتُ إِلَيْهِ فَقَالَ: ارْجِعْ إِلَى رَبِّكَ، فَإِنَّ أُمَّتَكَ لَا تُطِيقُ ذَلِكَ، فَرَاجَعْتُهُ فَقَالَ: هِيَ خَمْسٌ وَهِيَ خَمْسُونَ، لَا يُبَدَّلُ الْقَوْلُ لَدَيَّ. فَرَجَعْتُ إِلَى مُوسَى فَقَالَ: رَاجِعْ رَبَّكَ. فَقُلْتُ: اسْتَحْيَيْتُ مِنْ رَبِّي. ثُمَّ انْطَلَقَ بِي حَتَّى انْتَهَى بِي إِلَى سِدْرَةِ الْمُنْتَهَى، وَغَشِيَهَا أَلْوَانٌ لَا أَدْرِي مَا هِيَ، ثُمَّ أُدْخِلْتُ الْجَنَّةَ، فَإِذَا فِيهَا حَبَايِلُ اللُّؤْلُؤِ، وَإِذَا تُرَابُهَا الْمِسْكُ»**.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Anas ibn Malik said: Abu Dharr used to relate that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"The roof of my house was opened while I was in Makkah, and Jibril came down. He opened my chest and washed it with the water of Zamzam. Then he brought a basin of gold filled with wisdom and faith, emptied it into my chest, and closed it up again. Then he took me by the hand and ascended with me to the nearest heaven. When I reached the nearest heaven, Jibril said to the keeper of that heaven, 'Open.' He asked, 'Who is this?' He said, 'This is Jibril.' He asked, 'Is anyone with you?' He said, 'Yes, Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, is with me.' He asked, 'Has he been sent for?' He said, 'Yes.'"** [He then described his ascent through the heavens, his meeting with Adam, Idris, Musa, Isa and Ibrahim, peace be upon them, and their welcome of him, and then said:] **"Then Allah made fifty prayers obligatory upon my ummah. I came back with that until I passed by Musa, who asked, 'What has Allah made obligatory for you upon your ummah?' I said, 'He has made fifty prayers obligatory.' He said, 'Go back to your Lord, for your ummah will not be able to bear that.' So I went back to Him, and He removed a portion of them. I returned to Musa and said, 'He removed a portion of them.' He said, 'Go back to your Lord, for your ummah will not be able to bear it.' So I went back, and He removed a portion of them. I returned to him, and he said, 'Go back to your Lord, for your ummah will not be able to bear that.' So I went back to Him, and He said, 'They are five, and they are fifty. The word is not changed with Me.' I returned to Musa, and he said, 'Go back to your Lord.' I said, 'I feel too shy before my Lord.' Then he took me onward until he brought me to the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, which was veiled in colors I do not know. Then I was admitted into Jannah, and in it were strings of pearls, and its soil was musk."**[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Another narration in al-Bukhari and in Muslim reads: "and in it were *janabidh* of pearl," and *janabidh* means domes.[^4][^9] The words "They are five, and they are fifty" are explained by the hadith in Muslim: "They are five prayers every day and night, and each prayer counts as ten; so that makes fifty prayers."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

This is an eyewitness report, not a description from a distance: "I was admitted into Jannah... and its soil was musk." Most striking of all, the night on which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw the soil of Jannah was the very night on which the prayer was made obligatory. Every prostration in which we lay our foreheads on the dust of this world is a gift of that night.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2928b" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Fine White Flour, Pure Musk"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ أَنَّ ابْنَ صَيَّادٍ سَأَلَ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم عَنْ تُرْبَةِ الْجَنَّةِ، فَقَالَ: **«دَرْمَكَةٌ بَيْضَاءُ مِسْكٌ خَالِصٌ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri: Ibn Sayyad asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about the soil of Jannah, and he said: **"Fine white flour (*darmakah*), pure musk."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Darmak* is fine, pure white flour.[^9] In another narration in Muslim, it was the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who asked Ibn Sayyad about the soil of Jannah; Ibn Sayyad answered with this description, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said, "You have spoken the truth."[^5] Ibn al-Qayyim brought together the descriptions of the soil that have been reported (musk, *darmak* and saffron) and said, "There is no contradiction between them." Among the ways he reconciles them is that it may be "saffron with respect to its color, musk with respect to its scent"; and likewise its being likened to *darmak*, which he takes to mean "pure bread whose color tends toward yellow, together with its softness and fineness."[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

This one description gathers color (white), texture (soft as flour) and scent (pure musk). The soil of Jannah is not something you shake off your clothes; it is itself part of the bliss.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2526" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Its Pebbles Are Pearls and Rubies"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ قَالَ: [...] قُلْنَا: الْجَنَّةُ مَا بِنَاؤُهَا؟ قَالَ: **«لَبِنَةٌ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ وَلَبِنَةٌ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ، وَمِلَاطُهَا الْمِسْكُ الْأَذْفَرُ، وَحَصْبَاؤُهَا اللُّؤْلُؤُ وَالْيَاقُوتُ، وَتُرْبَتُهَا الزَّعْفَرَانُ، مَنْ يَدْخُلْهَا يَنْعَمْ وَلَا يَبْأَسْ، وَيُخَلَّدْ وَلَا يَمُوتْ، لَا تَبْلَى ثِيَابُهُمْ، وَلَا يَفْنَى شَبَابُهُمْ»**.[^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, who said: [...] We said, "And Jannah: what is it built of?" He said: **"A brick of silver and a brick of gold. Its mortar is musk of the richest fragrance, its pebbles are pearls and rubies, and its soil is saffron. Whoever enters it lives in bliss and never knows misery, lives forever and never dies. Their clothes never wear out, and their youth never fades."**[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-hasba'* means small pebbles. The chain of this hadith is disputed. At-Tirmidhi said, "Its chain is not that strong, and in my view it is not connected." Ahmad Shakir and al-Albani graded it authentic on the strength of its combined routes, while the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah* showed that the route in Ahmad's collection turns on Abu al-Mudillah, whom Ibn al-Madini declared unknown.[^6][^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

That is why it is cited here as a supporting witness, not as primary evidence; what the lesson relies on for the soil are the two hadiths of al-Bukhari and Muslim. The bricks of Jannah's buildings are studied in the next lesson.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-1914c" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### A Tree in the Middle of the Road

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«لَقَدْ رَأَيْتُ رَجُلًا يَتَقَلَّبُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ فِي شَجَرَةٍ قَطَعَهَا مِنْ ظَهْرِ الطَّرِيقِ، كَانَتْ تُؤْذِي النَّاسَ»**.[^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: **"I saw a man roaming freely in Jannah because of a tree he had cut down from the middle of the road, which used to harm people."**[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

"Roaming freely in Jannah" (*yataqallab fi al-jannah*) means enjoying its bliss, and this was because of a tree he removed from the middle of the road that had been hurting passersby. In another narration in Muslim, the man said, "By Allah, I will move this out of the Muslims' way so that it does not harm them."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The earth of this world is not merely a corridor to the earth of Jannah; it is the field where we work for it. Whoever clears harm from people's path has done a deed on this earth through which the bliss of that other earth is hoped for.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The ayah of az-Zumar establishes that the land of Jannah is an inheritance for those who work; the hadith of the Mi'raj confirms that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw its soil as musk; the hadith in Muslim describes its soil as white, soft and fragrant; the hadith in at-Tirmidhi adds its pebbles, though its chain is disputed; and the hadith of the tree brings us back to the ground we walk on today.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is meant by `the land` in the words `and made us inherit the land`? Which of the exegetes held this view?
2. What are the three descriptions of Jannah's soil in the hadith in Muslim? How did Ibn al-Qayyim reconcile musk with saffron?
3. Why is the hadith in at-Tirmidhi about the pebbles not used as primary evidence in this lesson?
4. What is the significance of the prayer being made obligatory on the very night the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw the soil of Jannah?
5. What hazard lies on a road you travel every day? Who is harmed by it?

### Activity: From Roadside Dust to Soil of Musk

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.036.activity.clear-the-path-sense-the-soil" -->

Put a handful of soil from a garden or roadside into a small container, and write down three observations: its color, its texture and its smell. Beside each observation, write the established description of Jannah's soil: "white," "fine flour" (*darmakah*), "pure musk." Then, within three days, choose a road or shared space (a building entrance, a sidewalk, a mosque courtyard, a park path) and safely remove one specific hazard from it: glass, branches, stones or trash. Write two lines: What did I remove? And who would it have hurt?

We walk on the ground every day. It has dirt and mud, and sometimes stones and thorns. ### An Imaginative Teaching Story: Yahya and the Path After the Rain

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.036.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imaginative teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

It rained in the morning, then the sun came out. Yahya went to the nearby park with his dad. Yahya was five, and his new shoes were shiny blue.

Two steps, and squelch! His shoe sank into the mud. "Yuck!" he said. "The mud is dirty, and it smells funny!"

Then he saw something sparkling in the gravel by the slide: broken glass! And little kids were running there barefoot.

Yahya wondered, "Should I just leave it and go play?" Then he shouted, "Dad! Dad! There's glass over here!"

Dad hurried over. "Good job, Yahya. Don't touch it with your hand; glass cuts." He put on a glove and gathered the pieces into a bag while Yahya pointed to every shiny bit: "And here! And here!" Then they threw the bag in the trash.

The little kids ran and laughed by the slide, and nobody got hurt.

On the bench, Dad said, "You know what? The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us he saw a man enjoying Jannah because he moved a tree that was hurting people out of the road."[^8]

"Like the glass?" asked Yahya. "Yes, a lot like it," said Dad. "And the Prophet also told us the soil of Jannah is musk."

Yahya sniffed his muddy shoe and giggled. "This isn't musk!" Dad laughed. "We walk on the dirt of this world and do good on it, and we ask Allah for a land whose soil is musk."

At home, Yahya washed his shoes by himself. Then Dad dabbed a little musk on his hand, and Yahya sniffed it for a long time. "I'll remember Jannah every time I smell musk!"

### A True Hadith: The Night the Prophet, Peace and Blessings Be upon Him, Saw the Soil of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-349" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-349" audience="4-7" -->

**This really happened. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told it himself, and Abu Dharr, may Allah be pleased with him, passed it on in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

One night, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was at home in Makkah. The angel Jibril, peace be upon him, came to him, washed his heart with the water of Zamzam, and filled it with faith and wisdom. Then he took him by the hand and rose up with him to the sky.

At each gate of the sky, the guard asked, "Who is this?" And Jibril said, "I am Jibril, and Muhammad is with me." And the gate opened.

That night, Allah gave the ummah of Muhammad a gift: the prayer. It started as fifty prayers, and the Prophet kept asking his Lord to make it lighter until it became five, with the reward of fifty.

Then the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, went into Jannah. He saw pearls there, and he saw that its soil was musk.[^4]

Think about it: the prayers we pray every day were given to us on the night the Prophet saw the soil of Jannah. When we go down in sujud, we put our foreheads on the ground and ask Allah for Jannah.

### Activity: From Roadside Dust to Soil of Musk

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.036.activity.clear-the-path-sense-the-soil" -->

With your parent, put a little soil from the yard on a plate. Touch it with your finger and smell it. Is it soft? Does it smell nice? ### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="4-7" id="lesson.036.dua.steps-on-earth-inherit-musk-land" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ خُطَانَا عَلَى الْأَرْضِ فِي طَاعَتِكَ، وَأَوْرِثْنَا أَرْضَ الْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي تُرَابُهَا الْمِسْكُ، بِرَحْمَتِكَ يَا أَرْحَمَ الرَّاحِمِينَ.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Earth's soil changes from place to place: red, black, sandy, muddy, and sometimes full of trash. But the Qur'an tells us the believers will inherit the land of Jannah and will say: `Praise be to Allah, who has fulfilled His promise to us and made us inherit the land, so that we may settle in Jannah wherever we wish`.[^1] The scholar as-Sa'di explained: "that is, the land of Jannah."[^2] And when Ibn Sayyad asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about the soil of Jannah, he answered: "Fine white flour, pure musk."[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imaginative Teaching Story: Salma and the Forgotten Corner

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.036.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imaginative teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

The school announced a "Best Garden Bed" contest; the winner's photo would run in the school magazine. Salma, a fifth grader, was in the gardening club with three classmates.

"Let's plant the front bed by the gate," said Lina. "The soil's ready, and everyone will see our picture!"

But Salma had noticed something else: the back corner of the schoolyard, by the bus lane. Kids tossed wrappers and cans there, sharp stones jutted up, and last week a little boy had tripped and cut his knee.

"What if we cleaned the back corner and planted there?" Salma asked. Lina laughed. "Nobody will ever see it! We won't win."

At home, reading the hadith of al-Isra' and al-Mi'raj in Sahih al-Bukhari with her mom, Salma paused at its last lines: "Then I was admitted into Jannah, and in it were strings of pearls, and its soil was musk."[^4] Then her mom read her another hadith: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw a man roaming freely in Jannah because of a tree he had cut out of the road that used to harm people.[^8]

"So cleaning a path could be a deed for Jannah?" Salma asked. "That's what the hadith points to," said her mom, "and the intention for Allah is the foundation."

The next day, Salma won over two friends; Lina stuck with the front bed. The girls pulled on gloves, filled trash bags, and cleared the sharp stones with their teacher supervising. The soil was gray, hard, and stinking of old cans. "Imagine if this were musk!" one said, and they all burst out laughing.

They mixed in compost and planted mint and basil.

On results day, the front bed won. Salma felt a little sad. But Uncle Saeed, the custodian, came over and said, "For two years I've cleaned that corner all by myself. Thank you." And the little boy came running down the bus lane without tripping.

In her notebook, Salma wrote: "The corner nobody sees, Allah sees."

### A True Hadith: From Makkah to Jannah in One Night

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-349" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-349" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Dharr, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tells us the roof of his house in Makkah opened. Jibril, peace be upon him, came down, opened his chest, washed it with Zamzam water, filled it with wisdom and faith from a basin of gold, and closed it. Then, taking his hand, he rose with him to the nearest heaven.

Jibril told its guard, "Open." "Who is this?" "Jibril." "Is anyone with you?" "Yes, Muhammad." "Has he been sent for?" "Yes." Then he rose through the heavens and met Adam, Idris, Musa, Isa and Ibrahim, peace be upon them, who welcomed him.

Allah made fifty prayers obligatory for his ummah. When he passed Musa, peace be upon him, Musa told him: Go back to your Lord; your ummah will not be able to bear that. He went back again and again until Allah said: "They are five, and they are fifty," meaning five prayers, each worth ten. Then the Prophet said: "I feel too shy before my Lord."

Then he reached the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, covered in colors he did not recognize. Then he was admitted into Jannah: it had pearls, and its soil was musk.[^4]

Notice: everything on this journey is tremendous, yet at its end we learn Jannah's soil is not dust that dirties, but a fragrance that delights.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-349" -->

- **`The Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary (Sidrat al-Muntaha)`** — a tremendous tree in the heavens, the farthest point the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, reached on the night of the Mi'raj.
- **`Fine white flour (ad-darmak)`** — pure, soft, white flour, used to describe Jannah's soil in its whiteness and softness.[^9]
- **`Pebbles (al-hasba')`** — small stones; a hadith with a disputed chain says Jannah's pebbles are pearls and rubies.[^6]

1. Why did Salma choose the back corner, even though it could not win?
2. What did Uncle Saeed say? And what changed in the bus lane?
3. Put the events of the Mi'raj in order: the prayer, entering Jannah, the washing of the chest, the gates of the heavens.
4. What are the three descriptions of Jannah's soil in the hadith "Fine white flour, pure musk"?
5. What is the forgotten corner in your school or neighborhood?

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Few things in this world cause more fights than land: property lines, square footage, inheritances that tear families apart. And everyone who "owns" a piece of land will leave it behind one day. The believers in Jannah, though, praise their Lord because He `made us inherit the land, so that we may settle in Jannah wherever we wish`.[^1] It is a land with no borders to fight over, where they settle wherever they choose, as as-Sa'di explains.[^2] And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, described its soil: "Fine white flour, pure musk."[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imaginative Teaching Story: Khalid and One Meter of Dirt

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.036.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imaginative teaching story. It is not a hadith or a historical report.**

Khalid's grandfather left behind a small plot of farmland, and it was divided between Khalid's father and his uncle. Years later, his uncle built a wall, and Khalid's father said the wall had crept a full meter into his land. From that day on, one meter of dirt weighed more than twenty years of brotherhood.

Khalid is fifteen. In the family group chat, his cousin Yusuf posted an angry message with an insult aimed at Khalid's father. Private messages started coming in for Khalid: "Answer him! Your dad's in the right!" And his friend texted: "If you stay quiet, you're weak."

Khalid typed a sharp reply, his thumb hovering over Send. Then he stopped. He remembered that his grandmother read the group chat every evening, and that she had cried the last time her two sons fought.

Khalid deleted the reply. But he could not think of anything to do besides stay silent.

That night, at the class in the mosque, the imam recited `And they will say, "Praise be to Allah, who has fulfilled His promise to us and made us inherit the land"`, and said, "This is the land of Jannah, and it is the one inheritance its heirs never fight over." Then he mentioned the hadith of the man whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw roaming freely in Jannah because of a tree he had cut down from the middle of the road, a tree that used to harm people.[^8]

Khalid thought about the irrigation ditch the two plots shared. Both sides used it, but nobody had looked after it since the dispute began. It had filled up with thorns and trash, and its water now spilled over onto the dirt road his grandmother walked to get home.

On Friday, Khalid took a shovel and a trash bag and went out alone. An hour later, Yusuf showed up and stood off at a distance. "What are you doing? This isn't your land." "It isn't yours either," Khalid said. "It's our grandmother's road." Yusuf said nothing. Then he took the bag and started picking up trash.

The dispute did not end. The case over the wall is still in court, and Khalid's father is still angry. But Yusuf deleted his message from the group chat and wrote, "Sorry for what I said." And that evening, their grandmother posted a photo of the clean ditch and wrote under it: "Alhamdulillah."

Khalid wrote in his journal: "I can't fix the wall. I can clear the road, and I can refuse to sell my tongue for a meter of dirt."

### A True Hadith: The Journey That Ended in Soil of Musk

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-349" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-349" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Dharr, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, relates that the roof of his house in Makkah was opened, and Jibril came down, opened his chest, washed it with the water of Zamzam, and poured wisdom and faith into it. Then he ascended with him to the heavens, and every gate opened only after a question: "Who is with you? Has he been sent for?" In the heavens he met Adam, Idris, Musa, Isa and Ibrahim, peace be upon them. Then fifty prayers were made obligatory, and on Musa's advice he kept going back to ask his Lord to lighten them until they became five, and Allah said: "They are five, and they are fifty. The word is not changed with Me." And the Prophet said: "I feel too shy before my Lord." Then he reached the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, veiled in colors he did not know, and then he was admitted into Jannah: "and in it were strings of pearls, and its soil was musk."[^4]

Reflect: on a single journey, the Prophet saw the land of Jannah and carried the prayer back to us. The land of Jannah is seen through truthful revelation, and it is sought through prostration on the land of this world. And when the dirt of this world becomes a reason for cutting family ties, remember that the only soil worth longing for is the soil of musk.

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.036.dua.steps-on-earth-inherit-musk-land" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ خُطَانَا عَلَى الْأَرْضِ فِي طَاعَتِكَ، وَأَوْرِثْنَا أَرْضَ الْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي تُرَابُهَا الْمِسْكُ، بِرَحْمَتِكَ يَا أَرْحَمَ الرَّاحِمِينَ.
>
> *"O Allah, let our steps on this earth be steps of obeying You, and let us inherit the land of Jannah whose soil is musk, by Your mercy, O Most Merciful of all who show mercy."*

This du'a holds two lands together: the land we walk on today, where we ask Allah to make our steps steps of obedience rather than of quarreling, and the land we hope to inherit. It closes with mercy as a reminder that the inheritance is a gift from Allah, even though our deeds are a means to it.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner recites the ayah of az-Zumar (39:74) and states the interpretation of "the land" as the land of Jannah and who held it; narrates al-Bukhari 349 through its major events up to "its soil was musk"; explains the descriptions in Muslim 2928 and Ibn al-Qayyim's reconciliation of musk and saffron; explains why at-Tirmidhi 2526 is treated as a supporting witness rather than primary evidence; and carries out the removal of a real hazard from a shared space.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; a container of garden soil for each group; a small piece of musk or a pleasant perfume; a board with two columns headed "Soil of this world" and "Soil of Jannah"; gloves and bags for the field activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on az-Zumar 39:74; chapter thirty-four of *Hadi al-Arwah* together with the editor's footnotes; the two narrations of Muslim 2928; and the hadith in Muslim 162 that explains "They are five, and they are fifty." The teacher also identifies a safe nearby site for the activity in advance.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher passes around the container of soil, then the musk, and asks: "If you were told that the soil of a certain land is musk, what would that tell you about the whole of that land?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Recite the ayah of az-Zumar and read the words of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir. Then read al-Bukhari 349 in full as it appears in the lesson, pointing out the marked omission and what it summarizes, as well as the narration with "domes" (*janabidh*). Then read Muslim 2928 in both its narrations, then the hadith in at-Tirmidhi with an explanation of the dispute over its chain, then Muslim 1914. Learners fill in the board as they go.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion of two questions: Why did the vision of Jannah's soil come on the very night the prayer was made obligatory? And how do we distinguish, within a single lesson, between primary evidence and a disputed supporting witness, without either discarding it or overstating it?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners carry out the observation part of the activity "From Roadside Dust to Soil of Musk" as described in the activity unit, and each learner settles on the place, the hazard and a time within the next three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes the established descriptions of Jannah's soil with the source of each one, plus one sentence on the status of the hadith about the pebbles. Close by inviting learners to share their two lines after three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the ayah of az-Zumar and the two hadiths of al-Bukhari and Muslim. Ask advanced learners to read Ibn al-Qayyim's three ways of reconciling the descriptions and to compare at-Tirmidhi's grading with those of al-Albani and Ahmad Shakir.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Treat reports about the unseen with acceptance, without speculating about how they are or measuring them against the earth of this world. Do not make the hadith in at-Tirmidhi primary evidence. *Hadi al-Arwah* contains other narrations that this lesson does not rely on: the hadith "The land of Jannah is white; its open plain is boulders of camphor" has a very weak chain, and the hadith "its land is pure gold (*'iqyan*)" was judged fabricated by the editor.[^7] Do not dwell on Ibn Sayyad in a way that pulls attention from the subject of the lesson. In the field activity, do not touch glass or sharp objects with bare hands, and do not enter anyone else's property without permission.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child states that the soil of Jannah is musk, that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, saw it on the night of the Mi'raj, and that the prayer was given on that night; points out a hazard on a path and asks for help to remove it safely; and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A plate with soil; a cotton ball with musk or a pleasant perfume on it; drawing paper and crayons; an old mud-stained shoe to show; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling Yahya's story, makes sure the plate contains nothing sharp, and prepares a simple picture of a path with a stone and a branch on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The educator shows the child the mud-stained shoe and asks: "Where did this mud come from? Does it smell nice?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell Yahya's story, then say, "And this is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us," and tell the account of the Mi'raj as it appears in the lesson in a calm voice, pausing at "its soil was musk" to smell the cotton ball.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The educator repeats with the child: "The soil of Jannah is musk." "The prayer is the gift of that night." "If you see something that can hurt people on the path, tell a grown-up."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The child does the plate-and-musk activity, then takes a short walk to the front door as described in the activity unit, and draws the path all clean.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What is the soil of Jannah?" and "What do you do if you see glass?" Then read the du'a and its meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, smelling, touching and drawing are enough. Older children can put three pictures in order: the house in Makkah, the sky, and Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The child must never touch glass or sharp objects, but should tell a grown-up instead. Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, Jibril, or any of the prophets. Do not go into detail about the opening of the chest in a way that frightens the child; "he washed his heart with the water of Zamzam" is enough. Be mindful of any child with a sensitivity to perfumes.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student puts the major events of the Mi'raj in order and states that the soil of Jannah is musk; explains the descriptions in "Fine white flour, pure musk"; recalls the hadith of the tree in the middle of the road; and carries out a documented hazard removal under adult supervision.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the two hadiths, written out; Mi'raj event cards for sequencing; soil samples in clear bags; the observation table; gloves.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the events of the Mi'raj on six cards and, with the school administration, identifies a safe corner for the cleanup.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes disputes over land in this world in light of the ayah of az-Zumar; narrates the account of the Mi'raj up to "its soil was musk"; distinguishes, in a conflict, between what is in their hands and what is not; and removes a hazard from a shared space without showing off.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; short scenario cards describing disputes (a property line, a parking spot, a shared walkway) with no names; soil samples; a private notebook for each student.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on the ayah, prepares the scenarios, and makes sure none of them resembles a real conflict known within the group.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask students: "Have you ever seen a dispute over land or a space ruin a relationship?" without mentioning names.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Recite the ayah of az-Zumar and read the words of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir; then read the account of the Mi'raj, then the hadith in Muslim on the soil, then the hadith of the tree, and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Khalid's story, and have groups discuss: "What was in Khalid's hands? And what was not?" Then the groups apply the same question to the scenarios.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students write their soil observations as described in the activity unit, and each one records in their private notebook their "shared road," the hazard they will remove, and when.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student completes an anonymous card: "The land worth competing for is... because...". Then read the du'a. After a week, anyone who wishes may share their paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to compare at-Tirmidhi's grading with al-Albani's for the hadith about the pebbles.

**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask any student to reveal a real family dispute. The story must not be taken to mean that claiming a legitimate right is wrong; the point is to give up insult and the cutting of family ties. [^4]: Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Prayer, Chapter: How the Prayer Was Made Obligatory during al-Isra', hadith 349, narrated by Anas ibn Malik from Abu Dharr, may Allah be pleased with them both; it includes the account of the prayer being made obligatory as narrated by Ibn Hazm and Anas ibn Malik. It is an authentic hadith: [sunnah.com/bukhari:349](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:349). The matn is given without its chain, and one passage, marked in square brackets, has been omitted (the ascent through the heavens and the prophets' welcome, as well as the sentence "Then I was taken up until I reached a level where I could hear the scratching of the pens," from the narration of Ibn Abbas and Abu Habbah); the omission does not change the meaning of anything relevant to this lesson. The narration "domes of pearl" (*janabidh al-lu'lu'*) is in Sahih al-Bukhari, hadith 3342: [sunnah.com/bukhari:3342](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:3342), and Sahih Muslim, hadith 163: [sunnah.com/muslim:163](https://sunnah.com/muslim:163). The explanation of "five, and they are fifty" is from Sahih Muslim, hadith 162 (narrated by Thabit from Anas): [sunnah.com/muslim:162a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:162a).
[^5]: Sahih Muslim, Book of Tribulations and the Portents of the Hour, Chapter: Mention of Ibn Sayyad, hadith 2928 (the second narration, via Abu Usamah from al-Jurayri from Abu Nadrah from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri). It is an authentic hadith: [sunnah.com/muslim:2928b](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2928b). The first narration (via Abu Maslamah from Abu Nadrah) states that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked Ibn Sa'id (that is, Ibn Sayyad), who answered, and the Prophet said, "You have spoken the truth": [sunnah.com/muslim:2928a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2928a).
[^6]: Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Chapters on the Description of Jannah, Chapter: What Has Come Regarding the Description of Jannah and Its Bliss, hadith 2526, narrated by Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him: [sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2526](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2526). Only the relevant passage is given; its opening, marked as omitted (the Companions asking the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about how tender their hearts were in his presence, and about the substance of creation), has been left out. At-Tirmidhi said: "This is a hadith whose chain is not that strong, and in my view it is not connected; this hadith has also been narrated with another chain from Abu Hurayrah." Ahmad Shakir and al-Albani graded it authentic on the strength of its combined routes, and Zubair Ali Zai graded it weak. For this reason it has not been relied on as primary evidence in this lesson.
[^7]: Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyyah, *Hadi al-Arwah ila Bilad al-Afrah* ('Ata'at al-'Ilm edition), Chapter Thirty-Four: On the Soil of Jannah, Its Clay, Its Pebbles and Its Buildings, pp. 280-288. It contains his statement, "These are three descriptions of its soil, and there is no contradiction between them," his ways of reconciling them, and the editor's footnotes: that Ahmad's hadith turns on Abu al-Mudillah, of whom Ibn al-Madini said, "unknown"; that the hadith "The land of Jannah is white; its open plain is boulders of camphor" (Ibn Abi ad-Dunya) has a very weak chain; and that the hadith of Ubayy ibn Ka'b, "and that its land is pure gold (*'iqyan*)," is fabricated: [shamela.ws/book/13652/333](https://shamela.ws/book/13652/333).
[^8]: Sahih Muslim, Book of Righteousness, Joining Ties of Kinship and Good Manners, Chapter: The Virtue of Removing Harm from the Road, hadith 1914 (the third narration in the Book of Righteousness, via al-A'mash from Abu Salih from Abu Hurayrah). It is an authentic hadith: [sunnah.com/muslim:1914d](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1914d). The narration "By Allah, I will move this out of the Muslims' way so that it does not harm them, and he was admitted into Jannah" is via Suhayl from his father: [sunnah.com/muslim:1914c](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1914c).

On the meaning of *janabidh* (plural of *junbudhah*, meaning a dome): Ibn al-Athir, *an-Nihayah fi Gharib al-Hadith wa al-Athar*, entry "j-n-b-dh."
