# The Calls of Jannah and the Glad Tidings on Entering

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of Sahih Muslim (2837), "A caller will call out: 'It is yours to be healthy and never fall ill, ever...'", in its complete wording; list its four glad tidings: health without illness, life without death, youth without old age, and bliss without misery; and name the ayah it is linked to at its end (al-A'raf 7:43).
- Distinguish three calls found in the texts: the call of safety, `O My servants, no fear shall be upon you this Day, nor shall you grieve` (az-Zukhruf 43:68), which as-Sa'di places on the Day of Resurrection; the call of congratulation, `And they will be called: "This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do"` (al-A'raf 7:43); and the content of the call in the hadith of Muslim.
- Explain what "inheriting through deeds" means, drawing on Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di: righteous deeds are the means by which Allah's mercy is attained, ranks in Jannah differ according to deeds, and entry itself is a favor from Allah.
- Distinguish the truthful glad tidings carried by the word "ever" in the news of the Hereafter from the overblown promises of this world, which have no power to make anything last.
- Carry out the "Sealed with the Call" activity: sorting what weighs on the heart (illness, weakness, grief, or the fear of loss) into envelopes bearing the words of the call, then doing a real act of mercy for someone living through one of those states right now.
- Recall the lesson's du'a, which asks Allah for well-being in this world, to make us among the people of Jannah who are healthy there and never fall ill, and to help us do the deeds by which it is inherited.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Good news in this world almost never arrives unmixed. The patient hears that he has recovered, and in the same breath is told to watch for a relapse. The graduate celebrates, and the worry about what comes next has already begun. The young man enjoys his strength, knowing full well that it will fade. Every piece of worldly good news has a "but" waiting in the wings. The glad tidings that the people of Jannah hear, however, are sealed with a single word that cancels every "but": *abadan*, "ever."

This lesson brings together three texts about the calls with which the believers are received: the call of safety in Surah az-Zukhruf, the call of congratulation and inheritance in Surah al-A'raf, and the hadith of Sahih Muslim, which spells out the content of the call in four glad tidings. Taken together, they yield three truths:

1. **The glad tidings remove whatever spoils bliss:** illness, death, old age, and misery, the four things from which no happiness in this world is ever safe.
2. **The call is congratulation and honor:** not a bare announcement, but a celebration of the people of Jannah after their long wait.
3. **Inheritance through deeds, sheltered by mercy:** deeds are the means, mercy is what admits, and ranks differ according to deeds.

The angels' greeting of peace to the people of Jannah was covered in the previous lesson, and the call of everlasting life, when death is slaughtered, is the subject of the next.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-43-68-73" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "O My Servants, No Fear Shall Be upon You This Day"

> **{يَا عِبَادِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ وَلَا أَنْتُمْ تَحْزَنُونَ * الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِآيَاتِنَا وَكَانُوا مُسْلِمِينَ * ادْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ أَنْتُمْ وَأَزْوَاجُكُمْ تُحْبَرُونَ * يُطَافُ عَلَيْهِمْ بِصِحَافٍ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ وَأَكْوَابٍ ۖ وَفِيهَا مَا تَشْتَهِيهِ الْأَنْفُسُ وَتَلَذُّ الْأَعْيُنُ ۖ وَأَنْتُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ * وَتِلْكَ الْجَنَّةُ الَّتِي أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ * لَكُمْ فِيهَا فَاكِهَةٌ كَثِيرَةٌ مِنْهَا تَأْكُلُونَ}** [الزخرف: ٦٨-٧٣][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"O My servants, no fear shall be upon you this Day, nor shall you grieve: you who believed in Our signs and were Muslims, in submission. Enter Jannah, you and your spouses, made joyful. Platters of gold and goblets will be passed around among them, and in it is whatever souls desire and eyes delight in, and in it you will abide forever. That is the Jannah you have been made to inherit for what you used to do. In it you will have fruit in abundance, of which you will eat."** (az-Zukhruf 43:68-73)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said that Allah, exalted is He, will call out to them on the Day of Resurrection with words that gladden their hearts and remove every harm and evil from them. The call means: "No fear will touch you in anything that lies ahead of you, and no grief will befall you over anything that has passed. And when every kind of harm has been removed, everything beloved and longed for is established."[^2] Ibn Kathir reports from Sulayman at-Taymi, through his son al-Mu'tamir, that when people are resurrected they will all be terrified. Then, when the call goes out, `O My servants, no fear shall be upon you this Day`, every one of them will hope it is for them; but when it is followed by `you who believed in Our signs and were Muslims, in submission`, all but the believers will lose hope of it. This is a Successor's understanding of the ayah, not a hadith traced to the Prophet. Ibn Kathir explains the description as referring to those "whose hearts and inner selves believed, and whose limbs and outward conduct yielded to the law of Allah," and explains `made joyful` (*tuhbarun*) as "given bliss and made happy." On `you have been made to inherit for what you used to do`, he said: "Your righteous deeds were the means by which Allah's mercy came to include you, for no one's deeds will admit him into Jannah; rather it is by Allah's bounty and mercy. And the degrees differ only according to how much good one has done."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The call opens by joining the servants to Allah Himself, `O My servants`, and then defines who it is for by description, not by lineage: faith within and submission without. This one passage brings together safety from fear and grief, permission to enter, everlasting life, `and in it you will abide forever`, and inheritance through deeds.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-7-42-43" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And They Will Be Called: 'This Is Jannah, Which You Have Been Made to Inherit'"

> **{وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَا نُكَلِّفُ نَفْسًا إِلَّا وُسْعَهَا أُولَٰئِكَ أَصْحَابُ الْجَنَّةِ ۖ هُمْ فِيهَا خَالِدُونَ * وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهِمُ الْأَنْهَارُ ۖ وَقَالُوا الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي هَدَانَا لِهَٰذَا وَمَا كُنَّا لِنَهْتَدِيَ لَوْلَا أَنْ هَدَانَا اللَّهُ ۖ لَقَدْ جَاءَتْ رُسُلُ رَبِّنَا بِالْحَقِّ ۖ وَنُودُوا أَنْ تِلْكُمُ الْجَنَّةُ أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ}** [الأعراف: ٤٢-٤٣][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"But those who believe and do righteous deeds (We do not burden any soul beyond its capacity): they are the companions of Jannah; in it they will abide forever. And We will remove whatever rancor is in their hearts; rivers will flow beneath them. And they will say, 'All praise belongs to Allah, who guided us to this. We would never have been guided had Allah not guided us. The messengers of our Lord did indeed come with the truth.' And they will be called: 'This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do.'"** (al-A'raf 7:42-43)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di said of `And they will be called`: "as congratulation to them, as an honor, as a greeting, and as a mark of respect"; and of `you have been made to inherit`: "you were its heirs." He also quoted one of the early Muslims (*Salaf*): "The people of Jannah were saved from the Fire by Allah's pardon, were admitted into Jannah by Allah's mercy, and divided its dwellings among themselves and inherited them through righteous deeds, and that too is part of His mercy; indeed, it is among the highest kinds of His mercy."[^5] Ibn Kathir said: "That is: because of your deeds, mercy reached you, so you entered Jannah, and you took up your dwellings in it according to your deeds."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Look at the order of the ayah. First the rancor is drawn out of their hearts; then the people of Jannah praise their Lord and admit that they would never have been guided had He not guided them; and only then does the call come. The praise is theirs to give; the congratulation is theirs to receive. And the words just before it, `We do not burden any soul beyond its capacity`, reassure the believer that the deeds asked of him lie within his strength.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2837" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "It Is Yours to Be Healthy and Never Fall Ill, Ever"

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ وَأَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنهما، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«يُنَادِي مُنَادٍ: إِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَصِحُّوا فَلَا تَسْقَمُوا أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَحْيَوْا فَلَا تَمُوتُوا أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَشِبُّوا فَلَا تَهْرَمُوا أَبَدًا، وَإِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَنْعَمُوا فَلَا تَبْتَئِسُوا أَبَدًا»**، فَذَلِكَ قَوْلُهُ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: {وَنُودُوا أَنْ تِلْكُمُ الْجَنَّةُ أُورِثْتُمُوهَا بِمَا كُنْتُمْ تَعْمَلُونَ}.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with them both, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"A caller will call out: 'It is yours to be healthy and never fall ill, ever. It is yours to live and never die, ever. It is yours to be young and never grow old, ever. It is yours to live in bliss and never suffer misery, ever.'"** And that is the meaning of the words of Allah, Mighty and Majestic: `And they will be called: "This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do."`[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

"It is yours to be healthy" means: lasting health is yours, and likewise for what follows. *Tasqamu* comes from *saqam*, which is illness; *tahramu* from *haram*, the frailty of old age; and *tabta'isu* from *bu's*, which is hardship of circumstance and a miserable life. The word "ever" (*abadan*) is repeated four times. The hadith links this call to the ayah of al-A'raf, while in at-Tirmidhi's narration it is linked to the ayah of az-Zukhruf: `That is the Jannah you have been made to inherit for what you used to do`.[^7]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith does not say who the caller is, so we do not name one without evidence. The four glad tidings answer four sources of anxiety that every human being knows: the body falls ill, life comes to an end, strength withers, and living grows hard. This call does not make light of the pain of the sick, the elderly, or the grieving today. Rather, it gives them a truthful horizon that no promise of this world can offer.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The call in az-Zukhruf assures the servants of safety from fear and grief and grants them permission to enter. The call in al-A'raf congratulates them on their inheritance after their hearts have been cleansed and they have praised their Lord. And the hadith of Muslim spells out the content of the glad tidings in four blessings that never end. The three texts meet on two points: permanence can be truthfully promised only in the Hereafter, and today's deeds are the means Allah has appointed as the road to that inheritance.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What are the four things the call in the hadith of Muslim rules out, and why are they the greatest spoilers of happiness in this world?
2. How did as-Sa'di explain `no fear shall be upon you this Day, nor shall you grieve`, and how, in his view, does fear differ from grief?
3. How can `you have been made to inherit for what you used to do` be reconciled with the fact that entry is by Allah's mercy, as Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di explain it?
4. Why does the call in the ayah of al-A'raf come only after the rancor has been removed and the people of Jannah have praised their Lord?
5. How would you share the glad tidings of this hadith with someone who is sick, elderly, or grieving without making light of their pain?

### Activity: Sealed with the Call

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.031.activity.sealed-with-the-call" -->

Prepare four envelopes, and write on each one a phrase from the call: «تَصِحُّوا فَلَا تَسْقَمُوا» ("be healthy and never fall ill"), «تَحْيَوْا فَلَا تَمُوتُوا» ("live and never die"), «تَشِبُّوا فَلَا تَهْرَمُوا» ("be young and never grow old"), «تَنْعَمُوا فَلَا تَبْتَئِسُوا» ("live in bliss and never suffer misery"). On small slips of paper, write what has been weighing on your heart lately: the fear of illness, a loss, growing older, or financial strain. Put each slip in the matching envelope and seal it. Then choose one envelope and identify someone who is living through that very state right now: a sick person you can visit, an elderly person you can serve, a grieving person you can comfort, or someone in need you can help. Do it within three days. Afterward, write two lines: What did you do? And how has the call changed the way you see what is inside that envelope?

### A Teaching Story: Rayyan Hears the Happy News

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.031.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Rayyan had chickenpox. Little red spots popped up all over him, and they itched and itched. The doctor said, "He needs to stay home for a week."

Rayyan was sad. On Thursday it was his cousin's birthday party, and all the kids would be playing there. "I want to go!" he said. His mom said gently, "If you go, the other children might get sick like you. Let's be patient for a little while." Rayyan was quiet. Then he said, "Okay... I'll be patient." Instead of going, he drew his cousin a card with a ball and a sun on it, and his dad took it to him.

The days went by. Rayyan took his medicine and said, "Alhamdulillah." Then one morning the phone rang. It was the doctor. Mom listened, then ran to the door of his room and called out in a loud voice, "Everybody, good news! Rayyan is all better!" Dad clapped, his little sister jumped up and down, and Rayyan laughed and laughed.

Then he asked, "Mom, does that mean I'll never, ever get sick again?"

His mom sat down beside him and answered honestly. "In this world we might get sick again. Then we take our medicine and we're patient, and Allah gives us a reward. But in Jannah, someone calls out to the people there: 'You are healthy, and you will never be sick again, ever.'"

Rayyan's eyes went wide. "Ever?" "Ever," she said. "And Allah told us that the people of Jannah were given it for the good things they used to do."

Rayyan thought about that. Then he got up, carried a glass of water to his grandma, who was feeling tired, and said, "Grandma, may Allah make you better." His grandma smiled and made du'a for him.

### An Authentic Hadith: The Call to the People of Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2837" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2837" audience="4-7" -->

**This is true. These are the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told to us by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with them. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah hear someone calling out four pieces of happy news:

- You are healthy, so you will never be sick, ever.
- You are alive, so you will never die, ever.
- You are young, so you will never grow old and weak, ever.
- You are happy and full of joy, so you will never be sad or tired, ever.[^7]

And then the hadith mentions the words of Allah: this Jannah has been given to you for the good things you used to do.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Glad tidings (*bisharah*) are good news that makes your heart happy. In this world we sometimes exaggerate and say "forever!" when forever isn't ours to promise. The people of Jannah hear glad tidings that are completely true. Allah says: `O My servants, no fear shall be upon you this Day, nor shall you grieve`,[^1] and He says about them: `And they will be called: "This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do."`[^4] The Qur'an commentator as-Sa'di explained that they are called "as congratulation to them, and as an honor."[^5]

### A Teaching Story: Hamza and the Morning Announcements

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.031.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

The school team had won the league trophy, and Hamza, a sixth grader, was chosen to read the news on the morning announcements. His teammates crowded around, and Ziyad said, "Write this: 'We're the champions forever, and their team is going to be losers forever!'" Everyone laughed, and Hamza wrote it down.

That evening Mr. Kamal looked over the announcement scripts. He didn't cross anything out. He just wrote one question in green pen: "Is the word 'forever' true here? Your call."

Hamza thought for a long time. Next year a different team might win. And the kids at the other school would hear what he said from their brothers and sisters; how would he feel if someone called him "a loser forever"? Then he remembered a hadith his dad had once read him about a call to the people of Jannah, where the word "ever" comes again and again. So he opened the hadith book with his dad and read it, word for word, from Sahih Muslim.

The next morning Hamza stood at the microphone and said, "Congratulations to our team on winning the trophy this year, and thank you to the other team for playing fair." Then he added a new segment he called "Good News That Never Ends," and in it he read: «إِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَصِحُّوا فَلَا تَسْقَمُوا أَبَدًا», "It is yours to be healthy and never fall ill, ever."[^7] Then he said, "That's the kind of 'ever' that's actually true."

At recess Ziyad was annoyed. "You killed the hype!" he said. But a little third grader came up to Hamza and asked quietly, "My grandpa's in the hospital... is it true that the people of Jannah never get sick, ever?" Hamza answered calmly, "That's what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about the people of Jannah. And right now, let's make du'a for your grandpa to get better." And they made du'a for him together.

At the end of the day Ziyad came over and said, "That last part... it was better than the cheering." And Hamza decided to ask himself before writing any news: Is it true?

### An Authentic Hadith: Four Pieces of Good News That Never End

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2837" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2837" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true report that Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with them, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that a caller will call out four glad tidings to the people of Jannah: it is yours to be healthy and never be sick, ever; to live and never die, ever; to stay young and never grow old and weak, ever; and to enjoy bliss and never be touched by misery or hardship, ever. Then the hadith mentions the words of Allah: `And they will be called: "This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do."`[^7]

Notice two things. Each piece of good news cancels something people fear in this world. And the hadith links this call to the righteous deeds that Allah has made the means of reaching His mercy.

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2837" -->

- **`Fall ill (tasqamu)`** — from *saqam*, which means illness.
- **`Grow old (tahramu)`** — from *haram*, the weakness that comes to the body in old age.
- **`Suffer misery (tabta'isu)`** — from *bu's*, which means hardship, a hard life, and sadness.
- **`You have been made to inherit it (urithtumuha)`** — you have been given it as a gift that stays with you, like an inheritance, and your righteous deeds were the means of reaching Allah's mercy.[^6]

> اللَّهُمَّ عَافِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ الَّذِينَ يَصِحُّونَ فِيهَا فَلَا يَسْقَمُونَ، وَيَنْعَمُونَ فَلَا يَبْتَئِسُونَ، وَأَعِنَّا عَلَى الْعَمَلِ الَّذِي نَرِثُهَا بِهِ بِرَحْمَتِكَ.

What it means: O Allah, give us well-being in this world, make us among the people of Jannah who are never sick or miserable there, and help us do the good deeds through which we hope to inherit it by Your mercy.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live surrounded by promises of "forever": beauty that never fades, youth that never ends, happiness "forever after." Not one of the people making those promises can actually keep them. Islam does not stop you from taking care of your body and your health, but it teaches you that the word "ever" is a trust (*amanah*), and that it has only ever been spoken with complete truth in the news of the Hereafter. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that the people of Jannah will hear the call: «إِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَشِبُّوا فَلَا تَهْرَمُوا أَبَدًا», "It is yours to be young and never grow old, ever,"[^7] and Allah says: `O My servants, no fear shall be upon you this Day, nor shall you grieve`.[^1]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Teaching Story: Joud's Contract

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.031.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith and not a historical report.**

Joud is sixteen. She posts short videos about planning a study day, and a few thousand people follow her. One day a message arrived from a skincare company: "Paid sponsorship offer." The fee would just about cover the laptop she needed. And the script was already written for her: "With this cream, you'll never age. Forever young!"

Joud tried the cream for a week. It was a good moisturizer, nothing more. Her friend Rana said, "Every influencer says stuff like that. It's just marketing. Nobody's going to take it literally." It sounded convincing, and Joud almost said yes.

That night, as usual, she sat down to read to her grandmother, who lives with the family. Her grandmother's hands trembled around her cup of tea as she asked Joud to read to her from the hadith book, and Joud's eyes fell on the hadith of Sahih Muslim: «إِنَّ لَكُمْ أَنْ تَشِبُّوا فَلَا تَهْرَمُوا أَبَدًا», "It is yours to be young and never grow old, ever." Her grandmother smiled and said, "That's the only promise of eternal youth I believe."

Joud stopped. She felt that the line she'd been asked to say was, without meaning to, mocking her grandmother's hands and every girl who would look in the mirror and panic at her first wrinkle. In the morning she wrote to the company: "I can say it's a good moisturizer, but I can't promise anyone they'll never age." The reply came two days later: "Sorry, the script cannot be changed."

Joud lost the deal. "You're way too idealistic," Rana said. Joud didn't post anything about her decision; she didn't want to collect likes for taking a stand. But she started tutoring the little girl next door in math, to save up for the laptop slowly.

In her private journal she wrote: "The word 'ever' is a trust. I won't sell it." Underneath she added: "For what you used to do." And she decided to make her evening hour of reading to her grandmother one of the deeds through which she hopes to hear that call.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.authenticated-story" kind="authenticated_story" -->

### An Authentic Hadith: The Call That Never Lies

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2837" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2837" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report that Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with them, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, tells us that a caller will call out four glad tidings to the people of Jannah, each one sealed with "ever": health never followed by illness, life never followed by death, youth never followed by old age, and bliss never followed by misery. Then the hadith mentions the words of Allah: `And they will be called: "This is Jannah, which you have been made to inherit for what you used to do."`[^7]

Think about it: the four things that so much of today's anxiety and advertising are built on (health, lifespan, youth, and a comfortable life) are exactly the four things this call guarantees. The difference is that an ad sells you an "ever" it doesn't own, while the call promises it from the One who does. And Ibn Kathir made clear that righteous deeds are the means of attaining mercy, and that ranks differ according to deeds.[^6]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2837" -->

- **`Old age (al-haram)`** — the frailty of the body in old age; the hadith casts no blame on the elderly, but promises that the frailty itself will be gone.
- **`Suffer misery (tabta'isu)`** — from *bu's*, which means hardship, a hard life, and the sadness that comes with them.
- **`They will be called (nudu)`** — a call of congratulation and honor, as as-Sa'di said: "as congratulation to them, as an honor, as a greeting, and as a mark of respect."[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did Rana's line, "It's just marketing," sound convincing? And what made Joud back out?
2. Was Joud's decision a loss? What did she lose, and what did she protect?
3. Why didn't Joud post anything about her decision? What does that have to do with sincerity (*ikhlas*)?
4. What are the four things the call guarantees? Which of them worries your generation most?
5. How can you take care of your body and your health without buying into this world's promises of "forever"?

<!-- unit:end -->

### Activity: Sealed with the Call

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.031.activity.sealed-with-the-call" -->

Put each slip in its envelope and seal it without letting anyone read it. Then choose one envelope, identify someone who is living through that very situation right now, and within a week do something real for them: time spent with an elderly relative, a message to a friend who is sick, or help for someone going through hard times. At the end, write a short paragraph: What does the call change about the way you deal with this fear?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.031.dua.wellbeing-and-heirs-of-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ عَافِنَا فِي الدُّنْيَا، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ الَّذِينَ يَصِحُّونَ فِيهَا فَلَا يَسْقَمُونَ، وَيَنْعَمُونَ فَلَا يَبْتَئِسُونَ، وَأَعِنَّا عَلَى الْعَمَلِ الَّذِي نَرِثُهَا بِهِ بِرَحْمَتِكَ.
>
> *"O Allah, grant us well-being in this world, and make us among the people of Jannah who are healthy there and never fall ill, who live in bliss and never know misery; and help us do the deeds by which we inherit it, by Your mercy."*

This du'a brings together two requests: well-being in this world, which is a legitimate thing to ask for, and the hope of lasting health and a bliss with no misery after it in Jannah. Ending it with "by Your mercy" is a reminder that deeds are the means, and that the inheritance itself is a favor from Allah.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of Muslim 2837 in its wording and lists its four glad tidings; distinguishes the three calls (az-Zukhruf 43:68, al-A'raf 7:43, and the hadith of Muslim); explains inheritance through deeds, drawing on Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; composes an honest word of comfort for someone sick, elderly, or grieving that does not make light of their pain; and carries out a real act of mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board divided into three columns headed "Safety," "Congratulation," and "Content"; four envelopes and small slips of paper for each learner.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the tafsir of as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir on az-Zukhruf 43:68-73 and al-A'raf 7:43, and the hadith of Muslim 2837 alongside at-Tirmidhi's narration (3246), and writes one of the four phrases of the call on each envelope.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What's the last piece of good news you heard? And what was the 'but' that came after it?" The teacher listens to three answers, then reads the hadith of Muslim without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayat of az-Zukhruf in full, then as-Sa'di's words on fear and grief, and the statement of Sulayman at-Taymi, pointing out that it is a Successor's understanding, not a hadith. Then read the two ayat of al-A'raf, as-Sa'di's phrase "as congratulation to them, as an honor," and Ibn Kathir's words on inheritance. Then read the hadith of Muslim in its wording, noting that Muslim's narration links it to al-A'raf and at-Tirmidhi's links it to az-Zukhruf. Learners fill in the three columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion of two questions: How does `you have been made to inherit for what you used to do` fit with the fact that entry is by Allah's mercy? And how do we bring glad tidings to people in hardship without telling them, "It's no big deal"? Each learner composes one sentence of comfort.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners carry out the "Sealed with the Call" activity as described in the activity unit, and each one decides on the person, the act, and the time, within three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes the four glad tidings from memory and one sentence explaining inheritance through deeds, attributing it to one of the commentators. The teacher closes by inviting anyone who wishes to share their two lines after the three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith of Muslim and the ayah of al-A'raf are enough. Advanced learners are asked to compare how the hadith is linked to the ayah of al-A'raf in Muslim and to the ayah of az-Zukhruf in at-Tirmidhi, and to explain at-Tirmidhi's remark about the narration that stops at a Companion (*mawquf*).

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not name the caller in the hadith without evidence. Do not declare with certainty that any particular person is among the people of Jannah. Anyone in the group who is ill or recently bereaved must not be asked to reveal what is in their envelope. Ibn al-Qayyim, in his chapter on "the caller of Jannah," gathers authentic hadiths together with weak reports, among them a report from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari whose chain hinges on a narrator whose narrations are rejected (*matruk*), so present only what is established.[^8] Leave the slaughter of death and the call of everlasting life to the next lesson, and the call of Allah's good pleasure (*ridwan*) and the vision of Allah to their own lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah hear happy news: no being sick and no being sad, ever; names one kind thing to do for someone who is sick or sad; and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Two large envelopes, one with a picture of a bandage and one with a sad face; paper and crayons; a plastic cup for role-play; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling Rayyan's story in a calm voice, and gets the "Caller" game ready: standing at the door and calling out a piece of happy family news.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver calls out from the door, "Good news, everybody! It's story time!" and then asks, "How do we feel when we hear happy news?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell Rayyan's story, then say, "And this is something true, from the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him," and read the four pieces of happy news slowly, with the child raising one finger for each one.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The caregiver and child repeat together: "In this world we might get sick, so we're patient." "In Jannah, no being sick, ever." "We do good and ask Allah for Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The child does the two-envelope activity as described in the activity unit, then plays Rayyan by carrying the cup of water to someone in the family.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child, "What do the people of Jannah hear?" and "What do we do for someone who's sick?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, use stickers instead of drawing and mention only two of the glad tidings. Older children count all four glad tidings on their fingers.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention death in the glad tidings calmly, without detail. Never tell the child that they will not get sick in this world. If someone in the family is ill or a relative has recently died, focus on mercy and du'a, not on fear. And do not let the story suggest that illness is a punishment.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student lists the four glad tidings and explains their key words, names the ayah linked to the hadith, tells truthful glad tidings apart from exaggeration, and plans an act of mercy to carry out with their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the hadith written out; cards for the four terms; four envelopes and small cards for each student; a short sample script for the morning announcements.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes an announcement script containing two exaggerations (such as "forever" and a jab at another team) for the students to correct.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What's the best news you've ever heard on the morning announcements? Have you ever heard an exaggeration on them?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the ayah of az-Zukhruf (43:68), then retell the hadith and have the students read its wording. Explain the terms, and connect the end of the hadith to the ayah of al-A'raf and as-Sa'di's phrase "as congratulation to them, and as an honor."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Hamza's story, pausing at Mr. Kamal's note to ask: "Why didn't he cross out the word himself?" Then the students correct the sample announcement script in pairs.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students do the envelope activity as described in the activity unit, and each one writes a plan for an act of mercy and the name of the adult who will go with them.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student lists the four glad tidings aloud and gives the meaning of *urithtumuha*, "you have been made to inherit it." Then read the du'a. A week later, students share their two sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who struggle receive envelopes with the words already written on them. Advanced students write a short, truthful announcement segment about the hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Students' cards are never read aloud. Visits to a sick person or a neighbor happen with a parent or with a parent's permission. Be mindful of any student with a sick or recently deceased family member, and allow them to choose a different envelope.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the "forever" promises of advertising in light of the hadith; explains the four glad tidings and the meaning of inheritance through deeds; distinguishes legitimate care for the body from believing promises of eternal youth; and carries out a real deed within a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; three real printed ads that promise permanence (with brand names removed); four envelopes for each student.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher removes the brand names from the ads and reviews Ibn Kathir's words on inheritance and as-Sa'di's words on the call.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the ads and ask: "What word do they all have in common? And who actually has the power to promise it?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the hadith of Muslim in its wording and the ayah of az-Zukhruf (43:68), then the ayah of al-A'raf (7:43) and as-Sa'di's words, then Ibn Kathir's words on inheritance, and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Joud's story, and have groups discuss: "Was her refusal an overreaction? And what if she had said yes?" Then ask: "Why didn't she post about her decision?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students do the envelope activity in silence, as described in the activity unit, and each one writes down the deed they will do and when.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** On an unnamed card, each student completes the sentence: "The difference between the ad's 'forever' and the call's 'ever' is...". Then read the du'a. A week later, anyone who wishes shares their paragraph.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who are uncomfortable writing may record a private voice note instead. Advanced students analyze one ad and write an honest alternative to it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never discuss any student's appearance or body. A student who shows signs of serious anxiety about body image or of disordered eating should be gently invited to a one-on-one conversation and referred to the appropriate professional support. Never disparage the elderly or old age; the point is that frailty will disappear, not that older people are flawed.
