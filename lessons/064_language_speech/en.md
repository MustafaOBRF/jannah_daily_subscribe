# The Language and Speech of the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate, in order, the account in Surah al-Baqarah (2:30-33) of Allah teaching Adam, peace be upon him, all the names: the announcement of a successor on earth, the angels' question, the presenting of the named things, the angels' reply `Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us`, and then Adam telling them the names.

- Explain that speech is a gift Allah taught to humankind, and that the Qur'an describes the speech of the people of Jannah by its quality, not by its language: no `idle talk and no sinful speech`, but `only the words "Peace! Peace!"` (al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26 and Maryam 19:62), along with glorification, greeting and praise (Yunus 10:10).

- Distinguish what revelation tells us about the speech of the people of Jannah from what we do not know: the reports that name Arabic as their language cannot be relied upon, and here we stop where the angels stopped: `We have no knowledge except what You have taught us`.

- Connect all this to the hadith in al-Bukhari (6478) about the word that pleases Allah, by which He raises a person many degrees.

- Carry out the "Sifting My Words" activity: sorting what is said in one specific setting into peace, idle talk and sin, then replacing one pattern with a word of peace and sorting again.

- Recite the lesson's bedtime du'a, asking for beneficial knowledge, a guarded tongue, and entry into a home where nothing is heard but peace.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Many people ask: what language do the people of Jannah speak? It is a fair question, but revelation chose to answer a deeper one: how do they speak, and what do they hear? The Qur'an never names the language of Jannah, yet it describes the speech there with great precision: no idle talk and no sin in it, only peace, glorification and praise.

This lesson begins at the very beginning of human speech, when Allah taught Adam, peace be upon him, all the names. Speaking, naming and understanding were a gift from Allah before they were ever a human skill. And the place where the angels stopped that day, `We have no knowledge except what You have taught us`, is the very method we follow when we face the question of Jannah's language. The ayah of Yunus 10:10 is the primary story of the lesson "The Last Call of the People of Jannah"; here we take it only as supporting evidence.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-2-30-33" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And He Taught Adam the Names, All of Them"

> **{وَإِذْ قَالَ رَبُّكَ لِلْمَلَائِكَةِ إِنِّي جَاعِلٌ فِي الْأَرْضِ خَلِيفَةً ۖ قَالُوا أَتَجْعَلُ فِيهَا مَنْ يُفْسِدُ فِيهَا وَيَسْفِكُ الدِّمَاءَ وَنَحْنُ نُسَبِّحُ بِحَمْدِكَ وَنُقَدِّسُ لَكَ ۖ قَالَ إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ * وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا ثُمَّ عَرَضَهُمْ عَلَى الْمَلَائِكَةِ فَقَالَ أَنْبِئُونِي بِأَسْمَاءِ هَٰؤُلَاءِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ صَادِقِينَ * قَالُوا سُبْحَانَكَ لَا عِلْمَ لَنَا إِلَّا مَا عَلَّمْتَنَا ۖ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْعَلِيمُ الْحَكِيمُ * قَالَ يَا آدَمُ أَنْبِئْهُمْ بِأَسْمَائِهِمْ ۖ فَلَمَّا أَنْبَأَهُمْ بِأَسْمَائِهِمْ قَالَ أَلَمْ أَقُلْ لَكُمْ إِنِّي أَعْلَمُ غَيْبَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَأَعْلَمُ مَا تُبْدُونَ وَمَا كُنْتُمْ تَكْتُمُونَ}** [البقرة: ٣٠-٣٣][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And when your Lord said to the angels, 'I am placing a successor on the earth,' they said, 'Will You place on it one who will spread corruption there and shed blood, while we glorify You with praise and proclaim Your holiness?' He said, 'I know what you do not know.' And He taught Adam the names, all of them; then He presented them to the angels and said, 'Tell Me the names of these, if you are truthful.' They said, 'Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. You are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.' He said, 'O Adam, tell them their names.' And when he had told them their names, He said, 'Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and that I know what you reveal and what you have been concealing?'"** (al-Baqarah 2:30-33)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir says that the angels' words were "not an objection to Allah, nor envy of the children of Adam... it was only a question seeking to learn and uncover the wisdom." On `the names, all of them`, he says: "The correct view is that He taught him the names of all things: their essences and their actions." As-Sa'di says that Allah taught him "both the name and the thing named, that is, the words and their meanings, even the full form of a noun, such as *qas'ah* (bowl), and its diminutive, such as *qusay'ah* (little bowl)." And on the angels' reply he says: "So they affirmed and acknowledged Allah's knowledge and wisdom, their own inability to know even the least thing, and Allah's favor upon them."[^1]

#### Lesson Explanation

The first thing that showed Adam's distinction over the angels was knowledge of the names: the ability to name things and to tell of them. Language, at its root, is something Allah teaches. And the angels, who glorify Him and never disobey, did not presume to speak about what they had not been taught; they said: `Glory be to You`.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-56-25-26" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Only the Words: Peace! Peace!"

> **{لَا يَسْمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغْوًا وَلَا تَأْثِيمًا * إِلَّا قِيلًا سَلَامًا سَلَامًا}** [الواقعة: ٢٥-٢٦][^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"They will hear in it no idle talk and no sinful speech, only the words: 'Peace! Peace!'"** (al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26)[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir explains *laghw*, `idle talk`, as "empty talk, that is, worthless speech devoid of meaning, or containing a petty or feeble meaning"; *ta'thim*, `sinful speech`, as "speech in which there is something ugly"; and `only the words: "Peace! Peace!"` as "only their greeting one another with peace... and their speech, too, is free of idle talk and sin." As-Sa'di says: "Only good speech, because it is the home of the good... and this shows the fine manners of the people of Jannah in how they address one another."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah rules out two kinds of speech: the empty kind that means nothing, and the ugly kind that makes the speaker sinful. And it affirms one kind, repeated: peace upon peace.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-19-62" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Hear in It No Idle Talk, Only Peace"

> **{لَا يَسْمَعُونَ فِيهَا لَغْوًا إِلَّا سَلَامًا ۖ وَلَهُمْ رِزْقُهُمْ فِيهَا بُكْرَةً وَعَشِيًّا}** [مريم: ٦٢][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"They will hear in it no idle talk, only peace, and they will have their provision there morning and evening."** (Maryam 19:62)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says that they hear there no "insult, no finding fault, no speech that disobeys Allah, and nothing that spoils one's peace of mind"; `only peace` means "only words free of every flaw: the remembrance of Allah, greetings, words of joy, glad tidings, good conversation exchanged among brothers, and hearing the address of the Most Merciful... because the home is the Home of Peace."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

There is conversation in Jannah, friends talking long into the evening, but it is talk that neither wounds nor spoils anyone's peace. The point is not silence; the point is speech that is whole and sound.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-10-10" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Their Call in It Will Be: Glory Be to You, O Allah"

> **{دَعْوَاهُمْ فِيهَا سُبْحَانَكَ اللَّهُمَّ وَتَحِيَّتُهُمْ فِيهَا سَلَامٌ ۚ وَآخِرُ دَعْوَاهُمْ أَنِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ}** [يونس: ١٠][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Their call in it will be, 'Glory be to You, O Allah,' and their greeting in it will be, 'Peace,' and the last of their call will be, 'All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of the worlds.'"** (Yunus 10:10)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

As-Sa'di says that their remembrance of Allah is "like breathing, without effort or strain," and that their greeting to one another "when they meet and visit each other" is peace, "that is, speech free of idle talk and sin."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah brings together what the people of Jannah say to their Lord (glorification and praise) and what they say to one another (peace). And the word `Glory be to You` here is the very word the angels said in the account of Adam.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-6478" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "By It Allah Raises Him Many Degrees"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«إِنَّ الْعَبْدَ لَيَتَكَلَّمُ بِالْكَلِمَةِ مِنْ رِضْوَانِ اللَّهِ لَا يُلْقِي لَهَا بَالًا، يَرْفَعُ اللَّهُ بِهَا دَرَجَاتٍ، وَإِنَّ الْعَبْدَ لَيَتَكَلَّمُ بِالْكَلِمَةِ مِنْ سَخَطِ اللَّهِ لَا يُلْقِي لَهَا بَالًا يَهْوِي بِهَا فِي جَهَنَّمَ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"A servant may speak a word that pleases Allah without giving it a second thought, and by it Allah raises him many degrees. And a servant may speak a word that angers Allah without giving it a second thought, and by it he plunges into Hell."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

On `without giving it a second thought` (*la yulqi laha balan*), Ibn Hajar says: "He does not weigh it in his mind, nor think about where it will lead." Al-Bukhari places the hadith in his chapter on guarding the tongue.[^5]

#### Lesson Explanation

A small word in this world may raise its speaker many degrees in Jannah, or send him plunging down. Training the tongue, then, is practice in the language of Jannah: the language of peace.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### What Do We Know, and What Do We Not Know?

Through revelation we know the quality of the speech of the people of Jannah: peace, with no idle talk and no sin in it; glorification and praise that flow from them without effort; and good conversation among brothers. As for naming their language, no authentic text from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, has established it. The hadith "Love the Arabs for three reasons: because I am an Arab, the Qur'an is in Arabic, and the speech of the people of Jannah is Arabic," and the report "The language of the people of Jannah is Arabic," when attributed to the Prophet, were both judged fabricated by the editor of *Hadi al-Arwah*. The statement attributed to Ibn Abbas as his own words has an abandoned narrator in its chain, and az-Zuhri's statement, "It has reached me that it is Arabic," is an unsourced report that proves nothing. Ibn Taymiyyah is reported to have said: "It is not known what language people will speak on that Day... nor is it authentic that Persian is the language of the people of Hell, nor that Arabic is the language of the people of everlasting bliss."[^6]

So we affirm nothing that has not been established, and we deny nothing that has not been denied, and we say what the angels said: `We have no knowledge except what You have taught us`. What each of us holds in our hands right now is not knowledge of Jannah's language, but the chance to practice the quality of its speech.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why was knowledge of the names the thing that showed Adam's distinction over the angels? And what does it mean that language is "taught by Allah"?

2. What is the difference between `idle talk` (*laghw*) and `sinful speech` (*ta'thim*) as Ibn Kathir explains them? Give a present-day example of each.

3. How does as-Sa'di's comment on Maryam 19:62 hold together the absence of idle talk and the presence of conversation and evening talk among brothers?

4. Why do we not state with certainty that the language of Jannah is Arabic, even though we love Arabic and honor it as the language of the Qur'an?

5. Which word in your day do you "not give a second thought," though it may be one that pleases Allah, or one that angers Him?

### Activity: Sifting My Words

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.064.activity.sieve-my-words-lagw-or-salam" -->

Choose a setting that comes up every day: the family at dinner, a group chat, or a meeting at work. Draw three columns: "Peace" (*salam*: useful or kind words, or the remembrance of Allah), "Idle talk" (*laghw*: empty words with no benefit), and "Sin" (*ithm*: lying, mockery, backbiting or obscenity).

For two days, right after that setting, put a tally mark in the right column for everything you said or wrote, honestly and without names.

On the third day, choose one pattern from the idle-talk or sin column and write a "word of peace" to replace it whenever that moment comes. Use it for five days, then sort again for two days, and write three lines: What changed in the columns? And which word had you been speaking without a second thought?

### A Story from the Qur'an: Adam Learns the Names

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-30-33" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-30-33" audience="4-7" -->

Long ago, before there were any people on the earth at all, Allah told the angels that He was going to place someone on the earth to live there and look after it. That was Adam, peace be upon him. Then Allah taught Adam the names of all things, even the names of little things, like a little bowl.

Then Allah showed these things to the angels and said to them, "Tell Me the names of these." The angels only know what Allah teaches them, so they answered very politely: `Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us.` That means: "Our Lord, we only know what You have taught us."

Then Allah said, "O Adam, tell them their names." And Adam told them every single name, just as Allah had taught him.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

This story teaches us that talking is a gift from Allah. And when we say thank you for this gift, we use it to say kind, good words, like the words of the people of Jannah.

### Activity: The Peace Jar

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.064.activity.sieve-my-words-lagw-or-salam" -->

With your mom or dad, get an empty jar or box and draw a little flower on it together: this is your "Peace Jar."

In the afternoon or at dinner, every time you say a kind word, like "As-salamu 'alaykum," "Thank you," "Here you go," or "Alhamdulillah," your mom or dad drops a bean or a button into the jar.

And if an unkind word slips out, that's okay: say it again with a kind word, and add a bean.

Do this for three days, and watch the jar fill up.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Imagine a whole day at school where you never hear a single mocking word, no name-calling, no lies, no annoying nonstop chatter: only greetings, kind words, and laughter that doesn't hurt anyone. That is what Allah describes in Jannah: `They will hear in it no idle talk, only peace`.[^3] And He says of its people: `Their call in it will be, "Glory be to You, O Allah," and their greeting in it will be, "Peace."`[^4]

You might wonder: what language do they speak? Neither Allah nor His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, has told us that in any authentic text, and the sayings going around that claim it is Arabic are not authentic hadiths. Allah has told us what really matters to us: what their speech is like.[^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-56-25-26" -->

- **`Idle talk (laghw)`** — empty words with no benefit and no useful meaning.[^2]

- **`Sinful speech (ta'thim)`** — ugly words that make the speaker sinful, like lying and mocking.[^2]

- **`"Peace! Peace!" (qilan salaman salaman)`** — good words free of anything bad, and the people of Jannah greeting one another with peace.[^2]

- **`Tell Me (anbi'uni)`** — inform Me; let Me hear what you know.[^1]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: The First Test of Names

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-30-33" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true story from the Noble Qur'an. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-30-33" audience="8-12" -->

Allah told the angels: `I am placing a successor on the earth`. The angels asked about the wisdom behind it: how could there be someone on earth who would spread corruption and shed blood, when they themselves glorified Allah and declared Him holy? Their question was not an objection to Allah; as Ibn Kathir explains, they asked so that they could understand the wisdom. Allah said: `I know what you do not know`.

Then Allah taught Adam the names, all of them: the names of things and what they mean, big things and small. He presented the things to the angels and said: `Tell Me the names of these, if you are truthful`.

Here came the important moment. The angels didn't guess, and they didn't talk about what they didn't know. They said: `Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. You are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.` Then Allah said: `O Adam, tell them their names`. And Adam told them every one, and his special honor, and the wisdom in his creation, became clear.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

The story teaches us two things. First: words and names are a gift from Allah, and the first thing that showed how special human beings are was that Adam learned the names. Second: good manners include saying "I don't know" when we don't know. That is why we don't say, "The language of Jannah is such-and-such," because Allah hasn't told us. But we do know how to get ready for it: by making our words the kind of words spoken there.

<!-- story:end -->

### Activity: Sifting My Words

Use it for four days, then sort again for one day, and write two sentences: What changed?

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

We live in a world that never stops producing words: texts, comments, clips, quick replies. Much of it is idle talk, words that fill time and leave nothing behind. And some of it is sin: a joke that makes the whole group laugh and wounds one person.

The Qur'an describes Jannah as a place where none of that is heard: `They will hear in it no idle talk and no sinful speech, only the words: "Peace! Peace!"` As-Sa'di said this "shows the fine manners of the people of Jannah in how they address one another."[^2] As for their language, no authentic text settles it; the well-known reports that it is Arabic cannot be relied upon, and Ibn Taymiyyah is reported to have said: "It is not known what language people will speak on that Day."[^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="quran-2-30-33" -->

- **`Successor (khalifah)`** — Adam and his descendants, who succeed one another, generation after generation, in building up and tending the earth.[^1]

- **`The names, all of them (al-asma'a kullaha)`** — the names of things and their meanings, both the things themselves and their actions, according to the view Ibn Kathir judged correct.[^1]

- **`Idle talk (laghw)`** — worthless speech devoid of meaning, or carrying only a petty meaning.[^2]

- **`Without giving it a second thought (la yulqi laha balan)`** — not weighing a word, and not thinking about where it will lead.[^5]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A Story from the Qur'an: When the Angels Said, "We Have No Knowledge"

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="quran-2-30-33" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account from the Noble Qur'an. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="quran-2-30-33" audience="13+" -->

The scene opens with an announcement: `I am placing a successor on the earth`. The angels ask about the wisdom in creating a being who will spread corruption and shed blood, while they glorify Allah with His praise. Ibn Kathir makes clear that the question was neither an objection nor envy, but "a question seeking to learn and uncover the wisdom." The answer comes back in a single line: `I know what you do not know`.

Then comes the demonstration. Allah teaches Adam the names, all of them, and presents the named things to the angels: `Tell Me the names of these, if you are truthful`. It is the kind of moment that would tempt anyone to guess rather than look ignorant. But the angels said: `Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us`. As-Sa'di says that they acknowledged Allah's knowledge and wisdom, their own inability to know even the least thing, and Allah's favor upon them. Then Adam tells them the names, and the scene closes: `Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth?`[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

This story holds two lessons about speech. The first is that language itself is a gift: your ability to name, to explain, to persuade was taught to you by Allah, and you will be asked about it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said that a servant may speak a word that pleases Allah without giving it a second thought, and Allah raises him many degrees by it; and a word that angers Him, without giving it a second thought, and by it he plunges into Hell.[^5]

The second is that saying "I don't know" is strength, not weakness. On the question of Jannah's language in particular, some people love to state with confidence whatever answer pleases them, but integrity means stopping where revelation stops. We know the quality of Jannah's speech, and we do not know its language. And the quality is exactly what we can practice, starting now.

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why is it so hard for people to say "I don't know" in front of others? And what do the angels teach us about it?

2. What is the difference between the angels' question and an objection to Allah?

3. Give two examples from your own online conversations: one of idle talk and one of sinful speech.

4. Why is it a mistake of method to declare that the language of Jannah is Arabic, even when the motive is love for Arabic?

5. What word did you say this week without giving it a second thought?

### Activity: Sifting My Conversations

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.064.activity.sieve-my-words-lagw-or-salam" -->

Choose one space where you talk: a friends' group chat, lunch at school, or online multiplayer gaming.

In a private note no one else will see, sort your messages or words in that space for two days into three columns: "Peace," "Idle talk," and "Sin," without copying anyone else's words or naming anyone.

Choose the pattern that comes up most in the idle-talk or sin column, and write a replacement from the "peace" kind. For example: instead of the sarcastic comment, a message of encouragement, or silence.

Use the replacement for five days, then sort again for two days, and write a private paragraph: What changed in the columns, and in you?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.064.dua.teach-us-guard-our-tongues-only-salam" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ عَلِّمْنَا مَا يَنْفَعُنَا، وَاحْفَظْ أَلْسِنَتَنَا مِنَ اللَّغْوِ وَالْإِثْمِ، وَأَدْخِلْنَا جَنَّتَكَ الَّتِي لَا نَسْمَعُ فِيهَا إِلَّا سَلَامًا.
>
> *"O Allah, teach us what benefits us, guard our tongues from idle talk and sin, and admit us into Your Jannah, where we will hear nothing but peace."*

What it means: O Allah, teach us what benefits us, as You taught our father Adam; guard our tongues from idle talk and sin; and admit us into Your Jannah, where nothing is heard but peace. Its wording draws on the account of Adam and on the ayat of al-Waqi'ah and Maryam.[^7]

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the account of al-Baqarah 2:30-33 in order and explains what it shows: that language is taught by Allah; explains `idle talk` (*laghw*), `sinful speech` (*ta'thim*) and `only the words: "Peace! Peace!"` using the statements of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; distinguishes what is established about the quality of the speech of the people of Jannah from the naming of their language, which is not established; and begins sifting their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a handout with the five texts; a board with two columns headed "What we know through revelation" and "What we do not know"; activity sheets.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on al-Baqarah 2:30-33, al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26 and Maryam 19:62, and reviews note 6 along with the section on the language of the people of Jannah in *Hadi al-Arwah* and its editor's notes. The teacher also prepares a balanced answer to the question "Isn't Arabic the noblest of languages?": the honor of Arabic is established by the Qur'an's revelation in it, and that is a different matter from declaring with certainty what the language of Jannah is.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "If someone asked you right now what language the people of Jannah speak, what would you say, and where did you get that answer?" Write the answers down without comment, and come back to them at the end of the session.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read al-Baqarah 2:30-33 with Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (8 minutes), then al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26, Maryam 19:62 and Yunus 10:10 (7 minutes), then the hadith in al-Bukhari 6478 (5 minutes). Transfer each text to the "What we know" column.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** Present the passage "What Do We Know, and What Do We Not Know?" and ask: "What is the difference between a fabricated hadith, a Companion's statement with an abandoned narrator in its chain, and an unsourced report from a Successor?"; "Why is the angels' answer a method for questions about the unseen?"; and "How is ruling out idle talk an affirmation of good conversation, rather than a call to silence?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each learner chooses a setting, draws the "Sifting My Words" columns, and tries them out on the past hour (10 minutes); then anyone who wishes may name the type of setting they chose, without details (2 minutes). Agree on a follow-up in nine days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Exit card: "Name two established qualities of the speech of the people of Jannah with their evidence, and one thing we do not know, with the reason." Return to the opening answers, and close by reciting al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, al-Baqarah 2:31-32 and al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the commentators' views on `the names, all of them` from Ibn Kathir and at-Tabari, and to explain why Ibn Kathir preferred the general meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not depict Adam, peace be upon him, or the angels. Do not relate Isra'iliyyat (narratives drawn from earlier scriptural traditions) about how Adam was created or the names of the angels. Do not present the weak reports about the language of Jannah as established, and do not let the lesson turn into ethnic favoritism or belittling non-Arabs. No one is asked to reveal what is in their sin column.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that Allah taught Adam the names; repeats the angels' words `Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us`; says that the people of Jannah hear only kind, good words; and starts the Peace Jar.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A basket of familiar objects (a cup, a spoon, an apple, a ball, a little bowl); a jar or box; beans or large, safe buttons; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver learns the simplified story by heart and chooses buttons large enough that there is no risk of a child swallowing them.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The caregiver takes the objects out one at a time: "What's this called?" Then: "Who taught you these names? Your mom and dad. And who taught the very first person the names?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** Read the story calmly and reverently, repeating the angels' words together with the child, then share the ayah of al-Waqi'ah in its simplified meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 5 minutes:** "What did the angels say when they didn't know?" "Is it bad to say, 'I don't know'?" "What kind of words do the people of Jannah hear?" Then the child practices three kind words.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Set up the Peace Jar as described in the activity unit, try dropping in the first bean during the session, and continue at home for three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** "Who taught Adam?" and "Tell me a word from the words of Jannah." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, two kind words are enough: "As-salamu 'alaykum" and "Thank you." Ask older children to tell the story in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw Adam or the angels. Do not count a child's unkind words or scold the child for them in front of others; instead, invite the child to say them again with a kind word. Do not tell the child that Jannah has one particular language.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the four terms; puts the four scenes of the story in order; gives two pieces of evidence for the quality of the speech of the people of Jannah; explains why we do not name its language; and begins sifting their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; four scene cards (the announcement, the teaching of the names, the angels' reply, Adam telling the names); the sieve sheet; a "word of peace" card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares six short school sentences for the sorting exercise, such as "Thanks for waiting for me," "LOL, look at his shoes," and "Endless talk about nothing," without using any real names.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** A quick game: the teacher names an unusual object and asks what it is called; anyone who doesn't know says, "I don't know." Then: "Who was the first one ever to learn the names?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Explain the terms, then read the story; students put the scene cards in order; then read the ayat of Maryam and Yunus.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Students sort the six sentences into the three boxes. Then ask: "Why didn't the angels guess?" and "What do we say to someone who says, 'The language of Jannah is such-and-such'?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students draw the sieve and write their "word of peace" card as described in the activity unit, and keep it for six days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each student writes the angels' words from memory and one quality of the speech of the people of Jannah; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, two boxes are enough: "Kind" and "Not kind." Ask advanced students to explain the difference between idle talk and sinful speech with two examples.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not act out Adam or the angels. Do not use real students' names in examples of mockery. Mention Hell in the hadith calmly and without frightening anyone, keeping the focus on how a kind word raises a person's degrees.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the angels' response as a method for questions about the unseen; connects the quality of the speech of the people of Jannah with the hadith of the word that pleases Allah; distinguishes what is established from what is not about the language of Jannah; and begins sifting their own conversations.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private activity sheet; three written cases: "A group passing around a classmate's photo to make fun of him," "A chat that goes on for three hours with nothing in it," and "A student sending a word of encouragement to a friend who failed a test."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews notes 5 and 6 and prepares a balanced answer to the question "Is all joking idle talk?": honest joking that doesn't hurt anyone is not a sin; the aim is to leave what has no benefit and what involves disobedience.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "How many messages did you send today? And how many of them do you wish you could keep?" A silent personal estimate; no one shares their numbers.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read al-Baqarah 2:30-33 and al-Waqi'ah 56:25-26 from the adults' academic section, then the hadith in al-Bukhari 6478, focusing on `without giving it a second thought`.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss the three cases in groups: "Which column? And what is the peace alternative?" Then read the passage "What Do We Know, and What Do We Not Know?" and ask: "Why does a confident answer draw us in more than an honest one?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students carry out the "Sifting My Conversations" activity individually as described in the activity unit, and keep their sheets.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** A short paragraph: "How can I practice the language of Jannah when I don't know what its language is?" Nine days later, ask the students a general question, "Did you finish the second sort?", without asking for any details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For anyone who finds self-review difficult, keeping only the "Peace" column and counting what is in it is enough. Ask advanced students to summarize, from the editor's notes to *Hadi al-Arwah*, the sourcing of the reports about the language of the people of Jannah and the grading of each.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not ask anyone to show their conversations or screenshots. Do not use the cases to shame anyone. Do not act out Adam or the angels. Point out that the honor of Arabic does not justify looking down on other languages, for the diversity of tongues is one of Allah's signs.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
