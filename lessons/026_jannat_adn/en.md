# Jannat 'Adn and What Allah Created with His Hand

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Explain that `Jannat 'Adn` (often rendered "the Gardens of Eden") are the Gardens of permanent residence, on the basis of the ayah of at-Tawbah (9:72) and the hadith of Abu Musa (al-Bukhari 7444).
- Affirm the attribute of the Hand for Allah, exalted is He, just as He affirmed it for Himself in His words `whom I created with My two Hands` (Sad 38:75), while negating any likeness through His words `There is nothing like unto Him` (ash-Shura 42:11), without likening, without asking "how," and without denial.
- Distinguish, on this subject, between what is firmly established from the Prophet (`I planted their honor with My own Hand`, Muslim 189), the report from Ibn Umar about 'Adn being created by the Hand, which is his own statement (*mawquf*) with an authentic chain, and narrations attributed to the Prophet on this point that are not authentic.
- Narrate the hadith of the debate between Adam and Musa, peace be upon them both (Muslim 2652), and explain that Adam, whom Allah created with His Hand and settled in His Jannah, left it because of a sin, and that Allah then accepted his repentance (Ta Ha 20:120-122).
- Tell the difference between appealing to divine decree over a calamity that has already happened, after repentance, which is what Adam did, and appealing to divine decree to excuse sin, which is false.
- Carry out the activity "The Road Back in Adam's Footsteps" by drawing three stations on the way back from a real slip, together with a commitment not to blame again someone who has repented and apologized.
- Say the lesson's du'a before sleep, asking for a repentance like Adam's and a home in Jannat 'Adn.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannat 'Adn are the Gardens of residence. In the Arabic language, *'adn* means abiding and permanence: one says *'adana bi al-makan* when someone settles in a place to stay.[^10] Ibn al-Qayyim held that "Jannat 'Adn" is a name for the gardens of Jannah as a whole rather than for one garden among them, since all of them are a home of residence from which no one ever departs. Allah has promised them to His believing servants and paired them with His good pleasure.[^1]

A tremendous meaning is bound up with this subject: Allah, exalted is He, singled out certain of His creations to be created by His Hand, as a mark of honor for them. The Qur'an states plainly that He created Adam with His two Hands; it is authentically reported in Muslim that He planted the honor of the highest people of Jannah with His Hand; and it is authentically reported from Ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, as his own statement, that 'Adn is among the four things He created with His Hand. The position of Ahl as-Sunnah here is to affirm the Hand as a real attribute of Allah that befits His majesty: without likening it to the hands of creatures, without asking how it is, and without twisting it into a figurative meaning such as "power" or "favor."

Both meanings meet in the hadith of the debate between Adam, whom Allah created with His Hand and settled in His Jannah, and Musa, for whom Allah wrote the Torah with His Hand. Its practical lesson is this: the road to the Home of Residence is repentance, as Adam repented, not an appeal to divine decree to excuse sin.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-72" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And Fine Dwellings in Jannat 'Adn"

> **{وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتِ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِنْ تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا وَمَسَاكِنَ طَيِّبَةً فِي جَنَّاتِ عَدْنٍ ۚ وَرِضْوَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ هُوَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ}** [التوبة: ٧٢][^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Allah has promised the believing men and the believing women gardens beneath which rivers flow, to abide therein forever, and fine dwellings in Jannat 'Adn, the Gardens of permanent residence. But the good pleasure of Allah is greater still. That is the supreme triumph."** (at-Tawbah 9:72)[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators explain that "fine dwellings" are homes beautifully built and pleasant to settle in, and that *'adn* means residence. Allah then closes the ayah by declaring that His good pleasure is greater than all of it.

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah defines Jannat 'Adn by two qualities: a good home, and a residence that never ends. Then it lifts our gaze above the place itself to the good pleasure of the One to whom the place belongs.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-7444" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of Abu Musa: "In Jannat 'Adn"

> عَنْ أَبِي مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«جَنَّتَانِ مِنْ فِضَّةٍ آنِيَتُهُمَا وَمَا فِيهِمَا، وَجَنَّتَانِ مِنْ ذَهَبٍ آنِيَتُهُمَا وَمَا فِيهِمَا، وَمَا بَيْنَ الْقَوْمِ وَبَيْنَ أَنْ يَنْظُرُوا إِلَى رَبِّهِمْ إِلَّا رِدَاءُ الْكِبْرِ عَلَى وَجْهِهِ فِي جَنَّةِ عَدْنٍ»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Two gardens of silver, their vessels and everything in them; and two gardens of gold, their vessels and everything in them. And nothing stands between the people and their looking upon their Lord except the Cloak of Majesty upon His Face, in Jannat 'Adn."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nawawi said that the words "in Jannat 'Adn" refer back to those who are looking, meaning: while they are in Jannat 'Adn. Al-Bukhari placed this hadith in the Book of Tawhid to establish that the believers will see their Lord, and the attributes mentioned in it are passed on as they came, without asking how.

#### Lesson Explanation

The highest thing to be attained in Jannat 'Adn is neither gold nor silver. It is looking upon the Noble Face of Allah.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-189b" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Planted Their Honor with My Own Hand"

> عَنِ الْمُغِيرَةِ بْنِ شُعْبَةَ رضي الله عنه، يَرْفَعُهُ: **«... قَالَ: رَبِّ، فَأَعْلَاهُمْ مَنْزِلَةً؟ قَالَ: أُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ أَرَدْتُ، غَرَسْتُ كَرَامَتَهُمْ بِيَدِي، وَخَتَمْتُ عَلَيْهَا، فَلَمْ تَرَ عَيْنٌ، وَلَمْ تَسْمَعْ أُذُنٌ، وَلَمْ يَخْطُرْ عَلَى قَلْبِ بَشَرٍ»**. قَالَ: وَمِصْدَاقُهُ فِي كِتَابِ اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: {فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَا أُخْفِيَ لَهُمْ مِنْ قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ}.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of al-Mughirah ibn Shu'bah, may Allah be pleased with him, tracing it back to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: **"... He said: 'My Lord, and the highest of them in rank?' He said: 'Those are the ones I have chosen. I planted their honor with My own Hand and set a seal upon it, so that no eye has seen it, no ear has heard of it, and it has never crossed the heart of any human being.'"** He said: And its confirmation in the Book of Allah, Mighty and Majestic is He: {No soul knows what delight of the eyes has been hidden away for them}.[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

This is the closing part of the hadith in which Musa asks his Lord about the lowest and the highest people of Jannah. Its opening, the description of the lowest in rank, has been left out because it does not change the meaning of this passage. It is the most authentic text traced back to the Prophet on this subject that affirms a planting by the Hand of Allah in Jannah, and Ibn al-Qayyim opened his argument for the special excellence of what Allah planted with His Hand with this very hadith.

#### Lesson Explanation

The honor that Allah planted with His Hand is hidden from every eye, every ear, and every heart. No one can describe it; one can only work for it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="athar-ibn-umar-four-by-hand" kind="athar" mode="canonical" -->

### The Report of Ibn Umar: Four Things by His Hand

> قَالَ عَبْدُ اللَّهِ بْنُ عُمَرَ رضي الله عنهما: «خَلَقَ اللَّهُ أَرْبَعَةَ أَشْيَاءَ بِيَدِهِ: الْعَرْشَ، وَالْقَلَمَ، وَعَدْنَ، وَآدَمَ، ثُمَّ قَالَ لِسَائِرِ الْخَلْقِ: كُنْ، فَكَانَ».[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, said: "Allah created four things with His Hand: the Throne, the Pen, 'Adn, and Adam. Then to all the rest of creation He said, 'Be,' and it was."[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

These are the words of a Companion, not a hadith traced back to the Prophet. Imams of the Sunnah such as ad-Darimi cited it in affirming the Hand, and Ibn al-Qayyim made it a cornerstone of his chapter "The Lord's creating some of the gardens with His Hand, to honor them above the rest." 'Adn here means a particular garden, which Ibn al-Qayyim called "the mistress of the gardens," adding: this garden among the gardens is like Adam among living creatures.

#### Lesson Explanation

From this report we take the meaning of honor, which agrees with the hadith about planting honor with the Hand. And we stop where it stops: we do not describe how the creating took place, and we add nothing to it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-38-75" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Whom I Created with My Two Hands"

> **{قَالَ يَا إِبْلِيسُ مَا مَنَعَكَ أَنْ تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَيَّ ۖ أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ أَمْ كُنْتَ مِنَ الْعَالِينَ}** [ص: ٧٥][^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"He said: 'O Iblis, what kept you from prostrating to the one whom I created with My two Hands? Have you grown arrogant, or were you among the exalted?'"** (Sad 38:75)[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Imams of the Sunnah, such as al-Ash'ari in *al-Ibanah* and Ibn Khuzaymah in *Kitab at-Tawhid*, cited this ayah as proof that the Hand is a real attribute and not merely power. Had it meant power, Adam would have had no distinction, for everything is created by His power, even Iblis.[^11]

#### Lesson Explanation

The mention of the two Hands here is an honor for Adam, and it is the very same meaning with which the creation of 'Adn is spoken of.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-42-11" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "There Is Nothing like unto Him"

> **{فَاطِرُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۚ جَعَلَ لَكُمْ مِنْ أَنْفُسِكُمْ أَزْوَاجًا وَمِنَ الْأَنْعَامِ أَزْوَاجًا ۖ يَذْرَؤُكُمْ فِيهِ ۚ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ}** [الشورى: ١١][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"The Originator of the heavens and the earth. He has made for you mates from among yourselves, and mates among the cattle, multiplying you thereby. There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."** (ash-Shura 42:11)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The ayah unites negation and affirmation: it negates any likeness in `There is nothing like unto Him`, and it affirms the attributes in `and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing`. This is the rule of Ahl as-Sunnah for every attribute: we affirm it, and we do not liken it to anything.

#### Lesson Explanation

We do not picture the Hand of Allah, we do not ask "how," and we do not deny it. We say what Allah said, and we stop where the text stops.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2652c" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### The Hadith of the Debate Between Adam and Musa, Peace Be upon Them Both

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«احْتَجَّ آدَمُ وَمُوسَى عَلَيْهِمَا السَّلَامُ عِنْدَ رَبِّهِمَا، فَحَجَّ آدَمُ مُوسَى. قَالَ مُوسَى: أَنْتَ آدَمُ الَّذِي خَلَقَكَ اللَّهُ بِيَدِهِ، وَنَفَخَ فِيكَ مِنْ رُوحِهِ، وَأَسْجَدَ لَكَ مَلَائِكَتَهُ، وَأَسْكَنَكَ فِي جَنَّتِهِ، ثُمَّ أَهْبَطْتَ النَّاسَ بِخَطِيئَتِكَ إِلَى الْأَرْضِ؟ فَقَالَ آدَمُ: أَنْتَ مُوسَى الَّذِي اصْطَفَاكَ اللَّهُ بِرِسَالَتِهِ وَبِكَلَامِهِ، وَأَعْطَاكَ الْأَلْوَاحَ فِيهَا تِبْيَانُ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ، وَقَرَّبَكَ نَجِيًّا، فَبِكَمْ وَجَدْتَ اللَّهَ كَتَبَ التَّوْرَاةَ قَبْلَ أَنْ أُخْلَقَ؟ قَالَ مُوسَى: بِأَرْبَعِينَ عَامًا. قَالَ آدَمُ: فَهَلْ وَجَدْتَ فِيهَا: {وَعَصَىٰ آدَمُ رَبَّهُ فَغَوَىٰ}؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ. قَالَ: أَفَتَلُومُنِي عَلَى أَنْ عَمِلْتُ عَمَلًا كَتَبَهُ اللَّهُ عَلَيَّ أَنْ أَعْمَلَهُ قَبْلَ أَنْ يَخْلُقَنِي بِأَرْبَعِينَ سَنَةً؟»** قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«فَحَجَّ آدَمُ مُوسَى»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Adam and Musa, peace be upon them both, argued their case before their Lord, and Adam got the better of Musa in argument. Musa said: 'You are Adam, whom Allah created with His Hand, into whom He breathed of His spirit, before whom He made His angels prostrate, and whom He settled in His Jannah. And then, through your sin, you brought mankind down to the earth?' Adam said: 'You are Musa, whom Allah chose for His message and for His speech, to whom He gave the Tablets in which everything is made clear, and whom He drew near in intimate converse. How long before I was created did you find that Allah had written the Torah?' Musa said: 'Forty years.' Adam said: 'And did you find in it: {And Adam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray}?' He said: 'Yes.' Adam said: 'Do you then blame me for doing a deed that Allah had written for me to do forty years before He created me?'"** The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"And so Adam got the better of Musa in argument."**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Hajjahu* means "he overcame him by argument." In another narration of the same hadith, the wording is: "Allah chose you for His speech, and wrote for you with His Hand."[^7] The scholars explain that Adam did not appeal to divine decree to argue that the sin was permissible. He had repented of it, and Allah had accepted his repentance, and there is no ground for blame after repentance. Musa's blame was really about the calamity, which was leaving Jannah, and Adam referred that calamity back to what Allah had decreed. Appealing to divine decree over calamities is legitimate.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

Two prophets, each honored by something Allah did with His Hand: Adam was created by it, and the Torah was written for Musa by it. The dialogue teaches us not to reopen a sin that its doer has already repented of, and not to make divine decree an excuse for a sin we have not repented of.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-20-120-122" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### The Tree of Eternity, Then Repentance

> **{فَوَسْوَسَ إِلَيْهِ الشَّيْطَانُ قَالَ يَا آدَمُ هَلْ أَدُلُّكَ عَلَىٰ شَجَرَةِ الْخُلْدِ وَمُلْكٍ لَا يَبْلَىٰ * فَأَكَلَا مِنْهَا فَبَدَتْ لَهُمَا سَوْآتُهُمَا وَطَفِقَا يَخْصِفَانِ عَلَيْهِمَا مِنْ وَرَقِ الْجَنَّةِ ۚ وَعَصَىٰ آدَمُ رَبَّهُ فَغَوَىٰ * ثُمَّ اجْتَبَاهُ رَبُّهُ فَتَابَ عَلَيْهِ وَهَدَىٰ}** [طه: ١٢٠-١٢٢][^8]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"But Satan whispered to him, saying: 'O Adam, shall I show you the Tree of Eternity, and a kingdom that never decays?' So the two of them ate from it, and their nakedness became apparent to them, and they began to cover themselves with leaves from the Garden. And Adam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray. Then his Lord chose him, turned to him in mercy, and guided him."** (Ta Ha 20:120-122)[^8]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir mentions that Satan made eating from the tree look attractive to Adam by promising him eternal life and a kingdom that would last forever. Allah then tells us that He chose Adam after that, accepted his repentance, and guided him.

#### Lesson Explanation

Satan promised Adam eternity by way of disobedience. True eternity, in the Home of Residence, is reached only by way of obedience and repentance.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The texts about 'Adn introduce the Home itself. The texts about the Hand show how Allah honored some of His creation, and they set the bounds for how we understand that. The hadith of Adam and Musa, together with the ayat of Ta Ha, carries the meaning over into action: the one who was honored left through a slip, and was chosen once more through repentance.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the linguistic evidence that Jannat 'Adn are Gardens of residence?
2. Why is it not valid to interpret the Hand in `whom I created with My two Hands` as power?
3. How do `I planted their honor with My own Hand` and the report of Ibn Umar differ in their standing as proof?
4. Why was Adam's argument accepted, while a sinner's claim, "This was decreed for me," is not?
5. When have you gone back to blaming someone for a mistake they had already repented of or apologized for?

### Activity: The Road Back in Adam's Footsteps

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.026.activity.adam-return-route" -->

Draw a road with three stations, ending at a door labeled "The Home of Residence." Choose one real slip from this month that you will not share with anyone, and at each station write: (1) a frank admission, with no excuses drawn from divine decree or circumstances; (2) a plea for forgiveness, with remorse and resolve; (3) a specific good deed to set against it within a week. Then, beneath the road, write the name of someone who apologized to you for an old mistake, and a commitment never to blame them for it again. Review the sheet after a week and check off every station you have completed.

## For Children Ages 4 to 7

<!-- reader:start audience="4-7" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In Jannah there are gardens called "Jannat 'Adn." The word *'adn* means staying, living somewhere forever. Whoever goes in stays there always, and never, ever has to leave.

Allah told us that He created our father Adam with His Hand.[^5] Allah has a Hand that suits His greatness. It is not like our hands, and we don't try to picture it in our minds. We say: Allah told us this, so we believe Him, and we love Him.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="4-7.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: Adam and Musa, Peace Be upon Them Both

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="muslim-2652c" authenticated="true" -->

**This is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us. It is not a made-up story.**

Allah created Adam with His Hand. He told the angels to bow down to him, and they did, and He gave him a home in Jannah.[^7] Then Shaytan came and told Adam a lie: "Eat from this tree, and you'll stay in Jannah forever!" Adam ate from it, and that was disobeying what his Lord had told him.[^8] So Adam came down from Jannah to the earth.[^7]

Then Adam turned back to his Lord, sorry for what he had done, and Allah accepted his "I'm sorry." Allah chose him and guided him.[^8]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2652c" audience="4-7" -->

Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, also told us that Musa, peace be upon him, met Adam before their Lord. Musa said to him: "You are Adam, whom Allah created with His Hand and gave a home in His Jannah. And then, because of your mistake, people came down to the earth?" Adam said: "Musa, didn't you find in the Torah that this was written down with Allah before I was even created?" Musa said: "Yes." And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: Adam won the argument with his answer.[^7]

The scholars explain: Adam had already said sorry to Allah, and Allah had forgiven him. So we don't keep blaming someone for a mistake they have already said sorry for.[^9]

### Bedtime Du'a

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Allah has promised the believers `fine dwellings in Jannat 'Adn`, and *'adn* means living somewhere permanently: beautiful homes that no one is ever thrown out of and no one ever has to move away from.[^1]

Allah honored some of His creation by creating them with His Hand, and first among them is our father Adam. Allah, exalted is He, says: `whom I created with My two Hands`.[^5] We believe that Allah has a real Hand that befits Him and is not like the hands of any created thing, because Allah says: `There is nothing like unto Him`.[^6] We don't draw it, we don't try to picture it, and we don't ask, "How?" The scholars also report that the Companion Abdullah ibn Umar, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, said that Allah created the Garden of 'Adn with His Hand as well. This is Ibn Umar's own statement, not a hadith from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.[^4]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2652c" -->

- **`Permanent residence ('adn)`** — staying for good in a place that no one ever leaves.[^10]
- **`To argue one's case (ihtajja)`** — to debate using proof and evidence; "Adam got the better of Musa in argument" (*hajja Adamu Musa*) means Adam won with his answer.
- **`Divine decree (al-qadar)`** — Allah's knowledge of everything, and His writing it down before it happens.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: When Adam and Musa Met, Peace Be upon Them Both

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="muslim-2652c" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih Muslim. It is not a made-up story.**

Allah gave Adam a home in Jannah. Then Shaytan whispered to him: `Shall I show you the Tree of Eternity, and a kingdom that never decays?` In other words: eat from it, and you'll stay in Jannah forever. So Adam and his wife ate from it, and that was disobeying what their Lord had commanded. Then Adam came down to the earth. But the story didn't end with the mistake: `Then his Lord chose him, turned to him in mercy, and guided him`.[^8]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2652c" audience="8-12" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that Adam and Musa, peace be upon them both, once debated before their Lord. Musa said: "You are Adam, whom Allah created with His Hand, into whom He breathed of His spirit, before whom He made His angels bow down, and whom He settled in His Jannah. And then, through your sin, you brought people down to the earth?" Adam answered: "And you are Musa, whom Allah chose for His message and for His speech, and to whom He gave the Tablets. How long before I was created did you find that Allah had written the Torah?" Musa said: "Forty years." Adam said: "And did you find in it `And Adam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray`?" He said: "Yes." Adam said: "Then do you blame me for a deed that Allah wrote for me forty years before He created me?" And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"And so Adam got the better of Musa in argument,"** meaning Adam won with his answer.[^7]

<!-- retelling:end -->

So why did Adam win? The scholars explain that Adam had already repented and Allah had forgiven him, so he was not to be blamed after that; and that what Musa blamed him for was leaving Jannah, which was a calamity that Allah had decreed. But someone who disobeys Allah and then says, "It was written for me," without repenting, has an argument that is worthless.[^9]

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. How had Allah honored Adam, according to what Musa said in the hadith?
2. What did Shaytan promise Adam? And where is the real "forever" found?
3. Why can't a student who skipped their homework say, "It was written for me"?
4. A teammate apologized for a mistake, but the others keep bringing it up every day. What would you say to them?

<!-- unit:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannat 'Adn is "the Home of Residence": fine dwellings that no one ever leaves, and above them, good pleasure from Allah that is greater still.[^1] This topic touches a subtle point of belief. It is authentically established that Allah created Adam with His Hand (Sad 38:75) and that He planted the honor of the highest people of Jannah with His Hand (Muslim 189), and it is authentically reported that Ibn Umar said 'Adn is among the things Allah created with His Hand.[^3][^4] The method of Ahl as-Sunnah here is clear: we affirm what Allah affirmed for Himself; we deny that He resembles His creation, `There is nothing like unto Him`; we do not ask how; and we do not twist the meaning into "power" to dodge what the text says.[^6][^11]

You may notice that some widely shared clips relate detailed hadiths about 'Adn, its bricks, and its speaking, and much of that is not authentically from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Integrity means telling apart an authentic hadith, a Companion's statement, and a weak narration before you share anything.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2652c" -->

- **`The attribute of the Hand (sifat al-yad)`** — an attribute of Allah established by the Qur'an and the Sunnah, which we affirm in the way that befits Him, without likening it, without asking how, and without denying it.[^11]
- **`A Companion's report (al-athar al-mawquf)`** — a statement attributed to a Companion rather than to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and cited along with its grade.[^4]
- **`Appealing to divine decree (al-ihtijaj bi al-qadar)`** — arguing that something was already written; valid for calamities, never valid as an excuse for sins.[^9]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: The Dialogue of Adam and Musa, Peace Be upon Them Both

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="other_prophets" source_id="muslim-2652c" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih Muslim. It is not an imagined scene.**

It started with a false promise. Satan said to Adam: `Shall I show you the Tree of Eternity, and a kingdom that never decays?` He was offering him eternity through a forbidden shortcut. Adam ate, and that was disobeying his Lord's command. Then came the turning point: `Then his Lord chose him, turned to him in mercy, and guided him`.[^8]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2652c" audience="13+" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, went on to tell us that Adam and Musa debated before their Lord. Musa began by listing the honors given to Adam: "Allah created you with His Hand, breathed into you of His spirit, made His angels prostrate before you, and settled you in His Jannah. And then, through your sin, you brought mankind down to the earth!" Adam replied by listing the honors given to Musa: "Allah chose you for His message and for His speech, gave you the Tablets in which everything is made clear, and drew you near in intimate converse." Then he asked him: "How long before I was created did you find that Allah had written the Torah?" Musa said: "Forty years." Adam said: "And did you find in it `And Adam disobeyed his Lord, and went astray`?" He said: "Yes." Adam said: "Do you then blame me for a deed that Allah wrote for me forty years before He created me?" The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"And so Adam got the better of Musa in argument."**[^7]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Look closely at the dialogue. Two prophets, and each one opens by naming the other's virtues before the debate even begins. Notice, too, that Adam never said, "The sin was written, so it's fine." He had repented of it, with a repentance Allah accepted. Musa's blame was really about the calamity, mankind's leaving Jannah, and Adam referred that back to what Allah had written. That is why someone who persists in a sin cannot say, "This is my destiny," while someone who has repented may rightly be at peace with what befell him, and we, for our part, should not blame him after his repentance.[^9]

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did each of the two prophets begin by mentioning the other's honor? What does that teach you about how to disagree?
2. What is the difference between saying "Allah decreed it, and what He willed, He did" after a calamity, and saying "It was written for me" to excuse an ongoing sin?
3. How would you handle a post that attributes details about 'Adn to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, when you haven't verified them?
4. How does the promise of the "Tree of Eternity" resemble today's promises of quick success through a forbidden route?
5. Do you sometimes keep reminding a friend or relative of a mistake they apologized for? What will you change?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Road Back in Adam's Footsteps

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.026.activity.adam-return-route" -->

On a private sheet that no one else will see, draw a road with three stations ending at a gate marked "The Home of Residence." Choose a real slip that keeps coming back (with your phone, your tongue, your prayer, or your family), and write: (1) an admission, without the words "it's my destiny" or "everyone does it"; (2) what you will say to Allah when you ask His forgiveness, and whom you need to apologize to; (3) a good deed to replace it, which you start today and keep up for seven days. At the bottom of the road, write the name of someone who apologized to you, and a commitment not to reopen their mistake in front of others. After a week, shade in the stations you have completed.

<!-- activity:end -->

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the meaning of Jannat 'Adn both linguistically and in the Shari'ah; affirms the attribute of the Hand according to the method of Ahl as-Sunnah, citing Sad 38:75 and ash-Shura 42:11; distinguishes between the authentic text traced back to the Prophet (Muslim 189), the Companion's statement (the report of Ibn Umar), and the weak narrations attributed to the Prophet about the creation of 'Adn; explains why Adam's argument prevailed over Musa without legitimizing an appeal to divine decree to excuse sins; and completes the "Road Back" map.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf; a printed sheet with the eight texts; blank paper and pens for the activity; a board divided into three columns: "Authentic, traced to the Prophet / Companion's statement with an authentic chain / Attributed to the Prophet but not authentic."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads Chapter Twenty-Three of Ibn al-Qayyim's *Hadi al-Arwah*, together with its editors' notes tracing the reports, as well as an-Nawawi's commentary and *Fath al-Bari* on the hadith of the debate between Adam and Musa. The teacher also prepares two examples of widely circulated weak narrations about 'Adn (the narration from Anas, "a brick of white pearl...," and the narration from Anas, "He built al-Firdaws with His Hand") in order to explain why they are not relied upon.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "What is the difference between something a company turns out by the thousand and something given special, personal care?" and then immediately cautions: "This is only an illustration of what honoring means among us. As for Allah, `There is nothing like unto Him`, and we do not measure His actions against ours."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the ayah of at-Tawbah 9:72 and the hadith of Abu Musa (4 minutes); then the hadith of al-Mughirah, the report of Ibn Umar, and the ayat of Sad and ash-Shura (8 minutes), sorting each text into the appropriate column on the board; then read the hadith of Adam and Musa in full, along with Ta Ha 20:120-122 (8 minutes), pausing at "And so Adam got the better of Musa in argument."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 15 minutes:** Key questions: "Why is power not a valid interpretation of 'My two Hands'?" and "If someone who drinks alcohol said, 'Allah wrote it for me,' would his words be like Adam's? What is the difference?" The teacher sums up the two answers: the repentance came first, and the blame was over the calamity, not the sin. Then discuss the promise of the "Tree of Eternity" set against "Jannat 'Adn" as two roads to everlasting life.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Learners carry out "The Road Back in Adam's Footsteps" individually and in silence (10 minutes); then anyone who wishes shares only the general idea, without details of the sin (5 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** An exit card with two questions: "Describe the method of Ahl as-Sunnah regarding the attribute of the Hand in one line," and "Name one difference between Adam's argument and that of a sinner who persists in his sin." Close by reciting Ta Ha 20:122.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, it is enough to cover the ayat of Sad and ash-Shura and the hadith of Adam and Musa. Advanced learners are asked to trace the report of Ibn Umar briefly in ad-Darimi and al-Hakim, and to compare an-Nawawi's reading of "in Jannat 'Adn" with other views.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Use no physical comparisons for the Hand, do not draw it, and do not gesture toward it with your own hand; and do not slip into interpreting it as power or favor. Do not present the report of Ibn Umar as a Prophetic hadith, and do not use weak narrations to describe 'Adn. Do not allow the hadith to be used to argue for fatalism or to lift moral responsibility. Do not ask anyone to disclose their sins.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that *'adn* means staying forever; retells in two sentences that Adam made a mistake, then repented, and Allah forgave him; and says "I forgive you" to someone who apologizes.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet showing a road with three circles and a door; colors; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver learns the simplified story by heart and prepares a short real-life moment from the child's own life in which the child apologized or someone apologized to the child.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** "Would you like to stay in a beautiful place and never have to leave? In Jannah there are gardens called Jannat 'Adn, and that means staying forever."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the simplified story calmly and reverently, pausing at the line "he went back to his Lord and asked for forgiveness."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 5 minutes:** "What did Adam do after his mistake? Was Musa upset with him because he didn't love him? No, they were both noble prophets. And when someone says sorry to us, we forgive them."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** The child does the road, circles, and heart activity as described in the activity unit; it can be finished at home.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What do we say when we make a mistake?" and "What do we say to someone who says sorry?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, the first circle and the heart are enough. Older children are asked to tell the story in their own words.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never tell the child "Allah's Hand is like...," and never ask the child to draw it or imagine it. If the child asks, "What is Allah's Hand like?" answer calmly: "We don't know how. There is nothing like Allah, and we believe what He told us." Do not depict Adam or Musa in drawings or role-play. Do not load the child with blame toward Adam, or the feeling that our being outside Jannah is his fault.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the meaning of *'adn*, *ihtijaj* (arguing one's case), and *qadar* (divine decree); retells the dialogue in order; explains why "It was written for me" is no excuse for negligence; and completes a Road Back map for a real mistake.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards for the three terms; the activity sheet; colored pencils; two cards labeled "Calamity" and "Sin."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares three short school scenarios for sorting (failing a test after working hard; neglecting homework; a classmate who apologized while the others kept reminding them of it).

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "If someone promised you that you could stay forever in a beautiful place if you disobeyed your parents, would you believe him?" Then link this to Shaytan's promise to Adam.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Explain the terms, then read the whole story. Two students read the dialogue aloud in two voices, one reading Musa's words and the other Adam's, without acting out either person.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Sort the three scenarios under the "Calamity" and "Sin" cards, and ask: "In which one is it right to say, 'Allah decreed it'? And in which one must we say, 'I got it wrong, and I repent'?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Students carry out the Road Back activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence they will say to someone who apologizes to them; then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, present the story on three cards in order. Advanced students are asked to explain Sad 38:75 and ash-Shura 42:11 together in two sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Point out that Ibn Umar's statement about the creation of 'Adn is a Companion's statement, not a Prophetic hadith. Use no physical comparisons for the Hand. Leave the choice of the mistake to be written down to the student, and do not show it to the class.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen applies the method of affirmation without likening, without asking how, and without denial to the attribute of the Hand; distinguishes between an authentic hadith, a Companion's statement, and a weak narration in widely shared content; analyzes Adam's argument and rejects using divine decree to justify sin; and completes a private seven-day Road Back.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a private activity sheet; three printed statements made to look like shared posts: one from an authentic hadith, one a Companion's statement, and one a weak narration about 'Adn.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews how the narrations are traced in the editors' notes to *Hadi al-Arwah*, and prepares brief answers to questions about divine decree and free choice: Allah's prior knowledge does not force the servant; the servant chooses and is held accountable.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** "'It was meant to be.' When is that phrase faith in divine decree, and when is it running away from responsibility?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayat of Ta Ha, then the full hadith from the academic section, then the report of Ibn Umar and the hadith of al-Mughirah. Students then sort the three posts by their grade.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss: the etiquette of disagreement between the two prophets; the difference between a calamity and a sin; and why the "Tree of Eternity" is a model for every promise of a forbidden shortcut.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Students carry out the Road Back activity individually as described in the activity unit, and keep the sheet themselves.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** A short paragraph: "How can I believe in divine decree and take responsibility for my actions at the same time?" After a week, ask the students a general question, "Did you complete your stations?", without asking for details.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For those who find the point of belief heavy going, the rule of ash-Shura 42:11 is enough. Advanced students are asked to summarize Ibn al-Qayyim's discussion of the difference between appealing to divine decree over calamities and over misdeeds.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Avoid open-ended theological debate about the divine attributes in front of beginners, and refer in-depth questions to a private session. Do not ask anyone to disclose their sins. Gently remind anyone struggling with an excessive sense of guilt that Ta Ha 20:122 is a door of hope, and follow up individually when needed.
