# Companionship and Visiting Among the People of Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Retell, in order, the hadith of the man who visited his brother in another town (Sahih Muslim 2567): the road, the angel's question, the answer "I love him for the sake of Allah," and the glad tidings of Allah's love.
- Explain the hadith's key words (*arsada*, *al-madrajah*, *ni'mah tarubbuha*) from the commentaries of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi's *al-Mufhim*, and show that the praiseworthy visit is the one stripped of worldly motives.
- Cite the ayah "Close friends on that Day will be enemies to one another, except the God-fearing" as proof that only companionship for the sake of Allah lasts, as Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di explain it.
- Explain the promise of companionship in the ayah "And how excellent are they as companions!" and the hadith "You will be with those you love" (al-Bukhari 3688), and reconcile being together in Jannah with the difference in its ranks.
- Quote Ibn al-Qayyim's statement on the people of Jannah visiting one another, and distinguish what is established from the detailed reports the lesson does not rely on because they are weak or disputed.
- Carry out the "Question on the Road" activity: reviewing one's companionships, answering the angel's question honestly before one visit made for Allah's sake with nothing to gain from it, and then writing down its effect.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Many of our relationships rest on mutual benefit: a colleague we need at work, a neighbor we trade favors with, a friend we share a hobby with. There is nothing wrong with that, but it raises a question: which of these ties will still be standing when every benefit has run out? The Qur'an answers that on the Day of Resurrection friendship turns into enmity, except the friendship of the God-fearing; it promises those who obey the company of the righteous in Jannah; and the Sunnah tells the story of a man who set out to see his brother with no purpose but love for the sake of Allah.

This lesson deals with companionship and visiting in Jannah, and with what leads to them in this world. The removal of rancor from the hearts, the market of Jannah, the gatherings and conversations of its people, and family and offspring each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2567" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "I Love Him for the Sake of Allah"

> عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ: **«أَنَّ رَجُلًا زَارَ أَخًا لَهُ فِي قَرْيَةٍ أُخْرَى، فَأَرْصَدَ اللَّهُ لَهُ عَلَى مَدْرَجَتِهِ مَلَكًا، فَلَمَّا أَتَى عَلَيْهِ قَالَ: أَيْنَ تُرِيدُ؟ قَالَ: أُرِيدُ أَخًا لِي فِي هَذِهِ الْقَرْيَةِ. قَالَ: هَلْ لَكَ عَلَيْهِ مِنْ نِعْمَةٍ تَرُبُّهَا؟ قَالَ: لَا، غَيْرَ أَنِّي أَحْبَبْتُهُ فِي اللَّهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ. قَالَ: فَإِنِّي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ إِلَيْكَ بِأَنَّ اللَّهَ قَدْ أَحَبَّكَ كَمَا أَحْبَبْتَهُ فِيهِ»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: **"A man set out to visit a brother of his in another town, and Allah posted an angel to watch for him along his road. When the man came up to him, the angel asked, 'Where are you headed?' He said, 'To a brother of mine in this town.' The angel asked, 'Is there some favor of yours with him that you are going to tend?' He said, 'No; it is only that I love him for the sake of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic.' The angel said, 'Then I am a messenger from Allah to you, to tell you that Allah loves you as you have loved him for His sake.'"**[^1]

#### Scholarly Commentary

An-Nawawi says: "The meaning of *arsadahu* is that He seated him to watch for him"; that "*al-madrajah*... is the road, so called because people *yadrujun* along it, that is, they pass and walk along it"; and that *tarubbuha* means "you undertake to maintain it, and you go to him on account of it." He adds: "This hadith shows the merit of love for the sake of Allah the Exalted, and that it is a cause of Allah the Exalted loving His servant; and it shows the merit of visiting the righteous and one's companions."[^2] Al-Qurtubi says of the man's answer: "That is, I did not visit him for any worldly purpose," and: "Love for the sake of Allah and visiting one another for His sake are among the best of deeds and the greatest means of drawing near to Him, when they are free of worldly purposes and the cravings of the self."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The angel did not ask the man how long the road was or how hard. He asked about the motive: is there some interest of yours with this man that you are going to look after or collect on? When the man denied it and named love for Allah's sake instead, the glad tidings came in the very shape of his deed: Allah loves you as you loved him for His sake. The question settled on that road was a question of intention, and the visit itself is the earthly form of what the believer hopes for: a companionship that lasts.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-43-67-68" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "Except the God-Fearing"

> **{الْأَخِلَّاءُ يَوْمَئِذٍ بَعْضُهُمْ لِبَعْضٍ عَدُوٌّ إِلَّا الْمُتَّقِينَ ۝ يَا عِبَادِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ وَلَا أَنْتُمْ تَحْزَنُونَ}** [الزخرف: ٦٧-٦٨][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Close friends on that Day will be enemies to one another, except the God-fearing. 'O My servants, there is no fear upon you this Day, nor will you grieve.'"** (az-Zukhruf 43:67-68)[^3]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Ibn Kathir says: "That is, every friendship and companionship for other than Allah turns on the Day of Resurrection into enmity, except what was for Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, for that endures as He endures." As-Sa'di says: "Because their close friendship and love in this world were for other than Allah, they turned into enmity on the Day of Resurrection. 'Except the God-fearing,' those who guarded against shirk and sins: their love endures and remains unbroken, as the One for whose sake they loved endures."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

The ayah measures a friendship by its cause, not its strength. A friendship can be very strong and still turn into enmity, because what it was built on has fallen away. The exception, "except the God-fearing," opens the way to the next ayah, in which the people of taqwa are called and assured that they are safe from fear and grief.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-4-69-70" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And How Excellent Are They as Companions!"

> **{وَمَنْ يُطِعِ اللَّهَ وَالرَّسُولَ فَأُولَٰئِكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ ۚ وَحَسُنَ أُولَٰئِكَ رَفِيقًا ۝ ذَٰلِكَ الْفَضْلُ مِنَ اللَّهِ ۚ وَكَفَىٰ بِاللَّهِ عَلِيمًا}** [النساء: ٦٩-٧٠][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be with those on whom Allah has bestowed His favor: the prophets, the steadfast affirmers of truth, the martyrs and the righteous. And how excellent are they as companions! That is the bounty from Allah, and Allah suffices as the All-Knowing."** (an-Nisa' 4:69-70)[^4]

#### Scholarly Tafsir

Ibn Kathir says: "Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, will settle him in the Abode of His Honor and make him a companion of the prophets, and then of those below them in rank." As-Sa'di says: "Everyone who obeys Allah and His Messenger according to his circumstances and the measure of what is required of him, male and female, young and old," and then: "So everyone who obeys Allah the Exalted will be with these in their company, 'and how excellent are they as companions,' by being gathered with them in the Gardens of Bliss and enjoying the closeness of their company in the neighborhood of the Lord of the worlds."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

Companionship here is the fruit of obedience, and it is "the bounty from Allah," not something the servant has earned as a right. As-Sa'di's words extend the promise to everyone who obeys as far as they are able, man or woman, young or old.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-3688" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "You Will Be with Those You Love"

> عَنْ أَنَسٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ: أَنَّ رَجُلًا سَأَلَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ عَنِ السَّاعَةِ، فَقَالَ: مَتَى السَّاعَةُ؟ قَالَ: **«وَمَاذَا أَعْدَدْتَ لَهَا؟»** قَالَ: لَا شَيْءَ، إِلَّا أَنِّي أُحِبُّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ ﷺ. فَقَالَ: **«أَنْتَ مَعَ مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ»**. قَالَ أَنَسٌ: فَمَا فَرِحْنَا بِشَيْءٍ فَرَحَنَا بِقَوْلِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ: «أَنْتَ مَعَ مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ». قَالَ أَنَسٌ: فَأَنَا أُحِبُّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ وَأَبَا بَكْرٍ وَعُمَرَ، وَأَرْجُو أَنْ أَكُونَ مَعَهُمْ بِحُبِّي إِيَّاهُمْ، وَإِنْ لَمْ أَعْمَلْ بِمِثْلِ أَعْمَالِهِمْ.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Anas, may Allah be pleased with him: A man asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, about the Hour. He said, "When is the Hour?" The Prophet replied: **"And what have you prepared for it?"** The man said, "Nothing, except that I love Allah and His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him." He said: **"You will be with those you love."** Anas said: "Nothing ever made us as happy as we were at the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him, 'You will be with those you love.'" Anas said: "So I love the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and Abu Bakr and 'Umar, and I hope to be with them because of my love for them, even though I have not done deeds like theirs."[^5]

#### Scholarly Commentary

Al-'Ayni raises an objection: "The ranks differ, so how can Anas be at the rank of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and with him?" And he answers: "What is meant is being together in Jannah, that is: I hope to be in the Abode of Reward, not of punishment."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

Being "with" someone, then, does not mean holding the same rank; it means being gathered in a single abode. Anas's hope, may Allah be pleased with him, is a humble hope, not a claim. The ayah of an-Nisa' ties companionship to obedience, and the hadith ties it to love. The way to bring them together, and the understanding this lesson takes, is that sincere love drives the lover to follow the one he loves; it does not excuse him from acting.

<!-- evidence:end -->

Ibn al-Qayyim, in his chapter on the people of Jannah visiting one another, says: "The people of Jannah visit one another there, and invite one another to visit, and by this their delight and joy are made complete."[^7] As for the details of these visits, such as mounts they ride or couches that carry their occupants along, the reports that describe them are not relied upon in this lesson, because their chains are weak or their status is disputed.[^8] So we affirm the core truth of companionship and gathering, and refrain from asserting how it happens beyond what is established.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why did the angel ask about "some favor of yours that you are going to tend" rather than about the hardship of the road? And what does that question reveal about the motives behind our own visits?
2. How do Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di explain friendship turning into enmity on the Day of Resurrection? And what makes the companionship of the God-fearing "endure as He endures," the One for whose sake it was formed?
3. How do you reconcile the ayah of an-Nisa', which ties companionship to obedience, with the hadith "You will be with those you love," which ties it to love?
4. What do we affirm about the people of Jannah visiting one another, and where do we hold back? Why?
5. If someone asked you today about the last visit you made, "Is there some favor of yours with him that you are going to tend?", what would your honest answer be?

### Activity: The Question on the Road

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.069.activity.the-question-on-the-road" -->

Write down the names of the people you spend the most time with each week, at home, at work, at the masjid and online. Next to each name, write your honest answer to the angel's question, "Is there some interest of mine with this person that I am tending?", and then the direction your time together takes you: it brings you both closer to Allah, it neither brings you closer nor takes you further away, or it takes you further away. Choose one person of good character with whom you have no interest at stake, and within seven days pay them a visit in which you want nothing from them, or call them if a visit is not possible, and gently tell them that you love them for the sake of Allah. Afterward, write three lines: What was said? How did you feel? Which tie on your list needs its intention reviewed?

### A True Hadith: The Man Who Visited His Brother

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2567" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. Muslim reported it in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2567" audience="4-7" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about a man who had a brother he loved. His brother lived in another town, far away from him.[^1]

So the man set off, walking down the road to visit his brother.[^1]

Allah sent an angel to wait for him on the road. When the man reached him, the angel asked him, "Where are you going?"[^1]

The man said, "I'm going to visit a brother of mine in this town."[^1]

Then the angel asked him, "Does he have something you want to get from him? Are you going there to get something for yourself?"[^1][^2]

The man said, "No. I just love him for Allah."[^1]

So the angel said, "I am a messenger from Allah to you. I came to tell you that Allah loves you, just as you loved your brother for Him."[^1]

What wonderful good news! The man visited his brother because he loved him for Allah, and so Allah loved him.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

'Umar was sick for a whole week and missed school. One classmate called him to ask about the homework, because he needed 'Umar's notebook, and then never called again. Another classmate came over with his dad, carrying a little drawing he had made for him. He sat with 'Umar for a while and then said, "I missed you." Both of them got in touch with him. But which visit stayed in 'Umar's heart?

Allah has told us that friendships change on the Day of Resurrection: "Close friends on that Day will be enemies to one another, except the God-fearing."[^3] That means friends will turn against one another, except for the friendship that was for Allah's sake; that one lasts. And He promised that whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger will be with the righteous in Jannah: "And how excellent are they as companions!"[^4] In an authentic hadith, we see a visit with nothing to gain from it, and Allah rewarded the visitor with His love.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2567" -->

- **`Posted (arsada)`** — placed someone at a spot to wait and watch for a person.[^2]
- **`His road (madrajatuhu)`** — the road he was walking along.[^2]
- **`A favor you are going to tend (ni'mah tarubbuha)`** — some interest of yours, or a favor you once did, that you are going to him to look after.[^2]
- **`Close friends (al-akhilla')`** — your closest friends; the singular is *khalil*.[^3]

### A True Hadith: A Question on the Road

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2567" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. Muslim reported it in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2567" audience="8-12" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, reports that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told of a man who went to visit a brother of his who lived in another town.[^1] This was no visit to the house next door: he had to leave his own town and walk the road to a different one.

Allah had posted an angel on his road; in other words, He had set the angel there to wait for him.[^1][^2] When the man reached him, the angel asked, "Where are you headed?" He answered, "To a brother of mine in this town."[^1]

Then came the big question: "Is there some favor of yours with him that you are going to tend?"[^1] In other words: do you have some interest with him, or a favor you did him, that you are going to look after? It was as if the angel were asking, "Is there something behind this visit that you want to get out of it?"

The man answered, "No; it is only that I love him for the sake of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic."[^1] He wanted no money from his brother and needed nothing from him. He simply loved him for Allah.

So the angel said, "Then I am a messenger from Allah to you, to tell you that Allah loves you as you have loved him for His sake."[^1]

The scholars say this hadith shows how precious it is to love someone for Allah's sake, that this love is a reason for Allah to love His servant, and how precious it is to visit righteous people and friends.[^2] And friendship for Allah's sake is the kind that lasts on the Day of Resurrection, and the people who share it will be together in Jannah.[^3][^4]

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Your contacts list holds hundreds of names, and your group chats dozens of faces. Some of them only message you when they need something. Some you stick with because pulling away would be awkward. And some make you better without saying much at all. The question most of us avoid is this: why am I with these people? And where is my time with them taking me?

The Qur'an measures a friendship by its cause: "Close friends on that Day will be enemies to one another, except the God-fearing."[^3] Ibn Kathir explains that every friendship for other than Allah turns into enmity on the Day of Resurrection, "except what was for Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, for that endures as He endures."[^3] And Allah promises those who obey Him the company of the righteous: "And how excellent are they as companions!"[^4] The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said to a man who could name no deed of his except his love for Allah and His Messenger: **"You will be with those you love,"** and Anas said that "nothing ever made us as happy" as those words did.[^5]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2567" -->

- **`A favor you are going to tend (ni'mah tarubbuha)`** — a favor or interest of yours with someone that you work to maintain, and that you go to see him because of.[^2]
- **`Love for the sake of Allah (al-mahabbah fi Allah)`** — love whose cause is obedience to Allah, not any worldly purpose.[^2]
- **`Being together in Jannah (al-ma'iyyah fi al-jannah)`** — being gathered in the Abode of Reward, even though the ranks differ.[^6]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### A True Hadith: A Visit with Nothing to Gain

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="muslim-2567" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened. Muslim reported it in his Sahih. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="muslim-2567" audience="13+" -->

Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, reports from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that a man set out to visit a brother of his in another town.[^1] It was a visit that cost him something: leaving his own town and walking the road.

On that road, Allah had posted an angel to watch for him.[^1][^2] The angel's first question was, "Where are you headed?" and the man said, "To a brother of mine in this town."[^1]

Then came the question that lays everything bare: "Is there some favor of yours with him that you are going to tend?"[^1] In other words: is there some interest or favor of yours with him that you are looking after, and that is the reason you are going to see him?[^2] It was a question about the motive, not about the road.

The man said, "No; it is only that I love him for the sake of Allah, the Mighty and Majestic."[^1] Al-Qurtubi explains it as: "I did not visit him for any worldly purpose."[^2]

And then the glad tidings came: "Then I am a messenger from Allah to you, to tell you that Allah loves you as you have loved him for His sake."[^1]

Notice that the hadith does not tell us what passed between the two brothers during the visit, and it does not describe the angel. All its weight rests on a single moment: an honest answer to the question of motive. And that same question can be put to every tie in your life. An-Nawawi says the hadith shows "the merit of love for the sake of Allah the Exalted, and that it is a cause of Allah the Exalted loving His servant."[^2] The companionship that passes this question is the one that remains after the others have turned into enmity, and its people hope to be gathered together in Jannah.[^3][^4]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. Why did the angel ask about the motive and not about the hardship of the journey?
2. Is every friendship that involves some benefit blameworthy? What is the difference between a friendship that includes benefit and a friendship that stands on nothing but benefit?
3. How do you reconcile "You will be with those you love" with the ayah "Whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger"? Is love enough without following?
4. Think, without naming anyone, of a tie you stay in out of fear of awkwardness or loneliness. What would make it pass the test of "except the God-fearing"?
5. Who is the person whose visit from you would be an honest answer: "I love him for the sake of Allah"?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: The Question on the Road

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.069.activity.the-question-on-the-road" -->

In a private note that you don't share, write down the people and groups you spend the most time with, in real life and on screens. Next to each one, answer the angel's question honestly: "Is there some interest of mine with them?" Then mark the direction of your time together: it brings me closer to Allah, it has no effect, or it takes me further away. Choose one person who brings you closer to Allah, and within seven days pay them a visit with no request and nothing to gain, or call them if a visit isn't possible, and tell them that you love them for the sake of Allah. Then choose one tie that takes you further away, and write down one small step to change its direction. On the seventh day, write a paragraph: What did the question on the road reveal to me?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.069.dua.loving-for-your-sake-gathered-in-jannah" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِنَ الْمُتَحَابِّينَ فِيكَ، الْمُتَزَاوِرِينَ فِيكَ، وَاجْعَلْ أَخِلَّاءَنَا مِنَ الْمُتَّقِينَ، وَاجْمَعْنَا بِمَنْ نُحِبُّ فِي جَنَّتِكَ.
>
> What it means: O Allah, make our love and our visits purely for You, not for gain and not for show. Make our closest friends people of taqwa, whose companionship remains on the Day when friendships turn into enmity, and bring us together with those we love for Your sake in Your Jannah.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner retells the hadith of Muslim 2567 in order and explains its key words from an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi; explains az-Zukhruf 43:67-68 from Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; sets out the meaning of companionship in an-Nisa' 4:69-70; reconciles the hadith of al-Bukhari 3688 with the difference in ranks, using al-'Ayni's words; quotes Ibn al-Qayyim on mutual visiting and identifies what is not relied upon; and begins the "Question on the Road" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with two columns, "Companionship for a purpose" and "Companionship for Allah"; the three-column activity sheet (name, answer to the angel's question, direction of the time spent); an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads an-Nawawi's commentary and al-Qurtubi's *al-Mufhim* on Muslim 2567; the tafsir of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di on az-Zukhruf 43:67 and an-Nisa' 4:69; *'Umdat al-Qari* on al-Bukhari 3688; and the fifty-ninth chapter of *Hadi al-Arwah* with the editor's notes. The teacher keeps in mind that the removal of rancor, the market of Jannah, its gatherings, and the family each have their own lessons.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Call to mind the last three visits you made. How many of them were made without needing anything?" Listen without comment.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Muslim 2567 in full, pausing at the angel's question before the answer, followed by the commentary of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi (7 minutes). Recite the two ayat of az-Zukhruf and read the two tafsirs (4 minutes), then the two ayat of an-Nisa' with the words of Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di (4 minutes), then the hadith of Anas with al-'Ayni's words (3 minutes), then Ibn al-Qayyim's statement, pointing out the reports that are not relied upon (2 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 3 and 4. The teacher guides learners to see that shared benefit does not spoil a companionship; what spoils it is benefit being its only cause. The teacher also makes clear that love does not take the place of obedience.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner writes their list and answers the angel's question next to each name (8 minutes), chooses one person and sets a date for the visit within seven days (4 minutes), and arranges with a partner to ask about it after a week (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "In two sentences, explain the meaning of 'Is there some favor of yours with him that you are going to tend?', and state what we affirm and where we hold back regarding the people of Jannah visiting one another." Close with the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, limit the study to the hadith of Muslim and the ayah of az-Zukhruf. Ask advanced learners to trace the reports about mutual visiting in *Hadi al-Arwah* and its notes, explain why each is not relied upon, and compare the gradings of at-Tirmidhi, al-Albani and Zubair 'Ali Za'i on the hadith of at-Tirmidhi 2008.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not describe the angel in the hadith in any way the text does not. Do not rely on the reports of noble mounts and moving couches, or on the hadith of Abu Umamah about the higher visiting the lower.[^8] Do not pass judgment on specific individuals in the discussion, and do not reveal learners' lists. Be mindful of anyone living with loneliness or isolation, and suggest that their visit can take the form of a call or a message.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child tells that a man visited his brother in another town, that an angel asked him why he was visiting, that he said "I love him for Allah," and that Allah loved him; draws the road of the visit; says "I love you for Allah" to a friend or relative; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet with a road drawn between two houses, with no writing on it; coloring pencils; a small doll or block to stand for the man as he walks; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The educator practices telling the account without adding to it, and sends families a short message asking them to accompany their child on a visit or a call during the week.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Who did you visit this week? And who came to visit you?" Move the doll along the drawn road.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation, then the account, moving the doll from the first house. Stop at the angel's question and ask, "What do you think the man will say?" Then finish.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 3, 4 and 5, and teach the children the sentence "I love you for Allah."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child draws their road and the friend's house, practices the question on the road and its answer with a classmate, and takes the sheet home to finish the smiling face after the visit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask: "Why did Allah love the man?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, drawing the house and saying the sentence is enough. Ask older children to tell the story to their family in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the angel, or any image of Allah the Exalted. If a child asks, "What did the angel look like?", answer: "The hadith didn't tell us that." A child goes on a visit only with their family. Do not compare children's friends, and never tell a child that their friend is bad.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the hadith in order; explains *arsada*, *madrajatuhu*, *ni'mah tarubbuha* and *al-akhilla'*; explains az-Zukhruf 43:67 and the promise "And how excellent are they as companions!"; sorts the time spent with friends, not the friends themselves; and plans a visit for Allah's sake during the week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; the three-column activity sheet; a mushaf; a large card with the angel's question written on it.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the two ayat on the board, prepares a sample activity sheet with made-up names, and sends families a note about the suggested visit.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the story of 'Umar who was sick, and ask: "If you were in his place, which visit would you remember?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the two ayat and their explanation (3 minutes), then the story, pausing before the man's answer to ask, "If the angel asked you this question, what would you answer?" (6 minutes), then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Pose questions 2, 4 and 5. The teacher makes clear that playing with friends is permitted and good, and that friendship for Allah's sake does not mean always being serious; it means a friendship Allah is pleased with.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student fills in the three columns, chooses a friend from the first column, writes their answer to the angel's question, and sets a day for the visit or the call.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student completes the sentence: "The friendship that lasts into Jannah is..." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, use only two columns: "We help each other do good" and "Other." Ask advanced students to memorize the hadith, or to write a paragraph on the difference between a friend you keep for what you need and a friend you keep out of love.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not show students' sheets to the class, and do not mention their friends' names. Do not label any classmate a "bad friend"; the judgment is on the time spent together. Be mindful of a student who has no friends, and suggest a relative or neighbor they love for Allah's sake. No visit takes place without the family's knowledge.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the angel's question as a question about motive; explains Ibn Kathir's tafsir of az-Zukhruf 43:67; reconciles "You will be with those you love" with an-Nisa' 4:69; applies the question on the road to their real-life and online ties in a private note; and plans a visit for Allah's sake along with a step to change the direction of one tie.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; short case cards about imaginary friendships (the friend of convenience, the friend kept out of politeness, the friend who helps you do good).

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes three imaginary cases that do not resemble any particular students, and reads the commentary of an-Nawawi and al-Qurtubi and the words of al-'Ayni.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "How many people in your phone do you contact only when you need them? Don't answer out loud." Then read the explanation.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the story, pausing at three moments: setting out, the question, and the answer. Then read the two ayat, the hadith of Anas and the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Groups analyze the three cases in light of questions 2 and 3, then answer question 4 in private writing. The teacher guides students to see that benefit does not spoil a friendship unless it is its only cause, and that changing the direction of a tie may mean inviting the other person toward good rather than cutting them off.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student writes their private list, answers the angel's question, and sets the visit and the small step.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a sentence they could say to a friend: "I love you for the sake of Allah because..." Then read the du'a. After a week, the teacher asks a general question about how it went.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who find writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the reports in *Hadi al-Arwah* about the people of Jannah visiting one another, and on why the lesson does not rely on them.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Private notes are not revealed, and no one is asked to name anyone. If a student discloses a harmful or exploitative tie, or bullying, the teacher follows up with them privately and involves the family or a counselor in line with the safeguarding policy. Do not encourage cutting family ties, or mistreating anyone, in the name of reviewing one's companionships.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
