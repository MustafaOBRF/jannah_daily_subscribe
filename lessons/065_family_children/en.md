# Family and Children in Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate, in order, the hadith of Qurrah ibn Iyas al-Muzani (an-Nasa'i 2088, authentic): a father and his little son in the gathering of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him; then the boy's death and the father's grief-stricken absence; then the Prophet asking after him and consoling him; and then the glad tidings that the son will reach the gate of Jannah before him and open it for him.

- Explain Allah's words `and he will return to his family rejoicing` (al-Inshiqaq 84:9) as the believer's return to his family in Jannah, following the tafsir of at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di, and set them against His words `He used to live among his family in carefree joy` (al-Inshiqaq 84:13).

- Show from the account of Nuh and his son (Hud 11:45-47) that the bond of family in the Hereafter is a bond of faith, and speak about relatives who are not Muslim with courtesy and mercy, without passing judgment on any particular person.

- Present the question of childbirth in Jannah as it appears in the hadith of at-Tirmidhi (2563), at-Tirmidhi's own comment on it, and Ibn al-Qayyim's conclusion, distinguishing what is established from what is disputed and from what is not relied upon.

- Carry out the "Who Is Missing from My Circle?" activity: draw circles of family and relatives, notice who has dropped out of them, then actually reach out to that person within a week and make du'a for them by name.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Every family has an empty place in it: a grandfather who has passed on, a mother who has departed, a child who went on ahead. So everyone who has loved and lost asks: Will I meet them again? Will the family be together in Jannah?

This lesson answers from three angles: the believer returns to his family in Jannah rejoicing; a loss in this world may turn into a reunion at the gates of Jannah; and the bond of family there is a bond of faith. It then sets out the question of childbirth in Jannah as the scholars have discussed it, because people ask about it. Two related subjects, Allah's joining of believers' offspring to their parents and raising them in rank, and the state of children who die young, each have a lesson of their own, so we do not repeat them here.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="nasai-2088" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then That Is Yours"

> عَنْ مُعَاوِيَةَ بْنِ قُرَّةَ، عَنْ أَبِيهِ قُرَّةَ بْنِ إِيَاسٍ الْمُزَنِيِّ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ، قَالَ: كَانَ نَبِيُّ اللَّهِ ﷺ إِذَا جَلَسَ يَجْلِسُ إِلَيْهِ نَفَرٌ مِنْ أَصْحَابِهِ، وَفِيهِمْ رَجُلٌ لَهُ ابْنٌ صَغِيرٌ يَأْتِيهِ مِنْ خَلْفِ ظَهْرِهِ فَيُقْعِدُهُ بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ، فَهَلَكَ، فَامْتَنَعَ الرَّجُلُ أَنْ يَحْضُرَ الْحَلْقَةَ لِذِكْرِ ابْنِهِ، فَحَزِنَ عَلَيْهِ، فَفَقَدَهُ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ فَقَالَ: **«مَا لِي لَا أَرَى فُلَانًا؟»** قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، بُنَيُّهُ الَّذِي رَأَيْتَهُ هَلَكَ. فَلَقِيَهُ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ فَسَأَلَهُ عَنْ بُنَيِّهِ، فَأَخْبَرَهُ أَنَّهُ هَلَكَ، فَعَزَّاهُ عَلَيْهِ، ثُمَّ قَالَ: **«يَا فُلَانُ، أَيُّمَا كَانَ أَحَبُّ إِلَيْكَ: أَنْ تَمَتَّعَ بِهِ عُمْرَكَ، أَوْ لَا تَأْتِي غَدًا إِلَى بَابٍ مِنْ أَبْوَابِ الْجَنَّةِ إِلَّا وَجَدْتَهُ قَدْ سَبَقَكَ إِلَيْهِ يَفْتَحُهُ لَكَ؟»** قَالَ: يَا نَبِيَّ اللَّهِ، بَلْ يَسْبِقُنِي إِلَى بَابِ الْجَنَّةِ فَيَفْتَحُهَا لِي لَهُوَ أَحَبُّ إِلَيَّ. قَالَ: **«فَذَاكَ لَكَ»**.[^1]

> وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ عِنْدَ النَّسَائِيِّ: أَنَّ رَجُلًا أَتَى النَّبِيَّ ﷺ وَمَعَهُ ابْنٌ لَهُ، فَقَالَ لَهُ: **«أَتُحِبُّهُ؟»** فَقَالَ: أَحَبَّكَ اللَّهُ كَمَا أُحِبُّهُ. فَمَاتَ، فَفَقَدَهُ، فَسَأَلَ عَنْهُ، فَقَالَ: **«مَا يَسُرُّكَ أَنْ لَا تَأْتِيَ بَابًا مِنْ أَبْوَابِ الْجَنَّةِ إِلَّا وَجَدْتَهُ عِنْدَهُ يَسْعَى يَفْتَحُ لَكَ؟»**[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Mu'awiyah ibn Qurrah reports from his father, Qurrah ibn Iyas al-Muzani, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: Whenever the Prophet of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, sat down, a group of his Companions would come and sit with him. Among them was a man who had a little son. The boy would come up to him from behind, and his father would sit him down in front of him. Then the boy died, and the man stopped coming to the circle, because it reminded him of his son, and he grieved for him. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, noticed that he was missing and said: **"Why do I not see so-and-so?"** They said, "Messenger of Allah, his little son, the one you used to see, has died." So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, met him and asked him about his little son, and the man told him that the boy had died. He consoled him for his loss, and then said: **"So-and-so, which would you love more: to enjoy him for the rest of your life, or that tomorrow you will not come to any of the gates of Jannah without finding that he has reached it before you and is opening it for you?"** He said, "Prophet of Allah, that he should reach the gate of Jannah before me and open it for me: that is dearer to me." He said: **"Then that is yours."**[^1]

> In another narration recorded by an-Nasa'i: A man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, with a son of his. He asked him: **"Do you love him?"** The man said, "May Allah love you as I love him." Then the boy died, and the Prophet noticed the man's absence and asked about him, and said: **"Would it not please you that you will not come to any of the gates of Jannah without finding him there, hurrying to open it for you?"**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

An-Nasa'i placed the first narration in the Book of Funerals, under the chapter on consoling the bereaved. On the second narration, as-Sindi said: "'May Allah love you' is a du'a for him, peace and blessings be upon him, that Allah's love for him increase; he means that he loves his son intensely, and he asks Allah to give you a love like it." And on "Would it not please you" (*ma yasurruka*), he said: "with an implied interrogative particle, that is: does it not please you?"[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

Notice the order of events: an open love between a father and his son, then a loss, then a withdrawal from the gathering out of grief. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did not wait for the man to come back. He noticed his absence, asked about him, went to meet him and consoled him, and only after consoling him did he open the door of hope. He did not reproach him for loving his son, nor ask him to forget him. He set two choices before him and left the choosing to him. Grief is understandable; hope turns it into patience. "Tomorrow" here means the Day of the Hereafter. And the hadith shows that the family death has parted, Allah brings together again at the gates of Jannah.

### "And He Will Return to His Family Rejoicing"

> **{فَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ ۝ فَسَوْفَ يُحَاسَبُ حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا ۝ وَيَنْقَلِبُ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهِ مَسْرُورًا ۝ وَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ وَرَاءَ ظَهْرِهِ ۝ فَسَوْفَ يَدْعُو ثُبُورًا ۝ وَيَصْلَىٰ سَعِيرًا ۝ إِنَّهُ كَانَ فِي أَهْلِهِ مَسْرُورًا}** [الانشقاق: ٧-١٣][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"As for the one who is given his record in his right hand, he will be judged with an easy reckoning, and he will return to his family rejoicing. But as for the one who is given his record behind his back, he will cry out for destruction, and he will burn in a blazing Fire. He used to live among his family in carefree joy."** (al-Inshiqaq 84:7-13)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tabari said: "And this person, judged with an easy reckoning, goes back to his family in Jannah rejoicing," and he relates from Qatadah: "to a family for whom Allah has prepared Jannah." Ibn Kathir said: "That is, he returns to his family in Jannah, as Qatadah and ad-Dahhak said," and "rejoicing means glad and delighted with what Allah, the Mighty and Majestic, has given him." As-Sa'di said: "because he has been saved from the punishment and has won the reward." And Ibn Kathir said on ayah 13: "That is, glad, not thinking about the consequences and not fearing what lay ahead of him, so that brief gladness was followed by long sorrow."[^3]

#### Lesson Explanation

Joy in one's family is mentioned twice: a joy in Jannah after an easy reckoning, and a joy in this world that went hand in hand with heedlessness of the end. So the question is not whether to delight in your family or not. It is whether your delight in them today is a joy that remembers the Hereafter and helps you toward it, rather than a joy that makes you forget it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-11-45-47" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "He Is Not of Your Family"

> **{وَنَادَىٰ نُوحٌ رَبَّهُ فَقَالَ رَبِّ إِنَّ ابْنِي مِنْ أَهْلِي وَإِنَّ وَعْدَكَ الْحَقُّ وَأَنْتَ أَحْكَمُ الْحَاكِمِينَ ۝ قَالَ يَا نُوحُ إِنَّهُ لَيْسَ مِنْ أَهْلِكَ ۖ إِنَّهُ عَمَلٌ غَيْرُ صَالِحٍ ۖ فَلَا تَسْأَلْنِ مَا لَيْسَ لَكَ بِهِ عِلْمٌ ۖ إِنِّي أَعِظُكَ أَنْ تَكُونَ مِنَ الْجَاهِلِينَ ۝ قَالَ رَبِّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ أَنْ أَسْأَلَكَ مَا لَيْسَ لِي بِهِ عِلْمٌ ۖ وَإِلَّا تَغْفِرْ لِي وَتَرْحَمْنِي أَكُنْ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ}** [هود: ٤٥-٤٧][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And Nuh called out to his Lord and said, 'My Lord, my son is of my family, and Your promise is the truth, and You are the most just of judges.' He said, 'Nuh, he is not of your family. His conduct was not righteous. So do not ask Me about what you have no knowledge of. I admonish you, lest you be among the ignorant.' He said, 'My Lord, I seek refuge with You from asking You about what I have no knowledge of. And unless You forgive me and have mercy on me, I will be among the losers.'"** (Hud 11:45-47)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: "`He is not of your family`, that is, of those whom I promised to save; for I promised you only the rescue of those of your family who believed." And he relates from Ibn Abbas: "He was his son, except that he opposed him in deed and in intention." As-Sa'di said: "`He is not of your family` whom I promised you to save."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

The boy truly was Nuh's son, and his father prayed for him with a father's heart. But the family promised rescue were the people of faith. So the bond that lasts into the Hereafter is a bond of faith. When we face a relative who has not accepted Islam, we learn from Nuh: we love them, treat them well and pray for their guidance as long as they live, and then we leave their affair to Allah, the most just of judges and the most merciful of those who show mercy, and we pass no judgment on any particular person.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### The Question of Childbirth in Jannah

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: **«الْمُؤْمِنُ إِذَا اشْتَهَى الْوَلَدَ فِي الْجَنَّةِ كَانَ حَمْلُهُ وَوَضْعُهُ وَسِنُّهُ فِي سَاعَةٍ كَمَا يَشْتَهِي»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"When the believer desires a child in Jannah, its conception, its birth and its growing to full age take place within a single hour, just as he desires."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

At-Tirmidhi said: "This is a *hasan gharib* hadith. The people of knowledge have differed on this: some of them said that in Jannah there is marital union but no child is born." He relates that Ishaq ibn Rahawayh said of the hadith: "but he will not desire it," and that al-Bukhari mentioned the hadith of Abu Razin al-'Uqayli: "The people of Jannah will have no children in it."[^5] After setting out the evidence, Ibn al-Qayyim concluded that if the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said this hadith, "then it is the truth in which there is no doubt," and that the denial of procreation "is a denial of procreation as it is known in this world; it does not deny a birth in which the carrying of the child, its delivery, its growth and its youth all take place within a single hour." Then he said: "This is as far as our limited knowledge has reached on this question."[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

What is established and agreed upon is that the people of Jannah are given whatever they desire, and that Jannah is an abode of permanence in which there is no death. Childbirth, however, is a question the scholars have disagreed on since the earliest generations, so we present it with the courtesy that disagreement calls for, and we assert no more than they said. Other reports are narrated on this subject, but the lesson does not rely on them because they are weak.[^7]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why did the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, begin by asking after the absent man and then consoling him, before giving him the glad tidings? What effect would that order have on someone in grief?

2. What is the difference between joy in one's family in ayah 9 and joy in one's family in ayah 13 of Surah al-Inshiqaq?

3. What does the account of Nuh and his son teach us about the meaning of "family" in the Hereafter? And how do we speak about a relative who is not Muslim without passing judgment on any particular person?

4. In the question of childbirth in Jannah, what is established, and what is disputed?

5. Who has recently gone missing from your gatherings or your family, and you have not yet asked after them?

### Activity: Who Is Missing from My Circle?

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.065.activity.who-is-missing-from-my-circle" -->

Draw three overlapping circles: your household; then your close relatives; then more distant relatives and family friends. Write in each circle the names of the people who belong there. Then ask the Prophet's question, peace and blessings be upon him: "Why do I not see so-and-so?" Put a mark next to everyone who has dropped out of your life or been cut off: someone you have not called in a long time, or who withdrew after an illness, a loss or a falling-out. Choose one of them, decide how you will reach out (a visit, a call, a message or a gift) and when, within seven days, and then do it. Afterward, write three lines: What did I do? How did they receive me? And a du'a for them by name. If the person you are missing has passed away, reach out instead by visiting their family, or by giving charity on their behalf and making du'a for them.

### An Authentic Hadith: The Boy Who Opens the Gate

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="nasai-2088" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by an-Nasa'i with an authentic chain. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="nasai-2088" audience="4-7" -->

Whenever the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, sat down, his Companions would sit around him in a circle. Among them was a man who had a little son he loved very much.[^1]

The little boy would come up behind his father, and his father would pick him up and sit him down in front of him, nice and close.[^1]

Then the little boy died. His father was very, very sad, and he stopped coming to the circle, because it made him think of his son.[^1]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, looked around at his Companions and didn't see the man. He said: "Why do I not see so-and-so?" That means: Where is our friend? Why don't I see him? They told him that the man's little son had died.[^1]

So the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, went to him, asked him about his son, and comforted him with kind words. Then he asked him a question: Which would you love more: for your son to stay with you in this world, or for you to come, on the Day of Resurrection, to one of the gates of Jannah and find that he got there first and is opening it for you?[^1]

The father said: "That he gets to the gate of Jannah before me and opens it for me. That is what I would love more." And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Then that is yours," which means: You will have it.[^1]

Just imagine: a great big gate of Jannah, and the son his father loves so much is opening it for him! That is how Allah brings a family of believers together again in Jannah after they have been apart. And notice something else: the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, noticed who was missing, and he went to him. We learn from him to ask about the people we haven't seen.

### Bedtime Du'a

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ يَنْقَلِبُ إِلَى أَهْلِهِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ مَسْرُورًا، وَاجْمَعْ شَمْلَنَا بِمَنْ نُحِبُّ مِنْ أَهْلِ الْإِيمَانِ، وَلَا تُفَرِّقْ بَيْنَنَا هُنَاكَ أَبَدًا.
>
> *"O Allah, make us among those who return to their families in Jannah rejoicing. Bring us together with the believers we love, and never let us be parted there."*

What it means: O Allah, let us go back to our families in Jannah, happy, and bring us together with everyone we love who believes, and never let us be apart there, ever.

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

During morning class, the teacher noticed that one student's seat had been empty for three days. She asked the class, and his friend said, "His grandpa died, and he's really sad." Some of the students forgot about it a minute later. Others asked, "What can we do for him?" Noticing when someone is missing is a beautiful trait, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught it to us in a true story.

And part of Allah's mercy is that a family of believers that death splits apart in this world comes together again in Jannah. Allah says of the believer who is given his record in his right hand: `and he will return to his family rejoicing`.[^3] That means he goes back to his family in Jannah, glad and delighted with what Allah has given him, as the scholars of tafsir explain.

### An Authentic Hadith: "Why Do I Not See So-and-So?"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="nasai-2088" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by an-Nasa'i with an authentic chain. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="nasai-2088" audience="8-12" -->

The Companion Qurrah ibn Iyas al-Muzani, may Allah be pleased with him, tells us that whenever the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, sat down, a group of his Companions would gather around him. Among them was a man who had a little son. The little boy would come up from behind his father's back, and his father would sit him down right in front of him. In another narration, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked the man: "Do you love him?" And the man answered, "May Allah love you as I love him."[^1]

Then the little boy died. His father grieved for him and stayed away from the circle, because it reminded him of his son.[^1]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, noticed he was missing and said: "Why do I not see so-and-so?" They said, "Messenger of Allah, his little son, the one you used to see, has died."[^1]

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, didn't stop at asking. He went to meet the man, asked him about his son, and when the man told him the boy had died, he consoled him. Then he said to him: "So-and-so, which would you love more: to enjoy him for the rest of your life, or that tomorrow you will not come to any of the gates of Jannah without finding that he has reached it before you and is opening it for you?"[^1]

The man said, "Prophet of Allah, that he should reach the gate of Jannah before me and open it for me: that is dearer to me." And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Then that is yours."[^1]

See how the sadness changed. The father who had stayed away from the circle because his son was gone now had a day to look forward to, when he would find his son waiting at the gate of Jannah. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, didn't tell him, "Forget your son." He told him something that turned his love into hope and patience. The story teaches us two things: that Allah brings a family of believers together in Jannah, and that we should ask about whoever is missing and go to them, just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, did.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The family group chat has dozens of names in it. Some people post every day; others have been silent for months. Nobody asks about the silent ones, because the chat is busy with whoever is talking. And one of those silent people might be going through grief, illness or loneliness.

Loss and absence are among the hardest things a person goes through. The Qur'an promises the believer that `he will return to his family rejoicing`,[^3] meaning he goes back to his family in Jannah. And in the very same passage, it warns against a joy in one's family in this world that makes a person forget where things are heading: `He used to live among his family in carefree joy`.[^3] The bond of family in the Hereafter is a bond of faith, as Nuh was told about his son: `He is not of your family`.[^4] So if you have a relative who is not Muslim, treat them well, pray for their guidance, leave their affair to Allah, and pass no judgment on any particular person.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="nasai-2088" -->

- **`He grieved for him (fa-hazina 'alayh)`** — the father's grief is not criticized here; instead, it is met with consolation and then with glad tidings.[^1]

- **`Tomorrow (ghadan)`** — in this hadith, the Day of the Hereafter, not the next day.[^1]

- **`May Allah love you as I love him (ahabbaka Allahu kama uhibbuh)`** — a du'a asking Allah to love the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, through which the father expresses how intensely he loves his son.[^2]

<!-- terminology:end -->

### An Authentic Hadith: The Absent Man the Prophet Missed

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="nasai-2088" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account, narrated by an-Nasa'i with an authentic chain. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="nasai-2088" audience="13+" -->

Qurrah ibn Iyas al-Muzani, may Allah be pleased with him, describes a scene from the gathering of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: a group of Companions sitting with him, and among them a man with a little son who would come up from behind him, and his father would sit him down in front of him.[^1] In another narration, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, asked him: "Do you love him?" And he said: "May Allah love you as I love him."[^1]

Then the little boy died. The father could not bear to sit in the place where his son used to sit in front of him, and so, in his grief, he stopped coming to the circle.[^1]

In a gathering of several Companions, one man's absence could easily have gone unnoticed. But the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Why do I not see so-and-so?" They told him that the man's son had died. So he went to meet him, asked him about his son, and consoled him.[^1]

Then he put a question to him: "Which would you love more: to enjoy him for the rest of your life, or that tomorrow you will not come to any of the gates of Jannah without finding that he has reached it before you and is opening it for you?" The father chose: "That he should reach the gate of Jannah before me and open it for me: that is dearer to me." And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Then that is yours."[^1]

Look at the sequence: noticing, then asking, then going, then consoling, then glad tidings. The good news did not come as a general line said from a distance to anyone who is grieving. It came after the man felt that his absence had been noticed and his grief understood. And the question itself respected the father's love and did not ask him to forget. It moved him from a love that wanted to hold on in this world to the hope of a reunion with no parting after it.

That is what so many people around us need: for someone to ask about their absence before anyone asks them to be patient.

### Discussion Questions

1. Why would it have been easy for the man's absence to go unnoticed? And what makes some people's absence go unnoticed today?

2. Why did the glad tidings come after the consolation and not before it?

3. How do you understand the difference between `He used to live among his family in carefree joy` and `he will return to his family rejoicing`?

4. How can you talk about a relative who is not Muslim with courtesy and mercy, without passing judgment on their final destiny?

5. Who is the silent one in your circle right now? And what is the first step to reaching out to them?

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of an-Nasa'i 2088 in order and draws from it the steps of the Prophet's way of consoling; explains al-Inshiqaq 84:7-13 from the tafsir of at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di; explains the meaning of "family" in the Hereafter from Hud 11:45-47; presents the question of childbirth in Jannah with the views held on it; and begins the "Who Is Missing from My Circle?" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a board with five boxes: noticing, asking, going, consoling, glad tidings; the three-circle sheet; an exit card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the hadith and its other narration (an-Nasa'i 1870) with as-Sindi's marginal commentary, the tafsir of the ayat in the works cited, at-Tirmidhi's comment on hadith 2563, and chapter fifty-six of *Hadi al-Arwah*. The teacher keeps in mind that the joining of offspring to their parents and the state of children who die young each have a separate lesson, and finds out in advance whether anyone in the group has recently lost a relative.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Who is the person you most hope to meet in Jannah?" Ask participants to answer in their hearts, not out loud. Then say: "This lesson is about that meeting."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith in full and fill in the five boxes on the board (7 minutes). Recite the ayat of al-Inshiqaq with the commentators' explanations, and compare the two kinds of joy (5 minutes). Recite the ayat of Hud with their tafsir, and explain the proper courtesy in speaking about relatives who are not Muslim (4 minutes). Then present the hadith of at-Tirmidhi, his comment on it, and Ibn al-Qayyim's conclusion (4 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Discuss questions 1, 2 and 3. The teacher draws out that grief is not censured in the hadith, that the glad tidings came after the consolation, and that judging the fate of any particular person is not our business.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner draws their circles and adds their marks (8 minutes), then chooses one person and writes down how and when they will reach out (4 minutes), and agrees with a partner that each will ask the other about it a week later (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: "List the five steps of consoling in the hadith, and state what is established and what is disputed in the question of childbirth in Jannah." Close with the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, the hadith and al-Inshiqaq 84:9 are enough. Ask advanced learners to gather the narrations of the hadith of Qurrah ibn Iyas from the *Musnad* and *Sahih Ibn Hibban* and compare their wordings, and to read chapter fifty-six of *Hadi al-Arwah* in full.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not declare that any particular relative, living or dead, is in Jannah or the Fire. Be sensitive to anyone who has lost a child or a relative: do not ask them to talk about their loss, and give them the option not to take part. Do not present the question of childbirth in Jannah as settled, and do not rely on the report "a child is part of the completeness of joy" or on the other weak routes.[^7]

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that a family of believers comes together in Jannah; tells that the little boy will open the gate of Jannah for his father; repeats the Prophet's question, peace and blessings be upon him, "Why do I not see so-and-so?"; draws their home and the circle of their relatives; and repeats the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A large sheet of paper; coloring pencils; a set of small chairs or cushions arranged in a circle; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the account calmly and without adding anything, finds out in advance whether any of the children has recently lost a relative, and lets the families know the topic of the lesson.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 4 minutes:** The children sit in a circle. Ask: "Who do you love in your family?" Then quietly take one cushion out of the circle and ask: "Who's missing from our circle?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Read the explanation and the ayah, then tell the account, pausing at the Prophet's question, peace and blessings be upon him, so the children can repeat it, and at the picture of the gate being opened.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask questions 2, 4 and 5, and stress that feeling sad when someone is gone is natural, and that Allah brings the believers together in Jannah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Each child draws their home and the circle of their relatives, colors the face of someone they haven't seen for a long time, and takes the sheet home to call that person with their family.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask: "Who is going to open the gate of Jannah for the father?" Then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, drawing the house and one face is enough. Ask older children to tell the story to their family in three sentences.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions, and do not act out the story through role-play. Do not talk about the details of death or illness. If a child says that a relative of theirs has died, reassure them gently and make du'a for that relative, without asking for details. If a child asks about a relative who is not Muslim, say: "We ask Allah to guide them, and Allah is the most merciful of those who show mercy," and refer the question to the child's family.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the hadith in order; puts in order the steps the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, took before giving the glad tidings; explains the meaning of `and he will return to his family rejoicing`; and draws their circles and plans to reach out to someone missing within a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Term cards; the three-circle sheet; five cards with the steps of consoling written on them, for students to put in order; a mushaf.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher writes the ayah on the board, shuffles the five cards, and finds out whether anyone in the class has lost a relative.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Read the scene of the empty seat, and ask: "What would you have done if this were your classmate?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the ayah and its explanation (3 minutes); then the story, pausing just before the Prophet's question to the father, peace and blessings be upon him: "What do you think he's going to say to him?" (6 minutes); then the terms (3 minutes).

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** Students put the five cards in order; then discuss questions 3, 4 and 5.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Each student draws their circles and adds the star, writes down how and when they will reach out, and takes the sheet home for a parent to sign the plan.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes one sentence: "I learned from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, that ..." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, provide circles with ready-made slots (grandpa, grandma, uncle, aunt...). Ask advanced students to memorize the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him, «مَا لِي لَا أَرَى فُلَانًا؟» ("Why do I not see so-and-so?") and to write a paragraph about the effect of asking after someone who is missing.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not draw the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, or the Companions. Do not make any student mention a relative who has died or talk about a family conflict. Do not ask a child to reach out to someone they are afraid of, or someone their family has told them not to contact; reaching out happens under the parents' supervision. Do not pass judgment on the fate of any particular person.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="50" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 50 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen analyzes the order of the Prophet's way of consoling (noticing, asking, going, consoling, glad tidings); compares the two kinds of joy in Surah al-Inshiqaq; speaks about relatives who are not Muslim with courtesy and without judging any particular person; and designs a private reach-out plan and carries it out within a week.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a private sheet that is not collected; a board with two columns: "Joy that makes us forget" and "Joy we hope for."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the hadith and its other narration and the tafsir of the ayat, and prepares general examples of "silent members" in group chats, without names.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "In your family group chats, who is the last person whose absence you noticed?" Listen without commenting.

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the explanation and the story, pausing at three moments: the father's withdrawal, the Prophet asking after him, peace and blessings be upon him, and the choice set before him. Then read the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 13 minutes:** Fill in the board with examples of the two kinds of joy, then discuss questions 1, 2 and 4 in groups, and have students answer question 5 in private writing.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student draws their circles and adds their marks, and writes the reach-out plan and the first sentence, with the honesty rule: if you don't carry it out, write down what stopped you.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 8 minutes:** Each student writes a paragraph: "Why did the glad tidings come after the consolation? And how will I put that into practice?" Then read the du'a. A week later, the teacher asks a general question about how it went.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Anyone who finds writing difficult may make a private voice recording instead. Ask advanced students to do a short piece of research on the question of childbirth in Jannah as at-Tirmidhi and Ibn al-Qayyim present it.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Private sheets are never revealed. Be sensitive to anyone living through a loss or a family conflict, and do not ask them to reach out to someone who harms them; where there is harm, advise them to turn to a counselor or a parent or guardian. Do not pass judgment on any particular relative who is not Muslim, and do not use the lesson to pressure anyone.
