# The Last Person to Enter Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of the last man to come out of the Fire and the last man to enter Jannah (al-Bukhari 6571), and explain how he turned back twice, convinced that Jannah was already full, before being given the like of this world and ten times as much.
- Explain why he asked, "Are You mocking me, though You are the King?" and why the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he told it: the words express astonishment at the sheer scale of the generosity, not doubt.
- Connect this hadith with the hadith of Abu Dharr (Muslim 190) on sins being exchanged for good deeds, and the hadith of Jabir (Muslim 191) on the scene of intercession and release from the Fire, as complementary pictures of one and the same destiny.
- Call to mind the ayah `Do not despair of the mercy of Allah` (az-Zumar 39:53) as the Qur'anic root that gathers the meaning of all three hadiths.
- Carry out the "My World × 10" activity, which links imagining one's most treasured things multiplied tenfold to the reality of what Allah gives the lowest-ranking person in Jannah.
- Recall the du'a "O Allah, never let us despair of Your mercy" as a daily reminder never to lose hope in Allah's mercy, however small one's deeds may seem or however heavy one's sins.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

It is part of Allah's vast mercy that He does not leave a believer who affirmed His oneness in the Fire forever, even if that believer sinned. Rather, He brings out of it everyone who had in his heart even the faintest speck of faith, however long that may take. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that he knows the last man to be brought out of the Fire and the last man to enter Jannah: a man who comes out stumbling (tripping and falling on his face in his weakness; in another narration, crawling). Allah commands him to enter Jannah, and he goes, but it seems to him that Jannah is already full of its people and that no place remains for him. So he goes back and says, "My Lord, I found it full!" This happens to him twice, until, the third time, Allah says to him, "Go and enter Jannah, for you shall have the like of this world and ten times as much." The man finds the gift so staggering that he says, "Are You mocking me, though You are the King?" And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he related this scene to his Companions. There was nothing frivolous in the scene. He laughed because Allah mocks no one; He simply gives beyond anything a human being can imagine, so that even the lowest-ranking person in Jannah receives what has never occurred to any heart.

This man is no stranger to the ummah of Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him. He is a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness, yet committed sins for which he deserved a period in the Fire. When that period ended, he was brought out by the mercy of the Most Merciful of those who show mercy, after the intercessors had interceded, and admitted into Jannah, though he is still the last to enter it and the lowest of its people in rank. Even so, his share of Allah's giving is ten times greater than this world and everything in it. What, then, of those ranked above him? The hadith of Abu Dharr makes this meaning clearer still, for in it the minor sins of the man it describes are exchanged for good deeds, while the hadith of Jabir sets this scene within the intercession and the release from the Fire of those who affirmed Allah's oneness, and tells that the one brought out keeps asking until he is given this world and ten times its like. As for the ayah of az-Zumar, it is at root a call to repentance, and it meets these hadiths in a single call: `Do not despair of the mercy of Allah`. So let no believer despair of Allah's mercy, however much he has sinned, and let none think little of the lowest rank with Him, for it is greater than everything the people of this world could ever wish for.

## Central Evidence

### The Hadith of the Last Man to Enter Jannah

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ مَسْعُودٍ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«إِنِّي لَأَعْلَمُ آخِرَ أَهْلِ النَّارِ خُرُوجًا مِنْهَا، وَآخِرَ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ دُخُولًا، رَجُلٌ يَخْرُجُ مِنَ النَّارِ كَبْوًا، فَيَقُولُ اللَّهُ: اذْهَبْ فَادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ. فَيَأْتِيهَا فَيُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ أَنَّهَا مَلْأَى، فَيَرْجِعُ فَيَقُولُ: يَا رَبِّ، وَجَدْتُهَا مَلْأَى. فَيَقُولُ: اذْهَبْ فَادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ. فَيَأْتِيهَا فَيُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ أَنَّهَا مَلْأَى، فَيَقُولُ: يَا رَبِّ، وَجَدْتُهَا مَلْأَى. فَيَقُولُ: اذْهَبْ فَادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ، فَإِنَّ لَكَ مِثْلَ الدُّنْيَا وَعَشَرَةَ أَمْثَالِهَا، أَوْ إِنَّ لَكَ مِثْلَ عَشَرَةِ أَمْثَالِ الدُّنْيَا. فَيَقُولُ: تَسْخَرُ مِنِّي، أَوْ تَضْحَكُ مِنِّي، وَأَنْتَ الْمَلِكُ؟»**. فَلَقَدْ رَأَيْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم ضَحِكَ حَتَّى بَدَتْ نَوَاجِذُهُ، وَكَانَ يُقَالُ: ذَلِكَ أَدْنَى أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ مَنْزِلَةً.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"I know the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it, and the last of the people of Jannah to enter it: a man who comes out of the Fire stumbling and falling. Allah says to him, 'Go and enter Jannah.' So he comes to it, and it seems to him that it is full. He goes back and says, 'My Lord, I found it full.' Allah says to him, 'Go and enter Jannah.' So he comes to it, and it seems to him that it is full. He says, 'My Lord, I found it full.' Allah says to him, 'Go and enter Jannah, for you shall have the like of this world and ten times as much' (or: 'you shall have ten times the like of this world'). He says, 'Are You mocking me' (or: 'Are You laughing at me') 'though You are the King?'"** I saw the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, laugh until his back teeth showed. And it used to be said: that is the lowest-ranking of the people of Jannah.[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Kabwan* comes from *kaba*, to trip and fall on one's face. The narration in Muslim (186), and the text of al-Bukhari on which Ibn Hajar commented, read *habwan*, "crawling"; the meanings are close: he comes out weak, stumbling and crawling. "It seems to him that it is full" means he supposes Jannah is already filled with its people and no place remains for him. This may be because he sees how many have reached it before him, or because he thinks so little of himself; it is not doubt in Allah's promise. Nor is his question "Are You mocking me?" (in Muslim's narration, *a-taskharu bi*) a doubt in the truthfulness of Allah, glory be to Him. Al-Nawawi mentions several explanations of it, among them that of al-Qadi 'Iyad: the man said it without weighing his words, overwhelmed by joy and amazement at what had never crossed his mind. It is astonishment at an overflowing generosity, not belief in the literal meaning of the words, so much so that the gift seemed to him nearer to jest than to earnest, and so Allah made clear to him that this was His true gift.[^6] The laughter of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, as he related it was wonder and delight at the breadth of Allah's mercy, never ridicule of the man's state.

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith is the centerpiece of the entire lesson. The lowest station in Jannah, given to the person with the fewest deeds and the latest entry, surpasses this whole world ten times over. If that is the share of the lowest, what of those above him? This alone is enough to settle the heart of every believer, however small his deeds may seem to him, and to cut off all despair of Allah's mercy, however many his sins may be.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### The Hadith of Abu Dharr: Bad Deeds Exchanged for Good

> عَنْ أَبِي ذَرٍّ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«إِنِّي لَأَعْلَمُ آخِرَ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ دُخُولًا الْجَنَّةَ، وَآخِرَ أَهْلِ النَّارِ خُرُوجًا مِنْهَا، رَجُلٌ يُؤْتَى بِهِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فَيُقَالُ: اعْرِضُوا عَلَيْهِ صِغَارَ ذُنُوبِهِ وَارْفَعُوا عَنْهُ كِبَارَهَا. فَتُعْرَضُ عَلَيْهِ صِغَارُ ذُنُوبِهِ فَيُقَالُ: عَمِلْتَ يَوْمَ كَذَا وَكَذَا كَذَا وَكَذَا، وَعَمِلْتَ يَوْمَ كَذَا وَكَذَا كَذَا وَكَذَا. فَيَقُولُ: نَعَمْ، لَا يَسْتَطِيعُ أَنْ يُنْكِرَ، وَهُوَ مُشْفِقٌ مِنْ كِبَارِ ذُنُوبِهِ أَنْ تُعْرَضَ عَلَيْهِ. فَيُقَالُ لَهُ: فَإِنَّ لَكَ مَكَانَ كُلِّ سَيِّئَةٍ حَسَنَةً. فَيَقُولُ: رَبِّ، قَدْ عَمِلْتُ أَشْيَاءَ لَا أَرَاهَا هَاهُنَا»**. فَلَقَدْ رَأَيْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم ضَحِكَ حَتَّى بَدَتْ نَوَاجِذُهُ.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Dharr, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"I know the last of the people of Jannah to enter Jannah, and the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it. He is a man who will be brought forward on the Day of Resurrection, and it will be said: 'Lay his minor sins before him and hold back his major ones.' So his minor sins are laid before him, and it is said: 'On such-and-such a day you did such-and-such, and on such-and-such a day you did such-and-such.' He says, 'Yes,' unable to deny any of it, all the while dreading that his major sins will be laid before him. Then it is said to him: 'In place of every bad deed, you shall have a good deed.' And he says, 'My Lord, I did things that I do not see here!'"** I saw the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, laugh until his back teeth showed.[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

This hadith describes the man in the very same words as the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud: "the last of the people of Jannah to enter it and the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it." Some scholars therefore held that both describe the same person from two angles, while al-Qadi 'Iyad allowed that those described as "last" in the various reports may be more than one, as Ibn Hajar relates in *Fath al-Bari*. In the hadith, the man's minor sins are laid before him so that he acknowledges them, while his major sins are held back and not shown to him. Then his minor bad deeds are exchanged for good deeds, and at that point he says, "I did things that I do not see here," meaning major sins that were never laid before him, as al-Mulla 'Ali al-Qari explains in *Mirqat al-Mafatih*. It is as though, seeing his minor sins turned into good deeds, he began to hope in Allah's generosity that his major sins would be turned into good deeds as well.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith brings the meaning into sharper focus. The exchange of bad deeds for good ones is yet another face of Allah's mercy, a mercy that outruns every human reckoning. It means that even a person weighed down by many sins need not despair of his Lord's mercy; instead, he may hope to be among those whose bad deeds Allah has turned into good.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### The Hadith of Jabir on the Scene of Intercession and Release from the Fire

> عَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رضي الله عنهما، وَقَدْ سُئِلَ عَنِ الْوُرُودِ، فَذَكَرَ مَشْهَدًا مِنْ مَشَاهِدِ يَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ ثُمَّ قَالَ: **«... ثُمَّ تَحِلُّ الشَّفَاعَةُ، وَيَشْفَعُونَ حَتَّى يَخْرُجَ مِنَ النَّارِ مَنْ قَالَ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَكَانَ فِي قَلْبِهِ مِنَ الْخَيْرِ مَا يَزِنُ شَعِيرَةً، فَيُجْعَلُونَ بِفِنَاءِ الْجَنَّةِ، وَيَجْعَلُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ يَرُشُّونَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْمَاءَ حَتَّى يَنْبُتُوا نَبَاتَ الشَّيْءِ فِي السَّيْلِ، وَيَذْهَبُ حُرَاقُهُ، ثُمَّ يَسْأَلُ حَتَّى تُجْعَلَ لَهُ الدُّنْيَا وَعَشَرَةُ أَمْثَالِهَا مَعَهَا»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Jabir ibn Abdullah, may Allah be pleased with him and his father: he was asked about *al-wurud* (the "coming to" the Fire mentioned in Maryam 19:71), and he described one of the scenes of the Day of Resurrection, then said: **"... Then intercession is permitted, and they intercede until there comes out of the Fire everyone who said 'There is no god but Allah' and had in his heart good weighing as much as a grain of barley. They are placed in the forecourt of Jannah, and the people of Jannah begin sprinkling water over them until they spring up the way a seed springs up in the wake of a flood, and their burns fade away. Then he [the one brought out] keeps asking until he is given this world and ten times its like along with it."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

This is part of a long hadith describing the stages of the Day of Resurrection. The ellipsis (...) at its opening signals that the description of earlier scenes preceding this passage has been left out here, without that omission affecting the meaning of the passage itself. Through this chain, the hadith is Jabir's own words, may Allah be pleased with him, and does not explicitly attribute them to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. Al-Nawawi relates from al-Qadi 'Iyad that Muslim included it among the Prophet's hadiths because it was also narrated from him through another route; and a report about the unseen like this one is not said from personal opinion, so it carries the ruling of a prophetic statement. It shows that intercession reaches as far as bringing out whoever said "There is no god but Allah" and had in his heart good weighing even a grain of barley. They are then healed of the Fire's marks by the water of Jannah, as withered plants revive after a flood, and then the one brought out of the Fire keeps asking until he is given this world and ten times its like. The pronoun in "asks" refers to the one brought out of the Fire, as al-Nawawi says, and it comes in the singular.[^6]

#### Lesson Explanation

This hadith sets the story of "the last to enter Jannah" in its wider context. Release from the Fire through intercession and mercy is not some rare, one-off case; it includes everyone who died affirming Allah's oneness with even the least good in his heart. Such a person is first healed of the Fire's traces, and then we are told that the one brought out keeps asking until he is given the like of this world and ten times as much. This widens the hope of every believer, however heavy his sins, so long as the root of tawhid remains with him.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-53" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### Do Not Despair of the Mercy of Allah

> **{قُلْ يَا عِبَادِيَ الَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِنْ رَحْمَةِ اللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْغَفُورُ الرَّحِيمُ}** [الزُّمَرِ: ٥٣][^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Say: O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the All-Forgiving, the Most Merciful."** (az-Zumar 39:53)[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, explained that this ayah is a call to all who have sinned, however far they have gone in wronging themselves, to repent and turn back to Allah, and a declaration that Allah forgives all sins, however many, for whoever repents of them and turns away from them. He stressed that the ayah cannot be read as applying without repentance, since shirk is not forgiven for one who does not repent of it. He also cited Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, as saying that it is the ayah of the Qur'an that brings the greatest relief.

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah meets every hadith that came before it in a single meaning. The last man to enter Jannah, the man whose sins were exchanged for good deeds, and those whom intercession brought out of the Fire are all living images of what this ayah means: that no one should despair of Allah's mercy, however far from deserving it he may consider himself. The ayah is at root a call to repentance, as explained above; the hope of a believer in Allah's oneness who dies without repenting rests instead on His words, `Indeed, Allah does not forgive that partners be associated with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills` (an-Nisa' 4:48),[^5] and on the hadiths of intercession.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

These four texts tell a single story from different angles. The hadith of Ibn Mas'ud captures the moment of entry and the immensity of the gift; the hadith of Abu Dharr reveals the scene of minor sins being exchanged for good deeds for one described in the very same words; the hadith of Jabir sets it within the wider scene of intercession; and the ayah of az-Zumar gathers it all into one direct command: do not despair. Allah's mercy, then, is not an abstract promise but a reality laid out in detail, scene by scene and cause by cause.

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. What is the wisdom in Allah letting the last man to enter Jannah see it as full, not once but twice, before giving him many times the like of this world?
2. How does the hadith of Abu Dharr bring together the laying out of minor sins and their exchange for good deeds?
3. What is the relationship between the hadith of intercession (Muslim 191) and the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud regarding the destiny of the last to leave the Fire?
4. How does the ayah of az-Zumar (39:53) bind these three hadiths into a single message?
5. How does this hadith change the way you look at a sin you consider minor, or a good deed you consider too small to matter?

### Activity: My World × 10

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.019.activity.ten-times-my-world" -->

List the five things you most love or long for in this world (wealth, health, standing, relationships, achievement). Then imagine every one of them multiplied ten times over, all at once, and write a line describing how that picture makes you feel. Next, read the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud again, and remember that this is precisely what has been promised to the lowest-ranking person in Jannah. Finally, choose one situation this week in which you will replace despair, or a dismissive attitude toward some small good deed, with sincere hope in the vastness of Allah's mercy.

### A True Hadith: The Last Person to Enter Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6571" authenticated="true" -->

**This is something true that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us about a man who will be the very last person to go into Jannah. Allah will say to him: `Go and enter Jannah!` Off the man goes, so happy. But then he thinks Jannah is all full up, with no room left for him. So he comes back and says, "My Lord, I found it full!" Allah tells him again: `Go and enter Jannah!` And the very same thing happens. Then, the third time, Allah says to him: `Go and enter Jannah, for you will have as much as the whole world, and ten times more as well!` The man is so amazed at this huge gift that he says, "Are You laughing at me, my Lord, when You are the great King?" Our Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he told us this story, because Allah gave this man, the very last one to go into Jannah, so much more than he ever thought he would get.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6571" audience="4-7" -->

So, put simply: a man thought Jannah was full and there would be no place for him, and Allah gave him far, far more than he could ever imagine. This teaches us that Allah's gifts and His love for us are bigger than anything we can picture.[^1]

## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="5.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told us that he knows who will be the last person to enter Jannah. That same person is also the last to come out of the Fire, after going through a time of cleansing because of sins he committed. He is the lowest-ranking of all the people of Jannah, and yet Allah gives him ten times more than this entire world! This teaches us that Allah's mercy is wider than anything we can imagine, and that the smallest gift He gives the lowest-ranking person is greater than everything the richest people on earth own, all put together.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6571" -->

- **`The lowest in rank among the people of Jannah (adna ahl al-jannah manzilatan)`** — the person with the lowest level in Jannah, who is also the last one allowed to enter it.
- **`Intercession (ash-shafa'ah)`** — when someone asks Allah to pardon another person or release them from punishment; on the Day of Resurrection, Allah gives His prophets and some of His righteous servants permission to do this.
- **`The like of this world and ten times as much (mithl ad-dunya wa 'asharatu amthaliha)`** — a way of describing a gift too big to picture, because it is ten times larger than the whole world.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: The Last Person to Enter Jannah

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6571" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "I know the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it, and the last of the people of Jannah to enter it." This man comes out of the Fire after spending in it as long as Allah willed because of his sins, and then Allah commands him to go to Jannah. Off he goes, but when he sees how many people have already entered before him, he decides it must be completely full, with no space left for him! So he returns to his Lord and says, "My Lord, I found it full!"

Allah tells him once more: "Go and enter Jannah." He goes, and exactly the same thing happens a second time. Then, the third time, Allah says to him: "Go and enter Jannah, for you shall have the like of this world and ten times as much!" The man is so stunned by the size of this gift that he says, "Are You making fun of me, when You are the Mighty King?" And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he told this story to his Companions, so hard that his back teeth showed. He laughed because Allah makes fun of no one. Instead, He gave this man, even though he was the lowest-ranking person in Jannah, a gift beyond anyone's wildest imagination.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6571" audience="8-12" -->

In other words: a man came out of the Fire late because of his sins. Allah commanded him to enter Jannah, but twice he thought it was full and that there was no room left for him. Then, the third time, Allah gave him the like of the whole world and ten times as much. He was astonished at how enormous the gift was, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he told it.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The hadith of "the last person to enter Jannah" is one of the greatest hadiths showing just how vast Allah's mercy is. The man in it is a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness, yet committed sins that earned him a period of time in the Fire. When that period came to an end, he was brought out by Allah's mercy, after the intercessors had interceded, and admitted into Jannah, the last of all to enter it and the lowest of its people in rank. And still, what he is given there is ten times greater than this entire world. That gives you two lessons that fit together. First, sins, however many, never cut a person of tawhid off from hope in Allah's mercy for good. Second, the lowest bliss of Jannah is greater than the highest anyone could ever reach in this world. So what about those ranked higher?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-6571" -->

- **`Cleansing in the Fire (at-tathir fi an-nar)`** — some sinful believers remain in the Fire for a period Allah wills, to cleanse them of their sins, and are then brought out by His mercy and the intercession of the intercessors. This applies only to someone who died affirming Allah's oneness and never associated partners with Him.
- **`Bad deeds exchanged for good (tabdil as-sayyi'at hasanat)`** — on the Day of Resurrection, Allah exchanges some of His servant's minor sins for good deeds, purely out of His mercy, not because the servant has earned it.
- **`Refusing to despair ('adam al-qunut)`** — complete certainty that Allah's mercy is wider than any sin, and that no one is permitted to despair of it, however great their sins.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Hadith: As Narrated by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, May Allah Be Pleased with Him

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6571" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "I know the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it, and the last of the people of Jannah to enter it: a man who comes out of the Fire stumbling and falling." This man is no strange exception. He is a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness but committed sins that earned him the Fire, and so he remained in it as long as Allah willed, until it had cleansed him of their traces. Then he was brought out by Allah's mercy, after the intercessors had interceded, the last of all to leave, and commanded to enter Jannah, the last of all to enter.

He sets off for Jannah, but when he sees the multitudes of creation who have reached it before him, it seems to him that it is full and that no place remains for him. So he goes back to his Lord and says, "My Lord, I found it full!" Allah commands him to enter a second time, and the same thing happens again, until, the third time, Allah says to him: "Go and enter Jannah, for you shall have the like of this world and ten times as much." Here the man's astonishment reaches its peak, and he says: "Are You mocking me, though You are the King?" The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he described the scene to his Companions, because Allah ridicules no one. This is simply what His power and His limitless generosity look like.

Some scholars have held that the hadith of Abu Dharr (Muslim 190), which describes him in the very same words, reveals another side of this same man's story: Allah lays his minor sins before him so that he acknowledges them, then exchanges them for good deeds, until the man himself is amazed at his Lord's favor toward him. And the hadith of Jabir (Muslim 191) places this destiny within the wider scene of intercession on the Day of Resurrection, a scene that includes everyone who died affirming Allah's oneness, even if their hearts held only the barest trace of good.[^1]

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-6571" audience="13+" -->

To put it another way: a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness deserved the Fire because of his sins, and remained in it until it had cleansed him. Then Allah's mercy brought him out, after the intercessors had interceded, the very last to leave. He was commanded to enter Jannah, yet twice he thought it was full, until, the third time, Allah gave him the like of this world and ten times as much. He was so astonished that he took it for a joke, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, laughed as he told it, in wonder at the breadth of Allah's generosity.[^1][^2][^3]

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. How does this hadith show that entering the Fire does not necessarily mean remaining in it forever, for someone who died affirming Allah's oneness?
2. Why is a gift of "the like of this world and ten times as much" to the lowest-ranking person in Jannah such clear proof of the vastness of Allah's mercy in particular?
3. How does this hadith hold together a warning to the believer against sin and a refusal to let him despair of Allah's mercy?
4. Connect this hadith to the ayah `Do not despair of the mercy of Allah`: what message do they share?
5. What sin or shortcoming sometimes makes you feel cut off from hope in Allah's mercy, and how does this hadith change the way you see it?

<!-- unit:end -->

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="45" -->

### Adults — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains the story of the last person to enter Jannah, connects the hadiths of Ibn Mas'ud and Abu Dharr with the hadith of intercession and the ayah of az-Zumar, and applies the principle of "never despairing of Allah's mercy" to a real situation in their own life.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A copy of the lesson; a mushaf for referring to the ayah of az-Zumar; sheets for the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the three hadiths and the ayah, along with their sources and gradings, and prepares a neutral personal example of hoping for Allah's mercy after a shortcoming.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask the learners: "Have you ever felt that some sin had put you out of reach of Allah's mercy?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 15 minutes:** Read the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud in full, pausing each time the man turns back; then the hadith of Abu Dharr; then the passage from the hadith of intercession; then the ayah of az-Zumar 39:53.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher discusses with the learners how this man can be at once a sinner and the recipient of a gift ten times greater than this world, and connects this to the meaning of never despairing.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Learners carry out the "My World × 10" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each learner is asked to say aloud how this lesson has changed the way they view a sin or shortcoming they had thought of as minor.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Beginners work only with the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud and the ayah of az-Zumar; advanced learners discuss the relationship among the three hadiths in greater depth.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Take care that the discussion does not turn into a public confession of sensitive sins; participation stays voluntary and general. The hadith must not be used to make sins seem trivial, but to strengthen hope while keeping wary of them.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="20" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 20 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child can retell the main idea of the story: Allah's gifts are far bigger than we can imagine, and this means we never give up hope in His love.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Drawing paper; child-safe crayons or markers; a du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver prepares the simplified story and practices a cheerful tone of voice for the repeated line "Go and enter Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "Would you like a surprise that is much, much bigger than you expected?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 6 minutes:** Tell the simplified story in an engaging voice, focusing on the happy surprise at the end rather than on any details of the Fire.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 3 minutes:** The caregiver explains that Allah loves His servants very much, and that His gifts are always bigger than we imagine.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 5 minutes:** The child carries out the "×10" drawing activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Observe whether the child can say, in one simple sentence, what Allah gave the man in the end; then read the du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For younger children, simply say "a very, very big gift" instead of giving the exact numbers.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Avoid any frightening description of the Fire or of punishment; keep the telling entirely focused on joy, the happy surprise, and Allah's mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="30" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 30 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student explains the hadith of the last person to enter Jannah in detail, explains the meaning of the three terms, and connects the story to the vastness of Allah's mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the hadith; the term cards; sheets for the activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the three terms and prepares a simple example to explain the meaning of intercession.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask the students: "What is the biggest gift you can possibly imagine?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Read the full story, explaining the three terms as they come up.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher discusses with the students why the man thought Jannah was full twice, and why his gift in the end was so enormous even though he was the lowest in rank.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 5 minutes:** Students carry out the "My World × 10" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 2 minutes:** Assess the students' answers by how accurately they explain the meaning of "the like of this world and ten times as much."

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, present the story in three illustrated stages; ask advanced students to compare this hadith with the hadith of Abu Dharr.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Mention the man's coming out of the Fire briefly and without frightening detail, stressing that it is over and he is completely saved from it; do not dwell on descriptions of its punishment.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="35" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 35 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes the relationship between cleansing in the Fire and the vastness of Allah's mercy; discusses the danger of despairing of Allah's mercy on the one hand and the danger of complacently relying on His pardon on the other; and carries out a personal activity linking the story to their own life.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the three hadiths; the ayah of az-Zumar 39:53; a private activity sheet.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the gradings and sources of the three hadiths, and prepares a balanced discussion of hope and fear.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Pose the question: "Can a serious sin and a tremendous gift from Allah ever go together? How?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the three hadiths and the ayah of az-Zumar, discussing how each completes the others to form a full picture of Allah's mercy.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 8 minutes:** The teacher explains that hoping for Allah's mercy does not mean taking sins lightly; it means that a sinner never gives up on repentance and returning to Allah, and that this balance is the way of Ahl as-Sunnah.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Students carry out the "My World × 10" activity as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Each student writes a short paragraph showing how fear of sin and hope in mercy fit together without contradiction.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, use only the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud and the ayah of az-Zumar; advanced students discuss the difference between this hadith and the fate of someone who died upon shirk.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** The teacher should take care that the hadith is not used to justify treating sins lightly or postponing repentance, and should avoid dwelling on painful details of punishment or stirring up excessive anxiety in anyone who already carries a heavy sense of guilt; a student who shows signs of excessive anxiety should be gently guided into a supportive one-on-one conversation.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
