# The Last Person to Enter Jannah

## Lesson Objectives And Outcomes

In this lesson, the learner meets the hadith of the last man to enter Jannah — who is also the very last person to come out of the Fire. He thinks Jannah has already filled up and there is no place left for him, yet Allah gives him the equivalent of the whole world and ten times more, until he wonders whether Allah is joking with him from the sheer size of the gift. The learner comes to understand that this man, despite being the lowest in rank among the people of Jannah, receives a gift beyond imagining — proof that Allah's mercy is wider than anything we can picture, and that no one should ever despair of it, no matter how small or flawed they feel their own deeds to be. By the end of the lesson, each learner, according to their age, should leave with renewed hope in the vastness of Allah's mercy, and certainty that even the smallest gift from Him is greater than everything the people of this world possess put together.

### For Adults

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Part of Allah's vast mercy is that He does not leave a believer who affirmed His oneness in the Fire forever — He brings out of it everyone who had even the smallest speck of faith in his heart, however long it takes. The Prophet ﷺ told us that he knows the last man to be brought out of the Fire and the last person to enter Jannah: a man who crawls out of the Fire, weakened. Allah commands him to go and enter Jannah, so he goes to it, but it appears to him as though it has already filled up and there is no place left for him. He goes back and says, "My Lord, I found it full!" Allah commands him again to enter, and the same thing happens again, until on the third time Allah says to him, "Go and enter Jannah, for you will have the like of the world and ten times as much." The man is so overwhelmed by this gift that he says, "Are You mocking me, though You are the King?" The Prophet ﷺ laughed as he related this scene, because Allah does not mock anyone — rather, He gives a gift that surpasses all human imagining, such that even the lowest-ranked person in Jannah receives something no heart could conceive of.

This man is not a stranger to the ummah of Muhammad ﷺ: he is a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness, but he committed sins that made him deserving of time in the Fire, until that time ended, and the intercession of those who intercede and the mercy of the Most Merciful reached him, so he was brought out of it and admitted into Jannah — the very last person to enter it, and the lowest in rank there. Yet even so, his share of Allah's mercy and generosity is greater than the whole world and ten times over; so what, then, of those ranked above him? This hadith teaches the believer never to despair of Allah's mercy no matter how much he has sinned, and never to regard even the lowest rank with Allah as small — for in itself it is greater than everything the people of this world could ever wish for.

عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ مَسْعُودٍ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «إِنِّي لَأَعْلَمُ آخِرَ أَهْلِ النَّارِ خُرُوجًا مِنْهَا، وَآخِرَ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ دُخُولًا، رَجُلٌ يَخْرُجُ مِنَ النَّارِ حَبْوًا، فَيَقُولُ اللَّهُ: اذْهَبْ فَادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ. فَيَأْتِيهَا فَيُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ أَنَّهَا مَلْأَى، فَيَرْجِعُ فَيَقُولُ: يَا رَبِّ وَجَدْتُهَا مَلْأَى، فَيَقُولُ: اذْهَبْ فَادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ. فَيَأْتِيهَا فَيُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ أَنَّهَا مَلْأَى. فَيَقُولُ: يَا رَبِّ وَجَدْتُهَا مَلْأَى، فَيَقُولُ: اذْهَبْ فَادْخُلِ الْجَنَّةَ، فَإِنَّ لَكَ مِثْلَ الدُّنْيَا وَعَشَرَةَ أَمْثَالِهَا، أَوْ إِنَّ لَكَ مِثْلَ عَشَرَةِ أَمْثَالِ الدُّنْيَا، فَيَقُولُ: تَسْخَرُ مِنِّي، أَوْ تَضْحَكُ مِنِّي، وَأَنْتَ الْمَلِكُ؟». فَلَقَدْ رَأَيْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم ضَحِكَ حَتَّى بَدَتْ نَوَاجِذُهُ، وَكَانَ يُقَالُ ذَلِكَ أَدْنَى أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ مَنْزِلَةً[^1].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Narrated Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Prophet ﷺ said: **"I know the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it, and the last of the people of Jannah to enter it: a man who comes out of the Fire crawling. Allah says to him, 'Go and enter Jannah.' So he comes to it, and it appears to him as though it has filled up. He goes back and says, 'My Lord, I found it full!' Allah says, 'Go and enter Jannah.' The same thing happens to him again. Then Allah says to him, 'Go and enter Jannah, for you will have the like of the world and ten times as much — or you will have ten times the like of the world.' He says, 'Are You mocking me, or laughing at me, though You are the King?'"** I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ laugh until his molar teeth showed, and it used to be said that this is the lowest in rank among the people of Jannah.[^1]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** Imam al-Nawawi, in his commentary on Sahih Muslim, explains that "it appears to him as though it has filled up" means the man sees the great number of people who entered before him and so assumes there is no room left for him — this reflects his utmost humility and low estimation of himself. His question, "Are You mocking me?" is not doubt in Allah's truthfulness, but astonishment at generosity so immense he had never imagined it, so much so that it seemed closer to jest than to seriousness — whereupon Allah made clear this was His true gift, and the Prophet's ﷺ laughter while narrating it was one of wonder and glad tidings at the vastness of Allah's mercy, not mockery.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This hadith is the very center of the whole lesson: the lowest rank in Jannah — for the person who did the least good and was the last to enter — still exceeds the entire world ten times over. If this is the share of the lowest, what then of everyone ranked above him? This alone should settle the heart of every believer, however small they judge their deeds to be, and should cut off every trace of despair in Allah's mercy no matter how many the sins.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-190" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->
عَنْ أَبِي ذَرٍّ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: «إِنِّي لَأَعْلَمُ آخِرَ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ دُخُولًا الْجَنَّةَ، وَآخِرَ أَهْلِ النَّارِ خُرُوجًا مِنْهَا، رَجُلٌ يُؤْتَى بِهِ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ فَيُقَالُ: اعْرِضُوا عَلَيْهِ صِغَارَ ذُنُوبِهِ وَارْفَعُوا عَنْهُ كِبَارَهَا. فَتُعْرَضُ عَلَيْهِ صِغَارُ ذُنُوبِهِ فَيُقَالُ: عَمِلْتَ يَوْمَ كَذَا وَكَذَا كَذَا وَكَذَا، وَعَمِلْتَ يَوْمَ كَذَا وَكَذَا كَذَا وَكَذَا. فَيَقُولُ: نَعَمْ، لَا يَسْتَطِيعُ أَنْ يُنْكِرَ، وَهُوَ مُشْفِقٌ مِنْ كِبَارِ ذُنُوبِهِ أَنْ تُعْرَضَ عَلَيْهِ. فَيُقَالُ لَهُ: فَإِنَّ لَكَ مَكَانَ كُلِّ سَيِّئَةٍ حَسَنَةً. فَيَقُولُ: رَبِّ، قَدْ عَمِلْتُ أَشْيَاءَ لَا أَرَاهَا هَاهُنَا». فَلَقَدْ رَأَيْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم ضَحِكَ حَتَّى بَدَتْ نَوَاجِذُهُ[^2].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Narrated Abu Dharr, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **"I know the last of the people of Jannah to enter it, and the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it: a man who is brought on the Day of Resurrection, and it is said, 'Show him his minor sins and withhold from him his major ones.' His minor sins are shown to him and it is said, 'You did such-and-such on such-and-such a day, and you did such-and-such on such-and-such a day.' He says, 'Yes,' unable to deny it, fearful that his major sins will be shown to him. Then it is said to him, 'For every evil deed you will have a good deed in its place.' He says, 'My Lord, I did things I do not see here!'"** I saw the Messenger of Allah ﷺ laugh until his molar teeth showed.[^2]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** Scholars explain that this hadith describes the same person mentioned in the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud, but from another angle: after he comes out of the Fire and is led toward Jannah, his minor sins are shown to him so he acknowledges them, while his major sins are lifted from him out of mercy and concealment. Then his minor evil deeds are converted into good deeds, until he himself is amazed at Allah's immense favor upon him and says, "I did things I do not see here" — meaning he committed many sins he expected to be held accountable for, yet does not find them.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This hadith makes the meaning even clearer: converting bad deeds into good ones is another picture of Allah's mercy surpassing all human reckoning — which allows even someone with many sins to never despair of their Lord's mercy, but to hope that they too may be among those whose evil deeds Allah has exchanged for good ones.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-191" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->
عَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رضي الله عنهما، أَنَّهُ سُئِلَ عَنِ الْوُرُودِ، فَقَالَ: ... ثُمَّ تَحِلُّ الشَّفَاعَةُ، وَيَشْفَعُونَ حَتَّى يَخْرُجَ مِنَ النَّارِ مَنْ قَالَ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَكَانَ فِي قَلْبِهِ مِنَ الْخَيْرِ مَا يَزِنُ شَعِيرَةً، فَيُجْعَلُونَ بِفِنَاءِ الْجَنَّةِ، وَيَجْعَلُ أَهْلُ الْجَنَّةِ يَرُشُّونَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْمَاءَ حَتَّى يَنْبُتُوا نَبَاتَ الشَّيْءِ فِي السَّيْلِ، وَيَذْهَبُ حُرَاقُهُ، ثُمَّ يَسْأَلُ حَتَّى تُجْعَلَ لَهُ الدُّنْيَا وَعَشَرَةُ أَمْثَالِهَا مَعَهَا[^3].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Narrated Jabir ibn Abdullah, may Allah be pleased with him and his father, when he was asked about the arrival at the Fire, and he described the scene of the Day of Resurrection, then said: **"...Then intercession will be permitted, and intercessors will intercede until there comes out of the Fire everyone who said, 'There is no god but Allah,' and who had in his heart good equal to the weight of a barley grain. They will be placed at the courtyard of Jannah, and the people of Jannah will sprinkle water over them until they sprout like a thing carried by a flood, and the traces of their burns disappear. Then each of them asks, until he is given the world and ten times its like along with it."**[^3]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** This is part of a longer hadith describing the stages of the Day of Resurrection. It states that the last to come out of the Fire are those who spoke the words of monotheism, even if only the smallest amount of good remained in their hearts. Allah brings them out through the intercession of those who intercede, then heals them of the Fire's traces with the water of Jannah, just as wilted plants are revived by floodwater — after which they are given, beyond that, a gift equal to the world ten times over.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This hadith makes clear that the story of "the last person to enter Jannah" is not a single, rare case, but a pattern repeated with everyone Allah brings out of the Fire through intercession or mercy: each is first healed of the effects of their sins, then given a gift beyond all reckoning. This widens the hope of every believer, however heavy their sins, so long as the root of monotheism remains with them.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-53" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->
{قُلْ يَا عِبَادِيَ الَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِنْ رَحْمَةِ اللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّهُ هُوَ الْغَفُورُ الرَّحِيمُ} [الزُّمَرِ: ٥٣][^4].

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"Say, 'O My servants who have transgressed against themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful.'"** (Az-Zumar 39:53)[^4]

**Scholarly Interpretation:** Al-Hafiz Ibn Kathir, may Allah have mercy on him, notes that this is among the most hope-filled verses in the Qur'an: a direct call from Allah to His sinning servants, however great their transgression against themselves, not to despair of His mercy — and that the word "all" covers every sin without exception, for whoever repents or whomever Allah wills to forgive.

**Explanation And Connection To The Lesson:** This verse is the Qur'anic foundation beneath everything above: the last person to enter Jannah, those whose evil deeds are converted to good ones, and those brought out of the Fire through intercession — all are living pictures of this verse's meaning: no one should ever despair of Allah's mercy, however far they may feel from deserving it.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### For Children Ages 8–12

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The Prophet ﷺ told us that he knows the last person to enter Jannah — who is also the very last person to come out of the Fire, after a period of being purified for sins he had committed. <!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.terms" kind="terminology" -->
<!-- terminology:start -->
**Lowest Rank In Jannah:** the least of all the ranks in Jannah, belonging to the last person allowed to enter it.
**Intercession (Shafa'ah):** when someone asks Allah to forgive another person or bring them out of punishment, and Allah permits His prophets and some of His righteous servants to do this on the Day of Judgment.
**"The World And Ten Times As Much":** an expression for a gift too enormous to picture, since it is bigger than the whole world, ten times over.
<!-- terminology:end -->

### For Teens 13+

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

The hadith of "the last person to enter Jannah" is one of the greatest hadiths on the vastness of Allah's mercy. This man is a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness but committed sins that made him deserving of time in the Fire, until that time ended and Allah's mercy and the intercession of those who intercede reached him, so he was brought out and admitted into Jannah — the very last to enter it, and the lowest ranked there. Yet his gift in Jannah still exceeds the entire world, ten times over. This teaches the teen two linked lessons: first, that sins, however many, do not permanently sever a person of true monotheism from hope in Allah's mercy; and second, that even the lowest reward in Jannah surpasses the highest a human could ever reach in this world — so what, then, of those ranked above him?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.story" kind="story" -->
<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="hadith" source_id="bukhari-6571" authenticated="true" -->

Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated that the Prophet ﷺ said, "I know the last of the people of the Fire to come out of it, and the last of the people of Jannah to enter it: a man who comes out of the Fire crawling." This man is not some extraordinary stranger — he is a believer who affirmed Allah's oneness but committed serious sins, so he remained in the Fire until it purified him of the effects of his sins. Then the intercession of those who intercede and Allah's mercy reached him, so he was brought out — the very last to leave it — and commanded to enter Jannah — the very last to enter it.

He goes to Jannah, but seeing the multitude of creation who entered before him, it appears to him as though it has filled up and there is no place left for him. He goes back to his Lord and says, "My Lord, I found it full!" Allah commands him to enter again, and the same thing happens, until on the third time Allah says to him, "Go and enter Jannah, for you will have the like of the world and ten times as much." Here the man's astonishment reaches its peak, and he says, "Are You mocking me, though You are the King?" The Prophet ﷺ laughed as he related the scene to his companions, because Allah never mocks anyone — this is simply the nature of His limitless power and generosity. Scholars have noted that this man, despite being the lowest-ranked in Jannah, has another side to his story shown in the hadith of Abu Dharr (Muslim 190): Allah shows him his minor sins so he acknowledges them, then converts them into good deeds, until he himself is amazed at his Lord's favor upon him.

<!-- story:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terminology" -->
<!-- terminology:start -->
**Purification In The Fire:** some monotheists who sinned remain in the Fire for a period Allah wills, to be purified of their sins, then are brought out by His mercy and the intercession of those who intercede — this applies specifically to those who died upon monotheism without associating partners with Allah.
**Converting Evil Deeds Into Good Ones:** Allah replacing some of a servant's minor sins with good deeds on the Day of Judgment, out of His mercy, not because the servant earned it.
**Never Despairing:** the complete certainty that Allah's mercy is wider than any sin, and that no one may give up hope in it, however great their sins may be.
<!-- terminology:end -->
<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

1. How does this hadith show that entering the Fire does not necessarily mean eternal residence there for one who died a monotheist?
2. Why is a gift of "the world and ten times as much" for the lowest-ranked person in Jannah, specifically, proof of the vastness of Allah's mercy?
3. How does this hadith balance warning the believer against sin with never letting him despair of Allah's mercy?
4. Connect this hadith to the verse "do not despair of the mercy of Allah" in terms of their shared message.
5. What sin or shortcoming sometimes makes you feel distant from hope in Allah's mercy, and how does this hadith change how you see it?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->
<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.019.activity.ten-times-my-world" -->

Write a list of five things in "your world": something you own, a relationship you treasure, an accomplishment you achieved, a dream you're pursuing, and something you wish you had. Then imagine each one multiplied ten times over, all at once, and write a short paragraph describing how that feels. Afterward, reflect that this is exactly what Allah promised the lowest-ranked person in Jannah, and write down one situation in your life where you will replace despair over a sin or shortcoming with sincere hope — along with one practical commitment for this week.

## Detailed Lesson Plans For Teachers

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="45" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
Learners explain the story of the last person to enter Jannah, connect the hadiths of Ibn Mas'ud and Abu Dharr with the hadith of intercession, and apply the principle of "never despairing of Allah's mercy" to a real situation in their own lives.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
Copy of the lesson, a Qur'an for reference to Az-Zumar, activity worksheets.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The teacher reviews the three hadiths, their sources and gradings, and prepares a neutral personal example about hoping in Allah's mercy after a shortcoming.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
Ask learners: Have you ever thought some sin might put you beyond Allah's mercy?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
Read the full hadith of Ibn Mas'ud, pausing at each point the man returns, then the hadith of Abu Dharr, then an excerpt from the hadith of intercession, then Az-Zumar 39:53.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
Discuss how this man can be both sinful and yet receive a gift ten times the world, and connect this to the meaning of never despairing.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
Run the "My World ×10" activity as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
Ask each learner to share verbally how their view of a sin or shortcoming they tend to underestimate has changed after this lesson.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
Beginners may focus on the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud and Az-Zumar 39:53 alone; advanced learners discuss the relationship among all three hadiths in more depth.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
Ensure discussion never turns into public confession of sensitive sins; keep sharing optional and general, and never use this hadith to minimize sin — only to strengthen hope alongside continued caution against it.
<!-- safeguards:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="20" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The child can retell the core idea of the story: that Allah's gift is much bigger than we imagine, and that this means we should never lose hope in His love.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
Drawing paper, safe coloring supplies.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The educator prepares the simplified story and practices a cheerful tone for the repeated "go and enter Jannah" line.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
Ask the child: Do you like getting a surprise that's much bigger than you expected?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
Tell the simplified story in an engaging voice, focusing on the happy surprise ending rather than any details of the Fire.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
The educator explains that Allah loves His servants very much, and His gifts are always bigger than we imagine.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
Run the "×10" drawing activity as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
Observe whether the child can retell in a simple sentence what Allah gave the man in the end.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
For younger children, it's enough to say "a really, really big gift" instead of exact numbers.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
Avoid any frightening description of the Fire or punishment; keep the telling entirely focused on joy, surprise, and Allah's mercy.
<!-- safeguards:end -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The student explains the hadith of the last person to enter Jannah in detail, defines the three key terms, and connects the story to the meaning of Allah's vast mercy.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
Hadith text, term cards, activity worksheets.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The teacher reviews the three terms and prepares a simple example to explain intercession.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
Ask students: What's the biggest gift you can imagine?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
Read the full story, explaining the three terms as they come up.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
Discuss with students why the man thought Jannah had filled up twice, and why his final gift was so enormous despite his being the lowest-ranked person.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
Run the "My World ×10" activity as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
Assess student answers by how accurately they explain the meaning of "the world and ten times as much."
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
For struggling students, present the story broken into three illustrated stages; advanced students compare this hadith with the hadith of Abu Dharr.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
Mention the man's coming out of the Fire only briefly and non-frighteningly, emphasizing that it is over and he is fully safe now, without elaborating on descriptions of its punishment.

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="35" -->

<!-- outcomes:start -->
The student analyzes the relationship between purification in the Fire and the vastness of Allah's mercy, discusses the danger of despairing of Allah's mercy on one hand and relying on sin on the other, and completes a personal activity connecting the story to their own life.
<!-- outcomes:end -->
<!-- materials:start -->
Text of the three hadiths, Az-Zumar 39:53, a dedicated activity worksheet.
<!-- materials:end -->
<!-- preparation:start -->
The teacher reviews the gradings and sources of the three hadiths and prepares a balanced discussion between hope and fear.
<!-- preparation:end -->
<!-- opening:start -->
Pose the question: Can a great sin coexist with a great gift from Allah? How?
<!-- opening:end -->
<!-- evidence:start -->
Read the three hadiths and Az-Zumar 39:53, discussing how they complete one another into a full picture of Allah's mercy.
<!-- evidence:end -->
<!-- instruction:start -->
Explain that hope in Allah's mercy does not mean being lax about sin, but means the sinner never despairs of repentance and returning — this balance is the way of Ahl al-Sunnah.
<!-- instruction:end -->
<!-- activity:start -->
Run the "My World ×10" activity as described in the activity unit.
<!-- activity:end -->
<!-- assessment:start -->
The student writes a short paragraph linking the concepts of fearing sin and hoping in mercy without contradiction.
<!-- assessment:end -->
<!-- differentiation:start -->
Struggling students may focus on the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud and Az-Zumar 39:53 alone; advanced students discuss the difference between this hadith's subject and the fate of one who died upon shirk.
<!-- differentiation:end -->
<!-- safeguards:start -->
The teacher must avoid letting this hadith be used to justify carelessness about sin or delaying repentance, and must avoid dwelling on painful details of punishment or provoking excessive anxiety in someone who feels weighed down by sin; direct anyone showing excessive distress to a supportive one-on-one conversation.
<!-- safeguards:end -->
