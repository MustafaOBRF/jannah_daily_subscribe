# Remembrance, the Qur'an, and Glorifying Allah in Jannah

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Explain that Jannah is an abode of reward, not of religious obligation, and that remembrance of Allah (dhikr) continues there as a delight rather than a duty, citing Ibn al-Qayyim's chapter heading: "On how acts of worship cease in Jannah, except the worship of dhikr, which is everlasting."
- Narrate the hadith of Jabir in Sahih Muslim (2835), "They will be inspired to glorify and praise Allah just as they are inspired to breathe," and explain, following Abu al-'Abbas al-Qurtubi, the point of the comparison with breathing.
- Recite the words of Allah Most High, "and it will be said: 'All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all the worlds'" (az-Zumar 39:75), and present what Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di said about why the speaker is left unnamed.
- Narrate the hadith of 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, "Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to recite in the world" (at-Tirmidhi 2914 and Abu Dawud 1464), and set out the scholars' disagreement over who is meant by "the companion of the Qur'an," together with 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr's own report about how often to complete the Qur'an (al-Bukhari 5054).
- Carry out the "Record, Listen, Recite Again" activity: recording a short recitation, listening back to choose one point of tartil to work on, practicing it for a set period, then recording a second time, comparing, and reflecting.
- Close the day with a lesson-authored du'a asking Allah to make His remembrance the comfort of our hearts, to make us love the Qur'an, and to make us among those who will be told in Jannah: "Recite and rise."

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah is an abode of reward, not of religious obligation (*taklif*): there is no prescribed prayer there, no fasting, no test. And yet the authentic texts tell us that the tongues of its people never stop glorifying and praising Allah, and that the companion of the Qur'an will be told there: "Recite." How can worship endure in a home from which obligation has been lifted?

This lesson brings together three truths:

1. **Dhikr is the worship that remains.** In *Hadi al-Arwah*, Ibn al-Qayyim gave a chapter the heading "Section: On how acts of worship cease in Jannah, except the worship of dhikr, which is everlasting." Under it he cited the hadith of Jabir and then said: "That is, their glorification and praise flow with their breaths, just as you are inspired to breathe."[^5]

2. **Inspiration, not obligation.** Glorifying Allah there is not done for a separate reward; it is itself a pleasure. Al-Ashqar quotes Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah as saying: "This is not an act of obligation for which a separate reward is sought; rather, the act itself is part of the bliss in which souls take delight and pleasure."[^6]

3. **The Qur'an is recited there, and by it one rises.** The companion of the Qur'an will be told: "Recite, rise, and recite with measured care." The recitation a believer builds in this world is the very recitation he will recite with there.

The seeds of Jannah planted through dhikr in this world, the final call of the people of Jannah, and Allah's speech to the people of Jannah each have a lesson of their own.

## Central Evidence

<!-- evidence:start id="muslim-2835" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "They Will Be Inspired to Glorify Allah Just as They Are Inspired to Breathe"

> عَنْ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقُولُ: **«إِنَّ أَهْلَ الْجَنَّةِ يَأْكُلُونَ فِيهَا وَيَشْرَبُونَ، وَلَا يَتْفُلُونَ، وَلَا يَبُولُونَ، وَلَا يَتَغَوَّطُونَ، وَلَا يَمْتَخِطُونَ»**. قَالُوا: فَمَا بَالُ الطَّعَامِ؟ قَالَ: **«جُشَاءٌ وَرَشْحٌ كَرَشْحِ الْمِسْكِ، يُلْهَمُونَ التَّسْبِيحَ وَالتَّحْمِيدَ كَمَا يُلْهَمُونَ النَّفَسَ»**.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"The people of Jannah will eat and drink there, but they will not spit, nor urinate, nor relieve themselves, nor blow their noses."** They asked, "Then what becomes of the food?" He said: **"A belch, and a sweat like the scent of musk. They will be inspired to glorify and praise Allah just as they are inspired to breathe."**[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Abu al-'Abbas al-Qurtubi said: "This glorification does not come from obligation and compulsion, because Jannah is not a place of obligation but a place of reward; it comes rather from ease and inspiration." He then said: "The point of the comparison is that a person cannot do without breathing, and breathing costs him no effort or hardship," and: "The secret of this is that their hearts have been illuminated by knowledge of Him [...] and their hearts are filled with love of Him and intimate friendship with Him, so their tongues cling to His remembrance and are bound to His thanks; for whoever loves something mentions it often."[^2]

#### Lesson Explanation

The hadith comes in the context of food, but its closing words describe the whole state of the people of Jannah: remembrance that costs them no effort, just as none of us has to make an effort to breathe. Other narrations in Muslim read "*tasbih* and *hamd*" (glorification and praise) and "*tasbih* and *takbir*" (glorification and declaring Allah's greatness).[^1] In this world, dhikr is a struggle with the self and a habit built over time; in Jannah it becomes second nature, flowing with every breath.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-39-75" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "And It Will Be Said: 'All Praise Belongs to Allah, Lord of All the Worlds'"

> **{وَتَرَى الْمَلَائِكَةَ حَافِّينَ مِنْ حَوْلِ الْعَرْشِ يُسَبِّحُونَ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّهِمْ ۖ وَقُضِيَ بَيْنَهُم بِالْحَقِّ وَقِيلَ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ}** [الزمر: ٧٥][^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And you will see the angels encircling the Throne, glorifying the praise of their Lord. Judgment will have been passed between them in truth, and it will be said: 'All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all the worlds.'"** (az-Zumar 39:75)[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir said: "The whole of creation, those that speak and the dumb beasts alike, gave voice to Allah, Lord of all the worlds, praising Him for His judgment and His justice. This is why the saying is not ascribed to any particular speaker but left unqualified, which shows that all created beings bore witness to His praise."[^4] As-Sa'di said: "He did not mention who the speaker was, to show that all of creation voiced the praise of their Lord and of His wisdom in what He decreed for the people of Jannah and the people of the Fire: praise for His grace and kindness, and praise for His justice and wisdom."[^4]

#### Lesson Explanation

This ayah comes after the people of Jannah have been led to it in throngs and have settled in it. The whole scene closes with glorification and praise: the glorification of the angels, and a praise left open so that it embraces all of creation. Praise, then, is no passing word; it is the sound with which the great story ends.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="tirmidhi-2914" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Recite, Rise, and Recite with Measured Care"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَمْرٍو رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا، عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَالَ: **«يُقَالُ - يَعْنِي لِصَاحِبِ الْقُرْآنِ -: اقْرَأْ وَارْتَقِ وَرَتِّلْ كَمَا كُنْتَ تُرَتِّلُ فِي الدُّنْيَا، فَإِنَّ مَنْزِلَتَكَ عِنْدَ آخِرِ آيَةٍ تَقْرَأُ بِهَا»**.[^7]
>
> وَلَفْظُ أَبِي دَاوُدَ: **«يُقَالُ لِصَاحِبِ الْقُرْآنِ: اقْرَأْ وَارْتَقِ وَرَتِّلْ كَمَا كُنْتَ تُرَتِّلُ فِي الدُّنْيَا، فَإِنَّ مَنْزِلَكَ عِنْدَ آخِرِ آيَةٍ تَقْرَؤُهَا»**.[^7]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, may Allah be pleased with them both, reported that the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"It will be said (meaning to the companion of the Qur'an): 'Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to in the world, for your rank will be at the last ayah you recite.'"**[^7]
>
> In the wording of Abu Dawud: **"It will be said to the companion of the Qur'an: 'Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to in the world, for your dwelling will be at the last ayah you recite.'"**[^7]

#### Scholarly Explanation

In *Mir'at al-Mafatih*, al-Mubarakfuri explains: "'It will be said': that is, in the Hereafter, upon entering Jannah"; "'Recite and rise' is a command from *irtiqa'*, meaning: ascend"; and "'and recite with measured care': that is, recite with *tartil* and do not hurry your recitation, 'as you used to recite in the world,' giving the letters their due and knowing where to pause."[^8] The scholars differed over who is meant by "the companion of the Qur'an." In *Dalil al-Falihin*, Ibn 'Allan restricted it to the one who has memorized it "by heart."[^9] Al-Mubarakfuri quotes at-Turbushti as saying that the companion of the Qur'an "is the one who stays devoted to it with resolve and care, sometimes by memorizing and reciting it, and sometimes by pondering it and acting on it," and at-Turbushti preferred the second view as "the more fitting and the more complete of the two."[^8] Al-Mubarakfuri also quotes another statement: "This recitation is for them like glorification for the angels: it does not distract them from their pleasures; rather, it is the greatest of their pleasures."[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

Two things meet in this hadith: recitation in Jannah is a delight and an ascent, and the way one recites there is a continuation of the way one recites here: "as you used to recite in the world." The companionship a believer builds with the Qur'an today, through memorizing, reciting, pondering, and acting, is the very thing he will rise by tomorrow.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="bukhari-5054" kind="hadith" mode="canonical" -->

### "Then Complete It in Seven, and Do Not Go Beyond That"

> عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَمْرٍو رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«اقْرَإِ الْقُرْآنَ فِي شَهْرٍ»**. قُلْتُ: إِنِّي أَجِدُ قُوَّةً، حَتَّى قَالَ: **«فَاقْرَأْهُ فِي سَبْعٍ، وَلَا تَزِدْ عَلَى ذَلِكَ»**.[^10]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Complete the Qur'an in a month."** I said, "I find I have the strength for more," until he said: **"Then complete it in seven, and do not go beyond that."**[^10]

#### Scholarly Explanation

In *'Umdat al-Qari*, al-'Ayni said: "'And do not go beyond that': that is, beyond seven," and he noted that the prohibition may be "in view of the person addressed," "or that the prohibition is not one of outright forbiddance."[^11]

#### Lesson Explanation

The narrator of the hadith "Recite and rise" was a Companion who loved the Qur'an so much that he asked to recite more of it, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, guided him to an amount he could keep up. Companionship with the Qur'an is an unhurried devotion that lasts, not a burst of zeal that burns out.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### How Do These Texts Fit Together?

The hadith of Jabir describes remembrance in Jannah as a breath that costs no effort; the ayah of az-Zumar closes the scene of settling in with praise; the hadith of 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr makes recitation there an ascent measured by one's tartil here; and his own exchange with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, teaches that steady, careful recitation is worth more than hurried quantity. As for the report that the steps of Jannah equal the number of ayat in the Qur'an, and the reports in books on the description of Jannah about how its people recite, they have not been relied upon here.[^12]

## Questions for Understanding and Reflection

1. Why does the continuation of dhikr in Jannah not contradict its being an abode of reward rather than of obligation?

2. According to al-Qurtubi, what is the point of comparing glorification with breathing? And what secret did he give for why remembrance never ceases?

3. Why, according to Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di, is the speaker left unnamed in Allah's words "and it will be said: 'All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all the worlds'"?

4. What are the two views on who is meant by "the companion of the Qur'an"? Which of them is closer to where you are today?

5. What does 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr's report about how often to complete the Qur'an add to our understanding of "recite with measured care, as you used to in the world"?

### Activity: Record, Listen, Recite Again

<!-- activity:start audience="adults" concept_id="lesson.076.activity.record-listen-recite-again" -->

1. Choose a passage you usually recite in your prayer (three to ten ayat). Record yourself reciting it on your phone exactly as you recite it in prayer, with no retakes and no polishing, and do not post the recording.

2. Listen to the recording twice, alongside a reliable recitation of the same passage or with a skilled teacher. Write three observations, on pace, on pausing at the ends of ayat, and on letters and elongations (*madd*), then choose just one of them to work on.

3. For seven days, recite the same passage once a day, focusing on that one observation alone. On the seventh day, record the recitation again, listen to the two recordings back to back, and write three lines: What changed? What stayed the same? And with which recitation would you love to be told, "Recite with measured care, as you used to in the world"?

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Jana and the Surah Stairs

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.076.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith or a true historical report.**

After 'Asr, Jana sat with her grandpa on the steps of their house. She wanted to go and play ball with her brother, but Grandpa had said, "Recite Surat al-Ikhlas for me first."

Jana recited super fast, as if she were running a race, until the words all tumbled into each other. She was done in a flash. Then she jumped up. "Finished! Can I go now?"

Grandpa smiled. "Jana, do you know what someone who loves the Qur'an will be told in Jannah? They'll be told: Recite, and climb up, and recite slowly, the way you used to recite in this world."

"Climb up?" said Jana. "Like these stairs?"

"Let's play a game," said Grandpa. "Every time you recite an ayah slowly, you climb one step. If you rush, you stay where you are."

Jana stood at the bottom step. She recited the first ayah slowly and stopped at the end of it. Then she climbed one step. She recited the second ayah, and climbed again. Then the third, and then the fourth. When she reached the fourth step, she turned around and smiled at her grandpa.

"What beautiful reciting today!" said Grandpa. "I heard every single word."

Her little brother came running. "I want to play too!" So Jana took his hand, and she recited with him one ayah at a time, while he repeated after her and climbed.

That evening, at bedtime, Jana told her mom, "I'm always going to recite slowly, because that's how I want to recite in Jannah."

### A True Report: The Companion Who Loved the Qur'an

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-2914" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-2914" audience="4-7" -->

**This really happened. It is not a made-up story.**

A Companion named 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, may Allah be pleased with them both, loved the Qur'an very, very much, and he loved to recite lots of it. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him: "Recite the whole Qur'an in one month." 'Abd Allah said, "I'm strong, I can do more!" He kept asking for more, until the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him: "Recite it in seven days, and don't do more than that."[^10]

And this same 'Abd Allah is the one who told us what the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "The companion of the Qur'an will be told: Recite, rise, and recite with care, the way you used to recite in this world."[^7]

That means: whoever lived with the Qur'an will be told, "Recite, and climb up in Jannah, and recite slowly and beautifully, the way you used to recite in this world."

### Activity: Record, Listen, Recite Again

<!-- activity:start audience="4-7" concept_id="lesson.076.activity.record-listen-recite-again" -->

Jannah is a home of reward, with no required prayers and no duties. ### An Imagined Teaching Story: Yamin and the Star Chart

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.076.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith or a true historical report.**

The Qur'an circle at the masjid had a big chart on the wall, and every time a student recited a new page from memory, the teacher put a gold star by their name. Ten-year-old Yamin was racing his friend Faris: who would have the most stars by the end of the month?

On Tuesday, Yamin had half a page of Surat an-Naba' to learn. Faris whispered, "Hurry up! If you recite two pages today, you'll pass me." So Yamin crammed both pages and recited them like a sprint, swallowing letters and never pausing at the ends of the ayat.

The teacher gently stopped him. "Yamin, the words are right, but where's your tartil?" Then he told the circle, "The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: The companion of the Qur'an will be told, 'Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to in the world, for your dwelling will be at the last ayah you recite.' Listen closely: as you used to recite in the world."

Yamin thought it over. If he redid both pages slowly, there would be no star today, and Faris would pull ahead. But one phrase kept echoing in his head: "as you used to recite in the world."

Yamin raised his hand. "Teacher, can I just redo the first page, slowly? I'll do the second one tomorrow."

Yamin recited the first page again. He paused at the end of every ayah and gave every letter its due. And when he reached the ayat describing the reward of the God-fearing, he felt as if he were hearing them for the first time.

That day Yamin got one star, and Faris got two. Walking home, Yamin found himself saying "SubhanAllah" at every tree he passed, without trying to count anything.

At the end of the month, Yamin wasn't at the top of the chart. But the teacher recorded his recitation of Surat an-Naba', sent it to his father, and wrote: "A masterful recitation." And Faris said, "Teach me how to recite like that."

### A True Report: 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr and the Hadith "Recite and Rise"

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-2914" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-2914" audience="8-12" -->

**This is a true report found in Sahih al-Bukhari and in the collections of at-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud. It is not a made-up story.**

'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As, may Allah be pleased with them both, was a young man who loved worship and the Qur'an. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him: "Complete the Qur'an in a month." "I find I have the strength," 'Abd Allah replied, meaning he could do more. He kept asking for more, until he was told: "Then complete it in seven, and do not go beyond that."[^10]

And 'Abd Allah himself narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "It will be said to the companion of the Qur'an: 'Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to in the world, for your dwelling will be at the last ayah you recite.'"[^7]

The scholars explain that he will be told this when he enters Jannah; that "rise" means climb; and that "recite with measured care" means recite slowly and don't rush.[^8]

Notice: when this Companion wanted more and more, he was guided to an amount he could keep up. What matters is staying with the Qur'an always and reciting it beautifully.

- **`Inspired (yulhamun)`** — Allah makes it flow through their hearts and off their tongues without strain.[^2]
- **`Rise (irtaqi)`** — climb up through the levels of Jannah.[^8]
- **`Recite with measured care (rattil)`** — recite slowly, pronouncing the letters properly and knowing where to pause.[^8]
- **`The companion of the Qur'an (sahib al-Qur'an)`** — someone devoted to the Qur'an: memorizing, reciting, pondering, and acting on it.[^8]

### Understanding and Reflection Questions

1. Why did Yamin rush his recitation? And which phrase changed his mind?

2. What did Yamin lose that day? And what did he gain by the end of the month?

3. What did 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ask for? And how was he guided?

4. What does "They will be inspired to glorify Allah just as they are inspired to breathe" mean?

5. Name a surah you know by heart, and one thing you want to improve in your tartil of it.

### Activity: Record, Listen, Recite Again

<!-- activity:start audience="8-12" concept_id="lesson.076.activity.record-listen-recite-again" -->

1. Choose a short surah you know by heart. Ask one of your parents to record you reciting it the way you normally do.

2. Listen and check off a card with three boxes: pace; pausing at the ends of ayat; and a letter or an elongation (*madd*) you need to get right. Pick just one box to work on.

3. For five days, recite the surah every day, focusing on that box. On the fifth day, record yourself again. Then ask a family member to listen to both recordings, without telling them which came first, and say which sounds more beautiful. Write two sentences: What improved? And what will you work on next?

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah is a home of reward with no obligations, and yet the tongues of its people never stop remembering Allah: "They will be inspired to glorify and praise Allah just as they are inspired to breathe."[^1] Al-Qurtubi explained that breathing "costs a person no effort or hardship," that their hearts are filled with love of Allah, and that "whoever loves something mentions it often."[^2] And the companion of the Qur'an will be told: "Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to in the world, for your dwelling will be at the last ayah you recite."[^7] The scholars differed: is this the one who has memorized it, or the one who stays devoted to the Qur'an by reciting it, pondering it, and living by it?[^8] Either way, the recitation you will recite with there is built here, far from anyone's eyes.

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### An Imagined Teaching Story: Rana and the Recording She Never Posted

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="creative" source_id="lesson-authored:lesson.076.primary" authenticated="false" -->

**This is an imagined teaching story. It is not a hadith or a true historical report.**

Rana was fifteen and had a beautiful recitation voice. On a school trip, she recited some ayat of Surat ar-Rahman on the bus, and the whole bus went quiet. Then her friend Lama said, "Your voice is amazing! Record a clip and post it. You'll get thousands of views."

At home, Rana recorded the recitation ten times. Every time, the same thoughts ran through her head: Does my voice sound good enough? Will they like it? She added a pretty, blurred background and wrote a caption under the clip. Her finger hovered over the "Post" button.

Then she stopped. She played the clip again, and this time she listened not to her voice but to the recitation itself. And she noticed something: every time, she had been stretching the words wherever the melody pleased her, not where tartil requires, and running some ayat into others to make the recitation sound more moving.

She remembered that week's lesson: "Recite with measured care, as you used to in the world." And she asked herself, "Who am I reciting for? The views, or the recitation I want to recite with in Jannah?"

Rana didn't post the clip. The next night, she sent the recording to her teacher at the Qur'an school, and to no one else, with a note: "I want you to correct my recitation, not compliment it."

The reply was blunt: three mistakes in the elongations, and one pause in the wrong place. Rana felt a sting of disappointment. Then she started practicing.

A week later, Lama asked, "So where's the clip?" Rana said, "I'm still learning the recitation itself. And maybe the clip just stays between me and myself."

Lama laughed, but a moment later she said, "Could you give me your teacher's number?"

That night, Rana recorded the recitation once, with no retakes, and sent it to her grandmother. Her grandmother replied with a voice message: "May Allah bless you, Rana. That's the most beautiful recitation I've ever heard from you."

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

### A True Report: 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, Between Zeal and Steadiness

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="authenticated" type="hadith" source_id="tirmidhi-2914" authenticated="true" -->

<!-- retelling:start source_id="tirmidhi-2914" audience="13+" -->

**This is a true report in Sahih al-Bukhari, together with a hadith narrated by at-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud. It is not an imagined scene.**

'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As, may Allah be pleased with them both, was one of the Companions who loved worship. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him: "Complete the Qur'an in a month." 'Abd Allah said, "I find I have the strength for more." He kept asking for more, until the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, told him: "Then complete it in seven, and do not go beyond that."[^10] Al-'Ayni said that the prohibition may have been in view of the person addressed, or that it is not one of outright forbiddance.[^11]

This same Companion narrated from the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: "It will be said to the companion of the Qur'an: 'Recite, rise, and recite with measured care, as you used to in the world, for your dwelling will be at the last ayah you recite.'"[^7] At-Tirmidhi said: *hasan sahih*.[^7]

The two reports teach a single lesson: zeal on its own is not enough. The Qur'an is an unhurried companionship that lasts, and the tartil you will be asked for in Jannah is the tartil you made your habit in this world.

<!-- retelling:end -->

<!-- story:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="muslim-2835" -->

- **`Inspired (yulhamun)`** — placed in their hearts and flowing off their tongues with no strain, which is why it is compared to breathing.[^2]
- **`Religious obligation (at-taklif)`** — the commands and prohibitions by which a servant of Allah is tested in this world; they are lifted in Jannah.[^6]
- **`The companion of the Qur'an (sahib al-Qur'an)`** — for some scholars, the one who has memorized it; for others, the one who stays devoted to it by reciting it, pondering it, and acting on it.[^8]

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.questions" kind="questions" -->

### Discussion Questions

1. What made posting so tempting for Rana? And what did she notice once she listened to the recitation rather than to her voice?

2. Why did Rana ask her teacher for correction rather than compliments? And what did that cost her?

3. How is 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr's report about how often to complete the Qur'an connected to the hadith "Recite with measured care, as you used to in the world"?

4. How can dhikr be an effort in this world, yet like breathing in Jannah?

5. Do you think posting a recitation is always wrong? When can it be beneficial, and when does it spoil the intention?

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.activity" kind="activity" -->

### Activity: Record, Listen, Recite Again

<!-- activity:start audience="13+" concept_id="lesson.076.activity.record-listen-recite-again" -->

1. Choose a passage you love (three to seven ayat). Record yourself reciting it once, with no retakes, and keep the recording to yourself. Don't post it.

2. Listen to it alongside a reliable recitation of the same passage, or send it to one teacher you trust. In a private notebook, write two observations: one about your tartil (pace, pausing, a letter, an elongation), and one about yourself: what were you thinking about while you recorded?

3. For seven days, recite the passage every day, focusing on your tartil observation. On the seventh day, record yourself once more, listen to both recordings, and then write a private paragraph: What improved? And who were you reciting for, each time?

### Bedtime Du'a

<!-- bedtime-dua:start audience="13+" id="lesson.076.dua.dhikr-solace-love-quran-recite-and-rise" provenance="lesson_authored" source_id="" attribution="not_prophetic" -->

**Lesson-authored thematic du'a; not attributed to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.**

> اللَّهُمَّ اجْعَلْ ذِكْرَكَ أُنْسَ قُلُوبِنَا، وَحَبِّبْ إِلَيْنَا الْقُرْآنَ، وَاجْعَلْنَا مِمَّنْ يُقَالُ لَهُمْ فِي الْجَنَّةِ: اقْرَأْ وَارْتَقِ.
>
> *"O Allah, make Your remembrance the comfort of our hearts, make the Qur'an beloved to us, and make us among those who will be told in Jannah: 'Recite, and rise.'"*

This du'a asks for three things: that remembrance be a comfort, not a burden; that love of the Qur'an live in the heart before it reaches the tongue; and that our companionship with the Qur'an today be the means of our rising tomorrow.

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner explains how dhikr continues in Jannah even though obligation has been lifted; narrates the hadith of Jabir (Muslim 2835) and presents al-Qurtubi's explanation of the comparison with breathing; presents Ibn Kathir's and as-Sa'di's tafsir of the closing ayah of az-Zumar (39:75); narrates the hadith "Recite and rise" in both its wordings together with the disagreement over "the companion of the Qur'an," and links it to the report in al-Bukhari 5054; and begins the "Record, Listen, Recite Again" activity.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; a mushaf; a phone or recording device for each learner; a reliable recorded recitation of a short passage; the activity card (three observations, the chosen observation, the comparison); a board with two columns: "Dhikr in this world: effort and habit" and "Dhikr in Jannah: inspiration and delight."

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews Ibn al-Qayyim's chapter in *Hadi al-Arwah*, al-Qurtubi's commentary in *al-Mufhim* on Muslim 2835, Ibn Kathir's and as-Sa'di's tafsir of az-Zumar 39:75, the commentary on the hadith in *Mir'at al-Mafatih* and *Dalil al-Falihin*, and al-'Ayni's remarks on al-Bukhari 5054, and chooses a short passage for practice.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Which act of worship do you wish could stay with you in Jannah, and why?" Then: "There is no obligation in Jannah, so why do its people glorify Allah?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of Jabir, al-Qurtubi's words, and Ibn al-Qayyim's statement, along with what al-Ashqar quotes from Ibn Taymiyyah, pointing out that it reaches us through an intermediary. Then read the ayah of az-Zumar with the two tafsirs, then the hadith "Recite and rise" in the wordings of at-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud, presenting the disagreement over "the companion of the Qur'an," then the report in al-Bukhari 5054 with al-'Ayni's remarks. Learners fill in the two columns on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher leads a discussion: How does dhikr turn from an effort into a breath? What is the difference between the quantity of recitation and its quality? Why does the hadith tie tartil in Jannah to tartil "in the world"? The teacher points out that the report linking the number of Jannah's steps to the number of ayat has not been relied upon.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Each learner records their first take in a quiet spot, listens to it alongside the reliable recitation, writes their three observations, and chooses one, as described in the activity unit, then sets a date for the second recording seven days later.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Each learner writes down: the point of the comparison with breathing, the wording of the hadith "Recite and rise," and the two views on "the companion of the Qur'an." The group agrees that anyone who wishes may share their three lines a week later.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, a passage of three ayat and a single observation about pausing are enough. Ask advanced learners to compare the narrations of Muslim 2835 (*tahmid*, *hamd*, and *takbir*) and to read at-Turbushti's words in full in *Mir'at al-Mafatih*.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not make definite claims about the nature and details of how the people of Jannah recite beyond what the authentic text says, and do not rely on reports in books on the description of Jannah about the Qur'an being lifted from its people, or about how they recite, except to note that they are not established.[^12] Do not let the session get caught up in the question of Allah's speech to the people of Jannah, which has a lesson of its own. Learners' recordings are not posted, no one who stumbles in recitation is embarrassed, and everyone is encouraged to keep going.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child says that the people of Jannah say "SubhanAllah" and "Alhamdulillah" as easily as we breathe; tells that the companion of the Qur'an will be told, "Recite, and climb up"; recites a short surah slowly, stopping at the end of each ayah; and repeats the lesson's du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A short, safe staircase, or four large cards laid on the floor like steps; a phone for recording; the activity sheet with empty steps drawn on it; coloring pencils; the du'a card.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling Jana's story and the true report in simple words, lays the cards out on the floor, and makes sure the children know Surat al-Ikhlas or al-Fatihah by heart.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** The caregiver asks the children to take three slow breaths, then asks: "Did that make you tired? The people of Jannah say SubhanAllah just as easily as that."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Evidence — 7 minutes:** Tell Jana's story, then say, "And this one really happened," and tell the report of 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr and the hadith "Recite and rise" in its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** The children play the stairs game: every ayah recited slowly earns a step onto a card, and anyone who rushes stays put and gently tries again.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 8 minutes:** The caregiver records each child's recitation, they listen to it together, and the child chooses "I'll stop at the end of every ayah," as described in the activity unit. Explain to parents how to complete the three days and record again on the last day.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 3 minutes:** Ask the child: "What will the companion of the Qur'an be told in Jannah?" and "How do we recite?" Then read the du'a and its simple meaning.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, repeating two ayat after the caregiver is enough. Older children recite the whole surah and teach the stairs game to a sibling or a friend.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Never compare one child's voice with another's, and never laugh at a mistake. Do not describe what Jannah or its levels look like with details that have not been reported. Recordings stay with the parents and are not posted. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and the Companions are not drawn.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="8-12" minutes="40" -->

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student narrates the hadith "Recite and rise" and the report of 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr; explains the four terms; interprets "They will be inspired to glorify Allah just as they are inspired to breathe"; analyzes Yamin's choice between speed and tartil; and starts the activity card.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The text of the two hadiths written out; term cards; the three-box activity card; phones or a recording device; a star chart drawn on the board.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews the commentary on the hadith in *Mir'at al-Mafatih*, chooses a short surah the students know, and prepares a reliable recitation of it.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Which is better: memorizing two pages quickly, or one page really well? Why?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 10 minutes:** Tell the report of 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr about how often to complete the Qur'an, then the hadith "Recite and rise," then the hadith of Jabir about glorifying Allah like breathing, and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Yamin's story, and have the teacher pause at the moment he stops to think: "What would you have done?" Then students discuss in pairs: "What makes tartil more important than the number of stars?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Working in pairs, students record each other reciting the chosen surah, listen back, check the boxes on the card, and choose one box, as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Students complete the sentences "Rattil means..." and "The people of Jannah glorify Allah just as...". Then read the du'a and agree to listen to both recordings in five days.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, choose a three-ayah surah and the pausing box only. Ask advanced students to write a dialogue in which Yamin gently explains the hadith to Faris.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not use competition charts in a way that embarrasses slower memorizers, and make sure the story is not taken as criticizing memorization or legitimate competitions; its point is to put mastery first. Do not play students' recordings in front of others without their permission and their parents' permission.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The teen explains that dhikr continues in Jannah as inspiration rather than obligation; presents the disagreement over "the companion of the Qur'an"; links the report in al-Bukhari 5054 with the hadith "Recite and rise"; analyzes Rana's choice between posting and correction; and begins the activity, including the observation about intention.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Copies of the lesson; private notebooks; a reliable recitation of a short passage; unnamed cards; phones for private recording.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reviews al-Qurtubi's commentary on Muslim 2835, the words of at-Turbushti and Ibn 'Allan on "the companion of the Qur'an," and al-'Ayni's remarks on al-Bukhari 5054, and prepares the discussion questions.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** The teacher asks: "Have you ever changed the way you talk or recite because you knew someone was recording you?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Read the hadith of Jabir with al-Qurtubi's words, then the report of 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr about how often to complete the Qur'an, then the hadith "Recite and rise" with the two views on "the companion of the Qur'an," and explain the terms.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Read Rana's story, and have groups discuss: "What is the difference between listening to the voice and listening to the recitation? Why does asking for correction cost more than asking for praise? And when is posting a recitation beneficial?"

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Each student makes a single private recording of their passage, listens to it alongside the reliable recitation, and writes their two observations in their notebook, as described in the activity unit.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes on an unnamed card: "One thing I want to improve in my recitation, and one thing I want to set right in my intention." Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** Students who feel awkward hearing their own voice may simply listen, without sharing. Ask advanced students to compare the view of Ibn 'Allan with that of at-Turbushti and to explain how each shapes our understanding of the hadith.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** No one is asked to play their recording for the group, and private notebooks are not read. Do not teach that posting a recitation is forbidden in itself; the point is to watch one's intention. Do not make definite claims about the details of how the people of Jannah recite beyond what the text says.
