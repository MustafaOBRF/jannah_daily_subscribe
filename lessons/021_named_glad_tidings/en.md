# The Leaders of Jannah and Those Promised It by Name

## Lesson Objectives and Outcomes

After this lesson, the learner will be able to:

- Narrate the hadith of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, at the well of Aris (al-Bukhari 3674): how he made himself doorkeeper for the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and carried the glad tidings of Jannah to Abu Bakr, then Umar, then Uthman, may Allah be pleased with them, and how Uthman's glad tidings came paired with news of a trial that would befall him.
- Name the ten promised Jannah in the hadith of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with him (at-Tirmidhi 3747), and identify those whose leadership in Jannah is stated explicitly: al-Hasan and al-Husayn, the leaders of its youth (at-Tirmidhi 3768); Fatimah, the leader of its women (al-Bukhari 3623-3624); and recognize the merit of Maryam and Khadijah, the best of the women of their times (al-Bukhari 3432).
- Distinguish between bearing witness to Jannah for those whom revelation named and hoping for it for all other believers without declaring it certain for any particular person, and connect this to the ayah `The first forerunners... and those who followed them in excellence` (at-Tawbah 9:100), which leaves the door open for us to join them.
- Draw out the lessons that glad tidings did not make those who received them stop working, did not spare Uthman, may Allah be pleased with him, from trial, and that loving all the Companions and the Prophet's family is part of faith.
- Carry out the "Honest Glad-Tidings Card" activity: noticing a real good deed in someone else, then writing that person sincere encouragement together with a du'a for Jannah, while keeping what the learner actually witnessed separate from what they only hope for.
- Learn by heart the lesson's bedtime du'a asking Allah for love of His Prophet, his Companions and his family, and to be joined with them in Jannah.

## Academic Section for Adults

<!-- reader:start audience="adults" estimated_minutes="7.0" -->

<!-- unit:start id="adults.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Jannah is not won by lineage or by wishful thinking, and no one has the authority to declare that a particular person belongs to it unless revelation has said so. Yet it is part of Allah's mercy that He told His Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, the names of specific people who are in Jannah, so that these glad tidings became settled knowledge: the ten promised Jannah; al-Hasan and al-Husayn, the leaders of the youth of the people of Jannah; Fatimah, the leader of its women; Maryam and Khadijah, the best of the women of their times; and others about whom an authentic text has come.

The people of the Sunnah have laid down that we bear witness to Jannah for whomever the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, bore witness to; that we hope for the good-doers among the believers and fear for the wrongdoers; and that we assign no specific person to Jannah or to the Fire without a text.[^7] This protects the heart from two dangers: the vanity of vouching for oneself or for those one loves, and the recklessness of passing judgment on others.

The clearest scene of these glad tidings is the hadith of Abu Musa at the well of Aris: glad tidings delivered at a gate, one person after another, and with them, for Uthman, news of a trial to come. The glad tidings were never an exemption from testing; they were a certainty that would carry him through it.

### The Hadith of the Well of Aris: "Let Him In, and Give Him the Glad Tidings of Jannah"

> عَنْ سَعِيدِ بْنِ الْمُسَيَّبِ، قَالَ: أَخْبَرَنِي أَبُو مُوسَى الْأَشْعَرِيُّ رضي الله عنه، أَنَّهُ تَوَضَّأَ فِي بَيْتِهِ ثُمَّ خَرَجَ، فَقُلْتُ: لَأَلْزَمَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم، وَلَأَكُونَنَّ مَعَهُ يَوْمِي هَذَا. قَالَ: فَجَاءَ الْمَسْجِدَ، فَسَأَلَ عَنِ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم، فَقَالُوا: خَرَجَ وَوَجَّهَ هَا هُنَا، فَخَرَجْتُ عَلَى إِثْرِهِ أَسْأَلُ عَنْهُ، حَتَّى دَخَلَ بِئْرَ أَرِيسٍ، فَجَلَسْتُ عِنْدَ الْبَابِ، وَبَابُهَا مِنْ جَرِيدٍ، حَتَّى قَضَى رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم حَاجَتَهُ فَتَوَضَّأَ، فَقُمْتُ إِلَيْهِ، فَإِذَا هُوَ جَالِسٌ عَلَى بِئْرِ أَرِيسٍ، وَتَوَسَّطَ قُفَّهَا، وَكَشَفَ عَنْ سَاقَيْهِ وَدَلَّاهُمَا فِي الْبِئْرِ، فَسَلَّمْتُ عَلَيْهِ ثُمَّ انْصَرَفْتُ فَجَلَسْتُ عِنْدَ الْبَابِ، فَقُلْتُ: لَأَكُونَنَّ بَوَّابَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم الْيَوْمَ.
>
> فَجَاءَ أَبُو بَكْرٍ فَدَفَعَ الْبَابَ، فَقُلْتُ: مَنْ هَذَا؟ فَقَالَ: أَبُو بَكْرٍ. فَقُلْتُ: عَلَى رِسْلِكَ. ثُمَّ ذَهَبْتُ فَقُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ، هَذَا أَبُو بَكْرٍ يَسْتَأْذِنُ. فَقَالَ: **«ائْذَنْ لَهُ وَبَشِّرْهُ بِالْجَنَّةِ»**. فَأَقْبَلْتُ حَتَّى قُلْتُ لِأَبِي بَكْرٍ: ادْخُلْ، وَرَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم يُبَشِّرُكَ بِالْجَنَّةِ. فَدَخَلَ أَبُو بَكْرٍ فَجَلَسَ عَنْ يَمِينِ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم مَعَهُ فِي الْقُفِّ، وَدَلَّى رِجْلَيْهِ فِي الْبِئْرِ كَمَا صَنَعَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم، وَكَشَفَ عَنْ سَاقَيْهِ. ثُمَّ رَجَعْتُ فَجَلَسْتُ، وَقَدْ تَرَكْتُ أَخِي يَتَوَضَّأُ وَيَلْحَقُنِي، فَقُلْتُ: إِنْ يُرِدِ اللَّهُ بِفُلَانٍ خَيْرًا -يُرِيدُ أَخَاهُ- يَأْتِ بِهِ.
>
> فَإِذَا إِنْسَانٌ يُحَرِّكُ الْبَابَ، فَقُلْتُ: مَنْ هَذَا؟ فَقَالَ: عُمَرُ بْنُ الْخَطَّابِ. فَقُلْتُ: عَلَى رِسْلِكَ. ثُمَّ جِئْتُ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَسَلَّمْتُ عَلَيْهِ، فَقُلْتُ: هَذَا عُمَرُ بْنُ الْخَطَّابِ يَسْتَأْذِنُ. فَقَالَ: **«ائْذَنْ لَهُ وَبَشِّرْهُ بِالْجَنَّةِ»**. فَجِئْتُ فَقُلْتُ: ادْخُلْ، وَبَشَّرَكَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم بِالْجَنَّةِ. فَدَخَلَ فَجَلَسَ مَعَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فِي الْقُفِّ عَنْ يَسَارِهِ، وَدَلَّى رِجْلَيْهِ فِي الْبِئْرِ. ثُمَّ رَجَعْتُ فَجَلَسْتُ، فَقُلْتُ: إِنْ يُرِدِ اللَّهُ بِفُلَانٍ خَيْرًا يَأْتِ بِهِ.
>
> فَجَاءَ إِنْسَانٌ يُحَرِّكُ الْبَابَ، فَقُلْتُ: مَنْ هَذَا؟ فَقَالَ: عُثْمَانُ بْنُ عَفَّانَ. فَقُلْتُ: عَلَى رِسْلِكَ. فَجِئْتُ إِلَى رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَأَخْبَرْتُهُ، فَقَالَ: **«ائْذَنْ لَهُ وَبَشِّرْهُ بِالْجَنَّةِ عَلَى بَلْوَى تُصِيبُهُ»**. فَجِئْتُهُ فَقُلْتُ لَهُ: ادْخُلْ، وَبَشَّرَكَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم بِالْجَنَّةِ عَلَى بَلْوَى تُصِيبُكَ. فَدَخَلَ فَوَجَدَ الْقُفَّ قَدْ مُلِئَ، فَجَلَسَ وِجَاهَهُ مِنَ الشِّقِّ الْآخَرِ. قَالَ شَرِيكٌ: قَالَ سَعِيدُ بْنُ الْمُسَيَّبِ: فَأَوَّلْتُهَا قُبُورَهُمْ.[^1]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab said: Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, told me that he made wudu' in his house and then went out, and said: I told myself, "I will stay close to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and spend this whole day of mine with him." He said: He came to the mosque and asked about the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and they said, "He went out and headed that way." So I set off on his trail, asking after him, until he went into the enclosure of the well of Aris. I sat down by the gate, which was made of palm stalks, until the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, had relieved himself and made wudu'. Then I went over to him, and there he was, sitting on the well of Aris, in the middle of its raised stone rim, his shins uncovered and his legs dangling into the well. I greeted him with salam, then went back and sat down by the gate, and said to myself, "Today I will be the doorkeeper of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him."
>
> Then Abu Bakr came and pushed at the gate. I said, "Who is it?" He said, "Abu Bakr." I said, "Just a moment." Then I went and said, "O Messenger of Allah, here is Abu Bakr asking permission to come in." He said: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah."** So I came back and said to Abu Bakr, "Come in, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, gives you the glad tidings of Jannah." Abu Bakr came in and sat on the right of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, beside him on the rim, and let his legs dangle into the well just as the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had done, uncovering his shins. Then I went back and sat down. I had left my brother making wudu', about to join me, so I said, "If Allah wants good for so-and-so" (meaning his brother), "He will bring him."
>
> Then someone rattled the gate. I said, "Who is it?" He said, "Umar ibn al-Khattab." I said, "Just a moment." Then I came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, greeted him with salam, and said, "Here is Umar ibn al-Khattab asking permission to come in." He said: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah."** So I came and said, "Come in, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, has given you the glad tidings of Jannah." He came in and sat with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, on the rim, at his left, and let his legs dangle into the well. Then I went back and sat down, and said, "If Allah wants good for so-and-so, He will bring him."
>
> Then someone came and rattled the gate. I said, "Who is it?" He said, "Uthman ibn Affan." I said, "Just a moment." I went to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and told him, and he said: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah, along with a trial that will befall him."** So I came to him and said, "Come in, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, has given you the glad tidings of Jannah, along with a trial that will befall you." He came in and found the rim full, so he sat facing them on the other side. Sharik said: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab said: "I took it to signify their graves."[^1]

#### Scholarly Explanation

*Al-quff* is the raised stonework built around the mouth of a well, and *'ala rislik* means "take your time; wait a moment." Ibn Hajar explains that Ibn al-Musayyab's interpretation refers to Abu Bakr and Umar being buried beside the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, while Uthman was buried apart from them, in al-Baqi'; and that "the trial" (*al-balwa*) was the strife that befell him until he was killed unjustly.[^8]

#### Lesson Explanation

The glad tidings here are news from revelation, not a human estimate. Abu Musa vouched for no one on his own authority; he simply passed on what he was told. And his wish that his brother would come and share in the very same glad tidings is a portrait of a heart that loves good for others.

### The Ten Promised Jannah

> عَنْ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنِ عَوْفٍ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«أَبُو بَكْرٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَعُمَرُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَعُثْمَانُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَعَلِيٌّ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَطَلْحَةُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَالزُّبَيْرُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَعَبْدُ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنُ عَوْفٍ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَسَعْدٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَسَعِيدٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَأَبُو عُبَيْدَةَ بْنُ الْجَرَّاحِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ»**.[^2]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Abu Bakr is in Jannah, Umar is in Jannah, Uthman is in Jannah, Ali is in Jannah, Talhah is in Jannah, al-Zubayr is in Jannah, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf is in Jannah, Sa'd is in Jannah, Sa'id is in Jannah, and Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah is in Jannah."**[^2]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Sa'd is Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, and Sa'id is Sa'id ibn Zayd. Naming these ten does not restrict the people of Jannah to them, since authentic texts have named others as well; but these ten were gathered together in a single statement, and so they became famous as "the Ten."

#### Lesson Explanation

Ten names, and behind each one a life story of being first to believe and of giving everything. Knowing their names is where the path begins; knowing their deeds is what turns the glad tidings into an example to follow.

### The Two Leaders of the Youth of the People of Jannah

> عَنْ أَبِي سَعِيدٍ الْخُدْرِيِّ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«الْحَسَنُ وَالْحُسَيْنُ سَيِّدَا شَبَابِ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ»**.[^3]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Al-Hasan and al-Husayn are the leaders of the youth of the people of Jannah."**[^3]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The scholars mention that this means either their leadership over those of the people of Jannah who died young, or their preeminence over the people of Jannah apart from the prophets and the Rightly Guided Caliphs.

#### Lesson Explanation

The two grandsons of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, belong to his family, and loving them and loving his Companions are both part of faith; the two loves do not compete.

### Fatimah, the Leader of the Women of the People of Jannah

> عَنْ عَائِشَةَ رضي الله عنها، قَالَتْ: أَقْبَلَتْ فَاطِمَةُ تَمْشِي كَأَنَّ مِشْيَتَهَا مَشْيُ النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم، فَقَالَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم: **«مَرْحَبًا بِابْنَتِي»**. ثُمَّ أَجْلَسَهَا عَنْ يَمِينِهِ أَوْ عَنْ شِمَالِهِ، ثُمَّ أَسَرَّ إِلَيْهَا حَدِيثًا فَبَكَتْ، فَقُلْتُ لَهَا: لِمَ تَبْكِينَ؟ ثُمَّ أَسَرَّ إِلَيْهَا حَدِيثًا فَضَحِكَتْ، فَقُلْتُ: مَا رَأَيْتُ كَالْيَوْمِ فَرَحًا أَقْرَبَ مِنْ حُزْنٍ. فَسَأَلْتُهَا عَمَّا قَالَ، فَقَالَتْ: مَا كُنْتُ لِأُفْشِيَ سِرَّ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم. حَتَّى قُبِضَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَسَأَلْتُهَا، فَقَالَتْ: أَسَرَّ إِلَيَّ: **«إِنَّ جِبْرِيلَ كَانَ يُعَارِضُنِي الْقُرْآنَ كُلَّ سَنَةٍ مَرَّةً، وَإِنَّهُ عَارَضَنِي الْعَامَ مَرَّتَيْنِ، وَلَا أُرَاهُ إِلَّا حَضَرَ أَجَلِي، وَإِنَّكِ أَوَّلُ أَهْلِ بَيْتِي لَحَاقًا بِي»**. فَبَكَيْتُ، فَقَالَ: **«أَمَا تَرْضَيْنَ أَنْ تَكُونِي سَيِّدَةَ نِسَاءِ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ، أَوْ نِسَاءِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ»**. فَضَحِكْتُ لِذَلِكَ.[^4]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Aishah, may Allah be pleased with her, who said: Fatimah came walking toward us, and her walk was just like the walk of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Welcome, my daughter."** Then he seated her at his right, or at his left, and told her something in confidence, and she wept. I said to her, "Why are you weeping?" Then he told her something else in confidence, and she laughed. I said, "I have never seen a day like today, with joy so close to sorrow." I asked her what he had said, and she said, "I would never disclose the secret of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him." Then, after the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, had passed away, I asked her, and she said: He told me in confidence: **"Jibril used to review the Qur'an with me once every year, and this year he has reviewed it with me twice. I can only take it to mean that my appointed time has come, and you will be the first of my family to follow me."** So I wept. Then he said: **"Are you not content to be the leader of the women of the people of Jannah (or: of the women of the believers)?"** And at that I laughed.[^4]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The words "or: of the women of the believers" reflect the narrator's uncertainty about the exact wording; either way the meaning stands. The hadith also shows that the glad tidings reached Fatimah, may Allah be pleased with her, in a moment of grief, and turned her tears into contentment.

#### Lesson Explanation

Fatimah kept her father's secret even when Aishah asked her about it, and the glad tidings were a consolation to her, not a cause for pride.

### The Best of Its Women Is Maryam, and the Best of Its Women Is Khadijah

> عَنْ عَلِيٍّ رضي الله عنه، قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ النَّبِيَّ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقُولُ: **«خَيْرُ نِسَائِهَا مَرْيَمُ ابْنَةُ عِمْرَانَ، وَخَيْرُ نِسَائِهَا خَدِيجَةُ»**.[^5]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> On the authority of Ali, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: I heard the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, say: **"The best of its women is Maryam, daughter of Imran, and the best of its women is Khadijah."**[^5]

#### Scholarly Explanation

The commentators explain that the meaning is this: Maryam was the best of the women of her time, and Khadijah the best of the women of this ummah in her time; each was preeminent within her own community.

#### Lesson Explanation

The merit to which revelation testifies does not belong to men alone. Here are two women who believed and endured, and Allah raised their remembrance high.

<!-- evidence:end -->

<!-- evidence:start id="quran-9-100" kind="quran" mode="canonical" -->

### "The First Forerunners... and Those Who Followed Them in Excellence"

> **{وَالسَّابِقُونَ الْأَوَّلُونَ مِنَ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ وَالَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُمْ بِإِحْسَانٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ وَأَعَدَّ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي تَحْتَهَا الْأَنْهَارُ خَالِدِينَ فِيهَا أَبَدًا ۚ ذَٰلِكَ الْفَوْزُ الْعَظِيمُ}** [التوبة: ١٠٠][^6]

<!-- evidence:translation -->

> **"And the first forerunners among the Emigrants and the Helpers, and those who followed them in excellence: Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him, and He has prepared for them gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will abide forever. That is the supreme triumph."** (at-Tawbah 9:100)[^6]

#### Scholarly Explanation

Ibn Kathir notes that Allah has announced His good pleasure with the forerunners among the Emigrants (*al-Muhajirun*) and the Helpers (*al-Ansar*) and with those who followed them in excellence, and that woe awaits whoever hates them or reviles them.[^9]

#### Lesson Explanation

The door did not close after them. `And those who followed them in excellence` is an invitation to every generation to join them by following them well.

<!-- evidence:end -->

### A True Account: The Little Doorkeeper by the Well

<!-- story:start audience="4-7" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3674" authenticated="true" -->

**This really happened to a Companion named Abu Musa, may Allah be pleased with him. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3674" audience="4-7" -->

Abu Musa made wudu' at home and said, "Today I'm going to stay with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, all day long!" He walked along, asking where the Prophet was, until he found him sitting on the edge of a well called the well of Aris. The place had a gate made of palm branches. Abu Musa said salam to him, then sat down by the gate and said, "Today, I'll be the doorkeeper."

Along came Abu Bakr and pushed the gate. "Wait just a moment," said Abu Musa. He went to ask the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who said: `Let him in, and give him the happy news of Jannah.` So Abu Musa opened the gate and told him the happy news, and Abu Bakr came in and sat down next to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him.

Now, Abu Musa had left his brother at home making wudu'. So he said to himself, "If Allah wants good for my brother, He will bring him here!" He wanted good things for his brother, too.

Then Umar came, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: `Let him in, and give him the happy news of Jannah.` Then Uthman came, and he said: `Let him in, and give him the happy news of Jannah`, and told him that he would go through a hard test. ## For Children Ages 8 to 12

<!-- reader:start audience="8-12" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="8-12.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

Can anyone say about a friend, "He's definitely going to Jannah"? No, because that is knowledge only Allah has. Among them are the ten whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, named in a single hadith: Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talhah, al-Zubayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Sa'id ibn Zayd, and Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah, may Allah be pleased with them.[^2] He also told us that al-Hasan and al-Husayn are the leaders of the youth of the people of Jannah,[^3] and that his daughter Fatimah is the leader of the women of the people of Jannah.[^4]

### A True Account: Abu Musa, Doorkeeper at the Well of Aris

<!-- story:start audience="8-12" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3674" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account that Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, narrated himself, recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not a made-up story.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3674" audience="8-12" -->

One day Abu Musa made wudu' at home and set out, saying to himself, "I will stick close to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and spend this whole day with him." He didn't find him at the mosque, so he followed his trail, asking around, until the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, went into a place with a well called the well of Aris. Its gate was made of palm stalks.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, sat on the stone rim, the *quff*, with his legs dangling into the well. Abu Musa greeted him with salam, then went back and sat by the gate, and made a decision: "Today I will be the doorkeeper of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him."

Someone pushed the gate, and Abu Musa asked, "Who is it?" "Abu Bakr." "*'Ala rislik*," he said, which means "Just a moment." He didn't open the gate on his own say-so. Instead he went to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, to ask permission, and the Prophet said: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah."** So he went back and said, "Come in, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, gives you the glad tidings of Jannah." Abu Bakr sat at the right of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and let his legs dangle into the well just as he had.

Abu Musa had left his brother making wudu', and his brother was going to catch up with him, so he said, "If Allah wants good for so-and-so, He will bring him." Next came Umar ibn al-Khattab. Abu Musa asked permission for him, the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said the very same words, and Umar sat on his left. Then came Uthman ibn Affan, and the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah, along with a trial that will befall him."** Uthman came in, found the rim already full, and sat facing them on the other side.[^1]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Abu Musa didn't hand out glad tidings based on his own opinion. He passed on the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, exactly as they were said, and he wished good for his brother. And Uthman was promised Jannah together with a test still to come, which shows that glad tidings don't mean an easy life.

## For Teens, Ages 13+

<!-- reader:start audience="13+" estimated_minutes="6.5" -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.explanation" kind="explanation" -->

In a world where people hand out titles like candy ("the GOAT," "a legend," "an icon"), this lesson teaches that real validation does not come from the crowd; it comes from Allah. No specific person has ever been declared bound for Jannah except through revelation: the ten promised Jannah;[^2] al-Hasan and al-Husayn, the leaders of the youth of the people of Jannah;[^3] Fatimah, the leader of its women;[^4] and Maryam and Khadijah, the best of the women of their times.[^5] We do not declare it certain for any particular person without a text, but we hope for those who do good and fear for ourselves.[^7]

Here is the surprising part: those who were promised Jannah did not treat the promise like a graduation certificate. They kept working, giving, and enduring. And the door is open to us, too: `and those who followed them in excellence`.[^6]

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.terms" kind="terms" -->

<!-- terminology:start source_id="bukhari-3674" -->

- **`Those promised Jannah (al-mubashsharun bil-jannah)`** — the people whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, named as being among the people of Jannah; the best known of them are the Ten.
- **`Vouching (at-tazkiyah)`** — declaring someone righteous; it cannot be asserted with certainty about the unseen without a text, and Allah has forbidden vouching for oneself out of pride.
- **`Along with a trial that will befall him ('ala balwa tusibuh)`** — that is, together with a severe test that would come to him; the scholars explained it as the strife in which Uthman, may Allah be pleased with him, was killed unjustly.

<!-- terminology:end -->

<!-- unit:end -->

<!-- unit:start id="13+.primary-story" kind="primary_story" -->

### A True Account: The Day Abu Musa Became the Doorkeeper of Glad Tidings

<!-- story:start audience="13+" role="primary" type="prophetic_era" source_id="bukhari-3674" authenticated="true" -->

**This is a true account narrated by Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, may Allah be pleased with him, in Sahih al-Bukhari. It is not an imagined scene.**

<!-- retelling:start source_id="bukhari-3674" audience="13+" -->

Abu Musa's day began with a personal decision. He made wudu' and went out, saying, "I will stick close to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and spend this whole day with him." Not finding him at the mosque, he tracked him down to the well of Aris, whose gate was made of palm stalks. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, was sitting on the well's stone rim with his legs dangling into it. Abu Musa greeted him, then chose for himself a role no one had asked him to take: "Today I will be the doorkeeper of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him."

Abu Bakr arrived. "Just a moment," Abu Musa told him, and he did not open the gate until he had asked permission. The answer came: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah."** He passed it on, and Abu Bakr sat at the right of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him. In a lull while he waited, Abu Musa remembered the brother he had left making wudu', and said, "If Allah wants good for so-and-so, He will bring him." He wanted the same glad tidings for his brother. Then Umar arrived, received the glad tidings, and sat on the left.

Then came Uthman, and this time the answer was different: **"Let him in, and give him the glad tidings of Jannah, along with a trial that will befall him."** Glad tidings and a warning of hardship, in a single sentence. He came in, found the rim full, and sat facing them on the other side. Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, one of the Successors (the generation after the Companions), later read this seating as a sign of their graves: Abu Bakr and Umar were buried beside the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and Uthman was buried somewhere else.[^1][^8]

<!-- retelling:end -->

Think about it: Abu Musa was not one of those receiving glad tidings in that gathering, yet for him the scene never turned into a comparison. His job was to deliver the message accurately and to wish good for others. And Uthman heard his glad tidings together with news of his ordeal, then lived on for years of giving, knowing all the while that the trial was coming.

<!-- story:end -->

## Detailed Teaching Plans

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="adults" minutes="60" -->

### Adults — 60 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The learner narrates the hadith of the well of Aris in sequence; names the ten promised Jannah and those whose leadership in Jannah is stated explicitly, together with the source of each text; explains the boundary laid down by the people of the Sunnah, namely bearing witness to Jannah for those whom revelation named and hoping for it for everyone else; and writes an honest glad-tidings message that keeps what was witnessed separate from what is hoped for.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Printed copies of the six pieces of evidence; a mushaf for the ayah of at-Tawbah 9:100; a board divided into two columns headed "Witness by text" and "Hope"; blank cards for the messages; private notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher reads the full hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari (Book of the Virtues of the Companions of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him); reviews Ibn Hajar's commentary on the meaning of *al-quff*, Ibn al-Musayyab's interpretation, and the explanation of "the trial"; reviews what at-Tahawi set down about bearing witness for the Ten; and prepares a one-line profile of each of the Ten.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "If someone told you today, with certainty, that you are one of the people of Jannah, what would change in your day?" Listen to two or three answers, then say: "Today we will see how those who really were given that news received it."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 20 minutes:** Read the hadith of the well of Aris in full, by a single reader, pausing at three points: Abu Musa's decision to be the doorkeeper, his wish for good for his brother, and the difference in Uthman's glad tidings. Then read briefly the hadiths of the Ten, of al-Hasan and al-Husayn, of Fatimah, and of Maryam and Khadijah, and close with the ayah of at-Tawbah 9:100. After each text, ask: "Who gave the news? And who passed it on?"

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** The teacher and the group fill in the two columns on the board together: names for which a text exists go under "Witness by text," while "a righteous relative of ours who has passed away" and "a famous scholar" go under "Hope." Say: "We love the first group and bear witness for them as revelation did; we hope for the second, and we vouch for no one before Allah." Then discuss: why did Uthman's glad tidings come paired with a trial?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 15 minutes:** Participants carry out the "Honest Glad-Tidings Card" activity as described in the activity unit: each writes their message, then the two lines "What I witnessed" and "What I only hope for" in their private notebook. Two volunteers read their messages aloud without naming the recipient.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 10 minutes:** Exit card: (1) Name five of the Ten. (2) Write one sentence distinguishing bearing witness from hoping. (3) What is the lesson of Uthman's glad tidings? Close with the question: "How can I be one of those who followed them in excellence this week?"

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For beginners, cover only the hadith of the well of Aris, the hadith of the Ten, and the ayah of at-Tawbah. Ask advanced learners to compare the narration in al-Bukhari 3674 with the narration of Abu Uthman al-Nahdi from Abu Musa in the same chapter, and to prepare a short profile of one of the Ten from the books of sirah.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Guard against turning the lesson into a polemical ranking of the Companions against the Prophet's family, or into reviling or attacking anyone; the approach taught here is to love them all and speak of them only with good. Do not allow anyone to declare Jannah or the Fire certain for specific people, whether living or deceased. Be mindful of those who have lost a relative: hoping for their loved one is legitimate and encouraged, and this lesson takes nothing away from it.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="4-7" minutes="25" -->

### Children Ages 4-7 — 25 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The child retells the story using three knocks at the door, names Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman, says that it was the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, who gave the glad tidings, and makes a card for a family member in whom they saw something good.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** A small cardboard door, or the door of the room; card stock and child-safe crayons or markers; the du'a card in clear handwriting.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The caregiver practices telling the story in a calm voice with real knocks on the door, and prepares the three names written in large letters. No picture of any Companion or of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, is shown.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 3 minutes:** Ask: "What's the best news you've ever heard? How did it make you feel?" Then say: "Today we're going to hear about the very best news of all."

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Reading the Story — 7 minutes:** The caregiver tells the story as given in the story unit, knocking on the door each time a Companion arrives, and the children repeat together: "Let him in, and give him the happy news of Jannah."

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 4 minutes:** Ask: "Did Abu Musa make up the happy news himself?" and stress: "No, he asked the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, first." Then ask: "What did he wish for his brother?" and stress that wanting good for others is a beautiful way to be.

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 7 minutes:** Carry out the "Honest Glad-Tidings Card" activity: drawing the door and the kind deed, then giving the card to a family member with the suggested sentence.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 4 minutes:** Notice whether the child can name at least one of the three, and whether they know who gave the glad tidings. Then read the bedtime du'a together.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For the youngest, one knock and the name Abu Bakr are enough, and the child may say the happy news aloud instead of drawing. Ask older children to tell the story to a younger sibling.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not mention any details of how Uthman, may Allah be pleased with him, was killed; "a hard test" is enough. Never tell a child "You're going to Jannah" as a reward; say instead, "I ask Allah to make you one of the people of Jannah."

### Children Ages 8-12 — 40 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student retells the events of the story in order, names the ten promised Jannah, explains the three terms, distinguishes between "I saw" and "I hope" when speaking about people, and writes an honest glad-tidings card.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** Cards with the names of the Ten; a simple drawing of the well, the rim, and the gate on the board; blank cards; notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher draws a seating diagram of the gathering: the rim in the middle, with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, in the center represented only by a dot or a word, and arrows marking where Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman sat. The teacher also prepares a one-sentence introduction for each of the Ten.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Ask: "Have you ever heard someone say about a person, 'He's going to Jannah for sure'? Can anyone actually know that?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Story — 10 minutes:** Read the story aloud while students place the three names in their seats on the diagram, explaining each term as it comes up.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Play "Who Said It?": the teacher reads out statements, and students hold up a card reading "Revelation" or "Opinion." Then hand out the cards of the Ten; students put them in order and read the hadith. Discuss: why was Uthman also told of a trial?

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 10 minutes:** Carry out the "Honest Glad-Tidings Card" activity as described in the activity unit. The teacher checks that every card names a specific, real deed, without exaggeration and without any claim of certainty.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 5 minutes:** Each student writes two names from the Ten, the meaning of "along with a trial that will befall him," and one correctly worded sentence asking Allah to grant someone Jannah. Then read the du'a.

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, present the story in four illustrated panels with no faces, and ask for only five of the Ten. Ask advanced students to research one well-known deed of one of the Ten and present it in a minute.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Cards are written for people the student knows and are handed over under supervision; no one is made to read their card aloud. Avoid any talk that belittles a Companion or a member of the Prophet's family. Do not go into the details of the strife.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->

<!-- lesson-plan:start audience="13+" minutes="45" -->

### Teens, Ages 13+ — 45 Minutes

<!-- lesson-plan:outcomes -->
**Learning Outcomes:** The student analyzes how Abu Musa responded to watching others receive glad tidings, explains the meaning of glad tidings paired with a trial, sets out the boundary against declaring certainty for any particular person without a text, and applies all of this in an honest private message and two lines of reflection.

<!-- lesson-plan:materials -->
**Materials:** The full text of the hadith from the adults' academic section; the ayah of at-Tawbah 9:100; a two-column worksheet; private notebooks.

<!-- lesson-plan:preparation -->
**Preparation:** The teacher prepares two neutral, current examples of over-the-top titles on social media, reviews Ibn al-Musayyab's interpretation and the explanation of "the trial," and prepares a calm answer to a likely question about the disagreements among the Companions.

<!-- lesson-plan:opening -->
**Opening — 5 minutes:** Show the two examples and ask: "Who has the power to give a title that never fades? And who gave Abu Bakr, Umar, and Uthman theirs?"

<!-- lesson-plan:evidence -->
**Studying the Evidence — 12 minutes:** Students read the hadith in turns in small groups, and each group identifies one moment of character-defining choice: Abu Musa choosing to be the doorkeeper, his wish for good for his brother, and Uthman receiving glad tidings together with a trial. Then read the ayah of at-Tawbah 9:100.

<!-- lesson-plan:instruction -->
**Guided Instruction — 10 minutes:** Lead a guided discussion using the questions in the questions unit, focusing on the second and fourth. The teacher makes clear: "We bear witness for those to whom the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, bore witness, we hope for everyone else, and we love the Companions and the Prophet's family, all of them."

<!-- lesson-plan:activity -->
**Activity — 12 minutes:** Carry out the "Honest Glad-Tidings Card" activity as described in the activity unit: writing the private message and the two lines of reflection, with a plan to send it within three days.

<!-- lesson-plan:assessment -->
**Assessment and Closing — 6 minutes:** Each student writes a three-sentence paragraph: what they learned from Abu Musa, what they learned from Uthman, and their next step toward being one of those who followed them in excellence. Follow up at the next session with the question: "Did you send the message?"

<!-- lesson-plan:differentiation -->
**Differentiation:** For students who struggle, focus only on the first scene (Abu Bakr) and Uthman's scene. Ask advanced students to prepare a short presentation on one of the Ten, highlighting a deed he became known for after receiving the glad tidings.

<!-- lesson-plan:safeguards -->
**Teaching Cautions:** Do not let the discussion turn into sectarian argument or the reviling of any Companion or any member of the Prophet's family. Be mindful of anyone who has lost a relative: hoping for the deceased is legitimate, and this lesson does not ask anyone to doubt Allah's mercy toward them. Messages stay private and are not posted publicly.

<!-- lesson-plan:end -->
